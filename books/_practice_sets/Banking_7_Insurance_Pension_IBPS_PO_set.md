### Q1. GIC Re was established in:
(a) 1971 (b) 1972 (c) 1973 (d) 1975

**Answer: (a) 1971**

**Solution:** GIC Re (General Insurance Corporation of India) was established in 1971 (incorporated), and became effective from 1 January 1973. GIC Re is the national re-insurer in India. GIC Re was the holding company for the 4 PSU general insurers (National Insurance, New India Assurance, Oriental Insurance, United India Insurance) until they were de-linked in 2000-2001. GIC Re provides reinsurance support to direct insurers. GIC Re is regulated by IRDAI. The current Chairman of GIC Re is Monica Kalyanpur (verify). GIC Re is headquartered in Mumbai.

### Q2. NPS Vatsalya is for:
(a) Senior citizens (b) Women (c) Minors (d) Disabled

**Answer: (c) Minors**

**Solution:** NPS Vatsalya is for minors (children below 18 years). It was launched in 2024 by PFRDA. NPS Vatsalya allows parents/guardians to open a pension account for their children. The account can be converted to a regular NPS Tier I account when the child turns 18. The contributions are made by the parent/guardian. The fund management options are similar to NPS Tier I (Active Choice and Auto Choice). NPS Vatsalya is part of the government's efforts to promote pension coverage from an early age. The scheme is regulated by PFRDA.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/07_Insurance_Pension.md, p.156-165 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q3. The maximum age to open an NPS account is:
(a) 55 (b) 60 (c) 65 (d) 70

**Answer: (c) 65**

**Solution:** The maximum age to open an NPS account is 65 years. NPS is open to all Indian citizens (resident and non-resident) aged 18-65. NPS was originally for government employees (started 1 January 2004) and was opened to all citizens from 1 May 2009. NPS Tier I is locked till 60 (or 65 for late retirement). The minimum contribution for Tier I: ₹500/month or ₹6,000/year. For Tier II: ₹1,000 minimum contribution (no lock-in). NPS is a defined-contribution pension scheme regulated by PFRDA.

### Q4. The pension range under APY is:
(a) ₹500 - ₹2,000 per month (b) ₹1,000 - ₹5,000 per month (c) ₹2,000 - ₹8,000 per month (d) ₹5,000 - ₹10,000 per month

**Answer: (b) ₹1,000 - ₹5,000 per month**

**Solution:** The pension range under APY (Atal Pension Yojana) is ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 per month at age 60. The choice of pension amount depends on the contribution and age of entry. APY is targeted at the unorganized sector. The eligibility is 18-40 years. The scheme is regulated by PFRDA. The contribution chart specifies the monthly contribution for each combination of age and pension amount. APY provides guaranteed pension by the Government of India. The scheme has crossed 9 crore gross enrollments as of 22 April 2026.

### Q6. PMJAY stands for:
(a) Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (b) Pradhan Mantri Jan Awas Yojana (c) Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (d) Pradhan Mantri Jal Yojana

**Answer: (a) Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana**

**Solution:** PMJAY stands for Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana. It is the world's largest health insurance/assurance scheme, launched on 23 September 2018. PMJAY provides health cover of ₹5 lakh per family per year to 50+ crore beneficiaries (bottom 40% of population as per SECC database). It covers secondary and tertiary care hospitalization. The scheme is part of Ayushman Bharat. The PMJAY Vay Vandana Card was announced in September 2024 for senior citizens 70+ years. NHA (National Health Authority) is the implementing agency.

### Q7. The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) is headquartered in:
(a) New Delhi (b) Mumbai (c) Hyderabad (d) Kolkata

**Answer: (a) New Delhi**

**Solution:** PFRDA is headquartered in New Delhi. The interim PFRDA was set up on 23 August 2003. The PFRDA Act was enacted on 19 September 2013 and enforced on 1 February 2014. PFRDA regulates NPS (National Pension System), APY (Atal Pension Yojana), and other pension schemes in India. PFRDA registers and regulates pension funds (PFMs), Points of Presence (POPs), and Central Recordkeeping Agencies (CRAs). The current Chairman is Deepak Mohanty. NPS is mandatory for central government employees (recruited after 1 January 2004) and is voluntary for others.

### Q8. The "premium" in insurance refers to:
(a) Amount paid on claim (b) Amount paid by policyholder to insurer (c) Sum assured (d) Maturity amount

**Answer: (b) Amount paid by policyholder to insurer**

**Solution:** Premium is the amount paid by the policyholder to the insurer for the insurance coverage. The premium is paid at regular intervals (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, or annually). The Sum Assured is the amount paid out on a claim (or on maturity for endowment policies). The Beneficiary is the person receiving the claim amount. The Nominee is the person designated to receive the policy proceeds in case of the policyholder's death. The premium depends on factors like age, sum assured, type of policy, tenure, and risk factors. Insurance penetration in India is low (~4.2% of GDP).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/07_Insurance_Pension.md, p.92-100 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. A "Term Insurance" plan provides:
(a) Insurance + Investment (b) Pure insurance; no maturity benefit (c) Pension only (d) Health insurance only

**Answer: (b) Pure insurance; no maturity benefit**

**Solution:** A Term Insurance plan is a pure insurance plan that provides coverage for a specific term (e.g., 20-30 years) but has no maturity benefit. If the policyholder dies during the term, the nominee gets the sum assured. If the policyholder survives, no payment is made (unlike endowment plans). Term insurance is the cheapest form of life insurance. Other types: Endowment Policy (combines insurance + savings; pays on maturity or death), Whole Life Insurance (covers entire life; pays on death), ULIP (Unit Linked Insurance Plan - insurance + market investment), Annuity (regular payment for a defined period).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/07_Insurance_Pension.md, p.100-110 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. NPS was notified on:
(a) 1 January 2004 (b) 22 December 2003 (c) 1 May 2009 (d) 1 April 2010

**Answer: (b) 22 December 2003**

**Solution:** NPS (National Pension System) was notified on 22 December 2003 and started formally on 1 January 2004 (for government employees). NPS was open to all citizens from 1 May 2009. NPS is a defined-contribution pension scheme. NPS Tier I is the basic pension account (locked till age 60). NPS Tier II is voluntary savings (withdrawable anytime). NPS is regulated by PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority). The NPS was originally called the "New Pension Scheme". NPS is mandatory for central government employees (recruited after 1 January 2004).
