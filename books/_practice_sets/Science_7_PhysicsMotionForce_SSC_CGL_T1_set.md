## Q1. SI unit of force (Easy)
The SI unit of force is:
(a) Joule (b) Newton (c) Watt (d) Pascal

**Answer:** (b) Newton
**Solution:** Force SI unit: newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg·m/s² (force needed to accelerate 1 kg at 1 m/s²). Joule = energy/work; Watt = power; Pascal = pressure.
**Shortcut:** Force = Newton (after Sir Isaac Newton). F = ma → kg·m/s².
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with joule (energy) or watt (power).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Distance vs displacement (Easy)
Distance is a ____ quantity while displacement is a ____ quantity:
(a) Scalar, scalar (b) Vector, vector (c) Scalar, vector (d) Vector, scalar

**Answer:** (c) Scalar, vector
**Solution:** Distance = total path length = scalar (only magnitude). Displacement = shortest straight-line distance between initial and final positions, with direction = vector (magnitude + direction).
**Shortcut:** Distance = odometer (only magnitude); Displacement = arrow (magnitude + direction).
**Common Mistake:** Saying both are vectors (distance is scalar).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q3. SI unit of acceleration (Easy)
The SI unit of acceleration is:
(a) m/s (b) m/s² (c) km/h (d) cm/s²

**Answer:** (b) m/s²
**Solution:** Acceleration = change in velocity / time. SI unit: m/s² (metres per second squared). m/s is velocity; km/h is non-SI unit of speed; cm/s² is CGS unit.
**Shortcut:** m/s = velocity; m/s² = acceleration.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing m/s (that's velocity, not acceleration).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q4. Newton's second law (Easy)
Newton's second law of motion is:
(a) F = ma (b) F = mg (c) F = mv (d) F = mv²

**Answer:** (a) F = ma
**Solution:** Newton's second law: net force F = mass × acceleration (ma). Equivalently F = dp/dt (rate of change of momentum). Newton's three laws: (1) inertia; (2) F = ma; (3) action-reaction.
**Shortcut:** 1st law = inertia; 2nd law = F = ma; 3rd law = action = reaction.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with F = mg (which is weight, a special case).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q5. Free fall acceleration (Medium)
The value of acceleration due to gravity (g) near Earth's surface is approximately:
(a) 9.8 m/s² (b) 10 m/s² (c) 100 m/s² (d) 1 m/s²

**Answer:** (a) 9.8 m/s²
**Solution:** Standard value: g = 9.8 m/s² (often approximated as 10 m/s² for numerical problems). g decreases with altitude and increases with depth to a point. On the Moon, g = 1.63 m/s² (~1/6 of Earth's).
**Shortcut:** g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (≈ 10 for easy maths). Moon's g = 1.63 m/s².
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with 9.8 cm/s² (wrong unit).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q6. Action-reaction pair (Medium)
According to Newton's third law:
(a) Action and reaction act on the same body (b) Action and reaction are equal and opposite, on different bodies (c) Action and reaction cancel each other (d) Action-reaction only exists for stationary objects

**Answer:** (b) Action and reaction are equal and opposite, on different bodies
**Solution:** Newton's third law: for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. The action-reaction pair acts on DIFFERENT bodies — so they never cancel. Example: Earth pulls apple down (gravity); apple pulls Earth up (equal magnitude).
**Shortcut:** Third law = "different bodies, equal+opposite". They never cancel.
**Common Mistake:** Saying they cancel (only true if they act on the same body, which they don't).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q7. Inertia (Medium)
The inertia of a body depends on its:
(a) Shape (b) Mass (c) Volume (d) Temperature

**Answer:** (b) Mass
**Solution:** Inertia is the natural tendency to resist change in motion. Mass is a measure of inertia. Heavier objects have more inertia — harder to start or stop moving. Examples: a cricket ball (heavier) is harder to throw than a tennis ball.
**Shortcut:** Inertia ∝ mass. More mass = more inertia.
**Common Mistake:** Saying shape (shape affects aerodynamics, not inertia).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Momentum (Medium — Trap)
The SI unit of momentum is:
(a) kg·m/s (b) N·s (c) Both (a) and (b) (d) kg·m²/s

**Answer:** (c) Both (a) and (b)
**Solution:** p = mv → kg × m/s = kg·m/s. Also, impulse = F × t = N·s = kg·m/s (since N = kg·m/s²). Both units are equivalent.
**Shortcut:** Momentum = kg·m/s = N·s (equivalent).
**Common Mistake:** Choosing only one (both are correct).
**Time target:** 45 sec

---

## Q9. Conservation of momentum (Medium)
The law of conservation of momentum is valid when:
(a) External forces are acting (b) No external force acts on the system (c) Only internal forces act (d) Both (b) and (c)

**Answer:** (d) Both (b) and (c)
**Solution:** Conservation of momentum: total momentum of an isolated system remains constant. Valid when there are no external forces, OR when only internal forces act (internal forces cancel by Newton's third law).
**Shortcut:** Momentum conserved when net external force = 0.
**Common Mistake:** Saying "external forces are acting" (that would change the momentum).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q10. Friction direction (Medium)
Friction always acts:
(a) In the direction of motion (b) Opposite to the direction of motion (c) Perpendicular to motion (d) At 45° to motion

**Answer:** (b) Opposite to the direction of motion
**Solution:** Friction is a force that opposes relative motion between two surfaces in contact. It acts parallel to the surface and opposite to the direction of motion (or intended motion for static friction).
**Shortcut:** Friction always opposes motion (or tendency to move).
**Common Mistake:** Saying in the direction of motion (then it would aid motion, which is impossible for friction).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q11. Work formula (Medium)
Work done by a force is given by:
(a) W = F × d × sin θ (b) W = F × d × cos θ (c) W = F × d × tan θ (d) W = F/d

**Answer:** (b) W = F × d × cos θ
**Solution:** W = F · d · cos θ, where θ is the angle between force and displacement. Maximum when θ = 0° (force parallel to displacement). Zero when θ = 90° (force perpendicular).
**Shortcut:** W = Fd cos θ. θ = 0° → max work; θ = 90° → zero work.
**Common Mistake:** Using sin instead of cos.
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q12. Power (Medium — Trap)
Power is defined as:
(a) Rate of doing work (b) Work × time (c) Force × velocity² (d) Mass × acceleration

**Answer:** (a) Rate of doing work
**Solution:** Power = rate of doing work = W/t. SI unit: watt (W) = J/s. Also, P = F·v (instantaneous power). 1 horsepower (HP) ≈ 746 W.
**Shortcut:** Power = Work/time. P = W/t = Fv.
**Common Mistake:** Saying "work × time" (that's energy, not power).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q13. Falling object velocity (Hard)
A ball is dropped from rest. After 2 seconds (using g = 10 m/s²), its velocity is:
(a) 10 m/s (b) 15 m/s (c) 20 m/s (d) 25 m/s

**Answer:** (c) 20 m/s
**Solution:** From rest, u = 0, a = g = 10 m/s². v = u + at = 0 + 10 × 2 = 20 m/s.
**Shortcut:** v = gt (from rest). v = 10 × 2 = 20 m/s.
**Common Mistake:** Using wrong formula (e.g., s = ut + ½at²).
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q14. KE calculation (Hard)
A 2 kg object moves at 3 m/s. Its kinetic energy is:
(a) 3 J (b) 6 J (c) 9 J (d) 18 J

**Answer:** (c) 9 J
**Solution:** KE = ½mv² = ½ × 2 × 3² = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.
**Shortcut:** KE = ½mv². Here: ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting the ½ factor (would give 18 J).
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q15. Universal law of gravitation (Hard)
According to Newton's law of gravitation, the force between two masses is:
(a) Directly proportional to the product of masses (b) Inversely proportional to the square of distance (c) Both (a) and (b) (d) Independent of distance

**Answer:** (c) Both (a) and (b)
**Solution:** Newton's law of gravitation: F = G × (m₁ × m₂)/r², where G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (gravitational constant). F ∝ m₁m₂ (product of masses) AND F ∝ 1/r² (inverse square of distance).
**Shortcut:** F = Gm₁m₂/r². G ≈ 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ (universal constant).
**Common Mistake:** Choosing only one of (a) or (b) (both apply).
**Time target:** 75 sec
