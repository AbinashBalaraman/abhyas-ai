### Q1. The MPC was constituted on:
(a) 1 April 2016 (b) 29 September 2016 (c) 1 January 2016 (d) 15 May 2016

**Answer: (b) 29 September 2016**

**Solution:** The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) was constituted on 29 September 2016 under Sections 45ZA and 45ZB of the RBI Act, 1934 (inserted by the Finance Act 2016). The MPC decides the policy repo rate to maintain inflation at 4% CPI (with 2-6% tolerance band). The MPC has 6 members: RBI Governor (Chairperson), RBI Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, one RBI officer nominated by Central Board, and three external members. The MPC meets at least 4 times a year (typically 6).

### Q2. The number of MPC members is:
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 7

**Answer: (c) 6**

**Solution:** The MPC has 6 members: 3 from RBI (Governor as Chairperson, RBI Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, and one RBI officer nominated by the Central Board) and 3 external members appointed by the Central Government. Each member has one vote; in case of tie, the Governor has casting vote. Quorum requires 4 members. External members have a 4-year term. The current external members (notified 1 October 2024) are Dr. Nagesh Kumar, Saugata Bhattacharya, and Prof. Ram Singh.

### Q3. The MPC's inflation target is:
(a) 3% ± 2% (b) 4% ± 2% (c) 5% ± 3% (d) 6% ± 2%

**Answer: (b) 4% ± 2%**

**Solution:** The MPC's inflation target is 4% CPI with a tolerance band of ±2%, meaning the acceptable range is 2% to 6%. If average inflation falls outside this band for 3 consecutive quarters, RBI must submit a report to the Central Government explaining reasons and remedial actions. The target was retained for 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031 (announced 25 March 2026). The previous target was retained in 2021. The framework was introduced in 2016.

### Q4. SDF was introduced in:
(a) April 2020 (b) April 2022 (c) April 2024 (d) April 2016

**Answer: (b) April 2022**

**Solution:** SDF (Standing Deposit Facility) was introduced in April 2022 to replace the fixed Reverse Repo Rate as the floor of the LAF corridor. SDF is an uncollateralised overnight deposit facility at a market-determined rate. The SDF Rate is 25 bps below the Repo Rate. Reverse Repo Rate still exists but is at RBI's discretion (currently 3.35% as of mid-2025, may vary). The Bank Rate is aligned with the MSF Rate under Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934.

### Q5. The LAF corridor ceiling rate is:
(a) Repo Rate (b) SDF Rate (c) MSF Rate (d) Bank Rate

**Answer: (c) MSF Rate**

**Solution:** The MSF (Marginal Standing Facility) Rate is the ceiling of the LAF corridor. It is a penal rate (25 bps above the Repo Rate) for emergency overnight borrowing by banks. Banks can dip into their SLR portfolio up to 2% below the prescribed SLR to borrow under MSF. The Bank Rate is aligned with the MSF Rate under Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934. The corridor structure: SDF (floor, 25 bps below Repo) → Repo (mid, policy rate) → MSF (ceiling, 25 bps above Repo). Corridor width: 50 bps.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/03_Monetary_Policy_Rates.md, p.97-135 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. The Bank Rate is aligned with:
(a) Repo Rate (b) MSF Rate (c) SDF Rate (d) Reverse Repo Rate

**Answer: (b) MSF Rate**

**Solution:** The Bank Rate is aligned with the MSF Rate under Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934. Both Bank Rate and MSF Rate move together. The Bank Rate is the rate at which RBI is prepared to buy or rediscount bills of exchange or other commercial paper. The Bank Rate is not actively used as a policy tool but signals RBI's policy stance. SDF Rate is the floor (25 bps below Repo), Repo is mid, and MSF is ceiling (25 bps above Repo). Corridor width is 50 bps.

### Q7. CRR is maintained under Section:
(a) Section 22, RBI Act (b) Section 42(1), RBI Act (c) Section 49, RBI Act (d) Section 35A, BR Act

**Answer: (b) Section 42(1), RBI Act**

**Solution:** The CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio) is maintained under Section 42(1) of the RBI Act, 1934. Banks must maintain a specified percentage of their Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) as cash balance with RBI. CRR is maintained daily. Currently CRR is around 4% (varies with policy). No interest is paid on CRR balances (until 2007; partial interest from 2007). Increasing CRR reduces lendable funds and tightens liquidity, while decreasing CRR expands liquidity.

### Q8. SLR is maintained under:
(a) Section 24, BR Act (b) Section 35A, BR Act (c) Section 49, RBI Act (d) Section 42, RBI Act

**Answer: (a) Section 24, BR Act**

**Solution:** SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio) is maintained under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Banks must maintain a specified percentage of their Net Demand and Time Liabilities in: Government securities, cash, and gold. SLR is currently 18%. Banks can maintain SLR in the form of gold (with RBI), Government of India securities, State Government securities (SDL), and other approved securities. SLR is maintained daily on a fortnightly average basis. SLR serves as a buffer for liquidity.

### Q9. In case of a tie in MPC vote, who has the casting vote?
(a) RBI Governor (b) Deputy Governor (c) Senior-most external member (d) PM

**Answer: (a) RBI Governor**

**Solution:** In case of a tie in MPC vote, the RBI Governor has the casting vote. This is mandated under the RBI Act. The RBI Governor is the Chairperson of the MPC. Each member has one vote. The quorum for an MPC meeting is 4 members. MPC decisions are typically by majority. The casting vote provision ensures that the Governor's decisive role in monetary policy. The 2016 amendment gives the Governor the casting vote to maintain RBI's autonomy and decisive role.

### Q10. External benchmarks for loan pricing were made mandatory from:
(a) 1 April 2018 (b) 1 April 2019 (c) 1 April 2020 (d) 1 April 2017

**Answer: (b) 1 April 2019**

**Solution:** External benchmarks for loan pricing were made mandatory from 1 April 2019, under RBI's directive. All new floating-rate personal, retail, and MSME loans must be linked to external benchmarks (RBI Repo Rate, 91-day T-Bill yield, 182-day T-Bill yield, or any other benchmark published by FBIL). This improved monetary policy transmission. It replaced the older internal benchmarks (MCLR, Base Rate). Common external benchmarks: RBI Repo Rate (most common), 91-day T-Bill yield, 182-day T-Bill yield.

### Q11. The MPC publishes minutes how many days after the meeting?
(a) 7 days (b) 14 days (c) 21 days (d) 30 days

**Answer: (b) 14 days**

**Solution:** The MPC publishes its meeting minutes 14 days after each meeting, as mandated by the RBI Act. This ensures transparency and accountability of monetary policy decisions. The MPC meets at least 4 times a year (typically 6). The 2026 MPC meetings are scheduled in April, June, August, October, December, and February 2027. The minutes include the rationale for the decision, individual member votes, and the Governor's statement. This practice aligns India with global central bank transparency standards.

### Q12. Which is the floor of the LAF corridor?
(a) MSF Rate (b) Bank Rate (c) SDF Rate (d) Reverse Repo Rate

**Answer: (c) SDF Rate**

**Solution:** The SDF (Standing Deposit Facility) Rate is the floor of the LAF corridor. It was introduced in April 2022 to replace the fixed Reverse Repo Rate as the floor. SDF is 25 bps below the Repo Rate. The Bank Rate is aligned with the MSF Rate (ceiling). The Reverse Repo Rate is still set by RBI at its discretion but is no longer the operative floor. The SDF is an uncollateralised overnight deposit facility for banks to park excess liquidity with RBI.

### Q13. When inflation exceeds 6% for 3 consecutive quarters:
(a) RBI is dissolved (b) RBI must submit a report to the Government (c) New MPC is formed (d) Governor resigns

**Answer: (b) RBI must submit a report to the Government**

**Solution:** When inflation exceeds 6% or falls below 2% for 3 consecutive quarters, RBI must submit a report to the Central Government explaining the reasons and remedial actions. The inflation target is 4% CPI with ±2% tolerance band (2-6%). This is the "failure trigger" under the Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) framework. RBI has not formally submitted such a report since the framework was introduced in 2016, though inflation has occasionally been near the upper band. The framework provides accountability for monetary policy.

### Q14. MSF allows banks to dip into the SLR portfolio up to:
(a) 1% (b) 2% (c) 5% (d) 10%

**Answer: (b) 2%**

**Solution:** Under MSF, banks can dip into their SLR portfolio up to 2% below the prescribed SLR. So if SLR is 18%, banks can dip up to 16% to access MSF. This is a penal facility for emergency overnight borrowing. Banks must restore the SLR within the time prescribed by RBI (usually the next day). MSF is a safety valve — penal rate discourages routine use, but ensures no bank fails due to overnight liquidity crunch. The Bank Rate is aligned with the MSF Rate.

### Q15. LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio) was introduced as part of:
(a) Basel I (b) Basel II (c) Basel III (d) Basel IV

**Answer: (c) Basel III**

**Solution:** The LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio) was introduced as part of Basel III. It requires banks to maintain sufficient High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to survive a 30-day stress scenario. Formula: LCR = HQLA / Net cash outflows over 30 days. Minimum LCR: 100%. Basel III was published in 2010/11 (post 2008 financial crisis). HQLA includes Level 1 assets (cash, government securities) and Level 2A/2B assets (corporate bonds, equities with haircuts). Indian banks must maintain LCR ≥ 100% as per RBI.
