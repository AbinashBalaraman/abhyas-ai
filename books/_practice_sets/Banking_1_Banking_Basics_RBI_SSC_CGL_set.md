### Q1. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) was established on:
(a) 1 April 1934 (b) 1 April 1935 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 1 January 1949

**Answer: (b) 1 April 1935**

**Solution:** RBI was established on 1 April 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The recommendation was based on the Hilton Young Commission (1926 Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance). RBI was nationalized on 1 January 1949. The first Governor was Sir Osborne Smith; first Indian Governor was C.D. Deshmukh (1943-1949). RBI's headquarters was originally in Kolkata and moved to Mumbai in 1937.

### Q2. RBI was nationalized on:
(a) 1 April 1935 (b) 1 January 1949 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 15 August 1947

**Answer: (b) 1 January 1949**

**Solution:** RBI was nationalized on 1 January 1949 — fully owned by the Ministry of Finance, Government of India. Before that, RBI was a private shareholders' bank. Nationalization brought RBI under the government's control as the central monetary authority. The first Indian Governor was C.D. Deshmukh. The current RBI Governor (as of June 2026) is Sanjay Malhotra (IAS), since 11 December 2024.

### Q3. The headquarters of RBI is located in:
(a) New Delhi (b) Kolkata (c) Mumbai (d) Chennai

**Answer: (c) Mumbai**

**Solution:** RBI's headquarters is in Mumbai, Maharashtra. Originally, RBI's headquarters was in Kolkata (Calcutta) and was shifted to Mumbai in 1937. Mumbai is India's financial capital. RBI has 4 regional boards: Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, and New Delhi. It has 27 zonal offices and a 21-member Central Board of Directors (1 Governor + 4 Deputy Governors + 2 Finance Ministry representatives + 10 government-nominated directors + 4 local board representatives).

### Q6. The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) of India consists of how many members?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 7

**Answer: (c) 6**

**Solution:** The MPC consists of 6 members: 3 from RBI (Governor as Chairperson, Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, and one RBI officer nominated by Central Board) and 3 external members appointed by the Central Government. Each member has one vote; in case of tie, the Governor has casting vote. Quorum of 4 is required. The current external members (notified 1 October 2024) are Dr. Nagesh Kumar, Shri Saugata Bhattacharya, and Prof. Ram Singh.

### Q7. The current inflation target of RBI under Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) is:
(a) 2% (b) 4% (c) 6% (d) 8%

**Answer: (b) 4%**

**Solution:** The current inflation target of RBI under FIT is 4% CPI (Consumer Price Index) with a tolerance band of ±2%, i.e., 2% to 6%. This was retained by the Central Government on 25 March 2026 for the period from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031. The framework was introduced in 2016 with the RBI Act amendment. If average inflation exceeds 6% or falls below 2% for three consecutive quarters, RBI must submit a report to the Government.

### Q8. The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) was introduced to replace:
(a) Repo rate (b) MSF rate (c) Reverse repo rate (d) Bank rate

**Answer: (c) Reverse repo rate**

**Solution:** The SDF (Standing Deposit Facility) was introduced in April 2022 to replace the fixed Reverse Repo Rate as the floor of the LAF corridor. SDF is an uncollateralised overnight deposit facility. The Reverse Repo Rate is still set by RBI at its discretion but is no longer the operative floor. The LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility) corridor has SDF as the floor (25 bps below repo), Repo as the mid-point, and MSF as the ceiling (25 bps above repo). Corridor width is 50 bps.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/01_Banking_Basics_RBI.md, p.124-135 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The corridor width of the LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility) is:
(a) 25 bps (b) 50 bps (c) 75 bps (d) 100 bps

**Answer: (b) 50 bps**

**Solution:** The LAF corridor has a width of 50 bps (basis points) — 25 bps above and 25 bps below the Repo Rate. The SDF is the floor (25 bps below Repo), the Repo is the mid-point (policy rate), and the MSF is the ceiling (25 bps above Repo). This structure was put in place after the SDF replaced the fixed reverse repo rate in April 2022. The Bank Rate is aligned with the MSF Rate under Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/01_Banking_Basics_RBI.md, p.121-135 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. BRBNM (Bharatiya Reserve Bank Note Mudran) has banknote printing presses in:
(a) Mysore only (b) Salboni only (c) Mysore and Salboni (d) Nashik and Dewas

**Answer: (c) Mysore and Salboni**

**Solution:** BRBNM has two banknote printing presses: one in Mysore (Karnataka) and another in Salboni (West Bengal). BRBNM was set up in 1995 as a wholly-owned subsidiary of RBI. Note: The government-owned Security Printing and Minting Corporation of India Ltd (SPMCIL) has presses in Nashik (Maharashtra) and Dewas (Madhya Pradesh) for printing currency notes and minting coins. RBI manages currency, while the Government of India issues coins.

### Q11. DICGC (Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation) was established in:
(a) 1951 (b) 1961 (c) 1971 (d) 1981

**Answer: (b) 1961**

**Solution:** DICGC was established in 1961 as a wholly-owned subsidiary of RBI. It provides deposit insurance to depositors of commercial banks, RRBs, UCBs, and some cooperative banks. The deposit insurance cover was raised to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in February 2020 (from ₹1 lakh earlier). DICGC was set up under the DICGC Act, 1961. It is one of the oldest deposit insurance schemes in the world.

### Q12. The MPC was first constituted on:
(a) 2014 (b) 2015 (c) 2016 (d) 2017

**Answer: (c) 2016**

**Solution:** The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) was first constituted on 29 September 2016, after the RBI Act was amended in May 2016 (Sections 45ZA and 45ZB). The MPC meets at least 4 times a year (typically 6) and minutes are published 14 days after each meeting. The MPC decides the policy repo rate to maintain inflation at the 4% target. The first MPC meeting was held on 3-5 October 2016. Each external member has a 4-year term.

### Q13. The SDF is the floor of the LAF corridor. What does SDF stand for?
(a) Special Deposit Fund (b) Standing Deposit Facility (c) Statutory Deposit Framework (d) Secured Deposit Facility

**Answer: (b) Standing Deposit Facility**

**Solution:** SDF stands for Standing Deposit Facility. It is the floor of the LAF corridor. SDF is an uncollateralised overnight deposit facility through which banks can park excess liquidity with RBI. It was introduced in April 2022 to replace the fixed Reverse Repo Rate as the floor. SDF is market-determined (the reverse repo remains at RBI's discretion). The Bank Rate, MSF, and SDF are all interconnected — Bank Rate = MSF Rate, both are 25 bps above and below the Repo Rate respectively.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/01_Banking_Basics_RBI.md, p.124-135 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q14. MSF is the ceiling of the LAF corridor. What does MSF stand for?
(a) Marginal Standing Facility (b) Maximum Statutory Funding (c) Money Supply Framework (d) Market Stabilisation Facility

**Answer: (a) Marginal Standing Facility**

**Solution:** MSF stands for Marginal Standing Facility. It is the ceiling of the LAF corridor (25 bps above the Repo Rate). MSF is a penal rate for emergency overnight borrowing by banks that face severe liquidity shortage. Banks can dip into their SLR portfolio up to 2% below the prescribed SLR to borrow under MSF. The Bank Rate is aligned with the MSF Rate under Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934. MSF is usually not used in normal liquidity conditions due to its penal nature.
