## Q1. Plaster of Paris (Easy)
The chemical formula of Plaster of Paris is:
(a) CaSO₄·2H₂O (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O (c) CaCO₃ (d) Ca(OH)₂

**Answer:** (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O
**Solution:** POP (Plaster of Paris) is calcium sulphate hemihydrate, CaSO₄·½H₂O. It is made by heating gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) at 373 K (100°C). On adding water, it sets to hard gypsum: CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O.
**Shortcut:** POP = hemihydrate (½ water); Gypsum = dihydrate (2 water).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing POP formula with gypsum.
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q2. pH of neutral solution (Easy)
The pH of a neutral solution is:
(a) 0 (b) 7 (c) 14 (d) 1

**Answer:** (b) 7
**Solution:** pH scale: 0-14. pH = 7 → neutral (pure water). pH < 7 → acidic; pH > 7 → basic/alkaline. pH = -log[H⁺]. For pure water, [H⁺] = 10⁻⁷ M, so pH = 7.
**Shortcut:** 7 = neutral. Memorize: HCl = 0, Blood = 7.4, NaOH = 14.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 0 (that's the most acidic, not neutral).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Most reactive metal (Easy)
Which of the following is the most reactive metal?
(a) Gold (Au) (b) Iron (Fe) (c) Sodium (Na) (d) Copper (Cu)

**Answer:** (c) Sodium (Na)
**Solution:** Reactivity series (most → least): K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu, Hg, Ag, Au. Sodium is near the top — highly reactive (must be stored in kerosene). Gold is least reactive (found native, doesn't corrode).
**Shortcut:** K-Na-Ca-Mg-Al-Zn-Fe-Pb-Cu-Hg-Ag-Au. "Please Stop Calling Me A Zebra, I Like Her Cat Smart Phone."
**Common Mistake:** Picking iron or copper (these are mid/low in series).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q4. Covalent bonds in methane (Easy)
The number of covalent bonds in a methane (CH₄) molecule is:
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5

**Answer:** (c) 4
**Solution:** Carbon is tetravalent (4 valence electrons). It shares 4 electrons with 4 hydrogen atoms, forming 4 covalent (single) bonds. So CH₄ has 4 C-H bonds.
**Shortcut:** Carbon = 4 bonds (tetravalent). Always.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 2 or 8 (8 = total valence electrons, not bonds).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q5. Functional group of alcohol (Medium)
The functional group present in an alcohol is:
(a) –CHO (b) –COOH (c) –OH (d) >C=O

**Answer:** (c) –OH
**Solution:** Functional groups: Alcohol = –OH (hydroxyl); Aldehyde = –CHO; Carboxylic acid = –COOH; Ketone = >C=O. Example: ethanol CH₃CH₂OH has –OH group.
**Shortcut:** Alcohol = OH (think "OH! it's alcohol").
**Common Mistake:** Choosing –CHO (aldehyde, e.g., formaldehyde).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Amphoteric oxide (Medium)
Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?
(a) Na₂O (b) CuO (c) Al₂O₃ (d) SO₂

**Answer:** (c) Al₂O₃
**Solution:** Amphoteric oxides react with both acids and bases. Examples: Al₂O₃, ZnO. Na₂O and CuO are basic oxides (react with acids only). SO₂ is acidic oxide (reacts with bases).
**Shortcut:** Amphoteric = "Al-Zn" (Al₂O₃, ZnO). Amphoteric = dual nature.
**Common Mistake:** Picking Na₂O (basic) or SO₂ (acidic).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q7. Tooth decay pH (Medium — Trap)
Tooth decay begins when the pH in the mouth falls below:
(a) 7.0 (b) 6.5 (c) 5.5 (d) 4.5

**Answer:** (c) 5.5
**Solution:** When pH < 5.5, the enamel (calcium phosphate) starts to corrode. Bacteria in the mouth produce acids from sugar, lowering the pH. Toothpaste is mildly basic to neutralize the acid.
**Shortcut:** Critical pH for enamel = 5.5.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 7 (neutral pH, but tooth enamel is safe even below neutral).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Baking soda (Medium)
The chemical name of baking soda is:
(a) Sodium carbonate (b) Sodium hydrogencarbonate (c) Sodium hydroxide (d) Sodium chloride

**Answer:** (b) Sodium hydrogencarbonate
**Solution:** Baking soda = NaHCO₃ (sodium hydrogencarbonate / sodium bicarbonate). Used in baking (releases CO₂ on heating → fluffy cakes), antacids, fire extinguishers. Washing soda = Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (different!).
**Shortcut:** Baking soda = NaHCO₃ (one H); Washing soda = Na₂CO₃ (no H).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with washing soda (Na₂CO₃).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q9. Na + water reaction (Medium)
The reaction 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ is an example of:
(a) Combination (b) Decomposition (c) Displacement (d) Double displacement

**Answer:** (c) Displacement
**Solution:** Sodium (more reactive) displaces hydrogen from water. This is a single displacement reaction. Hydrogen in water is replaced by sodium. Combination = 2 reactants → 1 product. Decomposition = 1 reactant → multiple products. Double displacement = exchange of ions between two compounds.
**Shortcut:** Displacement = "more reactive replaces less reactive".
**Common Mistake:** Calling it combination (NaOH + H₂ are two products, so it's not combination).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q10. Displacement in CuSO₄ (Medium)
When iron is dipped in copper sulphate solution:
(a) No reaction (b) Iron displaces copper (c) Copper displaces iron (d) Both dissolve

**Answer:** (b) Iron displaces copper
**Solution:** Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Iron is more reactive than copper (Fe above Cu in reactivity series), so iron displaces copper from CuSO₄. The blue colour of solution fades; reddish copper deposits.
**Shortcut:** Iron higher than Cu in reactivity series → Fe displaces Cu.
**Common Mistake:** Thinking "no reaction" because both are metals (but reactivity matters).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q11. Roasting (Medium)
Heating a metal sulphide ore in presence of excess air to form its oxide is called:
(a) Calcination (b) Roasting (c) Smelting (d) Refining

**Answer:** (b) Roasting
**Solution:** Roasting = heating sulphide ore in air (e.g., 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂). Calcination = heating carbonate ore in limited/absent air (e.g., ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂). Smelting = reduction of oxide to metal. Refining = purifying impure metal.
**Shortcut:** Roasting = SULPHIDE in AIR; Calcination = CARBONATE in LIMITED air.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing roasting (sulphide/air) with calcination (carbonate).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q12. Saturated hydrocarbons (Medium — Trap)
Which of the following burns with a sooty flame?
(a) Methane (CH₄) (b) Ethane (C₂H₆) (c) Ethene (C₂H₄) (d) Propane (C₃H₈)

**Answer:** (c) Ethene (C₂H₄)
**Solution:** Saturated hydrocarbons (alkanes — single bonds) burn with a clean, blue, non-sooty flame due to complete combustion. Unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes, alkynes — double/triple bonds) have higher C:H ratio, leading to incomplete combustion and a sooty/yellow flame.
**Shortcut:** Saturated = clean flame; Unsaturated = sooty flame.
**Common Mistake:** Picking an alkane (these burn cleanly).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q13. pH of HCl solution (Hard)
What is the pH of a 0.001 M HCl solution?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4

**Answer:** (c) 3
**Solution:** HCl is a strong acid, fully dissociates: [H⁺] = 0.001 M = 10⁻³ M. pH = -log[H⁺] = -log(10⁻³) = 3.
**Shortcut:** For strong acid: pH = -log(conc in M). 10⁻¹ M → pH 1; 10⁻² M → pH 2; 10⁻³ M → pH 3.
**Common Mistake:** Saying pH = 1 (confusing with 0.1 M concentration).
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q14. Soap cleansing action (Hard)
The cleansing action of soap is due to:
(a) Hydrophilic tail and hydrophobic head (b) Hydrophobic tail and hydrophilic head (c) Both ends hydrophobic (d) Both ends hydrophilic

**Answer:** (b) Hydrophobic tail and hydrophilic head
**Solution:** Soap micelle: long hydrocarbon tail (hydrophobic, attracts oil/dirt) inside; COO⁻Na⁺ head (hydrophilic, attracts water) outside. This traps grease in the centre; water washes away the micelle.
**Shortcut:** "Head loves water" (hydrophilic –COO⁻); "Tail hates water" (hydrophobic –CH₂– chain).
**Common Mistake:** Reversing head/tail properties.
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q15. Saponification (Hard)
Saponification is the reaction of an ester with:
(a) Acid (b) Alcohol (c) NaOH (d) H₂SO₄

**Answer:** (c) NaOH
**Solution:** Saponification = Ester + NaOH → Alcohol + Sodium salt of acid (soap). Example: fat/oil + NaOH → glycerol + sodium stearate (soap). Concentrated H₂SO₄ is used in esterification (reverse direction); acid hydrolysis is just hydrolysis with acid catalyst.
**Shortcut:** Saponi-"fy" → needs NaOH (alkaline) to make soap.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with esterification (H₂SO₄ catalyst).
**Time target:** 60 sec
