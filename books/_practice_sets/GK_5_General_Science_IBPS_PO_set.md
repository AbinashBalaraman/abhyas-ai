### Q1. The SI unit of energy is:
(a) Newton (b) Joule (c) Watt (d) Pascal

**Answer: (b) Joule**

**Solution:** The SI unit of energy/work is the Joule (J), named after James Prescott Joule. 1 J = 1 N·m. Kinetic Energy (KE) = ½mv². Potential Energy (PE) = mgh. Power = Work/Time, with SI unit Watt (1 W = 1 J/s). 1 horsepower (HP) = 746 W. The commercial unit of energy is kilowatt-hour (kWh) = 3.6 × 10⁶ J. 1 unit of electricity = 1 kWh. The Law of Conservation of Energy states that energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted.

### Q2. The lightest element is:
(a) Helium (b) Oxygen (c) Hydrogen (d) Nitrogen

**Answer: (c) Hydrogen**

**Solution:** Hydrogen (H, atomic number 1) is the lightest element and the most abundant element in the universe (~75% by mass). The lightest metal is Lithium. Helium (He) is the second lightest element. Oxygen is the most abundant element in Earth's crust (~46%), while Nitrogen is the most abundant gas in the atmosphere (~78%). Hydrogen has three isotopes: protium (¹H), deuterium (²H or D), and tritium (³H or T). Hydrogen's symbol is H and it has atomic number 1.

### Q3. The "suicide bag" of a cell is:
(a) Nucleus (b) Ribosome (c) Lysosome (d) Mitochondria

**Answer: (c) Lysosome**

**Solution:** Lysosomes are called the "suicide bags" of the cell because they contain digestive enzymes that can digest the cell's own components during apoptosis (programmed cell death). Lysosomes are membrane-bound organelles containing hydrolytic enzymes. Other cell organelles: Mitochondria (powerhouse - ATP production), Ribosomes (protein synthesis), Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER - rough: protein synthesis; smooth: lipid synthesis), Golgi apparatus (packaging), Vacuoles (storage), Chloroplasts (photosynthesis in plants).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.613, p.922 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q4. The number of chromosomes in a human cell is:
(a) 23 (b) 46 (c) 48 (d) 92

**Answer: (b) 46**

**Solution:** A normal human somatic (body) cell has 46 chromosomes arranged in 23 pairs: 22 pairs of autosomes and 1 pair of sex chromosomes (XX in females, XY in males). Gametes (sperm/egg) have 23 chromosomes (haploid). DNA contains the bases Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Cytosine (C), and Guanine (G); A pairs with T, and C pairs with G. RNA contains Uracil (U) instead of Thymine. Watson and Crick discovered the double-helix structure of DNA in 1953. The human genome has about 3 billion base pairs.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.625, p.932 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. The boiling point of water in Kelvin is:
(a) 100 K (b) 273 K (c) 373 K (d) 373°C

**Answer: (c) 373 K**

**Solution:** The boiling point of water in Kelvin is 373 K. The freezing point is 273 K (or 0°C). The Celsius to Kelvin conversion is K = °C + 273.15. So 0°C = 273.15 K, 25°C = 298.15 K, 100°C = 373.15 K. The triple point of water is 273.16 K and 611.657 Pa. The normal human body temperature is 37°C (98.6°F), which is 310.15 K. The Kelvin scale is the SI base unit of thermodynamic temperature, named after Lord Kelvin.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.18-25, p.1027 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. The normal human body temperature is:
(a) 36°C (b) 37°C (c) 38°C (d) 39°C

**Answer: (b) 37°C**

**Solution:** The normal human body temperature is 37°C (98.6°F). It is maintained by the hypothalamus in the brain through thermoregulation. The temperature is measured using a clinical thermometer (mercury or digital). High temperature is called fever/pyrexia (often due to infection); low temperature is called hypothermia. The body temperature varies slightly throughout the day (~0.5°C). Body temperature in Kelvin is 310.15 K. The average human has about 5 liters of blood and 206 bones.

### Q7. The "Master Gland" of the human body is:
(a) Thyroid (b) Pituitary (c) Pancreas (d) Adrenal

**Answer: (b) Pituitary**

**Solution:** The Pituitary gland is called the "Master Gland" because it controls the secretions of other endocrine glands. It is located at the base of the brain (in the sella turcica) and has two parts: anterior (adenohypophysis) and posterior (neurohypophysis). It secretes hormones like growth hormone (GH), TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, and prolactin. The thyroid gland produces thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3) for metabolism. The pancreas produces insulin and glucagon. The adrenal gland produces adrenaline.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.714-718, p.923 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. Vitamin D deficiency in children causes:
(a) Scurvy (b) Beriberi (c) Rickets (d) Pellagra

**Answer: (c) Rickets**

**Solution:** Vitamin D deficiency causes Rickets in children (soft, weak bones, bow legs) and Osteomalacia in adults. Vitamin D is synthesized in the skin using sunlight (UV-B). It regulates calcium and phosphorus absorption. Sources: sunlight, fish liver oil, eggs, fortified milk. Other deficiency diseases: Vitamin A → Night blindness, Vitamin B1 → Beriberi, Vitamin B3 → Pellagra, Vitamin B12 → Pernicious anemia, Vitamin C → Scurvy, Vitamin E → Sterility, Vitamin K → Slow blood clotting.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.754, p.938 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The most abundant metal in the Earth's crust is:
(a) Iron (b) Aluminum (c) Calcium (d) Sodium

**Answer: (b) Aluminum**

**Solution:** Aluminum (Al) is the most abundant metal in the Earth's crust (~8%). The most abundant element in the Earth's crust is Oxygen (~46%), followed by Silicon (~28%). Iron is the second most abundant metal (~5%), followed by Calcium, Sodium, Potassium, and Magnesium. Aluminum is the third most abundant element overall. The most abundant metal inside the Earth is iron (which is why the Earth's core is iron-rich and generates the magnetic field). The lightest metal is Lithium.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.892 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. The chemical name of baking soda is:
(a) Sodium Carbonate (b) Sodium Bicarbonate (c) Calcium Carbonate (d) Sodium Hydroxide

**Answer: (b) Sodium Bicarbonate**

**Solution:** Baking Soda is Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃). It is used in cooking, as an antacid, and in fire extinguishers. Other important compounds: Washing Soda is Sodium Carbonate (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O), Bleaching Powder is CaOCl₂, Common Salt is NaCl, Plaster of Paris is CaSO₄·½H₂O, Gypsum is CaSO₄·2H₂O. Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) is caustic soda. The chemical name of acetic acid is ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH); of H₂SO₄ is sulphuric acid; of HNO₃ is nitric acid.
