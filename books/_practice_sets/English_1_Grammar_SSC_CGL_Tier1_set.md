### Q1.
**Spot the error:** "She **is often coming** to me on Sundays."
- (a) She (b) is often coming (c) to me (d) on Sundays

**Answer:** (b) is often coming
**Solution:** "Often" indicates a habitual action → Simple Present. "Is often coming" (Present Continuous) implies annoyance, not habit. Correct: "She often **comes** to me on Sundays."
**Grammar Rule:** Simple Present for habitual actions with adverbs like "often, usually, always, sometimes, never, generally."
**Common Mistake:** Treating "often" as a present-continuous marker.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q2.
**Spot the error:** "I **have met** your brother **yesterday**."
- (a) I (b) have met (c) your brother (d) yesterday

**Answer:** (b) have met
**Solution:** "Yesterday" is a point of time in the past → Simple Past required. Present Perfect ("have met") cannot combine with point-of-time adverbs like "yesterday, ago, last week." Correct: "I **met** your brother yesterday."
**Grammar Rule:** Present Perfect is incompatible with specific past time markers. Use Simple Past with point-of-time adverbs.
**Common Mistake:** Combining Present Perfect with "yesterday/last week/ago."
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q3.
**Spot the error:** "He **is having** two cars."
- (a) He (b) is having (c) two (d) cars

**Answer:** (b) is having
**Solution:** "Have" (possession) is a **stative verb** → does NOT take continuous form. Correct: "He **has** two cars."
**Grammar Rule:** Stative (non-progressive) verbs — own, have, belong, know, like, want — never take -ing.
**Common Mistake:** Using "is having" for possession; reserve "is having" for "is eating/drinking" (dynamic sense).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q4.
**Spot the error:** "I **am knowing** him for ten years."
- (a) I (b) am knowing (c) him (d) for ten years

**Answer:** (b) am knowing
**Solution:** "Know" is stative. With "for ten years" (duration), use Present Perfect. Correct: "I **have known** him for ten years."
**Grammar Rule:** Stative verbs reject -ing form. For duration "for/since," use Perfect tenses.
**Common Mistake:** Mixing stative verbs with continuous tenses.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q5.
**Convert to Passive:** "The cat killed the mouse."
- (a) The mouse killed the cat. (b) The mouse **was killed** by the cat. (c) The cat was killed by the mouse. (d) The mouse is killed by the cat.

**Answer:** (b) The mouse was killed by the cat.
**Solution:** Object "the mouse" → subject of passive. Simple Past → "was/were + V3." "Was killed" matches singular subject "the mouse."
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule I — Simple Past → was/were + V3. Object of active becomes subject of passive.
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to change verb form to past participle (V3).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q6.
**Convert to Passive:** "She writes a letter."
- (a) A letter is written by her. (b) A letter was written by her. (c) A letter is being written by her. (d) A letter has been written by her.

**Answer:** (a) A letter is written by her.
**Solution:** Simple Present → "am/is/are + V3." "Letter" is singular → "is written."
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule I — Simple Present → is/am/are + V3.
**Common Mistake:** Using "was" (past) instead of "is" (present) for Simple Present active.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q7.
**Convert to Passive:** "Open the door."
- (a) The door is opened. (b) Let the door be opened. (c) The door was opened. (d) The door has been opened.

**Answer:** (b) Let the door be opened.
**Solution:** Imperative sentence → "Let + object + be + V3." Not "The door is opened" (that implies a statement, not command).
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule VIII — Imperative → "Let + object + be + V3."
**Common Mistake:** Converting imperative to simple passive as if it were a statement.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q8.
**Convert to Passive:** "He can write a letter."
- (a) A letter can be written by him. (b) A letter is written by him. (c) A letter can written by him. (d) A letter could be written by him.

**Answer:** (a) A letter can be written by him.
**Solution:** Modal passive = "modal + be + V3." "Can" stays; add "be" + V3.
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule III — Modal → modal + be + V3.
**Common Mistake:** Dropping "be" (writing "can written" instead of "can be written").
**Time Target:** 30 sec

### Q9.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** Ram said, "I am tired."
- (a) Ram said that he is tired. (b) Ram said that he was tired. (c) Ram says that he is tired. (d) Ram said that I was tired.

**Answer:** (b) Ram said that he was tired.
**Solution:** Reporting verb "said" (past) → back-shift Present Continuous "am" → Past Continuous "was." Pronoun "I" → "he" (subject of "said" is Ram, 3rd person).
**Grammar Rule:** Narration back-shift: Present Continuous → Past Continuous; pronoun I → he (matching reporting subject).
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to back-shift tense or change pronoun.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q10.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** He said, "The sun rises in the East."
- (a) He said that the sun rose in the East. (b) He said that the sun rises in the East. (c) He says the sun rises in the East. (d) He said that the sun will rise in the East.

**Answer:** (b) He said that the sun rises in the East.
**Solution:** Universal truth → **NO back-shift.** Tense remains present even with past reporting verb.
**Grammar Rule:** Universal truths and scientific facts are NOT back-shifted.
**Common Mistake:** Back-shifting universal truths to past tense.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q11.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** He said to me, "Are you coming?"
- (a) He asked me if I was coming. (b) He asked me that I was coming. (c) He said to me if I was coming. (d) He asked me if I am coming.

**Answer:** (a) He asked me if I was coming.
**Solution:** Interrogative → "asked + if/whether." Yes/no question uses "if/whether." "Said" → "asked." Back-shift Present Continuous "are coming" → "was coming." Pronoun "you" → "I."
**Grammar Rule:** Yes/no question in indirect → "asked + if/whether + back-shifted clause."
**Common Mistake:** Using "that" instead of "if/whether"; using "said" instead of "asked."
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q12.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** She said, "I will come tomorrow."
- (a) She said that she will come tomorrow. (b) She said that she would come the next day. (c) She said that she would come tomorrow. (d) She said that she will come the next day.

**Answer:** (b) She said that she would come the next day.
**Solution:** Back-shift "will" → "would." "Tomorrow" → "the next day" (time expression conversion).
**Grammar Rule:** Future "will" → "would" in indirect; time expressions shift (tomorrow → the next day, today → that day, here → there).
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to convert time expressions; keeping "tomorrow."
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q13.
**Convert to Passive:** "He gave me a book."
- (a) I was given a book by him. (b) A book was given to me by him. (c) Both (a) and (b) are correct. (d) A book was given by him to me.

**Answer:** (c) Both (a) and (b) are correct.
**Solution:** Two-object sentence (give + person + thing) → **two valid passives**: person-object or thing-object as subject. Both are grammatically correct.
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule X — Two objects (give, send, tell, etc.) → two possible passives.
**Common Mistake:** Assuming only one form is correct.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q14.
**Spot the error:** "He **does not know** to swim."
- (a) He (b) does not know (c) to swim (d) No error

**Answer:** (c) to swim
**Solution:** "Know" is followed by "how + to-infinitive," not bare "to-infinitive." Correct: "He does not know **how to** swim."
**Grammar Rule:** "Know" + "how to" + base verb (or "know + noun/gerund").
**Common Mistake:** Writing "know to do" instead of "know how to do."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q15.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** He said, "What a beautiful flower!"
- (a) He exclaimed that what a beautiful flower. (b) He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful flower. (c) He said that it was a beautiful flower. (d) He exclaimed what a beautiful flower was.

**Answer:** (b) He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful flower.
**Solution:** Exclamatory → "exclaimed + that + it was a very + adjective + noun." Reporting verb changes from "said" to "exclaimed."
**Grammar Rule:** Exclamatory sentences in indirect speech use "exclaimed" and convert to assertive form.
**Common Mistake:** Retaining "what" in indirect exclamatory; using "said" instead of "exclaimed."
**Time Target:** 60 sec
