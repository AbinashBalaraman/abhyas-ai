### Q1. The IBC came into force on:
(a) 1 December 2015 (b) 1 April 2016 (c) 1 December 2016 (d) 1 April 2017

**Answer: (c) 1 December 2016**

**Solution:** The IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code), 2016 came into force on 1 December 2016. It was enacted in 2016. The IBC created the IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) as the regulator, NCLT as the Adjudicating Authority, and NCLAT as the Appellate Authority. The default threshold was ₹1 lakh initially, later raised to ₹1 crore. The CIRP (Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process) has a maximum period of 180 + 90 = 270 days (extendable to 330 days). Major cases resolved: Bhushan Steel (Tata Steel), Videocon, DHFL.

### Q2. The maximum time for CIRP under IBC is:
(a) 180 days (b) 270 days (c) 330 days (d) 365 days

**Answer: (c) 330 days**

**Solution:** The maximum time for the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) under IBC is 330 days. The standard CIRP period is 180 days, with a one-time extension of 90 days, totaling 270 days. In exceptional cases, the NCLT can grant further extension up to 330 days. If no resolution plan is approved within this period, the company goes into liquidation. The IBC has significantly improved India's ranking in the World Bank's "Ease of Doing Business" report (Resolving Insolvency parameter). Insolvency Professionals (IPs) and Information Utilities (IUs) are key actors.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.160-165 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q3. The MSME lending target is:
(a) 5% of ANBC (b) 7.5% of ANBC (c) 10% of ANBC (d) 15% of ANBC

**Answer: (b) 7.5% of ANBC**

**Solution:** The MSME (Micro and Small Enterprises) lending target is 7.5% of ANBC. PSL categories: Agriculture (18%), MSME/Micro Enterprises (7.5%), Export Credit (2%), Weaker Sections (10%). Total PSL target is 40% of ANBC. Micro Enterprises are a separate target within MSME. Small Finance Banks have a 75% PSL target. RRBs have 75% PSL. PSL ensures that banks provide credit to priority sectors. Banks that don't meet PSL targets can deploy shortfall amounts to NABARD/SIDBI for specific purposes.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.78-85 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q4. A Personal Loan is:
(a) Secured (b) Unsecured (c) Fully collateralized (d) Government-backed

**Answer: (b) Unsecured**

**Solution:** A Personal Loan is an unsecured loan. It does not require any collateral. The interest rate is higher (typically 10-24% p.a.) than secured loans. Personal loans are given based on the borrower's credit history, income, and repayment capacity. The tenure is usually 1-5 years. There is no tax benefit on personal loans. Other types of retail loans: Home Loan (secured by property), Car Loan (secured by vehicle), Education Loan (co-obligation), Gold Loan (secured by gold), Loan Against Property (secured by property).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.14-15 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. Section 80E of the Income Tax Act provides deduction on:
(a) Home Loan interest (b) Education Loan interest (c) Personal Loan interest (d) Car Loan interest

**Answer: (b) Education Loan interest**

**Solution:** Section 80E of the Income Tax Act provides tax deduction on interest paid on Education Loans. The deduction is available for higher education (full-time courses) of the individual or their spouse, children, or legal ward. The deduction is available for the entire interest paid during the year, with no upper limit. The deduction is available for up to 8 years from the start of repayment or until the interest is fully paid, whichever is earlier. Section 80C provides deduction on the principal of Home Loan (up to ₹1.5 lakh) and Section 24(b) on interest (up to ₹2 lakh for self-occupied property).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.16-19 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. The Home Loan principal repayment is eligible for tax deduction under:
(a) Section 80C (b) Section 80E (c) Section 24(b) (d) Section 80D

**Answer: (a) Section 80C**

**Solution:** The Home Loan principal repayment is eligible for tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act (up to ₹1.5 lakh per year, combined with other 80C investments like PPF, ELSS, LIC, etc.). The Home Loan interest repayment is eligible for deduction under Section 24(b) up to ₹2 lakh per year for self-occupied property. Section 80E is for Education Loan interest (no upper limit). Section 80D is for Health Insurance premium. The home loan tax benefits are under Sections 80C and 24(b), making homeownership tax-efficient.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.15-17 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. The 4 PSU General Insurance Companies are:
(a) LIC, GIC, NIC, OIC (b) National, New India, Oriental, United India (c) SBI, BOB, PNB, BoI (d) HDFC, ICICI, Tata, Bajaj

**Answer: (b) National, New India, Oriental, United India**

**Solution:** The 4 PSU General Insurance Companies are: National Insurance Company, New India Assurance Company, Oriental Insurance Company, and United India Insurance Company. All four were nationalized in 1972. The GIC Re (General Insurance Corporation of India) is the national re-insurer, established in 1971 (effective 1 January 1973). A new reform in 2024 is set to merge 3 of the 4 PSU general insurers (Oriental, National, United India) into New India Assurance. The 4 PSU insurers together have a significant share of the Indian general insurance market.

### Q8. KCC provides short-term credit to:
(a) Salaried employees (b) Small businesses (c) Farmers (d) Senior citizens

**Answer: (c) Farmers**

**Solution:** The KCC (Kisan Credit Card) provides short-term credit to farmers. Launched in 1998 based on the R.V. Gupta Committee recommendations. The credit limit is based on land holding, cropping pattern, and other factors. The interest rate is 7% per annum, with a 3% subsidy for timely repayment (effective 4%). KCC has been expanded to include animal husbandry, fisheries, and other allied activities. KCC is one of the most successful credit delivery mechanisms in Indian agriculture, helping millions of farmers access formal credit.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.47-50 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. Working Capital Loan is used for:
(a) Buying machinery (b) Day-to-day operations (c) Buying property (d) Long-term investments

**Answer: (b) Day-to-day operations**

**Solution:** Working Capital Loan is used for the day-to-day operations of a business. It covers short-term needs like paying wages, buying raw materials, and managing cash flow. Working Capital Loans are typically for 1 year or less. Other types of business loans: Term Loan (fixed amount for fixed tenure, used for long-term investments like machinery), Cash Credit (flexible limit for working capital), Overdraft (withdraw beyond balance up to limit), Demand Loan (repayable on demand), Bill Discounting (discounting of bills receivable). The interest rate is typically higher than term loans.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.24-30 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. A Gold Loan is given against:
(a) Property (b) Vehicle (c) Gold ornaments (d) Stocks

**Answer: (c) Gold ornaments**

**Solution:** A Gold Loan is a secured loan given against gold ornaments. The loan-to-value (LTV) ratio is typically up to 75% of the gold value. The interest rate is usually 7-14% p.a. The tenure is short (6 months to 3 years). Gold Loans are popular in India due to the cultural affinity for gold. The RBI has set LTV caps for gold loans. Other secured loans: Home Loan (property), Car Loan (vehicle), Loan Against Shares (stocks). Gold loans are processed quickly and require minimal documentation. The Reserve Bank of India regulates gold loan NBFCs.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.19-20 | **VERIFY:** No
