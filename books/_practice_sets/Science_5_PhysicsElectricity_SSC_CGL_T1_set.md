## Q1. SI unit of charge (Easy)
The SI unit of electric charge is:
(a) Ampere (b) Volt (c) Coulomb (d) Ohm

**Answer:** (c) Coulomb
**Solution:** Charge is measured in coulombs (C). 1 C = charge of ~6 × 10¹⁸ electrons. One electron has charge 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (elementary charge). Current (rate of charge flow) is in ampere; potential difference in volt; resistance in ohm.
**Shortcut:** Charge = Coulomb (C); Current = Ampere (A); Voltage = Volt (V); Resistance = Ohm (Ω).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing ampere (current) with coulomb (charge).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Ohm's Law (Easy)
Ohm's law is given by:
(a) V = IR (b) V = I/R (c) V = I + R (d) I = V² × R

**Answer:** (a) V = IR
**Solution:** Ohm's law (1827, Georg Simon Ohm): V = IR, where V = potential difference (volts), I = current (amperes), R = resistance (ohms). Valid at constant temperature for ohmic conductors.
**Shortcut:** VIR → V = IR (Ohm's triangle).
**Common Mistake:** Inverting (V = I/R would mean current increases when resistance increases — wrong).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Resistivity of copper (Easy)
The electrical resistivity of copper at 20°C is approximately:
(a) 1.62 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m (b) 1.62 × 10⁻⁶ Ω·m (c) 1.62 × 10⁻³ Ω·m (d) 1.62 Ω·m

**Answer:** (a) 1.62 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m
**Solution:** Copper has one of the lowest resistivities (after silver): 1.62 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. This is why Cu is preferred for electrical wiring — minimal energy loss as heat. Silver = 1.60 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m (lowest), Aluminium = 2.63 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m.
**Shortcut:** Ag (1.60) < Cu (1.62) < Al (2.63). Memorise in 10⁻⁸ Ω·m.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing 10⁻⁸ with 10⁻⁶ (factor of 100).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q4. Connecting wires in homes (Easy)
Domestic appliances are connected in:
(a) Series (b) Parallel (c) Both series and parallel (d) Either series or parallel

**Answer:** (b) Parallel
**Solution:** Appliances in homes are connected in parallel. Each appliance gets the same 220 V, draws its required current independently, and can be operated with its own switch. Failure of one doesn't affect others. Series connection would not allow independent operation.
**Shortcut:** Home = parallel. Each appliance has full voltage.
**Common Mistake:** Saying series (then all have to be ON together, same current).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q5. Electric fuse (Medium)
An electric fuse works on the principle of:
(a) Magnetic effect of current (b) Heating effect of current (c) Chemical effect of current (d) Electrolysis

**Answer:** (b) Heating effect of current
**Solution:** A fuse contains a wire with low melting point. When current exceeds the safe limit (overload or short circuit), the wire heats up due to H = I²Rt and melts, breaking the circuit. This protects appliances from damage.
**Shortcut:** Fuse = "too much current → too much heat → wire melts → circuit breaks".
**Common Mistake:** Saying magnetic effect (that operates motors, not fuses).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Series resistance (Medium)
Three resistors of 6 Ω each are connected in series. The equivalent resistance is:
(a) 2 Ω (b) 6 Ω (c) 12 Ω (d) 18 Ω

**Answer:** (d) 18 Ω
**Solution:** Series: R_s = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ = 6 + 6 + 6 = 18 Ω. In series, resistances add.
**Shortcut:** Series = sum. n equal resistors of R in series = nR.
**Common Mistake:** Using parallel formula (would give 2 Ω).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Parallel resistance (Medium)
Three resistors of 6 Ω each are connected in parallel. The equivalent resistance is:
(a) 2 Ω (b) 6 Ω (c) 12 Ω (d) 18 Ω

**Answer:** (a) 2 Ω
**Solution:** Parallel: 1/R_p = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2 → R_p = 2 Ω. n equal resistors of R in parallel = R/n.
**Shortcut:** Parallel n equal = R/n. Here 6/3 = 2 Ω.
**Common Mistake:** Adding them up like series (gives 18 Ω).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Current in series (Medium — Trap)
In a series circuit, the:
(a) Current is different through each component (b) Voltage is the same across each component (c) Current is the same through each component (d) Total resistance is less than the smallest resistor

**Answer:** (c) Current is the same through each component
**Solution:** In series, the same charge flows through each component. So current is the same everywhere. Voltage divides across components (V = V₁ + V₂ + ...). Total resistance is greater than any individual.
**Shortcut:** Series: I same, V divides. Parallel: V same, I divides.
**Common Mistake:** Saying voltage is same (that's parallel).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q9. Earth wire function (Medium)
The earth wire in a household circuit:
(a) Carries current to the appliance (b) Returns current to the source (c) Provides safety against electric shock (d) Reduces power consumption

**Answer:** (c) Provides safety against electric shock
**Solution:** The earth wire (green insulation) is connected to the metal body of an appliance. In case of current leakage (e.g., damaged live wire touching the body), the earth wire provides a low-resistance path, preventing severe shock to the user.
**Shortcut:** Earth = safety (drains leakage to ground).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with live wire (which carries 220 V) or neutral (return path).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q10. Joule heating formula (Medium)
The heat produced in a resistor is given by:
(a) H = IRt (b) H = I²Rt (c) H = VIt (d) Both (b) and (c)

**Answer:** (d) Both (b) and (c)
**Solution:** H = I²Rt (Joule's law) and H = VIt (since V = IR, I²Rt = IR × It = VIt). Both formulas are valid and equivalent.
**Shortcut:** H = I²Rt = VIt = V²t/R (all equivalent).
**Common Mistake:** Picking only H = I²Rt (forgetting H = VIt is also correct).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q11. Power calculation (Medium)
A bulb is rated 100 W, 220 V. The current drawn by the bulb is:
(a) 0.45 A (b) 0.22 A (c) 2.2 A (d) 4.54 A

**Answer:** (a) 0.45 A
**Solution:** P = VI → I = P/V = 100/220 ≈ 0.4545 A ≈ 0.45 A.
**Shortcut:** I = P/V (in SI units). For 100W/220V: I ≈ 0.45 A.
**Common Mistake:** Calculating V/P instead of P/V.
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q12. 1 kWh in joules (Medium — Trap)
1 kWh is equal to:
(a) 3.6 × 10⁵ J (b) 3.6 × 10⁶ J (c) 3.6 × 10⁷ J (d) 36 × 10⁵ J

**Answer:** (b) 3.6 × 10⁶ J
**Solution:** 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 1 "unit" (commercial unit of electric energy).
**Shortcut:** kWh → J: multiply by 3.6 × 10⁶. Memorise: 1 unit = 3.6 MJ.
**Common Mistake:** Off by factor of 10 (3.6 × 10⁵ or 3.6 × 10⁷).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q13. Power of 110V operation (Hard)
A 220 V, 100 W bulb is connected to 110 V. The power consumed is:
(a) 100 W (b) 75 W (c) 50 W (d) 25 W

**Answer:** (d) 25 W
**Solution:** R = V²/P = 220²/100 = 484 Ω. At 110 V: P' = V²/R = 110²/484 = 12100/484 = 25 W. Since V is halved, P becomes 1/4 (P ∝ V² at constant R).
**Shortcut:** P ∝ V². V → V/2 → P → P/4.
**Common Mistake:** Saying P becomes half (linear instead of square relation).
**Time target:** 60 sec

---

## Q14. Resistance after stretching (Hard)
A wire of resistance R is stretched to double its length (volume constant). The new resistance is:
(a) R/2 (b) R (c) 2R (d) 4R

**Answer:** (d) 4R
**Solution:** R = ρL/A. New length L' = 2L. Volume = constant → A' = A/2. New resistance R' = ρ(2L)/(A/2) = 4ρL/A = 4R.
**Shortcut:** Stretch n times length → resistance becomes n² times.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 2R (only accounting for length, not area reduction).
**Time target:** 75 sec

## Q15. Cost calculation (Hard)
A 100 W bulb operates 10 hours/day for 30 days. The electricity cost at ₹5 per kWh is:
(a) ₹150 (b) ₹75 (c) ₹30 (d) ₹15

**Answer:** (a) ₹150
**Solution:** Energy = P × t = 100 W × 10 hr × 30 days = 30,000 Wh = 30 kWh. Cost = 30 × 5 = ₹150.
**Shortcut:** Energy (kWh) = (W × hours)/1000. Then × rate = cost.
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to convert W to kW (or Wh to kWh).
**Time target:** 75 sec
