### Q1. "He is ___ European who speaks many languages."
(a) a
(b) an
(c) the
(d) no article

**Answer:** (a) a

**Solution:** "European" starts with the **consonant sound /j/ ("yu")** even though "E" is a vowel. Use "a" before consonant sounds. This is a classic Sound Rule trap. [Source: §2.3, §2.4 — "a European"]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "an" because "E" is a vowel. The pronunciation starts with "yu" (consonant sound), so "a" is correct.

**Time Target:** 5 sec

### Q2. "The book is divided ___ three parts."
(a) in
(b) into
(c) between
(d) among

**Answer:** (b) into

**Solution:** "Divide **into**" is the correct collocation. "Divide" can also take "between/among" for distribution, but "divide into" means splitting a whole into parts. "In three parts" would be passive, but here the book performs division into parts. [Source: §3.6 — verb + preposition patterns]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "in" — "divided in three parts" is sometimes heard but "divided into" is the standard active-voice collocation.

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q3. "The teacher, along with the students, ___ present."
(a) are
(b) were
(c) was
(d) is being

**Answer:** (c) was

**Solution:** When subjects are joined by "**along with / with / as well as / together with**", the verb agrees with the **first subject** (the main subject). "Teacher" is singular → singular verb "was". [Source: Chapter 7 §2.4 Rule 2 — applies in conjunction context]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "are" because "students" is plural. But "along with" doesn't make the subject compound; only "and" does.

**Time Target:** 15 sec

---

### Q4. "He is incapable ___ doing the work."
(a) for
(b) to
(c) of
(d) with

**Answer:** (c) of

**Solution:** "Incapable **of**" is the standard adjective-preposition collocation. "Incapable of + gerund/noun" expresses inability. [Source: §3.6 — Adjective + Preposition patterns]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "to" (from "capable to" confusion) or (a) "for" (incorrect).

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q5. "Either the manager or the assistants ___ responsible."
(a) is
(b) was
(c) are
(d) has been

**Answer:** (c) are

**Solution:** With "**Either...or / Neither...nor**", the verb agrees with the **nearest subject**. "Assistants" is plural → plural verb "are". [Source: Chapter 7 §2.4 Rule 3, §4.7 Subject-Verb Agreement with Correlative Conjunctions]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "is" because "manager" is singular, but the verb follows the NEAREST subject, not the first.

**Time Target:** 15 sec

---

### Q6. "She has been working here ___ 2015."
(a) for
(b) since
(c) from
(d) by

**Answer:** (b) since

**Solution:** "2015" is a **point in time** (a year). Use "**since**" with point-in-time expressions. "**For**" is for duration. This is the classic since/for distinction. [Source: §3.5, §6.4]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "for" because the action has been ongoing for 9 years, but the sentence uses the starting POINT (2015), not a duration.

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q7. "No sooner did he arrive ___ he started working."
(a) when
(b) than
(c) but
(d) then

**Answer:** (b) than

**Solution:** "**No sooner...than**" is a fixed correlative conjunction pair. It is ALWAYS "no sooner...than" (not "no sooner...when" or "no sooner...but"). [Source: §4.5 Correlative Conjunctions]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "when" (common error) or (c) "but" (common error). Only "than" pairs with "no sooner".

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q8. "The teacher asked the students to open ___ books."
(a) their
(b) there
(c) they're
(d) his

**Answer:** (a) their

**Solution:** "Their" is the possessive pronoun for plural "students". "There" is a place word; "they're" is "they are". Note: This tests both pronoun-possessive and spelling. [Source: §3.7, §5.6 Homophones from Ch.10/Ch.12]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "there" (homophone confusion) or (c) "they're" (contraction error).

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q9. "She is senior ___ me in the office."
(a) than
(b) from
(c) to
(d) of

**Answer:** (c) to

**Solution:** "**Senior to**" is the correct adjective-preposition collocation. "Junior to" follows the same pattern. (Note: "Senior than" is INCORRECT despite sounding comparable to "older than".) [Source: §3.6 — Senior/junior + to, not than]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "than" because of "older than" analogy. "Senior" takes "to", not "than".

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q10. "I ___ have finished the work by tomorrow."
(a) can
(b) may
(c) shall
(d) must

**Answer:** (c) shall

**Solution:** "**Shall**" with first person (I/we) expresses future in formal/SSC usage. "I shall" = I will (formal). With "I", "shall" is preferred in SSC writing. [Source: §5.4 — Shall/Should: Future (with I/we)]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "can" (ability, not future), (b) "may" (possibility/permission), (d) "must" (obligation). Only "shall" fits formal future with "I".

**Time Target:** 15 sec

---

### Q11. "Hardly ___ he entered the room when the phone rang."
(a) had
(b) did
(c) has
(d) was

**Answer:** (a) had

**Solution:** "**Hardly...when**" follows the structure "**Hardly had + subject + V3**" (Past Perfect). Note: "Hardly" is placed BEFORE the auxiliary for inversion. [Source: §4.5 — "Hardly...when"]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "did" (Past Simple) — but the structure requires Past Perfect (had + V3) with inversion.

**Time Target:** 15 sec

---

### Q12. "He is blind ___ his own faults."
(a) to
(b) of
(c) with
(d) at

**Answer:** (a) to

**Solution:** "Blind **to**" is the correct collocation. "Blind to" means unable to notice/acknowledge. [Source: §3.6 — Adjective + Preposition]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "of" (incorrect). "Blind of" doesn't exist; use "blind to" for figurative blindness.

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q13. "Since he was tired, so he went home." Correct form:
(a) Since he was tired, so he went home
(b) Since he was tired, he went home
(c) He was tired, so he went home
(d) Both (b) and (c)

**Answer:** (d) Both (b) and (c)

**Solution:** "**Since...so**" combination is **redundant** (same as "although...but"). "Since" already indicates cause; adding "so" doubles it. Use one or the other, not both. [Source: §4.6 Common Errors — "Since...so"]

**Common Mistake:** Keeping both "since" and "so" together — this is incorrect in standard English.

**Time Target:** 15 sec

---

### Q14. "___ United States is a large country."
(a) A
(b) An
(c) The
(d) No article

**Answer:** (c) The

**Solution:** Countries with **"United", "United States of..."** take "**The**" because they are considered as a **collective union** of states. Examples: "The United States", "The United Kingdom", "The UAE", "The Philippines". [Source: Chapter 7 §2.4 Rule 24 — Countries as single units]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (d) "no article" — most country names take no article ("India", "France"), but "United" countries take "The".

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q15. "She must ___ the report by 5 PM today."
(a) submits
(b) submitted
(c) submit
(d) submitting

**Answer:** (c) submit

**Solution:** **Modals are followed by the base form (V1) of the verb** — no -s, no -ed, no -ing. "Must submit" (not "submits", "submitted", "submitting"). [Source: §5.3 Modal Characteristics, §5.5 Common Errors]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "submits" (adding -s) or (d) "submitting" (confusing with continuous form). Modals require bare infinitive.

**Time Target:** 10 sec
