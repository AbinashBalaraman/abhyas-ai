## Q1. Liquid metal at room temperature (Easy)
The metal that is liquid at room temperature is:
(a) Sodium (b) Mercury (c) Gallium (d) Iron

**Answer:** (b) Mercury
**Solution:** Mercury (Hg) is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature (melting point −39°C). Used in thermometers (though being phased out due to toxicity). Gallium melts on palm (29.8°C) but is solid at room temperature in many places.
**Shortcut:** Mercury = liquid metal. (Greek "hydrargyrum" = liquid silver.)
**Common Mistake:** Choosing sodium (it's solid at room temperature, soft enough to cut with knife).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Best conductor of electricity (Easy)
The best conductor of electricity among metals is:
(a) Copper (b) Silver (c) Aluminium (d) Gold

**Answer:** (b) Silver
**Solution:** Silver has the lowest resistivity (1.60 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m) — the best conductor. Copper is second (1.62 × 10⁻⁸), Aluminium is third (2.63 × 10⁻⁸). But copper is preferred for wiring (cheaper than silver).
**Shortcut:** Silver > Copper > Aluminium > Gold in conductivity.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing copper (very common, but silver is technically better).
**Time target:** 15 sec

---

## Q3. Hardest natural substance (Easy)
The hardest natural substance is:
(a) Iron (b) Diamond (c) Quartz (d) Talc

**Answer:** (b) Diamond
**Solution:** Diamond (an allotrope of carbon) is the hardest natural substance — 10 on Mohs scale. Used in cutting tools, drill bits, jewellery. Talc is the softest (1 on Mohs scale).
**Shortcut:** Diamond = 10 (Mohs). Talc = 1. Quartz = 7.
**Common Mistake:** Saying quartz (Mohs 7, but not the hardest).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q4. Most malleable metal (Easy)
The most malleable metal is:
(a) Iron (b) Copper (c) Gold (d) Aluminium

**Answer:** (c) Gold
**Solution:** Gold is the most malleable (can be beaten into thin sheets) and ductile (drawn into thin wires) metal. 1 g of gold can be drawn into ~2 km wire. Used in jewellery because it's soft and shiny.
**Shortcut:** Gold = most malleable + most ductile.
**Common Mistake:** Saying copper (malleable but not most).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q5. Amphoteric oxide (Medium)
Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?
(a) Na₂O (b) CuO (c) Al₂O₃ (d) SO₂

**Answer:** (c) Al₂O₃
**Solution:** Amphoteric oxides react with both acids and bases. Al₂O₃ (aluminium oxide) and ZnO (zinc oxide) are common amphoteric oxides. Na₂O and CuO are basic oxides. SO₂ is acidic.
**Shortcut:** Amphoteric = Al₂O₃, ZnO (and PbO, SnO).
**Common Mistake:** Picking Na₂O (basic, not amphoteric).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q6. Most reactive metal (Medium)
Which is the most reactive metal in the reactivity series?
(a) Gold (b) Iron (c) Sodium (d) Copper

**Answer:** (c) Sodium
**Solution:** Reactivity series (most → least): K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu, Hg, Ag, Au. Sodium (Na) is highly reactive — must be stored in kerosene to prevent reaction with air/moisture.
**Shortcut:** K, Na at top (most reactive). Au at bottom (least reactive).
**Common Mistake:** Picking iron or copper (mid/low in series).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Iron extraction (Medium)
In the extraction of iron, the reducing agent used in the blast furnace is:
(a) Coke (Carbon) (b) Limestone (c) Haematite (d) Slag

**Answer:** (a) Coke (Carbon)
**Solution:** In the blast furnace, coke (C) reacts with O₂ to form CO, which then reduces Fe₂O₃ to Fe: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Limestone (CaCO₃) is used to remove silica as slag.
**Shortcut:** Reduction of Fe₂O₃ uses CO (from coke).
**Common Mistake:** Saying limestone (that's for slag removal, not reduction).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q8. Thermite reaction (Medium — Trap)
The thermite reaction involves the reaction of aluminium with:
(a) CuO (b) Fe₂O₃ (c) ZnO (d) MgO

**Answer:** (b) Fe₂O₃
**Solution:** Thermite reaction: 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + Heat. The reaction is highly exothermic; molten iron is used for welding railway tracks, repairing broken machinery in situ.
**Shortcut:** Thermite = Al + Fe₂O₃ → Fe (welding).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with other metal oxides.
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q9. Stainless steel composition (Medium)
Stainless steel contains iron with:
(a) Cr and Ni (b) Cu and Zn (c) Mn and W (d) Al and Mg

**Answer:** (a) Cr and Ni
**Solution:** Stainless steel = Fe + Cr (chromium, ~18%) + Ni (nickel, ~8%). Chromium forms a passive Cr₂O₃ layer that prevents corrosion. Used in surgical instruments, kitchen utensils, etc.
**Shortcut:** Stainless steel = Fe + Cr + Ni (18-8 stainless steel).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with brass (Cu + Zn) or bronze (Cu + Sn).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q10. Displacement reaction (Medium)
When iron nails are placed in copper sulphate solution:
(a) No reaction (b) Iron displaces copper (blue colour fades) (c) Copper displaces iron (d) Both dissolve

**Answer:** (b) Iron displaces copper (blue colour fades)
**Solution:** Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Iron is more reactive than copper. Blue colour of CuSO₄ fades; reddish copper deposits on nails. Reaction shows Fe above Cu in reactivity series.
**Shortcut:** Higher in series → displaces lower. Fe above Cu → Fe displaces Cu.
**Common Mistake:** Thinking no reaction (both are metals, but reactivity matters).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q11. Roasting vs calcination (Medium)
Calcination is the heating of:
(a) Sulphide ore in air (b) Carbonate ore in limited air (c) Oxide ore in vacuum (d) Pure metal in air

**Answer:** (b) Carbonate ore in limited air
**Solution:** Calcination = heating carbonate ore in limited/absent air (e.g., ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂). Roasting = heating sulphide ore in air (e.g., 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂).
**Shortcut:** Calcination = CARBONATE + limited air. Roasting = SULPHIDE + air.
**Common Mistake:** Reversing these two.
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q12. Ionic compound property (Medium)
Ionic compounds in solid state:
(a) Conduct electricity (b) Do not conduct electricity (c) Conduct heat only (d) Are gases

**Answer:** (b) Do not conduct electricity
**Solution:** Ionic compounds conduct electricity only in molten state or aqueous solution (when ions are free to move). In solid state, ions are locked in the lattice and cannot move → no conduction.
**Shortcut:** Solid ionic = no conduction. Molten/aq = conduction (ions free).
**Common Mistake:** Saying they conduct (only when melted/dissolved).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q13. Reactivity series order (Hard)
Arrange in order of decreasing reactivity: Fe, Na, Cu, Au
(a) Na > Fe > Cu > Au (b) Fe > Na > Cu > Au (c) Au > Cu > Fe > Na (d) Na > Cu > Fe > Au

**Answer:** (a) Na > Fe > Cu > Au
**Solution:** From reactivity series: Na (top, most reactive) > Fe > Cu > Au (bottom, least reactive). Sodium reacts vigorously with water; gold is so unreactive it's found native.
**Shortcut:** Series: K Na Ca Mg Al Zn Fe Pb Cu Hg Ag Au.
**Common Mistake:** Saying Au > Cu (Au is least reactive, not most).
**Time target:** 45 sec

---

## Q14. Alloy composition (Hard)
Brass is an alloy of:
(a) Cu + Zn (b) Cu + Sn (c) Cu + Ni (d) Cu + Al

**Answer:** (a) Cu + Zn
**Solution:** Brass = Cu + Zn (used in musical instruments, decorative items, locks). Bronze = Cu + Sn (statues, medals). Solder = Pb + Sn. Steel = Fe + C. Stainless steel = Fe + Cr + Ni. Duralumin = Al + Cu + Mg + Mn.
**Shortcut:** Brass = Cu + Zn. Bronze = Cu + Sn.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing brass (Cu+Zn) with bronze (Cu+Sn).
**Time target:** 45 sec

---

## Q15. Extraction method (Hard)
Highly reactive metals like sodium and potassium are extracted by:
(a) Reduction with carbon (b) Reduction with CO (c) Electrolysis of molten ore (d) Roasting

**Answer:** (c) Electrolysis of molten ore
**Solution:** Highly reactive metals (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) are extracted by electrolysis of their molten ores (because no chemical reducing agent is strong enough). Less reactive metals (Zn, Fe, Sn) are reduced with C/CO. Least reactive (Cu, Ag, Au) are found native or by heating.
**Shortcut:** Highly reactive → electrolysis. Mid → C/CO reduction. Least → native.
**Common Mistake:** Saying reduction with carbon (doesn't work for Na/K — too reactive).
**Time target:** 60 sec
