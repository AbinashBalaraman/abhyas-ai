### Q1. The National Emblem of India is taken from:
(a) Sanchi (b) Sarnath (c) Konark (d) Khajuraho

**Answer: (b) Sarnath**

**Solution:** The National Emblem of India is adapted from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath (near Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh). The original Ashoka pillar dates to ~250 BCE during the Mauryan period (Emperor Ashoka). It shows 4 Asiatic lions standing back-to-back (only 3 visible in the emblem; the 4th is hidden). The motto is "Satyameva Jayate" (Truth Alone Triumphs) from the Mundaka Upanishad. It was adopted on 26 January 1950. Sanchi is famous for the Great Stupa; Konark for the Sun Temple; Khajuraho for the Group of Monuments.

### Q3. Sattriya dance is from which Indian state?
(a) Manipur (b) Assam (c) Nagaland (d) Tripura

**Answer: (b) Assam**

**Solution:** Sattriya is a classical dance form from Assam, recognized by the Sangeet Natak Akademi in 2000. It was founded by the saint-reformer Sankaradeva in the 15th-16th century and has Vaishnavite themes. The dance includes "ankiya nat" (one-act plays) and "apsara dance". India officially recognizes 8 classical dance forms: Bharatanatyam (Tamil Nadu), Kathak (UP), Kathakali (Kerala), Kuchipudi (AP), Odissi (Odisha), Mohiniyattam (Kerala), Manipuri (Manipur), and Sattriya (Assam). Manipuri is from Manipur.

### Q4. The Kailasa Temple is located at:
(a) Ajanta (b) Ellora (c) Elephanta (d) Mahabalipuram

**Answer: (b) Ellora**

**Solution:** The Kailasa Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora (Maharashtra) is the largest monolithic rock-cut temple in the world, dedicated to Lord Shiva. It was carved out of a single rock during the Rashtrakuta period under King Krishna I (8th century CE). Ellora has 34 caves (Buddhist, Hindu, Jain) and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Ajanta is famous for Buddhist paintings and cave sculptures. Elephanta is famous for Shiva sculptures (UNESCO). Mahabalipuram is famous for the Shore Temple and Pancha Rathas (UNESCO).

### Q5. Bharatanatyam is the classical dance of which state?
(a) Kerala (b) Tamil Nadu (c) Karnataka (d) Andhra Pradesh

**Answer: (b) Tamil Nadu**

**Solution:** Bharatanatyam is the classical dance of Tamil Nadu. It is the oldest classical dance form in India, with origins in temple traditions. It features balanced Nritta (pure dance), Nritya (expressive dance), and Natya (dance drama). Items include Alarippu, Jatiswaram, Varnam, Padam, and Thillana. Rukmini Devi Arundale revived Bharatanatyam and founded Kalakshetra in Chennai. Famous dancers: Balasaraswati, Padma Subrahmanyam, Alarmel Valli, Mallika Sarabhai. Other dances: Kathak (UP), Kathakali (Kerala), Kuchipudi (AP), Odissi (Odisha).

### Q6. The Taj Mahal was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in:
(a) 1980 (b) 1983 (c) 1985 (d) 1987

**Answer: (b) 1983**

**Solution:** The Taj Mahal (Agra, UP) was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983. The Taj Mahal was built by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal (1632-1653). It is made of white marble and is considered one of the Seven Wonders of the World. The first Indian UNESCO WHS were inscribed in 1983: Agra Fort, Ajanta Caves, Ellora Caves, and Taj Mahal. India has 43+ UNESCO WHS as of 2025-26 (recent: Maratha Military Landscapes of India in 2025).

### Q7. The "Radcliffe Line" separates:
(a) India and China (b) India and Pakistan (c) India and Bangladesh (d) India and Nepal

**Answer: (b) India and Pakistan**

**Solution:** The Radcliffe Line is the border between India and Pakistan, drawn by Sir Cyril Radcliffe in 1947 during the Partition. It divided the provinces of Punjab and Bengal. The McMahon Line separates India and China (Tibet border, drawn in 1914 by Sir Henry McMahon). The Durand Line separates Pakistan and Afghanistan. The Line of Control (LoC) is the military demarcation line between India and Pakistan in Kashmir. The Line of Actual Control (LAC) separates India and China. The 17th Parallel separated North and South Vietnam.

### Q8. Odissi is the classical dance of which state?
(a) Kuchipudi (b) Bharatanatyam (c) Odissi (d) Kathak

**Answer: (c) Odissi**

**Solution:** Odissi is the classical dance of Odisha. It is one of the oldest surviving dance forms with origins in the "Maharis" tradition. It features the Tribhanga (three-bend) posture. The Konark dance sculptures and temple carvings are famous examples. Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra is one of the most famous Odissi dancers. Sanjukta Panigrahi is another legendary performer. The dance has items like Mangalacharan, Batu, Pallavi, and Abhinaya. Odissi is characterized by its sculpturesque poses and emotional expressions.

### Q9. The Brihadeeswarar Temple is located at:
(a) Thanjavur (b) Madurai (c) Kanchipuram (d) Mahabalipuram

**Answer: (a) Thanjavur**

**Solution:** The Brihadeeswarar Temple (also called the Big Temple) is located at Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu. It was built by Raja Raja Chola I in 1011 CE. It is dedicated to Lord Shiva and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (part of "Great Living Chola Temples", 1987). The temple has the tallest temple tower (Vimana) in India at ~216 feet (66 m). It is one of the largest temples in India. The Kailasa Temple at Ellora is a rock-cut temple, and the Brihadeeswarar is a structural temple. Madurai is famous for the Meenakshi Temple.

### Q10. Madhubani (Mithila) painting is from:
(a) Uttar Pradesh (b) Bihar (c) Madhya Pradesh (d) Rajasthan

**Answer: (b) Bihar**

**Solution:** Madhubani (or Mithila) painting is from Bihar, India. It is characterized by geometric patterns, no empty space, and themes from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Krishna's life. Traditionally painted by women. Warli painting is a tribal art from Maharashtra using geometric patterns depicting daily life. Pattachitra is from Odisha, often featuring Jagannath themes. Tanjore painting is from Tamil Nadu, using gold foil. Kalamkari is from Andhra Pradesh, using pen (kalam) with natural dyes.

### Q11. Warli painting is a tribal art form of which state?
(a) Gujarat (b) Rajasthan (c) Maharashtra (d) Madhya Pradesh

**Answer: (c) Maharashtra**

**Solution:** Warli painting is a tribal art form from Maharashtra, especially the Warli tribe of North Maharashtra. It uses geometric patterns (circles, triangles, squares) to depict daily life, nature, and rituals. The art is done on walls using white rice paste on a brown/red base. Other tribal/folk arts: Madhubani (Bihar), Pattachitra (Odisha), Tanjore (Tamil Nadu), Kalamkari (AP), Phad (Rajasthan), Patna (Bihar), Chittara (Karnataka), Saora (Odisha), Gond (Madhya Pradesh).

### Q12. Kathak dance originated in:
(a) South India (b) North India (c) East India (d) West India

**Answer: (b) North India**

**Solution:** Kathak is the classical dance of North India, with origins in Uttar Pradesh. It is characterized by spins (chakkars), ghungroo (ankle bells), and themes from the life of Radha-Krishna. The dance developed in the Mughal courts and has 4 main gharanas (schools): Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras, and Raigarh. Birju Maharaj was a legendary Kathak dancer. Other dance forms: Bharatanatyam (TN - South), Kathakali (Kerala - South), Kuchipudi (AP - South), Odissi (Odisha - East), Manipuri (Manipur - NE), Sattriya (Assam - NE), Mohiniyattam (Kerala - South).

### Q13. The "Bharat Mata" painting was created by:
(a) Abanindranath Tagore (b) Nandalal Bose (c) Raja Ravi Varma (d) Amrita Sher-Gil

**Answer: (a) Abanindranath Tagore**

**Solution:** The "Bharat Mata" painting was created by Abanindranath Tagore (1871-1951), who founded the Bengal School of Art. Nandalal Bose (1882-1966) is called the "Father of Modern Indian Art" and designed the Emblem of the Republic of India. Raja Ravi Varma (1848-1906) is called the "Father of Modern Indian Art" for his oleographs and depictions of Indian mythology. Amrita Sher-Gil (1913-1941) is called the "Indian Frida Kahlo" and is known for her paintings of Indian rural life. M.F. Husain is the "Picasso of India".

### Q14. The "Bihu" festival is primarily celebrated in:
(a) Bihar (b) Assam (c) Bengal (d) Odisha

**Answer: (b) Assam**

**Solution:** The "Bihu" festival is the most important festival of Assam. It is celebrated three times a year: Bohag (Rongali) Bihu (April - marks Assamese New Year), Kati (Kongali) Bihu (October - austere), and Magh (Bhogali) Bihu (January - harvest festival). It is associated with agriculture and is celebrated with traditional dance (Bihu dance), music, and food. Other regional festivals: Onam (Kerala), Pongal (TN), Lohri (Punjab), Hornbill (Nagaland), Durga Puja (WB), Garba (Gujarat), Thrissur Pooram (Kerala).

### Q15. The McMahon Line is the border between:
(a) India and Pakistan (b) India and China (c) India and Nepal (d) India and Bhutan

**Answer: (b) India and China**

**Solution:** The McMahon Line is the border between India and China (specifically, between India and Tibet). It was drawn by Sir Henry McMahon in 1914 at the Simla Convention. The Line of Actual Control (LAC) is the de facto border between India and China (not officially demarcated). The Radcliffe Line separates India and Pakistan. The Durand Line separates Pakistan and Afghanistan. India-Bhutan border is ~700 km (India is the largest aid donor to Bhutan). India-Nepal border is open under the 1950 Indo-Nepal Treaty.
