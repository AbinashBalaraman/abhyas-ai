### Q1. The RBI Act was amended in May 2016 to provide statutory basis for the:
(a) Banking Regulation Act (b) Monetary Policy Committee (c) SARFAESI Act (d) IBC 2016

**Answer: (b) Monetary Policy Committee**

**Solution:** The RBI Act, 1934 was amended in May 2016 (Finance Act 2016) to provide statutory basis for the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC). Sections 45ZA and 45ZB were added. This was a major reform making India's monetary policy framework more institutional and less discretionary. The MPC was first constituted on 29 September 2016. The framework is called Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT). The 2016 amendment also brought in the inflation target (4% CPI with ±2% tolerance band) and the Government review of the target every 5 years.

### Q2. The operating target of monetary policy is:
(a) Repo Rate (b) MSF Rate (c) WACR (d) Call Money Rate

**Answer: (c) WACR**

**Solution:** The operating target of monetary policy is the Weighted Average Call Rate (WACR), which is aligned with the policy repo rate. RBI's monetary policy actions target WACR to move in line with the Repo Rate. The Call Money Rate is the overnight rate at which banks lend/borrow from each other. The WACR is the volume-weighted average of overnight call money transactions. RBI's policy rate (Repo) is the signal, and WACR is the variable that RBI tries to keep aligned with Repo.

### Q3. The 2014 Monetary Policy Framework Agreement was signed between:
(a) RBI and PM (b) RBI and Government (c) RBI and IMF (d) RBI and World Bank

**Answer: (b) RBI and Government**

**Solution:** The Monetary Policy Framework Agreement was signed in 2015 between the RBI and the Government of India. It was a precursor to the formal FIT framework introduced in 2016. The agreement committed the RBI to maintain inflation at 4% CPI (with ±2% tolerance band) as the primary objective of monetary policy. The agreement was initiated under RBI Governor Dr. Raghuram Rajan (2013-2016) and signed during his tenure. The 2016 RBI Act amendment formalized this framework as Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT).

### Q4. The first phase of interest rate deregulation began in:
(a) 1985 (b) 1991 (c) 2000 (d) 2014

**Answer: (a) 1985**

**Solution:** The first phase of interest rate deregulation in India began in 1985 with the Vaghul Working Group. This led to the phased deregulation of interest rates. The 1991 Narasimham Committee (Phase I) recommended major banking reforms. The Narasimham Committee II (1998) further reformed banking. In 1992-97, major reforms introduced auctions for G-Secs and the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF). The 2014 Monetary Policy Framework Agreement and 2016 amendment formalized the inflation targeting framework.

### Q5. The Quorum for an MPC meeting is:
(a) 3 members (b) 4 members (c) 5 members (d) 6 members

**Answer: (b) 4 members**

**Solution:** The quorum for an MPC meeting is 4 members (out of 6). This is mandated under the RBI Act. The MPC has 6 members: 3 from RBI (Governor as Chairperson, RBI Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, and one RBI officer nominated by the Central Board) and 3 external members appointed by the Central Government. If quorum is not met, the meeting cannot proceed. The 4-member quorum ensures broad consensus on monetary policy decisions. The MPC meets at least 4 times a year.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/03_Monetary_Policy_Rates.md, p.61-65 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. Open Market Operations (OMO) are used by RBI to:
(a) Issue currency (b) Manage durable liquidity (c) Set interest rates (d) Regulate banks

**Answer: (b) Manage durable liquidity**

**Solution:** Open Market Operations (OMO) are used by RBI to manage durable liquidity. OMO involves the outright purchase/sale of government securities (G-Secs) for injection/absorption of durable liquidity. There are different types: Outright OMO (direct purchase/sale), Repo OMO (with repurchase agreement), MSF (penal-rate overnight borrowing), and SDF (uncollateralised overnight deposit). OMOs are used to manage liquidity beyond what LAF can handle. The Market Stabilisation Scheme (MSS) uses Treasury Bills for liquidity management from large forex inflows.

### Q7. The Reverse Repo Rate is set at:
(a) Floor of corridor (b) Ceiling of corridor (c) Mid-point (d) RBI's discretion

**Answer: (d) RBI's discretion**

**Solution:** After SDF was introduced in April 2022, the Reverse Repo Rate is set at RBI's discretion (no longer the operative floor of the LAF corridor). The Reverse Repo Rate is currently 3.35% (as of mid-2025, may vary). It is the rate at which RBI absorbs liquidity from banks against collateral of G-Secs. The LAF corridor's actual floor is now the SDF Rate. The Reverse Repo remains a tool but is no longer the key operational rate.

### Q8. The current RBI Governor (who chairs the MPC) is:
(a) Shaktikanta Das (b) Sanjay Malhotra (c) Raghuram Rajan (d) Urjit Patel

**Answer: (b) Sanjay Malhotra**

**Solution:** Sanjay Malhotra (IAS) is the current RBI Governor (since 11 December 2024) and Chairperson of the MPC. He succeeded Shaktikanta Das (2018-2024). Previous MPC Chairpersons: Urjit Patel (2016-2018), Raghuram Rajan (2013-2016). The RBI Governor has the casting vote in case of a tie. The MPC has 6 members (3 from RBI + 3 external). The MPC meets at least 4 times a year (typically 6). The 2026 meetings are scheduled in April, June, August, October, December, and February 2027.

### Q9. The MPC was first constituted in:
(a) 2014 (b) 2015 (c) 2016 (d) 2017

**Answer: (c) 2016**

**Solution:** The MPC was first constituted on 29 September 2016, after the RBI Act was amended in May 2016 (Sections 45ZA, 45ZB). The first MPC meeting was held on 3-5 October 2016. Before the MPC, monetary policy was set by the RBI Governor alone (in consultation with the Technical Advisory Committee). The MPC brings more institutional and transparent monetary policy decision-making. The framework is called Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) and uses the Consumer Price Index (CPI) as the inflation measure.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/03_Monetary_Policy_Rates.md, p.43-50 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. Inflation is measured in India by:
(a) WPI (b) CPI (c) GDP Deflator (d) PPI

**Answer: (b) CPI**

**Solution:** Inflation in India is measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI) for monetary policy purposes. The Combined CPI (CPI-Combined) is the headline measure, which combines rural and urban. The base year is 2012=100. CPI is published by the National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics. The Wholesale Price Index (WPI) was previously used as the inflation measure (until 2014) but is now used more for producer prices. The inflation target under FIT is 4% CPI with ±2% tolerance band. The GDP Deflator measures the price level of all goods and services produced.
