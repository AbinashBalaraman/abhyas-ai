## Q1. Litmus in acid (Easy)
Blue litmus turns _____ in an acidic solution:
(a) Blue (b) Red (c) Green (d) Yellow

**Answer:** (b) Red
**Solution:** Litmus is a natural indicator extracted from lichen. Acids turn blue litmus red; bases turn red litmus blue. This is the simplest test to distinguish between acids and bases.
**Shortcut:** Acid + Blue litmus → Red. Base + Red litmus → Blue.
**Common Mistake:** Saying green (that's neutral, litmus doesn't turn green).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. pH of pure water (Easy)
The pH of a neutral solution (pure water at 25°C) is:
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 7 (d) 14

**Answer:** (c) 7
**Solution:** pH = -log[H⁺]. In pure water, [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ M. So pH = 7. pH < 7 = acidic; pH > 7 = basic.
**Shortcut:** 7 = neutral. < 7 acid; > 7 base.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 0 or 14 (those are extremes).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Sodium hydroxide uses (Easy)
Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) is NOT used in the manufacture of:
(a) Soap (b) Paper (c) Detergents (d) Glass

**Answer:** (d) Glass
**Solution:** NaOH (caustic soda) is used in making soap, paper, textiles, detergents, and drain cleaners. Glass is made from sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃), not NaOH.
**Shortcut:** NaOH = soap, paper, drain cleaner. Glass uses Na₂CO₃.
**Common Mistake:** Including glass (common confusion — glass needs Na₂CO₃).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q4. Common salt (Easy)
The chemical name of common salt is:
(a) Sodium carbonate (b) Sodium chloride (c) Sodium hydroxide (d) Sodium bicarbonate

**Answer:** (b) Sodium chloride
**Solution:** Common salt = NaCl (sodium chloride). It's the source of NaOH, Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃, and other sodium compounds. Obtained from seawater by evaporation.
**Shortcut:** NaCl = common salt (table salt).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃ = washing soda).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q5. Acid + metal carbonate (Medium)
When dilute HCl reacts with Na₂CO₃, the gas evolved is:
(a) H₂ (b) O₂ (c) CO₂ (d) Cl₂

**Answer:** (c) CO₂
**Solution:** Acid + metal carbonate → Salt + Water + CO₂. Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂. The CO₂ turns lime water (Ca(OH)₂) milky due to formation of CaCO₃.
**Shortcut:** Acid + carbonate → CO₂ (brings white ppt with lime water).
**Common Mistake:** Saying H₂ (that's for acid + metal reaction, not carbonate).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Tooth decay pH (Medium)
Tooth decay starts when the pH of the mouth falls below:
(a) 7.0 (b) 6.5 (c) 5.5 (d) 4.5

**Answer:** (c) 5.5
**Solution:** When mouth pH < 5.5, the tooth enamel (calcium phosphate) starts to dissolve. Bacteria break down sugar → produce acid → lower pH. Toothpaste is basic, neutralises the acid.
**Shortcut:** Critical pH = 5.5. Below this → enamel dissolves.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 7 (that's neutral; enamel is safe above 5.5).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Plaster of Paris formula (Medium — Trap)
Plaster of Paris is chemically:
(a) CaSO₄·2H₂O (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O (c) CaCO₃ (d) Ca(OH)₂

**Answer:** (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O
**Solution:** POP = calcium sulphate hemihydrate (½ water molecule). Made by heating gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) at 373 K. Sets to hard gypsum on adding water: CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O.
**Shortcut:** POP = hemihydrate (½ H₂O); Gypsum = dihydrate (2 H₂O).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with gypsum (which has 2 H₂O).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Chlor-alkali process (Medium)
In the chlor-alkali process, brine (NaCl solution) is electrolysed to obtain:
(a) Na metal, Cl₂, H₂ (b) NaOH, Cl₂, H₂ (c) NaCl, Cl₂, H₂ (d) Na₂CO₃, Cl₂, H₂

**Answer:** (b) NaOH, Cl₂, H₂
**Solution:** 2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl₂(g) + H₂(g). The products are: NaOH (caustic soda, used for soap), Cl₂ (used for bleaching powder, PVC), H₂ (used for fuels, ammonia).
**Shortcut:** Chlor-alkali = Cl₂ + alkali (NaOH) + H₂.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing Na metal (NaOH is the product, not Na metal).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q9. Antacid example (Medium)
Which of the following is an antacid used to relieve acidity?
(a) NaCl (b) Ca(OH)₂ (c) Milk of magnesia — Mg(OH)₂ (d) HCl

**Answer:** (c) Milk of magnesia — Mg(OH)₂
**Solution:** Milk of magnesia (Mg(OH)₂) is a mild base that neutralises excess HCl in the stomach, relieving acidity (heartburn). NaCl and Ca(OH)₂ are not antacids; HCl is the acid itself.
**Shortcut:** Antacid = mild base. Mg(OH)₂ is the most common.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing HCl (that would INCREASE acidity).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q10. pH of 0.001 M HCl (Medium — Trap)
The pH of 0.001 M HCl solution is:
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4

**Answer:** (c) 3
**Solution:** HCl is a strong acid, fully dissociates: [H⁺] = 0.001 M = 10⁻³ M. pH = -log[H⁺] = -log(10⁻³) = 3.
**Shortcut:** 10⁻¹ → pH 1; 10⁻² → pH 2; 10⁻³ → pH 3; 10⁻⁴ → pH 4.
**Common Mistake:** Saying pH = 1 (confusing with 0.1 M HCl).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q11. Acid rain pH (Medium)
The pH of acid rain is:
(a) Below 5.6 (b) Above 5.6 (c) Below 7 (d) Above 7

**Answer:** (a) Below 5.6
**Solution:** Normal rain is slightly acidic (pH ~5.6) due to dissolved CO₂ → H₂CO₃. Acid rain (caused by SO₂ and NOₓ from industries/vehicles) has pH < 5.6 and damages buildings, statues, crops, and aquatic life.
**Shortcut:** Acid rain = pH < 5.6 (more acidic than normal rain).
**Common Mistake:** Saying "below 7" (technically true, but the specific threshold is 5.6).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q12. Baking soda (Medium)
Baking soda is:
(a) Na₂CO₃ (b) NaHCO₃ (c) NaOH (d) NaCl

**Answer:** (b) NaHCO₃
**Solution:** Baking soda = sodium hydrogencarbonate (NaHCO₃). Used in baking (releases CO₂ → fluffy cakes), antacids, fire extinguishers. Na₂CO₃ = washing soda; NaOH = caustic soda; NaCl = common salt.
**Shortcut:** Baking = NaHCO₃ (mild, non-corrosive basic salt).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with washing soda (Na₂CO₃).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q13. Water of crystallisation (Hard)
Washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) loses water of crystallisation on exposure to air. This process is called:
(a) Deliquescence (b) Efflorescence (c) Sublimation (d) Hygroscopy

**Answer:** (b) Efflorescence
**Solution:** Efflorescence = loss of water of crystallisation on exposure to dry air (substance becomes powdery). Washing soda, Glauber's salt (Na₂SO₄·10H₂O) are efflorescent. Deliquescence = absorbing moisture from air and dissolving in it (e.g., CaCl₂, NaOH).
**Shortcut:** Efflorescence = loses water to air. Deliquescence = absorbs water from air.
**Common Mistake:** Reversing these two terms.
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q14. Heat calculation (Hard)
How much heat is released when 200 g of NaOH is dissolved in water to form 1 L solution? (ΔH_solution = −44.5 kJ/mol; M(NaOH) = 40 g/mol)
(a) 222.5 kJ (b) 44.5 kJ (c) 445 kJ (d) 4.45 kJ

**Answer:** (a) 222.5 kJ
**Solution:** Moles = 200/40 = 5 mol. Heat = 5 × (−44.5) = −222.5 kJ (released, so 222.5 kJ of heat is given out).
**Shortcut:** Heat = moles × ΔH per mole. 5 mol × 44.5 kJ/mol = 222.5 kJ.
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to convert mass to moles (would give 8900 kJ if mass × ΔH).
**Time target:** 75 sec

## Q15. pH of salt solution (Hard)
A salt formed from a strong acid and a weak base has pH:
(a) = 7 (b) < 7 (c) > 7 (d) = 14

**Answer:** (b) < 7
**Solution:** Salts of strong acid + strong base → pH = 7 (neutral, e.g., NaCl). Salts of strong acid + weak base → pH < 7 (acidic, e.g., NH₄Cl). Salts of weak acid + strong base → pH > 7 (basic, e.g., Na₂CO₃).
**Shortcut:** Strong acid + weak base → acidic salt (pH < 7).
**Common Mistake:** Saying pH = 7 (that's for strong acid + strong base).
**Time target:** 60 sec
