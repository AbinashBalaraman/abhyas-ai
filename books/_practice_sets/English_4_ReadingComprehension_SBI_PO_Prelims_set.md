## Passage 1

The shift toward renewable energy has accelerated globally, driven by both climate concerns and the falling costs of solar and wind power. India has set ambitious targets, aiming to install 500 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030. However, achieving this target requires significant investment in grid infrastructure, energy storage, and policy support. Recent reports indicate that while solar capacity has grown rapidly, the integration of variable renewable energy into the grid remains a challenge. Energy storage solutions, particularly battery technology, are critical to managing the intermittency of solar and wind power. Industry experts suggest that a combination of policy incentives, private investment, and technological innovation will be essential to meeting the 2030 target. The transition also offers economic opportunities, with potential for job creation in green sectors and reduced dependence on fossil fuel imports.

### Q1.
What is India's renewable energy target for 2030?
- (a) 100 GW (b) 250 GW (c) 500 GW (d) 1000 GW

**Answer:** (c) 500 GW
**Solution:** "India has set ambitious targets, aiming to install 500 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030."
**Strategy:** Factual question.
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q2.
According to the passage, what is a key challenge for renewable energy?
- (a) Lack of sunlight. (b) Grid integration of variable energy. (c) Public opposition. (d) Low costs.

**Answer:** (b) Grid integration of variable energy.
**Solution:** "the integration of variable renewable energy into the grid remains a challenge."
**Strategy:** Factual question.
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q3.
The word "intermittency" most nearly means:
- (a) Continuous supply (b) Irregular supply (c) High output (d) Low cost

**Answer:** (b) Irregular supply
**Solution:** "Intermittency" of solar/wind = not constant (depends on weather/time).
**Strategy:** Vocabulary in context.
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q4.
The author would most likely agree that the renewable transition:
- (a) Will fail.
- (b) Requires multi-faceted effort.
- (c) Is already complete.
- (d) Should be abandoned.

**Answer:** (b) Requires multi-faceted effort.
**Solution:** "a combination of policy incentives, private investment, and technological innovation will be essential."
**Strategy:** Author's view — note the multi-factor approach.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q5.
It can be inferred that energy storage is:
- (a) Unnecessary.
- (b) Critical for managing renewable variability.
- (c) Already perfect.
- (d) A barrier to adoption.

**Answer:** (b) Critical for managing renewable variability.
**Solution:** "Energy storage solutions, particularly battery technology, are critical to managing the intermittency of solar and wind power."
**Strategy:** Inference — note "critical."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

## Passage 2

The growth of India's startup ecosystem has been one of the most significant economic developments of the past decade. With over 100,000 recognized startups and more than 100 unicorns, India ranks third globally in startup numbers. Government initiatives like "Startup India" have provided funding support, tax benefits, and simplified compliance procedures. However, challenges remain, including access to late-stage funding, talent retention, and infrastructure gaps. Industry observers note that while the ecosystem has produced success stories in fintech, edtech, and e-commerce, sectors like deep tech and hardware have lagged. The next phase of growth, experts suggest, will require deeper capital markets, stronger intellectual property frameworks, and greater integration with global supply chains. Without these, India's startups may struggle to scale beyond domestic markets and compete with global players.

### Q6.
According to the passage, how many recognized startups does India have?
- (a) Over 10,000 (b) Over 50,000 (c) Over 100,000 (d) Over 1,000,000

**Answer:** (c) Over 100,000
**Solution:** "With over 100,000 recognized startups and more than 100 unicorns."
**Strategy:** Factual question.
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q7.
Which sector has NOT seen significant startup success according to the passage?
- (a) Fintech (b) Edtech (c) E-commerce (d) Deep tech and hardware

**Answer:** (d) Deep tech and hardware
**Solution:** "sectors like deep tech and hardware have lagged."
**Strategy:** Factual question.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q8.
The author mentions "Startup India" as:
- (a) A failure. (b) A government initiative providing support. (c) A private company. (d) A venture capital firm.

**Answer:** (b) A government initiative providing support.
**Solution:** "Government initiatives like 'Startup India' have provided funding support, tax benefits, and simplified compliance procedures."
**Strategy:** Factual question.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q9.
The word "lagged" most nearly means:
- (a) Led (b) Fallen behind (c) Disappeared (d) Prospered

**Answer:** (b) Fallen behind
**Solution:** "Lagged" = fallen behind, not kept pace. Context: deep tech/hardware "have lagged" compared to other sectors.
**Strategy:** Vocabulary in context.
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q10.
What is the author's overall view of the startup ecosystem?
- (a) Highly critical. (b) Cautiously optimistic. (c) Completely negative. (d) Indifferent.

**Answer:** (b) Cautiously optimistic.
**Solution:** The author acknowledges the success ("100,000 recognized startups") but notes challenges for the next phase.
**Strategy:** Tone — note the balanced view.
**Time Target:** 30 sec
