### Q1. The biggest continent by area is:
(a) Africa (b) North America (c) Asia (d) Europe

**Answer: (c) Asia**

**Solution:** Asia is the largest continent (44.58 million km²), followed by Africa (30.37 million), North America (24.71), South America (17.84), Antarctica (14.20), Europe (10.18), and Australia (8.60). Asia is the most populous continent (~5 billion people). It is home to both the world's highest peak (Mount Everest) and lowest point (Dead Sea shore). The Tropic of Cancer passes through Asia, Africa, and North America.

### Q2. The "Father of the White Revolution" (Operation Flood) in India is:
(a) M.S. Swaminathan (b) Verghese Kurien (c) Sam Pitroda (d) Norman Borlaug

**Answer: (b) Verghese Kurien**

**Solution:** Dr. Verghese Kurien is called the "Father of the White Revolution" (Operation Flood - milk). He was also called the "Milkman of India". M.S. Swaminathan is the "Father of the Green Revolution" (food grains). Sam Pitroda is associated with telecom revolution in India and Yellow Revolution (oilseeds). Norman Borlaug is the "Father of the Green Revolution" globally. Other revolutions: Blue (fish), Pink (meat/poultry), Golden (horticulture), Silver (cotton).

### Q3. The Kanha National Park (Madhya Pradesh) is famous as the inspiration for:
(a) The Jungle Book (b) Discovery of India (c) Panchayat Raj (d) Operation Flood

**Answer: (a) The Jungle Book**

**Solution:** Kanha National Park (MP) inspired Rudyard Kipling's "The Jungle Book". It is one of the largest national parks in India. Other famous wildlife parks: Jim Corbett (Uttarakhand - first national park), Ranthambore (Rajasthan), Kaziranga (Assam - one-horned rhino), Gir (Gujarat - Asiatic Lion), Sundarbans (WB - Royal Bengal Tiger), Periyar (Kerala - elephants). Project Tiger was launched on 1 April 1973.

### Q4. Black soil (regur soil) is best suited for which crop?
(a) Rice (b) Cotton (c) Wheat (d) Tea

**Answer: (b) Cotton**

**Solution:** Black soil (Regur or Black Cotton Soil) is found in the Deccan Plateau and is best suited for cotton. It is rich in iron, magnesium, and alumina; has high moisture retention capacity; and is self-ploughing (swelling/shrinking with moisture). Other major soil types: Alluvial (~43% of India, Indo-Gangetic plain), Red soil (Eastern Deccan), Laterite (Western Ghats, NE India), Mountain/Forest soil (Himalayas), Arid/Desert soil (Rajasthan), Saline/Alkaline (coastal/arid), Peaty/Marshy (Kerala, Sundarbans).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.232-239, p.602 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. The state with the largest forest cover in India is:
(a) Madhya Pradesh (b) Arunachal Pradesh (c) Chhattisgarh (d) Maharashtra

**Answer: (b) Arunachal Pradesh**

**Solution:** As per the India State of Forest Report (ISFR), Madhya Pradesh has the largest forest cover by area, while Arunachal Pradesh has the highest forest cover as a percentage of geographical area (~79%). The top states by forest cover: MP > Arunachal Pradesh > Chhattisgarh > Odisha > Maharashtra. India's total forest cover is about 21.71% of geographical area (ISFR 2023). The National Forest Policy aims for 33% forest cover.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.626-627 | **VERIFY:** Yes - verify from latest ISFR

### Q6. The Strait of Hormuz is located between:
(a) India and Sri Lanka (b) Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman (c) Red Sea and Mediterranean (d) Atlantic and Pacific

**Answer: (b) Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman**

**Solution:** The Strait of Hormuz is between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. About 20% of the world's oil passes through this strait, making it a critical chokepoint. Other key straits: Strait of Malacca (Indian Ocean - South China Sea; shortest sea route India-East Asia), Bab-el-Mandeb (Red Sea - Gulf of Aden), Palk Strait (India - Sri Lanka), Bering Strait (Asia - North America), English Channel (UK - France), Bosphorus & Dardanelles (Turkey). The Suez Canal connects Red Sea to Mediterranean.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.486-501 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. The "Palk Strait" separates India from:
(a) Bangladesh (b) Myanmar (c) Sri Lanka (d) Maldives

**Answer: (c) Sri Lanka**

**Solution:** The Palk Strait separates India from Sri Lanka (53 km wide). It is named after Robert Palk. The Strait is between Tamil Nadu (India) and Jaffna (Sri Lanka). The Adams Bridge (Rama Setu) is a chain of limestone shoals connecting Pamban Island (India) to Mannar Island (Sri Lanka). The Bay of Bengal lies to the east, and the Arabian Sea to the west. The Palk Bay is relatively shallow and not navigable for large ships.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.496 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. "Cherrapunji" and "Mawsynram" in Meghalaya are famous for being:
(a) Coldest places (b) Wettest places (c) Driest places (d) Highest peaks

**Answer: (b) Wettest places**

**Solution:** Mawsynram (Meghalaya, India) is the wettest place on Earth with ~11,871 mm annual rainfall. Cherrapunji (also in Meghalaya) is the second wettest (~11,619 mm). Both are located on the windward side of the Khasi Hills, which force the southwest monsoon to rise and cool, resulting in heavy orographic rainfall. The driest place is the Atacama Desert (Chile) with almost no rainfall. The cold inhabited place is Oymyakon (Siberia) at -67.7°C.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.535, p.542 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The capital of Australia is:
(a) Sydney (b) Melbourne (c) Canberra (d) Perth

**Answer: (c) Canberra**

**Solution:** Canberra is the capital of Australia (not Sydney, which is the largest city). Australia is also a continent (smallest) and a country. The country has 6 states and 2 major territories. The currency is the Australian Dollar (AUD). The official language is English. Other capitals: USA - Washington D.C., Japan - Tokyo, China - Beijing, Russia - Moscow, Canada - Ottawa, South Africa has 3 capitals (Pretoria, Cape Town, Bloemfontein). This is a commonly confused question in GK.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.494, p.547 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. The Tropic of Cancer passes through how many Indian states?
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 8 (d) 9

**Answer: (c) 8**

**Solution:** The Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram. It does NOT pass through Odisha. The Tropic of Cancer also passes through 16 countries globally including India, China, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, Bangladesh, Myanmar, etc. The Tropic of Capricorn (23.5°S) passes through 10 countries. The Equator (0°) passes through 13 countries.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.526 | **VERIFY:** No
