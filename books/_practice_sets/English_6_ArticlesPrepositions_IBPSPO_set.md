### Q1. "He is ___ honest man."
(a) a
(b) an
(c) the
(d) no article

**Answer:** (b) an

**Solution:** "Honest" has a **silent h** — pronounced /ˈɒnɪst/. Use "an" before vowel sounds. [Source: §2.3 Sound Rule, §2.4]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "a" because "h" looks like a consonant.

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q2. "She is good ___ playing chess."
(a) in
(b) at
(c) of
(d) with

**Answer:** (b) at

**Solution:** "Good **at**" expresses skill/proficiency. [Source: §3.6 — "good at"]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "in" — incorrect collocation.

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q3. "I have been working here ___ 2018."
(a) for
(b) since
(c) from
(d) at

**Answer:** (b) since

**Solution:** "2018" is a **point in time**. Use "**since**" with points. [Source: §3.5, §6.4]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "for" — "for" needs duration (e.g., "for 5 years").

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q4. "___ book you gave me is very interesting."
(a) A
(b) An
(c) The
(d) No article

**Answer:** (c) The

**Solution:** "The" refers back to a **specific book already mentioned/identified** (the one you gave me). [Source: §2.6 — Specific noun already mentioned]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "a" — but the book is specific, identified by context.

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q5. "He can ___ speak French."
(a) also
(b) to
(c) both
(d) either

**Answer:** (a) also

**Solution:** "Can" is followed by V1. "Also" adds to the statement. "Can also speak" is the correct structure. Note: "to" doesn't follow modals. [Source: §5.3 Modal Characteristics]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "to" — modals don't take "to" (no "can to go").

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q6. "Both Ram ___ Shyam are present."
(a) or
(b) nor
(c) and
(d) but

**Answer:** (c) and

**Solution:** "**Both...and**" is a correlative conjunction pair. [Source: §4.5 Correlative Conjunctions]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "or" — "both...or" is incorrect; must be "both...and".

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q7. "He is afraid ___ dogs."
(a) from
(b) of
(c) with
(d) at

**Answer:** (b) of

**Solution:** "Afraid **of**" is the standard collocation. [Source: §3.6 — "afraid of"]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "from" (incorrect).

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q8. "She is ___ university student."
(a) a
(b) an
(c) the
(d) no article

**Answer:** (a) a

**Solution:** "University" starts with the **consonant sound /j/ ("yu")**. Use "a" before consonant sounds. [Source: §2.3, §2.4 — "a university"]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "an" because "u" is a vowel letter. The sound is consonant.

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q9. "Although he worked hard, ___ failed."
(a) but he
(b) yet he
(c) he
(d) so he

**Answer:** (c) he

**Solution:** "**Although**" already introduces contrast. Don't add "**but**" or "**yet**" as a second conjunction in the same clause. Drop the redundant conjunction. [Source: §4.6, §6.6 Although-But Trick]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "but he" — redundant "although...but".

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q10. "I ___ like to thank everyone for coming."
(a) can
(b) would
(c) must
(d) shall

**Answer:** (b) would

**Solution:** "**Would**" expresses polite intention/willingness in formal speech. [Source: §5.4 — Would: polite request/willingness]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "can" (ability), (c) "must" (obligation), (d) "shall" (formal future with I/we). "Would" is correct for polite speech.

**Time Target:** 5 sec
