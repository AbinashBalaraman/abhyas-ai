# English Chapter 6: Articles, Prepositions, Conjunctions, Modals
## Practice Set — SSC CGL Tier-I (15 Questions, 60 sec each)

> **Source:** `books/English_Language/06_Articles_Prepositions.md`
> **Time Target:** 15 minutes total | **Difficulty:** Foundation to Intermediate
> **Coverage:** Articles (A/An/The), Prepositions (in/on/at, since/for, collocations), Conjunctions (FANBOYS, although/but, either/or), Modals (can/may/must)

### Q1. "I saw ___ elephant in the zoo."
(a) a
(b) an
(c) the
(d) no article

**Answer:** (b) an

**Solution:** Choice between "a" and "an" is based on **sound**, not letter. "Elephant" starts with a **vowel sound** (/ɛ/). Use "an" before words beginning with vowel sounds. This is the **Sound Rule for A vs An** [Source: §2.3, §2.4].

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "a" because "elephant" starts with a letter, not focusing on the sound.

**Time Target:** 5 sec

### Q2. "He is good ___ mathematics."
(a) in
(b) at
(c) of
(d) with

**Answer:** (b) at

**Solution:** "Good **at**" is the correct adjective-preposition collocation. It expresses skill or proficiency in a subject/activity. [Source: §3.6 — Adjective + Preposition list]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "in" because it sounds natural in some Indian English usage, but "good in" is non-standard; "good at" is the standard form tested in SSC/Banking.

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q3. "She is fond ___ music."
(a) in
(b) at
(c) of
(d) on

**Answer:** (c) of

**Solution:** "Fond **of**" is the fixed collocation. "Fond" expresses liking and must be followed by "of" + noun/gerund. [Source: §3.6 — Adjective + Preposition]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (d) "on" (incorrect), (a) "in" (incorrect), (b) "at" (incorrect). Only "of" follows "fond".

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q4. "___ sun rises in the east."
(a) A
(b) An
(c) The
(d) No article

**Answer:** (c) The

**Solution:** Use "**The**" for **unique** nouns. There is only one sun, so "The sun". This applies to all unique celestial bodies (the moon, the President, the Pope). [Source: §2.6 — When to use "The", §2.8 Common Errors]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (d) "no article" — many candidates skip the article entirely, but unique nouns require "the".

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q5. "He cans speak English fluently." Find the error.
(a) He
(b) cans
(c) speak
(d) fluently

**Answer:** (b) cans

**Solution:** **Modals do NOT take -s in the third person.** "He can speak" (not "cans"). This is a fundamental property of all 13 modals in English. [Source: §5.3 Modal Characteristics, §5.5 Common Errors]

**Common Mistake:** Adding -s to "can" by analogy with regular verbs (he runs, he plays). Modals are invariable.

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q6. "I have been living here ___ 5 years."
(a) since
(b) for
(c) from
(d) at

**Answer:** (b) for

**Solution:** Use "**for**" with **duration** (period of time: 5 years, 2 hours, a long time). Use "**since**" with **point in time** (since 2020, since Monday). "5 years" is a duration. [Source: §3.5 Common Preposition Errors, §6.4 The "Since vs For" Trick]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "since" because the action started in the past, but "since" requires a starting POINT, not a duration.

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q7. "___ honest man is respected everywhere."
(a) A
(b) An
(c) The
(d) No article

**Answer:** (b) An

**Solution:** "Honest" has a **silent h** (the "h" is not pronounced: /ˈɒnɪst/). Words with silent h take "an" because they begin with a vowel sound. [Source: §2.3 Sound Rule, §2.4 Examples]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "a" because "h" looks like a consonant. The test is the SOUND, not the letter — silent h means the word starts with a vowel sound.

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q8. "He is interested ___ history."
(a) of
(b) at
(c) in
(d) on

**Answer:** (c) in

**Solution:** "Interested **in**" is the correct collocation. "Interested" expresses attention/curiosity and must be followed by "in". [Source: §3.6 — Adjective + Preposition]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "at" (common error from "good at" confusion) or (d) "on" (incorrect).

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q9. "Neither he ___ I am going to the party."
(a) or
(b) nor
(c) and
(d) but

**Answer:** (b) nor

**Solution:** "**Neither...nor**" is a **correlative conjunction** pair. They must be used together (FANBOYS-style pairing). "Neither A nor B" is the only correct form. [Source: §4.5 Correlative Conjunctions, §4.6 Common Errors]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "or" — "neither...or" is incorrect; it must be "neither...nor".

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q10. "I ___ go to the market tomorrow."
(a) can
(b) may
(c) will
(d) All are possible

**Answer:** (c) will

**Solution:** "Tomorrow" is a future time signal. "**Will** + V1" expresses future tense (Future Simple). This is the standard future marker. [Source: §3.6 Future Tenses, §3.8 Time Signal Table]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "may" — "may" expresses possibility, not pure future. "I may go" means "I might go", not "I will definitely go tomorrow".

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q11. "She is ___ MBA from a top university."
(a) a
(b) an
(c) the
(d) no article

**Answer:** (b) an

**Solution:** "**M**BA" is pronounced "**em**-bee-ay" — starting with the **vowel sound /ɛ/**. Therefore, use "an". The article depends on the **sound** of the first letter of the abbreviation. [Source: §2.3 Sound Rule, §2.4 — "an MLA"]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "a" because "M" looks like a consonant. The pronunciation starts with "em" (vowel sound), so "an" is correct.

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q12. "He apologised ___ being late to the meeting."
(a) for
(b) of
(c) at
(d) with

**Answer:** (a) for

**Solution:** "Apologise **for**" is the correct verb-preposition collocation. "Apologise for + noun/gerund" is the standard pattern. [Source: §3.6 — Verb + Preposition]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (d) "with" (incorrect), (b) "of" (incorrect). Only "for" follows "apologise".

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q13. "We have been waiting ___ 2 hours."
(a) since
(b) for
(c) from
(d) during

**Answer:** (b) for

**Solution:** "2 hours" is a **duration**. Use "**for** + duration" and "**since** + point in time". This is a classic SVA/preposition collocation tested in SSC. [Source: §3.5 Common Preposition Errors, §6.4]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "since" — "since" needs a starting point (e.g., "since 10 AM"), not a duration.

**Time Target:** 10 sec

---

### Q14. "Although he is rich, but he is unhappy." The correct form is:
(a) Although he is rich, but he is unhappy
(b) Although he is rich, he is unhappy
(c) He is rich, although but he is unhappy
(d) No improvement

**Answer:** (b) Although he is rich, he is unhappy

**Solution:** "**Although**" and "**but**" are **redundant** when used together. "Although" already introduces a contrast, so adding "but" doubles the contrast marker. Use one or the other, not both. [Source: §4.6 Common Errors, §6.6 The "Although-But" Trick]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) — keeping both "although" and "but" together. This is the most common error in Indian English usage but is grammatically wrong.

**Time Target:** 15 sec

---

### Q15. "She is angry ___ me for forgetting her birthday."
(a) with
(b) at
(c) on
(d) All are possible

**Answer:** (a) with

**Solution:** Use "**angry with**" when referring to a **person**. Use "**angry at**" when referring to a **thing/situation** ("angry at the decision"). "Me" is a person, so "with" is correct. [Source: §3.6 — "angry with (someone) / angry at (something)"]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "at" — both can be correct depending on context, but for a person, "with" is preferred in standard English.

**Time Target:** 10 sec
