# SSC CGL Tier 1 — Chemistry: Periodic Classification of Elements

> **Exam:** SSC CGL Tier 1 | **Time:** 15 min (60 sec/Q) | **Total:** 15 Qs
> **Difficulty:** 5 Easy, 7 Medium, 3 Hard | **Trap Qs:** 2
> **Source basis:** Ch. 12 Chemistry — Periodic Classification (NCERT Class 10, Ch 5 + Class 11)

## Q1. Modern periodic law (Easy)
Modern periodic law is based on:
(a) Atomic weight (b) Atomic number (c) Atomic mass (d) Valency

**Answer:** (b) Atomic number
**Solution:** Moseley (1913) showed that the properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers. This solved anomalies in Mendeleev's table (which used atomic weight). Modern periodic table arranges elements in increasing order of atomic number.
**Shortcut:** Modern = atomic number. Mendeleev = atomic weight.
**Common Mistake:** Saying atomic weight (that's Mendeleev's basis).
**Time target:** 15 sec

---

## Q2. Number of groups (Easy)
The modern periodic table has _____ vertical columns called groups:
(a) 7 (b) 8 (c) 18 (d) 32

**Answer:** (c) 18
**Solution:** Modern periodic table: 18 vertical columns (groups 1-18) and 7 horizontal rows (periods 1-7). Groups 1-2 are s-block; 13-18 are p-block; 3-12 are d-block.
**Shortcut:** Modern table = 18 groups, 7 periods.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 7 (that's the number of periods).
**Time target:** 15 sec

---

## Q3. Alkali metals group (Easy)
Alkali metals are placed in which group?
(a) Group 1 (b) Group 2 (c) Group 17 (d) Group 18

**Answer:** (a) Group 1
**Solution:** Group 1 = Alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr). Soft, highly reactive, stored in kerosene. Group 2 = Alkaline earth metals. Group 17 = Halogens. Group 18 = Noble gases.
**Shortcut:** Group 1 = alkali (form strong alkalis with water). Group 2 = alkaline earth.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing group 1 with group 2.
**Time target:** 15 sec

---

## Q4. Halogens group (Easy)
Halogens are placed in which group of the modern periodic table?
(a) Group 1 (b) Group 2 (c) Group 16 (d) Group 17

**Answer:** (d) Group 17
**Solution:** Group 17 = Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At). Highly reactive non-metals. F₂ (pale yellow gas), Cl₂ (greenish-yellow gas), Br₂ (red-brown liquid), I₂ (violet solid).
**Shortcut:** Group 17 = halogens (salt-formers).
**Common Mistake:** Saying Group 16 (that's chalcogens/oxygen family).
**Time target:** 15 sec

---

## Q5. Atomic radius trend (Medium)
Across a period (left to right), the atomic radius:
(a) Increases (b) Decreases (c) Remains constant (d) First increases then decreases

**Answer:** (b) Decreases
**Solution:** Across a period, nuclear charge increases (more protons) while electrons are added to the same shell. The increased pull brings electrons closer → atomic radius decreases. Na > Mg > Al > Si > P > S > Cl (size decreases left to right in period 3).
**Shortcut:** Across period: size ↓. Down group: size ↑.
**Common Mistake:** Saying increases (that's down a group, not across a period).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q6. Most electronegative element (Medium)
The most electronegative element is:
(a) Oxygen (b) Chlorine (c) Fluorine (d) Nitrogen

**Answer:** (c) Fluorine
**Solution:** Electronegativity (Pauling scale): F = 4.0 (highest), O = 3.5, N = 3.0, Cl = 3.0. F is in Group 17, Period 2 — small atom with strong pull on bonding electrons.
**Shortcut:** F = 4.0 (most electronegative). Cs/Fr = most electropositive.
**Common Mistake:** Saying Oxygen (it's 2nd most, not 1st).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q7. Metallic character (Medium — Trap)
Metallic character across a period:
(a) Increases (b) Decreases (c) Remains constant (d) First increases then decreases

**Answer:** (b) Decreases
**Solution:** Across a period: metallic character decreases, non-metallic character increases. Going from Na to Cl, elements become less metallic and more non-metallic. Down a group: metallic character increases.
**Shortcut:** Across period: metallic ↓, non-metallic ↑. Down group: metallic ↑.
**Common Mistake:** Saying increases (that's down a group).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q8. Noble gases (Medium)
Noble gases are placed in which group?
(a) Group 1 (b) Group 17 (c) Group 18 (d) Group 2

**Answer:** (c) Group 18
**Solution:** Group 18 = Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn). Colourless, odourless, inert (very unreactive) due to fully filled valence shells (stable octet). Used in balloons (He), lighting (Ne), welding (Ar).
**Shortcut:** Group 18 = noble gases (rightmost, stable octet).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with Group 17 (halogens).
**Time target:** 20 sec

---

## Q9. Dobereiner's triads (Medium)
Dobereiner's law of triads (1829) grouped elements in sets of:
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 7

**Answer:** (b) 3
**Solution:** Dobereiner grouped elements with similar properties in sets of three (triads) where the atomic weight of the middle element ≈ average of the other two. Example: Li (7), Na (23), K (39) — middle (23) ≈ (7+39)/2 = 23. ✓
**Shortcut:** Triads = 3 elements. Newlands' octaves = 8 elements.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with Newlands' octaves (8).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q10. Period 2 elements (Medium)
Period 2 of the modern periodic table contains:
(a) 2 elements (b) 8 elements (c) 18 elements (d) 32 elements

**Answer:** (b) 8 elements
**Solution:** Periods 1 = 2 elements (H, He). Periods 2 and 3 = 8 elements each (short periods). Periods 4 and 5 = 18 elements each (long periods). Period 6 = 32 elements (longest). Period 7 = 32 elements (incomplete).
**Shortcut:** Period 1 = 2; 2 & 3 = 8; 4 & 5 = 18; 6 & 7 = 32.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 18 (that's period 4 or 5).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q11. Element with Z = 17 (Medium)
Element with atomic number 17 belongs to:
(a) Group 1, Period 3 (b) Group 17, Period 3 (c) Group 17, Period 2 (d) Group 18, Period 3

**Answer:** (b) Group 17, Period 3
**Solution:** Z = 17 is Chlorine (Cl). Period 3 (third row). Group 17 (halogen, since 17-10 = 7, so 7 valence electrons). Cl is a halogen, greenish-yellow gas.
**Shortcut:** Cl = Z=17, Group 17, Period 3.
**Common Mistake:** Saying Group 18 (noble gases).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q12. Valency of Group 14 (Medium — Trap)
The valency of elements in Group 14 of the modern periodic table is:
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4

**Answer:** (d) 4
**Solution:** Group 14 elements (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb) have 4 valence electrons, so valency = 4. Group 13 = valency 3; Group 15 = valency 3 (gain 3); Group 16 = valency 2; Group 17 = valency 1.
**Shortcut:** Group 14 = 4 valence e⁻ → valency 4. Same for whole group.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 3 (that's group 13).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q13. Atomic radius calculation (Hard)
Arrange in increasing atomic radius: Li, Na, K, Rb
(a) Li < Na < K < Rb (b) Rb < K < Na < Li (c) K < Na < Li < Rb (d) Li < K < Na < Rb

**Answer:** (a) Li < Na < K < Rb
**Solution:** Group 1 (alkali metals): atomic radius increases down the group (new shells added). So Li (smallest) < Na < K < Rb (largest).
**Shortcut:** Down a group = atomic radius increases. So top is smallest.
**Common Mistake:** Reversing the order (top has largest radius — wrong).
**Time target:** 45 sec

---

## Q14. Position of hydrogen (Hard)
Hydrogen resembles both alkali metals (Group 1) and halogens (Group 17). Its position in the modern periodic table is:
(a) Group 1 only (b) Group 17 only (c) Not fixed (placed at top, sometimes with Group 1, sometimes with Group 17) (d) Group 18

**Answer:** (c) Not fixed (placed at top, sometimes with Group 1, sometimes with Group 17)
**Solution:** Hydrogen is unique — 1 valence electron like Group 1 (alkali metals), but also needs 1 electron to complete shell like Group 17 (halogens). Forms H⁺ (like Na⁺) and H⁻ (like Cl⁻). Position debated; often placed at top separately.
**Shortcut:** Hydrogen = anomaly. Resembles both Group 1 and 17.
**Common Mistake:** Saying Group 1 only (ignores halogen resemblance).
**Time target:** 60 sec

---

## Q15. Ionisation energy trend (Hard)
Across a period, ionisation energy:
(a) Decreases (b) Increases (c) Remains constant (d) First decreases then increases

**Answer:** (b) Increases
**Solution:** Across a period, nuclear charge increases and atomic radius decreases → electrons held more tightly → more energy needed to remove an electron → ionisation energy increases. Down a group, IE decreases.
**Shortcut:** Across period: IE ↑ (harder to remove e⁻). Down group: IE ↓.
**Common Mistake:** Saying decreases (that's down a group).
**Time target:** 60 sec
