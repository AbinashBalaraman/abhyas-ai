## Q1. Covalent bonds in methane (Easy)
The number of covalent bonds in methane (CH₄) is:
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5

**Answer:** (c) 4
**Solution:** Carbon is tetravalent (4 valence electrons). It shares 4 electrons with 4 H atoms, forming 4 single covalent bonds in CH₄.
**Shortcut:** Carbon always forms 4 bonds (tetravalent).
**Common Mistake:** Saying 8 (that's total valence electrons, not bonds).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Allotrope of carbon — conductor (Easy)
Which allotrope of carbon is a good conductor of electricity?
(a) Diamond (b) Graphite (c) Fullerene (d) Coal

**Answer:** (b) Graphite
**Solution:** Graphite has delocalised electrons between its hexagonal layers → conducts electricity. Used as electrodes in batteries and electrolysis. Diamond has all valence electrons in bonds → doesn't conduct.
**Shortcut:** Graphite = conductor (delocalised e⁻). Diamond = insulator.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing diamond (hardest but doesn't conduct).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Functional group of alcohol (Easy)
The functional group of an alcohol is:
(a) –CHO (b) –COOH (c) –OH (d) >C=O

**Answer:** (c) –OH
**Solution:** Functional groups: –OH (alcohol), –CHO (aldehyde), –COOH (carboxylic acid), >C=O (ketone). Methanol (CH₃OH) and ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) are common alcohols.
**Shortcut:** Alcohol = –OH (think: "OH! Alcohol").
**Common Mistake:** Picking –CHO (that's aldehyde).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q4. Saturated hydrocarbon formula (Easy)
The general formula of an alkane (saturated hydrocarbon) is:
(a) CₙH₂ₙ (b) CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (c) CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (d) CₙH₂ₙ₋₁

**Answer:** (b) CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
**Solution:** Alkanes (single C–C bonds): CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Alkenes (one C=C): CₙH₂ₙ. Alkynes (one C≡C): CₙH₂ₙ₋₂. Examples: CH₄, C₂H₆, C₃H₈.
**Shortcut:** Alkane = 2n+2. Alkene = 2n. Alkyne = 2n-2.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing alkene with alkyne formulas.
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q5. Functional group of carboxylic acid (Medium)
The functional group present in ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH) is:
(a) –OH (b) –CHO (c) –COOH (d) >C=O

**Answer:** (c) –COOH
**Solution:** Ethanoic acid (acetic acid, vinegar) has the –COOH (carboxyl) functional group. –CHO is aldehyde; –OH is alcohol; >C=O is ketone.
**Shortcut:** Acetic acid = –COOH.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing –CHO (that's aldehyde, e.g., ethanal CH₃CHO).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q6. Ethanol IUPAC name (Medium)
The IUPAC name of CH₃CH₂OH is:
(a) Methanol (b) Ethanol (c) Propanol (d) Ethanal

**Answer:** (b) Ethanol
**Solution:** CH₃CH₂OH has 2 carbons → "eth-" prefix. Contains –OH → "-ol" suffix. So IUPAC name: ethanol. CH₃OH = methanol; CH₃CH₂CH₂OH = propanol.
**Shortcut:** Count carbons: 1=meth, 2=eth, 3=prop. –OH = -ol.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with methanol (1 C) or propanol (3 C).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Soot in flame (Medium)
Unsaturated hydrocarbons burn with a:
(a) Clean blue flame (b) Sooty/smoky yellow flame (c) Colourless flame (d) Green flame

**Answer:** (b) Sooty/smoky yellow flame
**Solution:** Saturated hydrocarbons (alkanes) burn with a clean blue flame (complete combustion). Unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes, alkynes) have higher C:H ratio and burn with a sooty/smoky yellow flame (incomplete combustion, unburnt carbon particles glow).
**Shortcut:** Saturated = clean flame. Unsaturated = sooty.
**Common Mistake:** Saying clean (that's for saturated).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Hardest allotrope of carbon (Medium — Trap)
The hardest allotrope of carbon is:
(a) Graphite (b) Diamond (c) Fullerene (d) Coal

**Answer:** (b) Diamond
**Solution:** Diamond is the hardest natural substance (10 on Mohs scale) because of its rigid 3D tetrahedral network where each carbon is bonded to 4 others. Graphite is soft/slippery (used as lubricant).
**Shortcut:** Diamond = hardest, conductor of heat, no electricity. Graphite = soft, conductor of electricity.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing diamond's hardness with graphite's conductivity.
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q9. Soap vs detergent (Medium)
Soap does not work well in hard water because:
(a) Hard water is acidic (b) Soap forms insoluble Ca/Mg salts (scum) (c) Detergents are better (d) Soap is too expensive

**Answer:** (b) Soap forms insoluble Ca/Mg salts (scum)
**Solution:** Hard water contains Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions. These react with soap (sodium stearate) to form insoluble calcium/magnesium stearate (scum). Detergents work in hard water because their Ca/Mg salts are soluble.
**Shortcut:** Hard water + soap → scum. Detergents → no scum (work in hard water).
**Common Mistake:** Saying soap is too expensive (irrelevant to chemistry).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q10. Catalyst in esterification (Medium)
In esterification (acid + alcohol → ester + water), the catalyst used is:
(a) HCl (b) NaOH (c) Concentrated H₂SO₄ (d) Mg(OH)₂

**Answer:** (c) Concentrated H₂SO₄
**Solution:** Esterification is catalysed by concentrated H₂SO₄ (an acid catalyst). E.g., CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O (ethyl ethanoate, sweet smell). NaOH is used in saponification (reverse + hydrolysis).
**Shortcut:** Esterification = H₂SO₄ catalyst. Saponification = NaOH.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with saponification catalyst (NaOH).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q11. Glacial acetic acid (Medium — Trap)
Glacial acetic acid is:
(a) Dilute acetic acid (b) Pure ethanoic acid (freezes at 16.6°C) (c) Acetic acid + ice (d) Acetic anhydride

**Answer:** (b) Pure ethanoic acid (freezes at 16.6°C)
**Solution:** Glacial acetic acid is pure (100%) ethanoic acid. The name comes from its tendency to form ice-like crystals at temperatures below 16.6°C (its freezing point). In cold countries, pure acetic acid solidifies.
**Shortcut:** Glacial = pure (forms ice-like crystals).
**Common Mistake:** Saying it's dilute (the opposite).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q12. Vinegar composition (Medium)
The main component of vinegar is:
(a) Citric acid (b) Acetic acid (5-8%) (c) Formic acid (d) Lactic acid

**Answer:** (b) Acetic acid (5-8%)
**Solution:** Vinegar is a 5-8% solution of acetic acid (ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH) in water. Used as a food preservative, in pickles. Citric acid is in citrus fruits; formic acid in ant stings; lactic acid in sour milk.
**Shortcut:** Vinegar = acetic acid 5-8% solution.
**Common Mistake:** Picking citric acid (that's in lemons).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q13. Catenation (Hard)
The self-linking property of carbon to form long chains is called:
(a) Tetravalency (b) Catenation (c) Hybridisation (d) Isomerism

**Answer:** (b) Catenation
**Solution:** Catenation = carbon's ability to bond with other carbon atoms forming long chains (straight, branched, or rings). Combined with tetravalency, this gives rise to millions of organic compounds. Si also shows catenation but to a much lesser extent.
**Shortcut:** Catenation = self-linking. Tetravalency = 4 bonds.
**Common Mistake:** Saying tetravalency (that's about the number of bonds, not self-linking).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q14. Soap cleansing action (Hard)
Soap micelles have:
(a) Hydrophobic interior, hydrophilic exterior (b) Hydrophilic interior, hydrophobic exterior (c) Both ends hydrophobic (d) Both ends hydrophilic

**Answer:** (a) Hydrophobic interior, hydrophilic exterior
**Solution:** Soap molecule: long hydrocarbon chain (hydrophobic, oil-loving) + –COO⁻Na⁺ head (hydrophilic, water-loving). In water, soap forms micelles with hydrophobic tails inside (trapping oil/dirt) and hydrophilic heads outside (interacting with water). Water washes away the dirt-laden micelles.
**Shortcut:** Micelle: dirt in middle (hydrophobic tail inside), water-friendly head outside.
**Common Mistake:** Reversing interior/exterior.
**Time target:** 60 sec

---

## Q15. Covalent bond sharing (Hard)
In a covalent bond between two atoms:
(a) Electrons are transferred from one atom to another (b) Electrons are shared between the atoms (c) Both atoms lose electrons (d) Both atoms gain electrons

**Answer:** (b) Electrons are shared between the atoms
**Solution:** Covalent bond = sharing of electron pairs between two atoms. Each atom contributes one electron to the shared pair. Contrast with ionic bond = transfer of electrons. Carbon forms covalent bonds (cannot easily lose/gain 4 electrons).
**Shortcut:** Covalent = sharing. Ionic = transfer.
**Common Mistake:** Saying transfer (that's ionic bond).
**Time target:** 45 sec
