### Q1. The SI unit of force is:
(a) Joule (b) Watt (c) Newton (d) Pascal

**Answer: (c) Newton**

**Solution:** The SI unit of force is the Newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². It is named after Sir Isaac Newton (Second Law: F = m·a). Joule is the SI unit of energy/work. Watt is the SI unit of power (1 W = 1 J/s). Pascal is the SI unit of pressure (1 Pa = 1 N/m²).

### Q2. Which of the following is NOT a noble gas?
(a) Helium (b) Neon (c) Hydrogen (d) Argon

**Answer: (c) Hydrogen**

**Solution:** Noble gases (Group 18) are Helium (He), Neon (Ne), Argon (Ar), Krypton (Kr), Xenon (Xe), and Radon (Rn). They are inert (chemically non-reactive) because they have a complete octet (or duet for He). Hydrogen, although placed in Group 1 sometimes, is not a noble gas — it is reactive and forms compounds. Radon is radioactive.

**Time Target:** 15s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.40 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q3. The chemical symbol of Sodium is:
(a) So (b) S (c) Na (d) Sn

**Answer: (c) Na**

**Solution:** The chemical symbol of Sodium is Na (from Latin "natrium"). Atomic number 11; Group 1 alkali metal. It is highly reactive, stored in kerosene. Other symbols: So is not a real element symbol; S is Sulfur; Sn is Tin (from Latin "stannum"). Symbols are derived from English or Latin names.

### Q4. The pH of a neutral solution is:
(a) 0 (b) 7 (c) 14 (d) 1

**Answer: (b) 7**

**Solution:** The pH scale (0-14) measures acidity/basicity. pH 7 is neutral (pure water at 25°C). pH < 7 is acidic; pH > 7 is basic (alkaline). pH stands for "potential of Hydrogen." Lemon juice has pH ~2; blood pH ~7.4; stomach acid ~1.5; sodium hydroxide ~13.

**Time Target:** 15s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.55 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. Which is the largest organ in the human body?
(a) Liver (b) Brain (c) Skin (d) Heart

**Answer: (c) Skin**

**Solution:** Skin is the largest organ in the human body with a surface area of ~2 m² and weight of ~3.5-5 kg. The liver is the largest internal organ (~1.5 kg). The brain weighs ~1.4 kg. The heart weighs ~300 g. The skin protects against pathogens, regulates temperature, and senses touch.

### Q6. Newton's First Law of Motion is also known as the Law of:
(a) Action and Reaction (b) Inertia (c) Acceleration (d) Gravitation

**Answer: (b) Inertia**

**Solution:** Newton's First Law: A body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion stays in motion with constant velocity, unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force. This is the Law of Inertia. The Second Law (F = m·a) gives acceleration; the Third Law is action-reaction. Inertia depends on mass — heavier objects have more inertia.

**Time Target:** 20s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.10 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. Which of the following vitamins is water-soluble?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin D (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin K

**Answer: (c) Vitamin C**

**Solution:** Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid) is water-soluble. Other water-soluble vitamins are B-complex (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B7, B9, B12). Fat-soluble vitamins are A, D, E, and K. Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy (bleeding gums). Vitamin A: vision; Vitamin D: bones (sunlight); Vitamin K: blood clotting.

### Q8. The hardest substance in the human body is:
(a) Bone (b) Enamel (c) Dentin (d) Cartilage

**Answer: (b) Enamel**

**Solution:** Tooth enamel is the hardest substance in the human body. It is composed of ~96% hydroxyapatite (calcium phosphate mineral). Enamel covers the visible part of the tooth (crown). Dentin is the second hardest, beneath the enamel. Bone is harder than dentin but less hard than enamel.

### Q9. The speed of light in vacuum is approximately:
(a) 3 × 10⁶ m/s (b) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (c) 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s (d) 3 × 10⁵ m/s

**Answer: (b) 3 × 10⁸ m/s**

**Solution:** The speed of light in vacuum is c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Light is the fastest thing in the universe. In denser media (water, glass), light travels slower. Speed of sound in air ~340 m/s (1 million times slower than light). Light from the Sun takes ~8 min 20 sec to reach Earth.

### Q10. Which blood group is the universal donor?
(a) A (b) B (c) AB (d) O negative

**Answer: (d) O negative**

**Solution:** O negative blood group is the universal donor for red blood cells, as it has no A, B, or Rh antigens and is compatible with all blood types. AB positive is the universal recipient. Karl Landsteiner discovered blood groups (A, B, AB, O) and won the Nobel Prize in 1930. The Rh factor was later discovered by Landsteiner and Wiener in 1940.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.108 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q11. Which part of the plant cell is responsible for photosynthesis?
(a) Mitochondria (b) Chloroplast (c) Ribosome (d) Nucleus

**Answer: (b) Chloroplast**

**Solution:** Chloroplasts are the organelles responsible for photosynthesis in plant cells. They contain chlorophyll, the green pigment that absorbs light. Mitochondria are the "powerhouse" of the cell (ATP production via cellular respiration). Ribosomes synthesize proteins. The nucleus controls cell activities and contains DNA.

### Q12. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Carbon dioxide (d) Helium

**Answer: (c) Carbon dioxide**

**Solution:** Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the most prominent greenhouse gas, along with methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), water vapour, and fluorinated gases. Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere, causing global warming. Oxygen and Nitrogen are the most abundant atmospheric gases (~21% and ~78%) but are not greenhouse gases.

**Time Target:** 20s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.135 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q13. The atomic number of Carbon is:
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 14

**Answer: (a) 6**

**Solution:** Carbon (C) has atomic number 6 (6 protons) and atomic mass 12 (6 protons + 6 neutrons). It is in Group 14 and Period 2 of the periodic table. Carbon forms the basis of all organic compounds (millions of compounds) due to its unique ability to catenate (form long chains). Carbon has allotropes: diamond, graphite, fullerene, graphene.

**Time Target:** 20s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.42 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q14. The image formed by a convex mirror is always:
(a) Real and inverted (b) Virtual and erect (c) Real and erect (d) Virtual and inverted

**Answer: (b) Virtual and erect**

**Solution:** A convex mirror (diverging mirror) always forms a virtual, erect, and diminished image, regardless of object position. Convex mirrors are used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles because they provide a wider field of view and erect images. A concave mirror can form real or virtual images depending on object position relative to focus.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/05_General_Science_Recap.md, p.18 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q15. Which is the longest cell in the human body?
(a) Liver cell (b) Nerve cell (Neuron) (c) Muscle cell (d) Red blood cell

**Answer: (b) Nerve cell (Neuron)**

**Solution:** The nerve cell (neuron) is the longest cell in the human body. Some neurons (e.g., the motor neurons connecting the spinal cord to the foot muscles) can be over 1 metre long. Red blood cells (RBCs) are the most abundant (~5 million/mm³). The smallest cell is the sperm cell in males.
