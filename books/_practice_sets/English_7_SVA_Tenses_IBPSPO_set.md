### Q1. "Everyone ___ here."
(a) are
(b) is
(c) were
(d) have been

**Answer:** (b) is

**Solution:** "**Everyone**" is a singular indefinite pronoun → singular verb "is". [Source: §2.4 Rule 5 — Anyone, Everyone, Someone, No one, Nobody, Somebody → singular]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "are" — being misled by the -one ending (which sounds plural but is singular).

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q2. "She ___ to school yesterday."
(a) goes
(b) went
(c) has gone
(d) is going

**Answer:** (b) went

**Solution:** "Yesterday" → **Past Simple** (V2). [Source: §3.5 Past Simple, §3.8]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (c) "has gone" (Present Perfect — wrong with "yesterday").

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q3. "The news ___ shocking."
(a) are
(b) is
(c) were
(d) have been

**Answer:** (b) is

**Solution:** "**News**" is singular (despite -s ending). [Source: §2.4 Rule 7 — News, Mathematics, Physics → singular]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "are" (thinking plural due to -s).

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q4. "I ___ coffee every morning."
(a) drink
(b) drinks
(c) am drinking
(d) have drunk

**Answer:** (a) drink

**Solution:** "I" (first person) + present simple habitual → base form "drink" (no -s for I/you/we/they). [Source: §2.2]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "drinks" (adding -s reserved for he/she/it).

**Time Target:** 3 sec

---

### Q5. "Either Ram or his friends ___ coming."
(a) is
(b) was
(c) are
(d) has

**Answer:** (c) are

**Solution:** With "**Either...or**", verb agrees with the **nearest subject**. "Friends" is plural → "are". [Source: §2.4 Rule 3 — Closest subject wins]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "is" (taking first subject's verb).

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q6. "He ___ just ___ lunch."
(a) has, eat
(b) has, eaten
(c) is, eating
(d) had, eat

**Answer:** (b) has, eaten

**Solution:** "Just" → **Present Perfect** (has + V3). "Just" indicates recent past. [Source: §3.4 Present Perfect, §3.8 Time Signal Table]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (c) "is eating" (Present Continuous — wrong with "just").

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q7. "The teacher with the students ___ here."
(a) are
(b) is
(c) were
(d) have been

**Answer:** (b) is

**Solution:** When joined by "**with**", the verb agrees with the **first/main subject** ("teacher" is singular). [Source: §2.4 Rule 2 — "with/as well as" → first subject wins]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "are" (thinking compound subject due to "with").

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q8. "I ___ TV when the phone rang."
(a) watched
(b) was watching
(c) am watching
(d) have watched

**Answer:** (b) was watching

**Solution:** "When the phone rang" (interrupting past action) → **Past Continuous** (was + V1+ing) for the background action. [Source: §3.5 Past Continuous, §3.8]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "watched" (Past Simple — doesn't show ongoing interrupted action).

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q9. "None of the answers ___ correct."
(a) is
(b) are
(c) was
(d) has

**Answer:** (b) are

**Solution:** "**None of**" can be singular (formal) or plural (informal). With SSC/Banking preference for informal usage, "are" is accepted. [Source: §2.4 Rule 13 — "None" can be singular or plural]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (a) "is" (strict formal usage, less common in modern exams).

**Time Target:** 5 sec

---

### Q10. "By tomorrow, she ___ the work."
(a) completes
(b) will complete
(c) will have completed
(d) has completed

**Answer:** (c) will have completed

**Solution:** "**By tomorrow**" (future reference) + completion → **Future Perfect** (will have + V3). [Source: §3.6 Future Perfect, §3.8]

**Common Mistake:** Choosing (b) "will complete" (Future Simple — doesn't emphasize completion by the future point).

**Time Target:** 5 sec
