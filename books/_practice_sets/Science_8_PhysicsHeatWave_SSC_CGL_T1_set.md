## Q1. SI unit of temperature (Easy)
The SI unit of temperature is:
(a) Celsius (b) Fahrenheit (c) Kelvin (d) Rankine

**Answer:** (c) Kelvin
**Solution:** SI unit of thermodynamic temperature is Kelvin (K). 0 K = −273.15°C (absolute zero). Celsius (°C) and Fahrenheit (°F) are commonly used but not SI units.
**Shortcut:** SI temperature = Kelvin (K). 0°C = 273.15 K.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing Celsius (commonly used but not SI).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Heat unit (Easy)
The SI unit of heat is:
(a) Calorie (b) Joule (c) Watt (d) BTU

**Answer:** (b) Joule
**Solution:** SI unit of heat (and all forms of energy) is Joule (J). 1 calorie = 4.186 J. Calorie and BTU are non-SI units.
**Shortcut:** Heat = energy = Joule. 1 cal = 4.186 J.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing calorie (common unit, but not SI).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Specific heat of water (Easy)
The specific heat capacity of water is:
(a) 420 J/(kg·K) (b) 4200 J/(kg·K) (c) 42000 J/(kg·K) (d) 42 J/(kg·K)

**Answer:** (b) 4200 J/(kg·K)
**Solution:** Water has a very high specific heat capacity: 4200 J/(kg·K) (= 1 cal/g·°C). This is why water is used in car radiators (coolant) and as a heat reservoir — it can store/absorb a lot of heat with little temperature change.
**Shortcut:** Water = 4200 J/(kg·K) = 1 cal/(g·°C). Highest among common substances.
**Common Mistake:** Off by factor of 10 (420 vs 4200).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q4. Conduction (Easy)
Heat transfer by conduction is most effective in:
(a) Solids (b) Liquids (c) Gases (d) Vacuum

**Answer:** (a) Solids
**Solution:** Conduction involves transfer of heat through a material without actual movement of particles. Most effective in solids (especially metals like silver, copper, aluminium). Vacuum has no medium → conduction = 0.
**Shortcut:** Conduction = solids (metals best). Convection = fluids. Radiation = any (even vacuum).
**Common Mistake:** Saying vacuum (no medium → conduction impossible).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q5. Anomalous expansion of water (Medium)
Water has maximum density at:
(a) 0°C (b) 4°C (c) 25°C (d) 100°C

**Answer:** (b) 4°C
**Solution:** Water has anomalous expansion: it contracts from 0°C to 4°C (becoming denser), then expands from 4°C upward. Maximum density at 4°C. This is why ice floats (less dense) and why aquatic life survives in cold climates (water at bottom is 4°C, not frozen).
**Shortcut:** Water is densest at 4°C → ice floats → lakes freeze from top.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 0°C (that's the freezing point, but density is lower there).
**Time target:** 30 sec

---

## Q6. Convection (Medium)
Heat transfer by convection occurs in:
(a) Solids only (b) Liquids and gases (c) Vacuum only (d) Metals only

**Answer:** (b) Liquids and gases
**Solution:** Convection is the actual movement of heated fluid particles carrying heat. It occurs in fluids (liquids and gases), not in solids. Causes: sea breeze, land breeze, boiling water, room ventilation.
**Shortcut:** Convection = fluids (liquids + gases). Movement of bulk fluid.
**Common Mistake:** Saying solids (conduction, not convection, is in solids).
**Time target:** 20 sec

---

## Q7. Radiation (Medium)
Heat transfer by radiation:
(a) Requires a medium (b) Does not require a medium (c) Occurs only in solids (d) Occurs only at high temperatures

**Answer:** (b) Does not require a medium
**Solution:** Radiation transfers heat through electromagnetic waves (mainly infrared). It does not need a medium — the Sun's heat reaches Earth through the vacuum of space. All bodies above absolute zero emit radiation.
**Shortcut:** Radiation = EM waves, no medium needed. Light from Sun proves this.
**Common Mistake:** Saying medium is required (that's wrong — that's why Sun's heat reaches us).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Black vs white surface (Medium — Trap)
A black surface is:
(a) Good absorber, poor emitter (b) Poor absorber, good emitter (c) Good absorber, good emitter (d) Poor absorber, poor emitter

**Answer:** (c) Good absorber, good emitter
**Solution:** By Kirchhoff's law, good absorbers are good emitters. Black surfaces are the best absorbers and emitters of heat radiation. White/shiny surfaces are poor absorbers but good reflectors — that's why we wear light clothes in summer.
**Shortcut:** Black = absorbs + emits. White = reflects.
**Common Mistake:** Saying "absorber but poor emitter" (that's against Kirchhoff's law).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q9. Latent heat of fusion of ice (Medium)
The latent heat of fusion of ice is:
(a) 3.34 × 10³ J/kg (b) 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg (c) 3.34 × 10⁶ J/kg (d) 3.34 × 10⁷ J/kg

**Answer:** (b) 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg
**Solution:** Latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg. This is the heat required to convert 1 kg of ice at 0°C to water at 0°C. Latent heat of vaporisation of water is much higher: 22.5 × 10⁵ J/kg.
**Shortcut:** L_fusion (ice) = 3.34 × 10⁵; L_vap (water) = 22.5 × 10⁵ (about 7× more).
**Common Mistake:** Off by factor of 10 or 100.
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q10. Sound requires medium (Medium)
Sound cannot travel through:
(a) Solids (b) Liquids (c) Gases (d) Vacuum

**Answer:** (d) Vacuum
**Solution:** Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave — it requires a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) for propagation. In vacuum (like space), there are no particles to vibrate, so sound cannot travel. This is why astronauts use radio communication.
**Shortcut:** Sound = mechanical wave → needs medium → no vacuum.
**Common Mistake:** Saying gases (sound travels in gases like air; we hear speech).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q11. Speed of sound in air (Medium)
The approximate speed of sound in air at 20°C is:
(a) 343 m/s (b) 1500 m/s (c) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (d) 30 m/s

**Answer:** (a) 343 m/s
**Solution:** Speed of sound in air at 20°C ≈ 343 m/s. Speed of light = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (a million times faster). Speed of sound in water ≈ 1500 m/s; in steel ≈ 5000 m/s.
**Shortcut:** Sound in air ≈ 340 m/s. Light ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s (lightning seen before thunder).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q12. Pitch (Medium — Trap)
The pitch of a sound is determined by its:
(a) Amplitude (b) Frequency (c) Wavelength (d) Speed

**Answer:** (b) Frequency
**Solution:** Pitch is determined by frequency. High frequency = high pitch (whistle); low frequency = low pitch (drum, male voice). Amplitude determines loudness, not pitch. Quality/timbre distinguishes sounds of same pitch/loudness from different sources.
**Shortcut:** Pitch = frequency. Loudness = amplitude. Quality = timbre.
**Common Mistake:** Saying amplitude (that's loudness).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q13. Heat calculation (Hard)
How much heat is required to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water by 10°C? (specific heat = 4200 J/(kg·K))
(a) 4200 J (b) 8400 J (c) 42000 J (d) 84000 J

**Answer:** (d) 84000 J
**Solution:** Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4200 × 10 = 84000 J.
**Shortcut:** Q = mcΔT. 2 × 4200 × 10 = 84,000 J = 84 kJ.
**Common Mistake:** Using wrong specific heat or forgetting to multiply.
**Time target:** 60 sec

---

## Q14. Echo minimum distance (Hard)
The minimum distance to hear an echo is approximately:
(a) 10 m (b) 17.2 m (c) 34 m (d) 100 m

**Answer:** (b) 17.2 m
**Solution:** To hear a distinct echo, the reflected sound must reach the ear at least 0.1 s after the original. Distance = speed × time/2 (round trip) = 343 × 0.1 / 2 = 17.15 m ≈ 17.2 m.
**Shortcut:** Minimum echo distance ≈ 17.2 m (for 0.1 s gap in air at 20°C).
**Common Mistake:** Not dividing by 2 for round trip.
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q15. Newton's law of cooling (Hard)
According to Newton's law of cooling, the rate of loss of heat is proportional to:
(a) Temperature of the body (b) Difference of temperature between body and surroundings (c) Mass of the body (d) Specific heat of the body

**Answer:** (b) Difference of temperature between body and surroundings
**Solution:** Newton's law of cooling: dQ/dt ∝ (T − T_s), where T is body temperature and T_s is surroundings temperature. The rate depends on the temperature difference, not just the temperature itself.
**Shortcut:** Rate ∝ (T − T_surroundings). Bigger difference → faster cooling.
**Common Mistake:** Saying "temperature of body" (should be the difference).
**Time target:** 60 sec
