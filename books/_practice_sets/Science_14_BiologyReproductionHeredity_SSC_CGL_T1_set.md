## Q1. Asexual reproduction (Easy)
Asexual reproduction involves:
(a) Two parents (b) One parent (c) Gametes (d) Fertilization

**Answer:** (b) One parent
**Solution:** Asexual reproduction = single parent produces offspring without fusion of gametes. Offspring are genetically identical (clones). Examples: binary fission (Amoeba), budding (Hydra, yeast), fragmentation (Spirogyra), spore formation.
**Shortcut:** Asexual = 1 parent = clones. Sexual = 2 parents = variation.
**Common Mistake:** Saying two parents (that's sexual reproduction).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Site of fertilization (Easy)
In human females, fertilization normally occurs in the:
(a) Uterus (b) Vagina (c) Fallopian tube (d) Cervix

**Answer:** (c) Fallopian tube
**Solution:** Fertilization (sperm + egg → zygote) occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct). The zygote then travels to the uterus for implantation (~7 days after fertilization). Uterus = implantation; cervix = lower part of uterus.
**Shortcut:** Fertilization = fallopian tube. Implantation = uterus.
**Common Mistake:** Saying uterus (implantation is there, not fertilization).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Monohybrid F₂ ratio (Easy)
Mendel's monohybrid cross F₂ phenotypic ratio is:
(a) 1:1 (b) 3:1 (c) 9:3:3:1 (d) 1:2:1

**Answer:** (b) 3:1
**Solution:** Monohybrid cross (Tt × Tt): F₂ shows 3 dominant phenotype : 1 recessive phenotype. Genotype ratio is 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1:2:1). Dihybrid = 9:3:3:1.
**Shortcut:** Mono = 3:1 (phenotype), 1:2:1 (genotype). Di = 9:3:3:1.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing 9:3:3:1 (that's dihybrid).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q4. Sex determination (Easy)
Sex of a human child is determined by:
(a) Mother's X chromosome (b) Father's X or Y chromosome (c) Both parents equally (d) Random at conception

**Answer:** (b) Father's X or Y chromosome
**Solution:** Mother = XX (always gives X). Father = XY (gives X → XX girl, or Y → XY boy). So father determines the sex. Probability: 50% boy, 50% girl per conception.
**Shortcut:** Father = "decider" (X or Y).
**Common Mistake:** Saying mother (she has only X).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q5. Vegetative propagation (Medium)
Vegetative propagation by leaves occurs in:
(a) Potato (b) Bryophyllum (c) Ginger (d) Onion

**Answer:** (b) Bryophyllum
**Solution:** Bryophyllum reproduces vegetatively by leaf buds (adventitious buds on leaf margins). Potato = stem (tuber); Ginger = stem (rhizome); Onion = bulb (modified stem).
**Shortcut:** Leaf = Bryophyllum. Stem tuber = Potato. Rhizome = Ginger.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with potato (that's stem).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Dihybrid F₂ ratio (Medium)
Mendel's dihybrid cross F₂ phenotypic ratio is:
(a) 3:1 (b) 1:2:1 (c) 9:3:3:1 (d) 1:1

**Answer:** (c) 9:3:3:1
**Solution:** Dihybrid cross (RrYy × RrYy): F₂ shows 9 dominant both : 3 dominant first, recessive second : 3 recessive first, dominant second : 1 both recessive. This proves Law of Independent Assortment.
**Shortcut:** Dihybrid = 9:3:3:1.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with monohybrid 3:1.
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Testes function (Medium)
The testes in human males:
(a) Produce sperm and testosterone (b) Store urine (c) Produce eggs (d) Filter blood

**Answer:** (a) Produce sperm and testosterone
**Solution:** Testes (male gonads) produce sperm (male gametes) and the hormone testosterone (responsible for male secondary sexual characteristics). Located in scrotum (outside body for lower temperature).
**Shortcut:** Testes = sperm + testosterone. Ovaries = ova + oestrogen/progesterone.
**Common Mistake:** Saying produce eggs (that's ovaries).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Phenotype vs genotype (Medium — Trap)
The observable characteristics of an organism are called:
(a) Genotype (b) Phenotype (c) Karyotype (d) Allele

**Answer:** (b) Phenotype
**Solution:** Phenotype = observable trait (e.g., tall, dwarf, blood group, eye colour). Genotype = genetic makeup (e.g., Tt, TT, tt). Phenotype = Genotype + Environment.
**Shortcut:** Phenotype = "PHysical" appearance. Genotype = "GENetic" code.
**Common Mistake:** Reversing these (genotype is genetic, phenotype is observable).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q9. Pollination types (Medium)
Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma of the same flower is called:
(a) Cross-pollination (b) Self-pollination (c) Fertilization (d) Germination

**Answer:** (b) Self-pollination
**Solution:** Self-pollination = pollen from anther lands on stigma of the same flower (or another flower on the same plant). Cross-pollination = pollen from one plant lands on stigma of a flower of another plant (same species).
**Shortcut:** Self = same plant. Cross = different plant.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with cross-pollination.
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q10. Mendel's experimental organism (Medium)
Mendel performed his experiments on:
(a) Maize (b) Pea plants (c) Drosophila (d) Mice

**Answer:** (b) Pea plants
**Solution:** Gregor Mendel (1822-1884), Austrian monk, used garden pea plants (Pisum sativum) for his experiments. Chosen because: short life cycle, many contrasting traits (tall/dwarf, round/wrinkled, yellow/green seeds), clear-cut characteristics, self-pollination.
**Shortcut:** Mendel = pea plants. Morgan = Drosophila (fruit flies).
**Common Mistake:** Saying Drosophila (that's Thomas Hunt Morgan).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q11. Colour blindness (Medium — Trap)
Colour blindness is more common in males because:
(a) Males have two X chromosomes (b) It's X-linked recessive (males have only one X) (c) Females are immune (d) It's Y-linked

**Answer:** (b) It's X-linked recessive (males have only one X)
**Solution:** Colour blindness gene is on the X chromosome, recessive. Males have XY — one recessive allele on their only X → they express the trait. Females have XX — need two recessive alleles → much less common. So more common in males.
**Shortcut:** X-linked recessive: males (XY) express with one copy; females (XX) need two copies.
**Common Mistake:** Saying Y-linked (it's X-linked, not Y-linked).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q12. Law of segregation (Medium)
Mendel's law of segregation states that:
(a) Two alleles separate during gamete formation (b) Dominant alleles are stronger (c) Genes on different chromosomes assort together (d) Phenotype = Genotype + Environment

**Answer:** (a) Two alleles separate during gamete formation
**Solution:** Law of segregation: each organism has two alleles for each trait; during gamete formation, these alleles separate, so each gamete carries only one allele. At fertilization, the offspring gets one allele from each parent, restoring the pair.
**Shortcut:** Segregation = alleles SEPARATE into gametes.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with Law of Independent Assortment.
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q13. Menstrual cycle length (Hard)
The normal menstrual cycle in human females is approximately:
(a) 14 days (b) 21 days (c) 28 days (d) 40 days

**Answer:** (c) 28 days
**Solution:** Menstrual cycle ≈ 28 days. Ovulation (release of egg from ovary) occurs around day 14. If fertilization does not occur, menstruation begins around day 28-30.
**Shortcut:** 28 days total. Ovulation at day 14.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 14 days (that's when ovulation occurs, not cycle length).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q14. Darwin's theory (Hard)
Darwin's theory of evolution is based on:
(a) Use and disuse (b) Natural selection (survival of the fittest) (c) Inheritance of acquired characters (d) Mutation only

**Answer:** (b) Natural selection (survival of the fittest)
**Solution:** Charles Darwin (1859, "Origin of Species"): natural selection = individuals with favourable variations survive and reproduce, others die out. "Survival of the fittest." Lamarck's theory = use and disuse / inheritance of acquired characters (now discredited).
**Shortcut:** Darwin = natural selection. Lamarck = use-disuse (wrong).
**Common Mistake:** Saying Lamarck's theory (Darwin proved this wrong).
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q15. Blood group genetics (Hard)
A father has blood group A and mother has blood group B. They have a child with blood group O. What are the genotypes of the parents?
(a) I^A I^A and I^B I^B (b) I^A i and I^B i (c) I^A I^B and ii (d) ii and ii

**Answer:** (b) I^A i and I^B i
**Solution:** Blood group O genotype = ii. For child to be ii, both parents must carry the i allele. Father A = I^A i, Mother B = I^B i. The child inherits i from each parent → ii (group O).
**Shortcut:** O child needs ii from both parents → both must be heterozygous (I^A i and I^B i).
**Common Mistake:** Saying both parents are homozygous (then child can't be O).
**Time target:** 90 sec
