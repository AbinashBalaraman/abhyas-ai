## Q1. Functional unit of kidney (Easy)
The functional unit of the kidney is:
(a) Neuron (b) Nephron (c) Alveolus (d) Villus

**Answer:** (b) Nephron
**Solution:** Each kidney contains about 1 million nephrons (functional/structural units). Each nephron has a glomerulus (filtration) and tubule (reabsorption). Neuron = nerve cell; Alveolus = lung air sac; Villus = intestinal absorption unit.
**Shortcut:** Nephron = kidney; Neuron = nerve; Alveolus = lung; Villus = intestine.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing nephron (kidney) with neuron (nerve cell).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q2. Salivary enzyme (Easy)
The enzyme present in saliva is:
(a) Pepsin (b) Trypsin (c) Amylase (d) Lipase

**Answer:** (c) Amylase
**Solution:** Salivary amylase (ptyalin) breaks down starch into maltose (sugar). Pepsin works in stomach (proteins); Trypsin in small intestine (proteins); Lipase in small intestine (fats).
**Shortcut:** Saliva = starch → sugar (amylase).
**Common Mistake:** Picking pepsin (that's for proteins in the stomach).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Energy currency of cell (Easy)
The energy currency of the cell is:
(a) ADP (b) ATP (c) GTP (d) NADPH

**Answer:** (b) ATP
**Solution:** ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) releases ~30.5 kJ/mol when its terminal phosphate bond is broken, forming ADP + Pi. ATP is produced in mitochondria during cellular respiration.
**Shortcut:** A-P-P-P → Adenosine TriPhosphate. ATP → ADP + Pi + Energy.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing ADP (which is the lower-energy form, not the energy currency).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q4. Respiratory pigment (Easy)
The respiratory pigment in human blood is:
(a) Chlorophyll (b) Haemoglobin (c) Melanin (d) Myoglobin

**Answer:** (b) Haemoglobin
**Solution:** Haemoglobin (in RBCs) binds O₂ in lungs (forms oxyhaemoglobin) and releases it in tissues. Chlorophyll is in plants; Melanin is skin pigment; Myoglobin stores O₂ in muscles.
**Shortcut:** Hb + O₂ → HbO₂ (oxyhaemoglobin, bright red).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with myoglobin (similar but in muscles).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q5. Insulin secretion (Medium)
Insulin is secreted by the:
(a) Liver (b) Pancreas (c) Thyroid (d) Adrenal gland

**Answer:** (b) Pancreas
**Solution:** Insulin is secreted by β (beta) cells of the Islets of Langerhans in the pancreas. It lowers blood glucose. Deficiency causes Diabetes Mellitus. The pancreas is both endocrine (insulin, glucagon) and exocrine (digestive enzymes).
**Shortcut:** Pancreas = "sugar manager" (insulin + glucagon).
**Common Mistake:** Saying liver (largest gland, but doesn't make insulin).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Master gland (Medium)
The "master gland" of the human body is:
(a) Thyroid (b) Pituitary (c) Adrenal (d) Pancreas

**Answer:** (b) Pituitary
**Solution:** The pituitary gland (in the brain) controls other endocrine glands (thyroid, adrenal, gonads). It produces growth hormone (GH), TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin, etc. Hence called "master gland."
**Shortcut:** Pituitary = boss of all glands.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing thyroid (which is controlled by pituitary via TSH).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Phototropism hormone (Medium — Trap)
Phototropism in plants is caused by:
(a) Gibberellin (b) Auxin (c) Cytokinin (d) Ethylene

**Answer:** (b) Auxin
**Solution:** Auxin (esp. IAA — indole acetic acid) accumulates on the shaded side of the stem, causing those cells to elongate more, bending the stem toward light. Gibberellin = stem elongation, seed germination; Cytokinin = cell division; Ethylene = fruit ripening.
**Shortcut:** Auxin + light → "bend to bright".
**Common Mistake:** Picking gibberellin (also promotes growth but not phototropism).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q8. Mendel's monohybrid F₂ ratio (Medium)
Mendel's monohybrid cross F₂ phenotypic ratio is:
(a) 1:1 (b) 3:1 (c) 9:3:3:1 (d) 1:2:1

**Answer:** (b) 3:1
**Solution:** Monohybrid cross (Tt × Tt): F₂ shows 3 dominant phenotype : 1 recessive phenotype (3:1). Genotype ratio is 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1:2:1). The 9:3:3:1 ratio is for dihybrid cross.
**Shortcut:** Mono = 3:1 (phenotype), 1:2:1 (genotype). Di = 9:3:3:1.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing 9:3:3:1 (that's dihybrid).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q9. Sex determination (Medium)
The sex of a human child is determined by:
(a) Mother's X chromosome (b) Father's X or Y chromosome (c) Both parents equally (d) Randomly at conception

**Answer:** (b) Father's X or Y chromosome
**Solution:** Mother is XX (only X to give). Father is XY — he gives X (→ XX girl) or Y (→ XY boy). So father determines the child's sex. Probability: 50% girl, 50% boy per conception.
**Shortcut:** Father = "decider" (X or Y).
**Common Mistake:** Saying mother (she has only X).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q10. 10% law (Medium)
The 10% law of energy transfer in ecosystems was given by:
(a) Darwin (b) Mendel (c) Lindeman (d) Lamarck

**Answer:** (c) Lindeman
**Solution:** Lindeman's 10% law (1942): Only 10% of energy at one trophic level transfers to the next; 90% is lost as heat during metabolism. This limits food chains to 3-4 levels.
**Shortcut:** 10% rule = Lindeman; Darwin = natural selection; Mendel = genetics; Lamarck = use-disuse.
**Common Mistake:** Attributing to Darwin (he proposed natural selection, not energy transfer).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q11. Site of complete digestion (Medium)
The site of complete digestion of food in humans is:
(a) Stomach (b) Small intestine (c) Large intestine (d) Mouth

**Answer:** (b) Small intestine
**Solution:** Small intestine receives bile (from liver, emulsifies fats) and pancreatic juice (trypsin, lipase). Intestinal enzymes complete digestion: proteins → amino acids, carbohydrates → glucose, fats → fatty acids + glycerol. Villi absorb the nutrients.
**Shortcut:** Small intestine = "complete digestion + absorption".
**Common Mistake:** Stomach (only partial digestion of proteins begins here).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q12. Ozone depletion (Medium — Trap)
Ozone layer depletion is mainly caused by:
(a) CO₂ (b) CFCs (c) SO₂ (d) NO₂

**Answer:** (b) CFCs
**Solution:** CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons, used in refrigerants and aerosol propellants) release chlorine radicals in the stratosphere, which catalytically destroy ozone (O₃). One Cl atom can destroy ~1 lakh O₃ molecules. CO₂ causes global warming; SO₂/NO₂ cause acid rain.
**Shortcut:** CFCs → ozone hole. Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs.
**Common Mistake:** Picking CO₂ (that's greenhouse gas, not ozone-depleting).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q13. ATP energy released (Hard)
The energy released by hydrolysis of one mole of ATP is approximately:
(a) 7.3 kcal (b) 30.5 kJ (c) 100 kJ (d) 500 kJ

**Answer:** (b) 30.5 kJ
**Solution:** ATP → ADP + Pi releases ~30.5 kJ/mol (≈ 7.3 kcal/mol). This is the standard free energy change under cellular conditions.
**Shortcut:** ~30.5 kJ/mol = ~7.3 kcal/mol per ATP hydrolysis.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing units (kJ vs kcal — 30.5 kJ ≈ 7.3 kcal).
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q14. Trophic level energy (Hard)
If 100,000 J of energy is available at the producer level, how much reaches the tertiary consumer level?
(a) 1000 J (b) 100 J (c) 10 J (d) 1 J

**Answer:** (b) 100 J
**Solution:** 10% law: at each transfer, only 10% passes.
- Producers: 100,000 J
- Primary consumers (1st transfer): 10,000 J
- Secondary consumers (2nd transfer): 1,000 J
- Tertiary consumers (3rd transfer): 100 J
**Shortcut:** Each level → divide by 10. Three transfers = divide by 1000.
**Common Mistake:** Dividing only once (saying 10,000 J for tertiary).
**Time target:** 75 sec

---

## Q15. Fertilisation site (Hard)
In human females, fertilisation normally occurs in the:
(a) Uterus (b) Vagina (c) Fallopian tube (d) Cervix

**Answer:** (c) Fallopian tube
**Solution:** Fertilisation (sperm + ovum → zygote) occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct). The zygote then travels to the uterus for implantation (~7 days after fertilisation).
**Shortcut:** Fertilisation = tube; Implantation = uterus.
**Common Mistake:** Saying uterus (implantation happens there, not fertilisation).
**Time target:** 60 sec
