## Q1. SI unit of electric current (Easy)
The SI unit of electric current is:
(a) Volt (b) Ampere (c) Ohm (d) Coulomb

**Answer:** (b) Ampere
**Solution:** The SI unit of electric current is the Ampere (A), named after André-Marie Ampère. 1 A = 1 C/s (Coulomb per second). Volt is the SI unit of potential difference; Ohm is the SI unit of resistance; Coulomb is the SI unit of charge. The charge of one electron is 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.
**Shortcut:** Ampere = current; Volt = potential; Ohm = resistance; Coulomb = charge.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing Coulomb (charge) with Ampere (rate of flow of charge).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Power of lens (Easy)
The SI unit of power of a lens is:
(a) Watt (b) Joule (c) Dioptre (d) Candela

**Answer:** (c) Dioptre
**Solution:** Power of a lens P = 1/f (with f in metres). SI unit is dioptre (D). 1 D = 1 m⁻¹. For a convex lens, power is positive; for concave, negative.
**Shortcut:** Dioptre = 1/f (in metres). Power of +2.5D means f = +0.4 m = 40 cm (converging).
**Common Mistake:** Saying watt (which is the unit of power consumption, not lens power).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Speed of light (Easy)
The speed of light in vacuum is:
(a) 3 × 10⁶ m/s (b) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (c) 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s (d) 3 × 10⁵ m/s

**Answer:** (b) 3 × 10⁸ m/s
**Solution:** Speed of light in vacuum c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s. It is the highest attainable speed. Light slows in denser media (e.g., glass: 2 × 10⁸ m/s; water: 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s).
**Shortcut:** Memorize c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s; speed of sound ≈ 340 m/s (1 million times slower).
**Common Mistake:** Choosing 3 × 10⁵ m/s (speed of radio waves confused — actually EM waves of all frequencies travel at c in vacuum).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q4. Ohm's Law (Easy)
Ohm's law states that (at constant temperature):
(a) V is inversely proportional to I (b) V is directly proportional to I (c) I = V/R² (d) R = V/I²

**Answer:** (b) V is directly proportional to I
**Solution:** Ohm's law (1827, Georg Simon Ohm): V = IR, where V is potential difference, I is current, R is resistance (constant). So V ∝ I at constant R and temperature. V/I = R (constant).
**Shortcut:** V = IR; if V doubles, I doubles (when R is constant).
**Common Mistake:** Saying V is inversely proportional to I (this would be I = k/V, which is wrong).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q5. Myopia correction (Medium)
Myopia (near-sightedness) is corrected by using a:
(a) Convex lens (b) Concave lens (c) Bifocal lens (d) Cylindrical lens

**Answer:** (b) Concave lens
**Solution:** In myopia, the image of a distant object forms in front of the retina (eyeball too long or lens too curved). A concave (diverging) lens spreads the rays before they enter the eye, pushing the image back onto the retina.
**Shortcut:** Myopia = "MY-OPE-ah" → eye focuses too much → need diverging (concave) lens.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing convex (used for hypermetropia/far-sightedness).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Refraction formula (Medium)
The absolute refractive index of a medium is given by:
(a) n = v × c (b) n = c/v (c) n = c − v (d) n = c/v²

**Answer:** (b) n = c/v
**Solution:** Absolute refractive index n = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in medium = c/v. For diamond, n = 2.42 (highest natural); for air, n ≈ 1.0003 (≈ 1); for water, n = 1.33.
**Shortcut:** n_m = c/v_m. Light travels fastest in vacuum, slowest in diamond.
**Common Mistake:** Inverting the formula (v/c instead of c/v).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q7. Mirror formula (Medium — Trap)
A convex mirror has radius of curvature R = 3 m. What is the focal length?
(a) −1.5 m (b) +1.5 m (c) +3 m (d) −3 m

**Answer:** (b) +1.5 m
**Solution:** Focal length f = R/2. For a convex mirror, by sign convention, R and f are taken as positive (centre of curvature and focus are behind the mirror). So f = +3/2 = +1.5 m. Note: by new Cartesian convention, f is positive for convex mirror (focus behind mirror).
**Shortcut:** f = R/2. Magnitude is always |R|/2. Sign convention: convex mirror f = +R/2; concave f = −R/2.
**Common Mistake:** Taking f = −1.5 m (mixing up concave and convex).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q8. Resistance of stretched wire (Medium)
A wire is stretched to double its original length (no change in volume). Its resistance becomes:
(a) Half (b) Same (c) Double (d) Four times

**Answer:** (d) Four times
**Solution:** Original: R = ρL/A. After stretching: new length L' = 2L. Volume = constant, so A' = A/2. New R' = ρ(2L)/(A/2) = 4ρL/A = 4R.
**Shortcut:** Stretch to n times length → resistance becomes n² times.
**Common Mistake:** Saying "doubled" (only accounts for length, not cross-section reduction).
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q9. Series-parallel current (Medium)
Two resistors of 6 Ω each are connected first in series, then in parallel across a 12 V source. The ratio of currents (series : parallel) is:
(a) 1 : 4 (b) 4 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 2 : 1

**Answer:** (a) 1 : 4
**Solution:** Series: R_s = 12 Ω, I_s = 12/12 = 1 A. Parallel: R_p = 3 Ω, I_p = 12/3 = 4 A. Ratio I_s : I_p = 1 : 4.
**Shortcut:** Parallel resistance = R/n for n equal resistors. So R_p = R/2 = 3 Ω; current doubles per branch, so total current is 4× series.
**Common Mistake:** Using inverse resistance ratio incorrectly.
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q10. Joule's heating (Medium)
An electric iron has resistance 20 Ω and draws 5 A. Heat produced in 30 seconds is:
(a) 1500 J (b) 5000 J (c) 15000 J (d) 30000 J

**Answer:** (c) 15000 J
**Solution:** H = I²Rt = 5² × 20 × 30 = 25 × 20 × 30 = 15000 J.
**Shortcut:** H = I²Rt (with I in A, R in Ω, t in s → H in J).
**Common Mistake:** Using H = VIt instead and forgetting to find V first.
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q11. Domestic AC supply (Medium)
Domestic electric supply in India is:
(a) 110 V, 60 Hz (b) 220 V, 50 Hz (c) 220 V, 60 Hz (d) 440 V, 50 Hz

**Answer:** (b) 220 V, 50 Hz
**Solution:** India uses 220 V alternating current (AC) at 50 Hz frequency. The US uses 110 V at 60 Hz. AC is used because it can be easily stepped up/down by transformers; DC cannot.
**Shortcut:** India = 220 V / 50 Hz; US/Japan = 110 V / 60 Hz; UK = 230 V / 50 Hz.
**Common Mistake:** Saying DC (mains supply is AC; batteries supply DC).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q12. Fleming's left-hand rule (Medium — Trap)
Fleming's left-hand rule is used to find the direction of:
(a) Current in a conductor (b) Magnetic field around a wire (c) Force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field (d) Induced EMF

**Answer:** (c) Force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field
**Solution:** Fleming's left-hand rule (for motors): forefinger = field (B), second finger = current (I), thumb = force/motion (F). The right-hand rule is for generators (induced current direction). Right-hand thumb rule gives magnetic field direction around a current-carrying wire.
**Shortcut:** LEFT = motor (force); RIGHT = generator (induced current).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with right-hand thumb rule (for field around a wire).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q13. Sky is blue (Hard)
The blue colour of the sky is due to:
(a) Reflection from the ocean (b) Refraction in the atmosphere (c) Scattering of light by air molecules (d) Dispersion by water droplets

**Answer:** (c) Scattering of light by air molecules
**Solution:** Air molecules are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light. They scatter shorter (blue) wavelengths more effectively than longer (red) wavelengths (Rayleigh scattering). This scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions.
**Shortcut:** Scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴. Blue (shorter λ) scatters more than red.
**Common Mistake:** Saying "reflection from ocean" — that's why the sea looks blue, but the sky's blue is due to scattering.
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q14. Power of 110V operation (Hard)
A 220 V, 100 W bulb is operated at 110 V. Its actual power consumption is:
(a) 100 W (b) 75 W (c) 50 W (d) 25 W

**Answer:** (d) 25 W
**Solution:** R = V²/P = 220²/100 = 484 Ω (constant). At 110 V: P' = V²/R = 110²/484 = 12100/484 = 25 W. Since voltage halved, power becomes 1/4 (P ∝ V²).
**Shortcut:** P ∝ V² (at constant R). If V → V/2, then P → P/4.
**Common Mistake:** Saying 50 W (assuming linear relation P ∝ V).
**Time target:** 75 sec

## Q15. kWh conversion (Hard)
1 kWh equals:
(a) 3.6 × 10³ J (b) 3.6 × 10⁶ J (c) 3.6 × 10⁵ J (d) 36 × 10⁶ J

**Answer:** (b) 3.6 × 10⁶ J
**Solution:** 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 1 unit (commercial unit of electrical energy).
**Shortcut:** kWh → J: multiply by 3.6 × 10⁶. J → kWh: divide by 3.6 × 10⁶.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with 3.6 × 10³ (which would be watt-seconds × 1000).
**Time target:** 45 sec
