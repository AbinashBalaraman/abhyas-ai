### Q1. The Planning Commission of India was established on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 15 March 1950 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 1 January 1955

**Answer: (b) 15 March 1950**

**Solution:** The Planning Commission was established on 15 March 1950 by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet, based on the recommendations of the Advisory Planning Board (1946) under PM Nehru. It was dissolved on 1 January 2015 and replaced by NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India). The Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission was effectively the second-most powerful person after PM.

### Q2. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on:
(a) 1 January 2014 (b) 1 January 2015 (c) 26 January 2015 (d) 15 August 2014

**Answer: (b) 1 January 2015**

**Solution:** NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015. The Chairperson is the Prime Minister (ex officio); the Vice Chairperson is appointed by PM. The first VC was Arvind Panagariya. NITI Aayog is a policy think tank and advisory body, unlike the directive/target-based Planning Commission.

### Q3. Which Five Year Plan focused on industrialization and was based on the Mahalanobis model?
(a) First (b) Second (c) Third (d) Fifth

**Answer: (b) Second**

**Solution:** The Second Five Year Plan (1956-61) focused on rapid industrialization and was based on the **P.C. Mahalanobis model** of economic growth. The First Plan (1951-56) was based on the Harrod-Domar model and focused on agriculture. The Third Plan (1961-66) aimed at self-reliant economy. India had 12 Five Year Plans, with Plan Holidays in 1966-69 and 1990-92.

### Q4. Who is considered the "Father of Modern Economics"?
(a) Karl Marx (b) John Maynard Keynes (c) Adam Smith (d) Alfred Marshall

**Answer: (c) Adam Smith**

**Solution:** Adam Smith (1723-1790), a Scottish moral philosopher and political economist, is considered the "Father of Modern Economics" or "Father of Capitalism." His book "An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations" (1776) laid the foundations of classical economics. He introduced the concept of the "invisible hand" of the market. The term "economics" comes from Greek "oikonomia" (household management).

### Q5. GDP stands for:
(a) Gross Domestic Product (b) Gross Demand Product (c) General Domestic Product (d) Government Domestic Plan

**Answer: (a) Gross Domestic Product**

**Solution:** GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is the total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country's geographical boundary in a given year. Formula: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M), where C = Consumption, I = Investment, G = Government Spending, X = Exports, M = Imports. GNP = GDP + NFIA (Net Factor Income from Abroad). India's base year for GDP is 2011-12.

### Q6. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) was established in:
(a) 1925 (b) 1935 (c) 1947 (d) 1950

**Answer: (b) 1935**

**Solution:** The RBI was established on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act 1934, based on the recommendations of the Hilton Young Commission. Initially it was a private shareholders' bank; nationalised on 1 January 1949. The first Governor was Sir Osborne Smith; the first Indian Governor was C.D. Deshmukh. The current Governor (2025-26) is Sanjay Malhotra. RBI is the monetary authority and regulator of banks.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/04_Indian_Economy.md, p.10 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. GST in India was rolled out on:
(a) 1 April 2017 (b) 1 July 2017 (c) 1 January 2018 (d) 1 April 2018

**Answer: (b) 1 July 2017**

**Solution:** Goods and Services Tax (GST) was rolled out in India on 1 July 2017, replacing multiple central and state taxes like excise, VAT, service tax, etc. It is one of the biggest indirect tax reforms since Independence. The GST has 5 slabs: 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%. Petroleum products and alcohol are currently outside GST.

### Q8. The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act was passed in:
(a) 2000 (b) 2003 (c) 2005 (d) 2010

**Answer: (b) 2003**

**Solution:** The FRBM Act was passed in 2003 to ensure fiscal discipline by reducing fiscal deficit. The original target was to reduce fiscal deficit to 3% of GDP and eliminate revenue deficit. Targets have been revised multiple times due to COVID-19 and economic conditions. It applies to central and state governments.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/04_Indian_Economy.md, p.16 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. Which is the largest component of GDP in India (by sector)?
(a) Agriculture (b) Industry (c) Services (d) Manufacturing

**Answer: (c) Services**

**Solution:** The Services sector is the largest contributor to India's GDP (~53%), followed by Industry (~26%) and Agriculture (~18%) as per FY24 estimates. The Service sector includes IT, banking, insurance, transport, communication, real estate, and public administration. Despite this, ~58% of India's population is dependent on agriculture.

### Q10. SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) was established in:
(a) 1988 (b) 1990 (c) 1992 (d) 1995

**Answer: (a) 1988**

**Solution:** SEBI was established in 1988 as a non-statutory body and given statutory powers through the SEBI Act, 1992. Its headquarters is in Mumbai. It regulates stock exchanges, mutual funds, and other securities markets. The current Chairman is Tuhin Kanta Pandey (2025). SEBI's primary functions include protecting investor interests and promoting development of the securities market.

### Q11. The Small Savings Schemes in India are operated through:
(a) RBI (b) SEBI (c) Post Offices and Banks (d) NABARD

**Answer: (c) Post Offices and Banks**

**Solution:** Small Savings Schemes include Public Provident Fund (PPF), National Savings Certificate (NSC), Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP), Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY), and Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS). They are operated through Post Offices and Banks, administered by the Ministry of Finance. The interest rates are reviewed quarterly.

### Q12. The PMJDY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana) was launched on:
(a) 15 August 2014 (b) 28 August 2014 (c) 2 October 2014 (d) 26 January 2015

**Answer: (b) 28 August 2014**

**Solution:** PMJDY was launched on 28 August 2014 by PM Modi. It is a national mission for financial inclusion to ensure access to financial services (banking, savings, credit, insurance) for all households. It offers zero-balance savings accounts with RuPay debit card, accident and life insurance cover. Over 50+ crore accounts have been opened under the scheme.

### Q13. MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) guarantees how many days of employment in a financial year?
(a) 50 days (b) 75 days (c) 100 days (d) 150 days

**Answer: (c) 100 days**

**Solution:** MGNREGA, enacted in 2005, guarantees 100 days of wage employment per financial year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work. It was later renamed to "Mahatma Gandhi NREGA." The scheme is demand-driven, and unemployment allowance must be paid if work is not provided within 15 days.

### Q14. The Finance Commission of India is constituted by the President under which Article?
(a) Article 256 (b) Article 266 (c) Article 280 (d) Article 300

**Answer: (c) Article 280**

**Solution:** The Finance Commission is constituted by the President under Article 280 of the Constitution. It reviews the financial position of the Union and States and recommends the principles for sharing tax revenues between them. The 16th Finance Commission (constituted 2024) is chaired by Arvind Panagariya. It is a quasi-judicial body.

### Q15. The "Twin Balance Sheet" problem refers to:
(a) RBI and SEBI (b) Banks and Corporates (c) Centre and States (d) Imports and Exports

**Answer: (b) Banks and Corporates**

**Solution:** The "Twin Balance Sheet" problem refers to the stress on banks (rising NPAs - Non-Performing Assets) and corporates (high debt) simultaneously. This was a major issue in 2015-2020. The IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016) was introduced to address corporate stress. Government recapitalisation of PSBs (~₹2.7 lakh crore over 2017-19) helped banks clean up their books.
