### Q1.
"One who helps another to commit a crime" is called:
- (a) Accomplice (b) Accomplish (c) Accord (d) Account

**Answer:** (a) Accomplice
**Solution:** Accomplice = a partner in crime. (#R = 8, very high frequency in SSC).
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Person category.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with "Accomplish" (verb: to complete).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q2.
"To give up one's authority or throne" is called:
- (a) Abdicate (b) Abrogate (c) Abolish (d) Absolve

**Answer:** (a) Abdicate
**Solution:** "Abdicate" specifically refers to giving up throne/authority. "Abrogate" is for laws.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Person category; specific verb for renouncing throne.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing Abdicate (throne) with Abrogate (laws).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q3.
"A place where animals are slaughtered for food" is called:
- (a) Abattoir (b) Aviary (c) Aquarium (d) Apiary

**Answer:** (a) Abattoir
**Solution:** Abattoir = slaughterhouse. Aviary = bird place. Aquarium = water animal place. Apiary = bee place.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Place category; Latin root.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing similar-sounding "place" words.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q4.
"Deviation from the right course" is called:
- (a) Aberration (b) Ablution (c) Abolition (d) Absolution

**Answer:** (a) Aberration
**Solution:** Aberration = departure from what's normal/right. Ablution = washing. Abolition = ending (laws). Absolution = forgiveness.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — State/condition category.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing similar -tion nouns.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q5.
"The act of washing oneself" is called:
- (a) Ablution (b) Abolition (c) Absorption (d) Acceleration

**Answer:** (a) Ablution
**Solution:** Ablution = ceremonial or religious washing.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Action category.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with "abolition" (ending something).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q6.
"ABATE" is most similar in meaning to:
- (a) Diminish (b) Increase (c) Maintain (d) Cause

**Answer:** (a) Diminish
**Solution:** Abate = decrease; Diminish = decrease. They are synonyms.
**Grammar Rule:** Synonym: words with similar meaning.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with antonyms (increase).
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q7.
"ACCELERATE" is most opposite in meaning to:
- (a) Speed up (b) Decelerate (c) Maintain (d) Stop

**Answer:** (b) Decelerate
**Solution:** Accelerate = speed up; Decelerate = slow down. Direct antonym pair.
**Grammar Rule:** Antonym: words with opposite meaning.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing "stop" (which is stronger than "decelerate").
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q8.
"To bell the cat" means:
- (a) To delay unnecessarily (b) To take a difficult task upon oneself (c) To be the leader (d) To be noisy

**Answer:** (b) To take a difficult task upon oneself
**Solution:** "Bell the cat" = undertake a risky task that benefits the group (from a fable where mice plan to bell a cat).
**Grammar Rule:** Idiom — figurative meaning.
**Common Mistake:** Taking literally (a cat with a bell).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q9.
"To bite the bullet" means:
- (a) To eat fast (b) To face a tough situation bravely (c) To lose courage (d) To be cowardly

**Answer:** (b) To face a tough situation bravely
**Solution:** "Bite the bullet" = face difficulty with courage (origin: soldiers biting bullets during surgery without anesthesia).
**Grammar Rule:** Idiom — figurative meaning.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with cowardice.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q10.
Choose the correctly spelt word:
- (a) Occurence (b) Occurrence (c) Occurance (d) Ocurrence

**Answer:** (b) Occurrence
**Solution:** "Occurrence" = double 'c' (occur) + double 'r' + -ence.
**Grammar Rule:** Spelling — root "occur" doubled.
**Common Mistake:** Single 'c' or 'r'.
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q11.
Choose the correctly spelt word:
- (a) Accomodate (b) Acommodate (c) Accommodate (d) Acomodate

**Answer:** (c) Accommodate
**Solution:** "Accommodate" = double 'c' + double 'm' + -ate.
**Grammar Rule:** Spelling — root doubled before suffix.
**Common Mistake:** Single 'c' or 'm'.
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q12.
The French word "résumé" means:
- (a) Resume (start again) (b) Summary (c) Pause (d) Career

**Answer:** (b) Summary
**Solution:** "Résumé" (with accents) in English = a summary, especially of one's career.
**Grammar Rule:** Foreign word — French origin.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with English "resume" (start again).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q13.
"One who knows many languages" is called:
- (a) Polyglot (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Monolingual

**Answer:** (a) Polyglot
**Solution:** Polyglot = knows multiple languages. Bilingual = two languages. Linguist = studies language. Monolingual = one language.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Person category.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing polyglot with bilingual.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q14.
"Bigoted" is most similar in meaning to:
- (a) Tolerant (b) Prejudiced (c) Open-minded (d) Kind

**Answer:** (b) Prejudiced
**Solution:** Bigoted = having strong, unfair opinions; prejudiced. Tolerant is the antonym.
**Grammar Rule:** Synonym — meaning match.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing "tolerant" (which is the opposite).
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q15.
"Once in a blue moon" means:
- (a) Every month (b) Very rarely (c) Always (d) Never

**Answer:** (b) Very rarely
**Solution:** "Blue moon" = second full moon in a calendar month (rare event, every 2-3 years). Hence "very rarely."
**Grammar Rule:** Idiom — frequency.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing "blue" with "always."
**Time Target:** 30 sec
