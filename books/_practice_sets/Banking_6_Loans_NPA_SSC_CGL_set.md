### Q1. The Priority Sector Lending target is ____ of ANBC.
(a) 30% (b) 40% (c) 50% (d) 60%

**Answer: (b) 40%**

**Solution:** The Priority Sector Lending (PSL) target is 40% of ANBC (Adjusted Net Bank Credit) or CEOB (Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure), whichever is higher. PSL categories include: Agriculture (18% of ANBC), MSME/Micro Enterprises (7.5% of ANBC), Export Credit (2% of ANBC), and Weaker Sections (10% of ANBC). Foreign banks with 20+ branches also have a 40% target; those with <20 branches have 32%. RRBs have a 75% PSL target. PSL is a critical area for inclusive growth.

### Q2. An asset becomes NPA when overdue for more than:
(a) 30 days (b) 60 days (c) 90 days (d) 180 days

**Answer: (c) 90 days**

**Solution:** An asset (loan/advance) becomes a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) when interest and/or installment of principal remains overdue for more than 90 days. For agriculture loans, it is overdue for 2 cropping seasons (short duration) or 1 cropping season (long duration). NPAs are classified as Sub-Standard (NPA for up to 12 months), Doubtful (NPA for more than 12 months), or Loss Asset. NPA classification is important for provisioning requirements. The 90-day rule was introduced by RBI in 2004.

### Q3. SMA-1 category applies when overdue for:
(a) 1-30 days (b) 31-60 days (c) 61-90 days (d) 91-180 days

**Answer: (b) 31-60 days**

**Solution:** SMA-1 (Special Mention Account - 1) applies when principal or interest is overdue for 31-60 days. SMA-0 is for 1-30 days overdue. SMA-2 is for 61-90 days overdue. Beyond 90 days, the account becomes an NPA. SMA classification is for early identification of stress in loan accounts. SMAs help banks to take preventive measures before the account becomes NPA. The SMA framework was introduced by RBI in 2022 (effective February 2022). The SMA classification was designed to prevent situations like the YES Bank crisis.

### Q4. Provisioning for Doubtful D2 assets is:
(a) 25% (b) 40% (c) 75% (d) 100%

**Answer: (b) 40%**

**Solution:** Provisioning for Doubtful D2 assets is 40%. Provisioning norms (RBI) for different categories: Standard Assets (0.25-1%), Sub-Standard (15% secured / 25% unsecured), Doubtful D1 (25%), Doubtful D2 (40%), Doubtful D3 (100%), Loss Assets (100%). Provisioning is the amount banks set aside from profits to cover potential losses from NPAs. The provisioning requirements are mandatory under RBI's prudential norms. Higher provisioning reduces the bank's profit but strengthens the balance sheet.

### Q5. The SARFAESI Act was passed in:
(a) 1999 (b) 2000 (c) 2002 (d) 2004

**Answer: (c) 2002**

**Solution:** The SARFAESI Act (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act) was enacted in 2002. It allows banks and financial institutions to recover NPAs through three mechanisms: Securitisation, Asset Reconstruction (via ARCs), and Enforcement of Security Interest without court intervention. The borrower is given a 60-day notice. The minimum loan threshold is ₹2 lakh (raised to ₹20 lakh in some cases). SARFAESI does not apply to agricultural land below specified limits. SARFAESI was a major reform for NPA recovery.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.137-145 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. The IBC was enacted in:
(a) 2014 (b) 2016 (c) 2018 (d) 2020

**Answer: (b) 2016**

**Solution:** The IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code) was enacted in 2016 and came into force on 1 December 2016. The IBC created the IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) as the regulator, NCLT as the Adjudicating Authority, and NCLAT as the Appellate Authority. The CIRP (Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process) has a maximum period of 180 + 90 = 270 days (extendable to 330 days). If no resolution plan is approved, the company goes into liquidation. The IBC is a major reform for resolving corporate insolvency.

### Q7. NCLT stands for:
(a) National Company Law Tribunal (b) National Civil Law Tribunal (c) National Company Legal Tribunal (d) New Company Law Tribunal

**Answer: (a) National Company Law Tribunal**

**Solution:** NCLT stands for National Company Law Tribunal. It is the adjudicating authority under the IBC, 2016. NCLAT (National Company Law Appellate Tribunal) is the appellate authority. NCLT was established under the Companies Act, 2013. NCLT benches are in major cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, etc.). NCLT deals with company law matters, mergers, demergers, and insolvency cases. The threshold for IBC was raised to ₹1 crore (from ₹1 lakh). NCLT orders can be appealed to NCLAT and then to the Supreme Court.

### Q8. PMMY was launched in:
(a) 2014 (b) 2015 (c) 2016 (d) 2018

**Answer: (b) 2015**

**Solution:** PMMY (Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana) was launched on 8 April 2015 by PM Narendra Modi. The administrator is MUDRA Ltd. It targets non-corporate, non-farm small/micro enterprises. The three categories are: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (₹50,001 to ₹5 lakh), and Tarun (₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh). Loans are collateral-free. PMMY has helped millions of small entrepreneurs in India. MUDRA Ltd provides refinance to banks, NBFCs, and MFIs. The scheme aims to bring small businesses into the formal banking system.

**Answer: (c) ₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh**

**Solution:** Under PMMY, Tarun loans are from ₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh. The three PMMY categories are: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (₹50,001 to ₹5 lakh), and Tarun (₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh). Some Tarun Plus categories are extended to ₹20 lakh. PMMY loans are collateral-free and help small businesses access formal credit. The scheme has three categories designed to cover the entire spectrum of micro-enterprises. The loans are available from banks, NBFCs, and MFIs.

### Q10. KCC was launched based on the recommendation of:
(a) R.V. Gupta Committee (b) Narasimham Committee (c) Chakravarty Committee (d) Rangarajan Committee

**Answer: (a) R.V. Gupta Committee**

**Solution:** The KCC (Kisan Credit Card) was launched in 1998 based on the recommendations of the R.V. Gupta Committee. It provides short-term credit to farmers. The credit limit is based on land holding, cropping pattern, and other factors. The interest rate is 7% per annum, with a 3% subsidy for timely repayment (effective 4%). KCC has been expanded to include animal husbandry, fisheries, and other allied activities. The KCC scheme is one of the most successful credit delivery mechanisms in Indian agriculture. It is operated by commercial banks, RRBs, and cooperative banks.

### Q11. The effective interest rate for KCC with timely repayment is:
(a) 4% (b) 7% (c) 9% (d) 12%

**Answer: (a) 4%**

**Solution:** The effective interest rate for KCC with timely repayment is 4%. The base interest rate is 7% per annum, with a 3% subsidy provided by the Government of India for timely repayment. This makes the effective rate 4% for farmers who repay on time. The 3% interest subsidy was introduced in 2018-19 to provide relief to farmers. KCC has been expanded to include fisheries (2018-19) and animal husbandry. The KCC scheme has been hugely successful in providing timely and adequate credit support to farmers.

### Q14. CRR is maintained as:
(a) Cash with RBI (b) Government securities (c) Gold (d) Cash + Gold

**Answer: (a) Cash with RBI**

**Solution:** CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio) is maintained as cash with RBI. Banks must maintain a specified percentage of their Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) as cash balance with RBI under Section 42(1) of the RBI Act. Currently CRR is around 4%. No interest is paid on CRR balances (until 2007; partial interest from 2007). CRR is maintained daily. Increasing CRR reduces lendable funds and tightens liquidity, while decreasing CRR expands liquidity. CRR is one of the key monetary policy tools of RBI.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/06_Loans_NPA.md, p.190-198 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q15. SLR is maintained as:
(a) Cash with RBI (b) Government securities, cash, gold (c) Only government securities (d) Only gold

**Answer: (b) Government securities, cash, gold**

**Solution:** SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio) is maintained in the form of Government securities, cash, and gold. Banks must maintain a specified percentage of their NDTL in these assets under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Currently SLR is 18%. SLR is maintained daily on a fortnightly average basis. SLR is one of the key monetary policy tools of RBI. Banks can maintain SLR in the form of gold (with RBI), Government of India securities, State Government securities (SDL), and other approved securities. SLR serves as a buffer for liquidity.
