### Q1.
"One who is all-powerful" is called:
- (a) Omnipotent (b) Omniscient (c) Omnipresent (d) Plenary

**Answer:** (a) Omnipotent
**Solution:** Omnipotent = all-powerful. Omniscient = all-knowing. Omnipresent = everywhere at once.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Person category; "omni-" (all) + "potent" (powerful).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing "potent/potentate" with "scient" (knowing).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q2.
"A person who is afraid of heights" is called:
- (a) Acrophobic (b) Agoraphobic (c) Claustrophobic (d) Xenophobic

**Answer:** (a) Acrophobic
**Solution:** Acrophobia = fear of heights (acro = high). Agora = open spaces. Claustro = enclosed spaces. Xeno = strangers.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Person with "phobia" suffix.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing the four common phobias.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q3.
"EPHEMERAL" is most similar to:
- (a) Permanent (b) Short-lived (c) Ancient (d) Eternal

**Answer:** (b) Short-lived
**Solution:** Ephemeral = lasting a very short time. Permanent and eternal are antonyms.
**Grammar Rule:** Synonym — Latin/Greek root "ephemeros" (lasting only a day).
**Common Mistake:** Choosing "permanent" (antonym).
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q4.
"To add fuel to the fire" means:
- (a) To make a situation worse (b) To pour water (c) To start a fire (d) To be calm

**Answer:** (a) To make a situation worse
**Solution:** Adding fuel to fire worsens the fire. The idiom means worsening a situation.
**Grammar Rule:** Idiom — figurative meaning.
**Common Mistake:** Taking literally.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q5.
"OBSTINATE" is most opposite to:
- (a) Stubborn (b) Flexible (c) Determined (d) Resolute

**Answer:** (b) Flexible
**Solution:** Obstinate = stubbornly unyielding. Antonym = flexible/cooperative. Determined/resolute are similar in meaning.
**Grammar Rule:** Antonym — meaning opposite.
**Common Mistake:** Choosing "determined" (which is similar in meaning).
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q6.
"One who eats too much" is called:
- (a) Glutton (b) Gourmet (c) Gourmand (d) Gluttonist

**Answer:** (a) Glutton
**Solution:** Glutton = eats excessively. Gourmet = connoisseur of fine food (positive). Gourmand = loves food (neutral/positive). Gluttonist is not standard.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Person category.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing glutton (excess) with gourmand (love).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q7.
"To read between the lines" means:
- (a) To read slowly (b) To understand hidden meaning (c) To read aloud (d) To skim

**Answer:** (b) To understand hidden meaning
**Solution:** Read between the lines = understand subtext/implied meaning.
**Grammar Rule:** Idiom — figurative meaning.
**Common Mistake:** Taking literally (between physical lines).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q8.
The French word "déjà vu" means:
- (a) Already seen (b) For the first time (c) Never seen (d) Memory loss

**Answer:** (a) Already seen
**Solution:** "Déjà vu" (French) = the feeling that an event has happened before (literally "already seen").
**Grammar Rule:** Foreign word — French origin.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with "never seen."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q9.
Choose the correctly spelt word:
- (a) Entrepreneur (b) Enterpreneur (c) Entreprenur (d) Entrepeneur

**Answer:** (a) Entrepreneur
**Solution:** "Entrepreneur" (French origin) — ends in "-eur" (NOT "-eur" misspelled).
**Grammar Rule:** Spelling — French-derived.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing "-preneur" with "-preneur."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

### Q10.
"One who collects stamps" is called:
- (a) Philatelist (b) Philanthropist (c) Philologist (d) Philosopher

**Answer:** (a) Philatelist
**Solution:** Philatelist = stamp collector. Philanthropist = lover of humanity. Philologist = language scholar. Philosopher = wisdom lover.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Person with "phil-" prefix.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing "phil-" words.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q11.
"AMELIORATE" is most similar in meaning to:
- (a) Worsen (b) Improve (c) Maintain (d) Destroy

**Answer:** (b) Improve
**Solution:** Ameliorate = make better; improve. Worsen is the antonym.
**Grammar Rule:** Synonym — Latin root "melior" (better).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with "worsen" (antonym).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q12.
"A study of the origin and history of words" is called:
- (a) Etymology (b) Entomology (c) Ethnology (d) Etiology

**Answer:** (a) Etymology
**Solution:** Etymology = word origin study. Entomology = insect study. Ethnology = culture study. Etiology = cause study.
**Grammar Rule:** OWS — Field of study.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing similar-sounding fields.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q13.
"To cry over spilt milk" means:
- (a) To waste milk (b) To regret something that cannot be undone (c) To be very hungry (d) To be careless

**Answer:** (b) To regret something that cannot be undone
**Solution:** "Spilt milk" = past action that can't be reversed. The idiom means to uselessly regret the past.
**Grammar Rule:** Idiom — figurative meaning.
**Common Mistake:** Taking literally.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q14.
Choose the correctly spelt word:
- (a) Priviledge (b) Privilege (c) Privelege (d) Privelige

**Answer:** (b) Privilege
**Solution:** "Privilege" = 'i' before 'l', 'e' after 'g'. NOT "priviledge."
**Grammar Rule:** Spelling — i before e (but exceptions).
**Common Mistake:** Spelling "priviledge" (which is a common error).
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q15.
"CASTE" antonym (most opposite):
- (a) Class (b) Equality (c) System (d) Hierarchy

**Answer:** (b) Equality
**Solution:** Caste = a hereditary social class. Antonym = equality (no class distinction).
**Grammar Rule:** Antonym — meaning opposite.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with "class" (which is a related word, not opposite).
**Time Target:** 30 sec

## Tier 2 Specific Strategies

1. **OWS by category:** Practice grouping (Person, Place, Action, Field, Fear, Love).
2. **Root-based decoding:** "phil-" (love), "omni-" (all), "-phobia" (fear).
3. **Idiom imagery:** Visualize the literal meaning first, then decode the figurative sense.
4. **Foreign words:** Memorize origin language (French résumé, Latin per se, etc.).
