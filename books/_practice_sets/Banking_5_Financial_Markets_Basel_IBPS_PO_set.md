### Q1. A Commercial Paper (CP) is a:
(a) Long-term government bond (b) Short-term unsecured promissory note (c) Bank deposit receipt (d) Equity instrument

**Answer: (b) Short-term unsecured promissory note**

**Solution:** A Commercial Paper (CP) is a short-term unsecured promissory note issued by corporates to raise short-term funds. CP has a tenure of 15-365 days. The minimum amount is ₹5 lakh. CP is issued at a discount and redeemed at face value. CP is a money market instrument. It is regulated by RBI. CP is typically issued by large, highly-rated corporates. Other money market instruments: T-Bills (government securities), CD (Certificate of Deposit - issued by banks), Repo/Reverse Repo, CBLO.

### Q2. A Certificate of Deposit (CD) is issued by:
(a) Companies (b) Banks (c) Government (d) Mutual Funds

**Answer: (b) Banks**

**Solution:** A Certificate of Deposit (CD) is a short-term time deposit issued by banks. CD has a tenure of 7 days to 1 year. The minimum amount is ₹5 lakh. CDs are negotiable instruments and can be traded in the secondary market. CDs are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value. CDs are money market instruments and are regulated by RBI. CDs are a way for banks to raise short-term funds. The difference between CD and Fixed Deposit: CDs are tradable; FDs are not.

### Q3. SEBI is headquartered in:
(a) New Delhi (b) Mumbai (c) Hyderabad (d) Chennai

**Answer: (b) Mumbai**

**Solution:** SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) is headquartered in Mumbai. SEBI was constituted as a non-statutory body on 12 April 1988 and became a statutory body under the SEBI Act 1992. SEBI regulates the securities market in India, including stock exchanges (BSE, NSE), brokers, sub-brokers, mutual funds, and other intermediaries. SEBI's preamble is "to protect the interests of investors in securities and to promote the development of, and to regulate the securities market". SEBI issues major regulations like ICDR (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements), LODR (Listing Obligations), PIT (Prohibition of Insider Trading), etc.

### Q7. Basel II was published in:
(a) 1988 (b) 2000 (c) 2004 (d) 2010

**Answer: (c) 2004**

**Solution:** Basel II (New Capital Framework) was published in 2004. It has 3 Pillars: (1) Minimum Capital Requirements (credit risk, operational risk, market risk); (2) Supervisory Review Process (SRP); (3) Market Discipline (disclosures). Basel II expanded the scope from Basel I (which only covered credit risk) to include operational and market risks. The Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) remained at 8%. Basel II was criticized for contributing to the 2008 subprime crisis. Basel III (2010/11) was published in response. "Basel 2.5" (2009-10) added stressed VaR, IRC for market risk.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/05_Financial_Markets_Basel.md, p.197-215 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. Basel III was published in:
(a) 2008 (b) 2010/2011 (c) 2013 (d) 2015

**Answer: (b) 2010/2011**

**Solution:** Basel III was published in 2010/2011 in response to the 2008 global financial crisis. It introduced higher quality capital requirements (CET1 emphasis), Capital Conservation Buffer (2.5%), Counter-cyclical Buffer (0-2.5%), Leverage Ratio (3% minimum), LCR (100%), and NSFR (100%). The capital requirements: CET1 minimum 4.5% (7% with CCB), Tier 1 6% (8.5% with CCB), Total 8% (10.5% with CCB). Basel III also introduced CVA risk capital, IRRBB (Interest Rate Risk in Banking Book), large exposures framework, and SA-CCR (Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/05_Financial_Markets_Basel.md, p.218-237 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The three pillars of Basel II are:
(a) Capital, Liquidity, Leverage (b) Minimum Capital, Supervisory Review, Market Discipline (c) Credit, Operational, Market Risk (d) CAR, LCR, NSFR

**Answer: (b) Minimum Capital, Supervisory Review, Market Discipline**

**Solution:** The three pillars of Basel II are: (1) Pillar 1 - Minimum Capital Requirements (covering credit, operational, and market risks); (2) Pillar 2 - Supervisory Review Process (SRP) - supervisory assessment of capital adequacy; (3) Pillar 3 - Market Discipline - disclosure requirements. The minimum CAR remains 8% under Basel II. Pillar 1 has standardized and internal models-based approaches. Pillar 2 requires banks to assess their own capital adequacy and supervisors to review. Pillar 3 requires market disclosures of risk and capital information. The three pillars together form the comprehensive Basel II framework.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/05_Financial_Markets_Basel.md, p.197-215, p.537 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. An IPO is a:
(a) Issue of bonds (b) First-time share offering to the public (c) Bank deposit (d) Mutual fund

**Answer: (b) First-time share offering to the public**

**Solution:** IPO (Initial Public Offering) is the first time a company offers its shares to the public. Through an IPO, a private company becomes a publicly listed company. The company raises fresh capital from the public. FPO (Follow-on Public Offering) is when an already-listed company issues more shares. OFS (Offer for Sale) is when promoters sell shares to the public. Rights Issue is when existing shareholders are given the right to buy more shares. Bonus Issue is free shares to existing shareholders. QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement) is when shares are issued to institutional investors only. IPOs are regulated by SEBI.
