### Q1.
**Spot the error:** "She **has** gone to school yesterday."
- (a) She (b) has (c) gone (d) to school yesterday

**Answer:** (b) has
**Solution:** "Yesterday" is a point-of-time adverb → Simple Past required. Present Perfect ("has gone") cannot combine with "yesterday." Correct: "She **went** to school yesterday."
**Grammar Rule:** Present Perfect (have/has + V3) cannot be used with point-of-time adverbs (yesterday, ago, last week).
**Common Mistake:** Combining Present Perfect with "yesterday."
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q2.
**Spot the error:** "**The furniture** are expensive."
- (a) The (b) furniture (c) are (d) expensive

**Answer:** (c) are
**Solution:** "Furniture" is uncountable → singular verb "is."
**Grammar Rule:** Uncountable nouns (furniture, luggage, advice, information, scenery) → singular verb.
**Common Mistake:** Treating "furniture" as countable.
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q3.
**Spot the error:** "He **does not know** to swim."
- (a) He (b) does not know (c) to swim (d) No error

**Answer:** (c) to swim
**Solution:** "Know" + "how to" + V1. Correct: "He does not know **how to** swim."
**Grammar Rule:** "Know" does not take bare "to-infinitive"; requires "how to."
**Common Mistake:** Writing "know to" without "how."
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q4.
**Spot the error:** "I **am** working here since 2010."
- (a) I (b) am working (c) here (d) since 2010

**Answer:** (b) am working
**Solution:** "Since + point in time" → Present Perfect Continuous ("have been working"). Correct: "I **have been working** here since 2010."
**Grammar Rule:** "Since" + point in time → Present Perfect (Continuous).
**Common Mistake:** Using Present Continuous with "since."
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q5.
**Spot the error:** "**He** is one of those who never **admits** mistakes."
- (a) He (b) is one of those (c) who never (d) admits mistakes

**Answer:** (d) admits mistakes
**Solution:** "Those" is plural; "who" refers to "those" → plural verb "admit." Correct: "who never **admit** mistakes."
**Grammar Rule:** "One of those who + plural verb."
**Common Mistake:** Singular verb with "those who."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q6.
**Spot the error:** "The teacher, along with the students, **are** going on a trip."
- (a) The teacher (b) along with the students (c) are going (d) on a trip

**Answer:** (c) are going
**Solution:** "Along with / as well as / with" → verb agrees with the **first** subject ("teacher" is singular → "is"). Correct: "The teacher, along with the students, **is** going."
**Grammar Rule:** "A, along with B" → verb with A.
**Common Mistake:** Plural verb because of "students" after the comma.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q7.
**Sentence Improvement:** "He is **more smarter** than his brother."
- (a) more smart (b) smarter (c) most smart (d) No improvement

**Answer:** (b) smarter
**Solution:** "Smarter" is already comparative. "More" is redundant. Correct: "He is **smarter** than his brother."
**Grammar Rule:** Don't combine "-er" and "more" for comparatives.
**Common Mistake:** Double comparative.
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q8.
**Spot the error:** "**Either** he **or** she **are** wrong."
- (a) Either he (b) or she (c) are (d) wrong

**Answer:** (c) are
**Solution:** "Either...or" → verb agrees with **nearest** subject. "She" is singular → "is." Correct: "Either he or she **is** wrong."
**Grammar Rule:** "Either...or / Neither...nor" → verb with nearest subject.
**Common Mistake:** Defaulting to plural with "either...or."
**Time Target:** 25 sec

---

### Q9.
**Spot the error:** "**Neither** he **nor** I **were** present."
- (a) Neither he (b) nor I (c) were (d) present

**Answer:** (c) were
**Solution:** "Neither...nor" → verb with nearest subject. "I" requires "was" (NOT "were" outside subjunctive). Correct: "Neither he nor I **was** present."
**Grammar Rule:** "Neither...nor" + nearest subject; "I" + was (indicative).
**Common Mistake:** Using "were" with "I" outside hypothetical.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q10.
**Spot the error:** "**She** is **taller than me**."
- (a) She (b) is (c) taller than (d) me

**Answer:** (d) me
**Solution:** Formal: "taller than **I**" (subject case). In standard exam English, the formal form is preferred.
**Grammar Rule:** "Than" + subject case in formal English.
**Common Mistake:** Always defaulting to "I" or always to "me" — context matters.
**Time Target:** 25 sec

## SBI PO Prelims Strategy

1. **Subject-finding:** Identify the head noun ignoring modifiers and prepositional phrases.
2. **"A and B" / "A or B":** Different rules — "and" = plural, "or/nor" = nearest.
3. **Comparative form:** -er/-est or more/most — never both.
4. **Time markers:** since/for with Perfect; yesterday/ago with Simple Past.
