### Q1. Which is the largest planet in our solar system?
(a) Saturn (b) Jupiter (c) Neptune (d) Uranus

**Answer: (b) Jupiter**

**Solution:** Jupiter is the largest planet in our solar system (mass ~318 times Earth; diameter ~140,000 km). It is a gas giant with the famous Great Red Spot (a giant storm). Jupiter has 95+ known moons, with Ganymede being the largest. Saturn is the second largest and is known for its rings. Saturn is the least dense planet — it would float in water.

### Q2. The Indian Standard Time (IST) is based on the longitude:
(a) 75.5°E (b) 80.5°E (c) 82.5°E (d) 85.5°E

**Answer: (c) 82.5°E**

**Solution:** IST is based on 82.5°E longitude (passing through Mirzapur, near Allahabad, UP). IST = UTC + 5:30 hours. The 82.5°E was chosen as it passes through the centre of India. Each degree of longitude = 4 minutes of time difference. Mirzapur (UP) is the reference point.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.10 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q3. Which planet is known as the "Red Planet"?
(a) Venus (b) Mars (c) Jupiter (d) Mercury

**Answer: (b) Mars**

**Solution:** Mars is known as the "Red Planet" due to iron oxide (rust) on its surface. It has the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, and two small moons: Phobos and Deimos. Mars has the largest dust storms in the solar system. India's Mangalyaan (2014) made India the first Asian country to reach Mars orbit.

### Q4. The largest continent by area is:
(a) Africa (b) North America (c) Asia (d) Europe

**Answer: (c) Asia**

**Solution:** Asia is the largest continent (~44.58 million km²) and is the most populous (~4.7 billion). It is followed by Africa (30.37 million km²), North America (24.71 million km²), South America (17.84 million km²), Antarctica (14.20 million km²), Europe (10.18 million km²), and Australia/Oceania (8.60 million km²).

### Q5. The Tropic of Cancer does NOT pass through which Indian state?
(a) Gujarat (b) Madhya Pradesh (c) Odisha (d) Tripura

**Answer: (c) Odisha**

**Solution:** The Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram. Odisha is NOT on this line. The Tropic of Cancer divides India roughly into two halves.

### Q6. The Earth's innermost layer is called:
(a) Crust (b) Mantle (c) Outer Core (d) Inner Core

**Answer: (d) Inner Core**

**Solution:** The Earth's internal structure has 4 concentric layers: Crust (5-70 km thick), Mantle (~2,900 km, solid rock that flows slowly), Outer Core (~2,200 km, liquid iron-nickel; generates Earth's magnetic field), and Inner Core (~1,220 km radius, solid iron-nickel; temperature ~5,400°C). The Inner Core is the innermost and hottest layer.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.7 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. The Himalayas were formed by the collision of which two tectonic plates?
(a) Pacific and North American (b) Indian and Eurasian (c) African and South American (d) Indian and Pacific

**Answer: (b) Indian and Eurasian**

**Solution:** The Himalayas are young fold mountains formed by the collision of the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate around 50 million years ago. They extend ~2,400 km in length with a width of 400-150 km. They are divided into Greater Himalayas (Himadri), Lesser Himalayas (Himachal), and Outer Himalayas (Shivaliks). Mount Everest (8,849 m) is the highest peak.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.12 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. The longest river in India is:
(a) Godavari (b) Yamuna (c) Ganga (d) Brahmaputra

**Answer: (c) Ganga**

**Solution:** The Ganga is the longest river within India (~2,525 km), originating from the Gangotri Glacier (Devprayag, Uttarakhand) where Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet. The Brahmaputra (longest in India if we include Tibet) is longer overall (3,848 km total, ~916 km in India). Godavari is the longest river in South India (~1,465 km), called "Vridha Ganga" or "Dakshin Ganga."

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.18 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. Which is the highest peak in India?
(a) Nanda Devi (b) Kanchenjunga (c) K2 (Godwin-Austin) (d) Mount Everest

**Answer: (c) K2 (Godwin-Austin)**

**Solution:** K2 (Mount Godwin-Austin, 8,611 m) in the Ladakh region (PoK) is the highest peak in India. Mount Everest (8,849 m) is in Nepal. Kanchenjunga (8,586 m) is the highest peak in India proper (Sikkim). Nanda Devi (7,816 m) is the highest peak entirely within India. K2 is the second highest peak in the world.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.13 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. The Sundarbans delta is formed by:
(a) Ganga and Yamuna (b) Ganga and Brahmaputra (c) Godavari and Krishna (d) Mahanadi and Godavari

**Answer: (b) Ganga and Brahmaputra**

**Solution:** The Sundarbans is the world's largest delta, formed by the confluence of the Ganga, Brahmaputra, and Meghna rivers in West Bengal (India) and Bangladesh. It is also the largest mangrove forest in the world, home to the Royal Bengal Tiger. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Q11. The "Loo" is a hot, dusty summer wind that blows in:
(a) Rajasthan (b) Kerala (c) Assam (d) Tamil Nadu

**Answer: (a) Rajasthan**

**Solution:** "Loo" is a hot, dry, dusty wind that blows across the Indo-Gangetic Plain, especially Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, and UP during May-June. Temperatures can reach 50°C. The "Mango showers" (pre-monsoon rain in Kerala and Karnataka) are called "Cherry blossoms" or "April showers." The Western Disturbances affect North India in winter.

### Q12. Which is the deepest ocean in the world?
(a) Atlantic (b) Indian (c) Arctic (d) Pacific

**Answer: (d) Pacific**

**Solution:** The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest ocean in the world. The Mariana Trench in the Pacific contains the deepest point on Earth, the Challenger Deep (~11,034 m). The Pacific covers ~46% of the global ocean area. Other oceans in decreasing size: Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.9 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q13. The Western Ghats are also known as:
(a) Sahyadri (b) Aravalli (c) Nilgiri (d) Vindhya

**Answer: (a) Sahyadri**

**Solution:** The Western Ghats are also known as "Sahyadri" in Maharashtra. They run parallel to the western coast of India for ~1,600 km from Gujarat to Kerala. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the eight "hottest hotspots" of biological diversity. The highest peak is Anamudi (2,695 m) in Kerala. The Eastern Ghats are discontinuous and lower.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.16 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q14. Which soil is best suited for cotton cultivation?
(a) Red Soil (b) Black Soil (Regur) (c) Laterite Soil (d) Alluvial Soil

**Answer: (b) Black Soil (Regur)**

**Solution:** Black Soil (Regur) is ideal for cotton cultivation due to its high moisture retention capacity. It is also called "Black Cotton Soil" and is found mainly in the Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP, Karnataka). It is rich in calcium, magnesium, and iron but deficient in nitrogen, phosphorus, and organic matter. Alluvial soil is best for wheat and rice.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/03_Geography.md, p.27 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q15. The Barren Island, the only active volcano in India, is located in:
(a) Lakshadweep (b) Andaman & Nicobar Islands (c) Gulf of Kutch (d) Gulf of Mannar

**Answer: (b) Andaman & Nicobar Islands**

**Solution:** Barren Island is India's only active volcano, located in the Andaman Sea (about 135 km northeast of Port Blair). It last erupted in 2017. Narcondam is another volcanic island in the Andaman group but it is dormant. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands have 572 islands (only 38 inhabited) and are separated by the 10° Channel.
