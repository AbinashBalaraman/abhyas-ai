## Questions

### Q1. The Constituent Assembly of India first met on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 9 December 1946 (d) 26 November 1949

**Answer: (c) 9 December 1946**

**Solution:** The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was the temporary Chairman; Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946. H.C. Mukherjee was Vice-President. B.N. Rau was the Constitutional Adviser. The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.3 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q2. The Indian Constitution came into force on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 26 November 1949 (d) 2 October 1950

**Answer: (b) 26 January 1950**

**Solution:** The Indian Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 but came into force on 26 January 1950, chosen to commemorate the Purna Swaraj declaration of 1930. This date is celebrated as Republic Day. The Constituent Assembly had 299 members (after partition) and met for 165 days over 2 years, 11 months, 18 days. The original Constitution had 395 Articles + 8 Schedules + 22 Parts.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.3 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q3. Who is called the "Father of the Indian Constitution"?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (d) Dr. Rajendra Prasad

**Answer: (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**

**Solution:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar is called the "Father of the Indian Constitution" for chairing the Drafting Committee. The original Drafting Committee had 7 members including N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadulla, B.L. Mitter, D.P. Khaitan (replaced by N. Madhava Rau). Dr. Ambedkar also called Article 32 the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.3-4 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q4. Which Article of the Indian Constitution is called the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 19 (c) Article 21 (d) Article 32

**Answer: (d) Article 32**

**Solution:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution because it guarantees remedies for enforcement of fundamental rights. It provides writ jurisdiction to the Supreme Court. The five writs are Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo Warranto.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.4 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. The word 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by the:
(a) 1st Amendment (b) 42nd Amendment (c) 44th Amendment (d) 86th Amendment

**Answer: (b) 42nd Amendment**

**Solution:** The 42nd Amendment (1976), passed during Indira Gandhi's emergency, added "Socialist", "Secular", and "Integrity" to the Preamble. It is also called the "Mini-Constitution" due to its extensive changes (59 changes). The 44th Amendment (1978) under Janata Party reversed some changes, restoring "Unity" instead of "Integrity".

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.4 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. Fundamental Rights are enshrined in which Part of the Constitution?
(a) Part I (b) Part III (c) Part IV (d) Part V

**Answer: (b) Part III**

**Solution:** Fundamental Rights (Articles 12-35) are in Part III of the Constitution. Originally there were 7 Fundamental Rights; after the 44th Amendment (1978), the Right to Property was removed (made a legal right under Article 300A). The remaining 6 are: Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural & Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.5 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. Directive Principles of State Policy are mentioned in which Part?
(a) Part II (b) Part III (c) Part IV (d) Part V

**Answer: (c) Part IV**

**Solution:** Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are mentioned in Part IV (Articles 36-51) of the Constitution. They are borrowed from the Irish Constitution. They are non-justiciable (not enforceable by courts) but are fundamental in governance. Article 40 (Panchayats), Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code), Article 48 (Cow slaughter) are important DPSPs.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.5 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by the:
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 73rd Amendment (d) 86th Amendment

**Answer: (a) 42nd Amendment**

**Solution:** Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976), based on the Swaran Singh Committee recommendation. They were borrowed from the USSR Constitution. Originally 10 duties; one was added by the 86th Amendment (2002) making 11 duties (for parents to provide education to children 6-14 years).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.5 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The Supreme Court of India consists of how many judges (including Chief Justice)?
(a) 25 (b) 30 (c) 33 (d) 34

**Answer: (d) 34**

**Solution:** The Supreme Court currently has 34 judges (1 Chief Justice + 33 other judges). This was increased from 31 to 34 by the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 2019. The original Constitution (1950) had 8 judges (1 Chief Justice + 7). The President appoints judges after consulting the CJI. The retirement age is 65 years.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.8 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. Which Schedule of the Constitution deals with the Anti-Defection Law?
(a) 8th Schedule (b) 9th Schedule (c) 10th Schedule (d) 11th Schedule

**Answer: (c) 10th Schedule**

**Solution:** The 10th Schedule (added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985) deals with the Anti-Defection Law, based on recommendations of the Dinesh Goswami Committee. The 8th Schedule lists 22 languages (originally 14). The 9th Schedule deals with land reforms laws (immunised from judicial review). The 11th Schedule deals with Panchayats (29 subjects).

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.7 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q11. The Rajya Sabha can have a maximum strength of:
(a) 238 (b) 245 (c) 250 (d) 252

**Answer: (c) 250**

**Solution:** The maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha is 250 members, of which 238 are elected by the elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (through single transferable vote) and 12 are nominated by the President (from literature, science, art, social service). The current strength is 245 (with some vacancies). Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.9 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q12. Which of the following is NOT a Constitutional Body?
(a) Election Commission (b) Finance Commission (c) Planning Commission (d) UPSC

**Answer: (c) Planning Commission**

**Solution:** Planning Commission was NOT a constitutional body; it was an extra-constitutional body established by executive resolution in 1950 and dissolved on 1 January 2015. It was replaced by NITI Aayog. Election Commission (Art. 324), Finance Commission (Art. 280), UPSC (Art. 315), and CAG (Art. 148) are constitutional bodies.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.10 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q13. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment is related to:
(a) Urban Local Bodies (b) Panchayati Raj (c) Anti-Defection Law (d) Right to Education

**Answer: (b) Panchayati Raj**

**Solution:** The 73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992) gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs). It added Part IX (Art. 243-243O) and 11th Schedule (29 subjects). The 74th Amendment (1992) deals with Urban Local Bodies (Municipalities) — Part IX-A and 12th Schedule (18 subjects). The 86th Amendment (2002) made education a fundamental right (Art. 21A).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.11 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q14. Which Article abolishes Untouchability?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 17 (d) Article 21

**Answer: (c) Article 17**

**Solution:** Article 17 abolishes Untouchability and forbids its practice. It is a Fundamental Right enforceable by law. Article 14 is Right to Equality before law. Article 15 prohibits discrimination. Article 18 abolishes titles (except military/academic). The Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 enforces Article 17.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.6 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q15. The President of India is elected by:
(a) Direct popular vote (b) Elected members of Parliament only (c) An electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and Legislative Assemblies of States (d) Nomination by the Prime Minister

**Answer: (c) An electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and Legislative Assemblies of States**

**Solution:** The President is elected by an electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) and elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States and Union Territories (with UTs having legislatures). Nominated members do not vote. The election uses proportional representation with single transferable vote. The term is 5 years.
