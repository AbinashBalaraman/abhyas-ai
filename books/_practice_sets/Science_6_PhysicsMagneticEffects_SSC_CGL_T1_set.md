## Q1. Oersted's discovery (Easy)
Oersted discovered the connection between electricity and magnetism in the year:
(a) 1800 (b) 1820 (c) 1827 (d) 1831

**Answer:** (b) 1820
**Solution:** Hans Christian Oersted accidentally discovered in 1820 that a compass needle deflects when current flows through a nearby wire — proving electricity produces magnetism. The oersted unit of magnetic field strength is named after him.
**Shortcut:** Oersted 1820, Ohm 1827, Faraday 1831.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing dates of Oersted vs Ohm vs Faraday.
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q2. Like magnetic poles (Easy)
Like magnetic poles:
(a) Attract each other (b) Repel each other (c) Have no effect on each other (d) Neutralise each other

**Answer:** (b) Repel each other
**Solution:** Like poles (N-N or S-S) repel; unlike poles (N-S) attract. Magnetic poles always exist in pairs (no magnetic monopoles).
**Shortcut:** Like repels, unlike attracts (works for both charges and poles).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with unlike poles behaviour.
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q3. Right-hand thumb rule (Easy)
The right-hand thumb rule is used to find the direction of:
(a) Current in a wire (b) Magnetic field around a current-carrying wire (c) Force on a conductor (d) Induced EMF

**Answer:** (b) Magnetic field around a current-carrying wire
**Solution:** Right-hand thumb rule: thumb points in direction of current; curled fingers show direction of magnetic field (which forms concentric circles around the wire). Used for straight current-carrying conductors.
**Shortcut:** RIGHT thumb = field direction; LEFT = force direction (Fleming's left rule).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with Fleming's left-hand rule (force direction).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q4. Solenoid field (Easy)
The magnetic field inside a long straight solenoid carrying current is:
(a) Zero (b) Same at all points (uniform) (c) Decreases towards the ends (d) Increases towards the ends

**Answer:** (b) Same at all points (uniform)
**Solution:** Inside a long solenoid, magnetic field lines are parallel and equally spaced — uniform field. Outside, the field is weak. Inserting a soft iron core makes it an electromagnet.
**Shortcut:** Inside solenoid = uniform; outside = weak (like a bar magnet).
**Common Mistake:** Saying zero (that's outside the solenoid, not inside).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q5. Fleming's left-hand rule (Medium)
Fleming's left-hand rule is used to determine:
(a) Direction of current (b) Direction of magnetic field (c) Direction of force on a current-carrying conductor (d) Direction of induced EMF

**Answer:** (c) Direction of force on a current-carrying conductor
**Solution:** Fleming's left-hand rule (for motors): forefinger = field (B), second finger = current (I), thumb = force/motion (F). For finding the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.
**Shortcut:** LEFT = Motor (force); RIGHT (generator rule) = Generator (induced current).
**Common Mistake:** Confusing left and right-hand rules.
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Magnetic field lines (Medium)
Magnetic field lines:
(a) Emerge from S pole and merge at N pole (b) Emerge from N pole and merge at S pole (c) Are open curves (d) Can cross each other

**Answer:** (b) Emerge from N pole and merge at S pole
**Solution:** Outside the magnet, field lines emerge from N pole and enter/merge at S pole. Inside the magnet, they go from S to N — making them closed curves. They never cross (would mean two directions at one point).
**Shortcut:** Outside: N → S. Closed curves. No crossing.
**Common Mistake:** Saying they're open curves (electric field lines are open; magnetic field lines are closed).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Force on conductor (Medium)
A current-carrying conductor placed perpendicular to a magnetic field experiences:
(a) No force (b) Maximum force (c) Minimum force (d) Force parallel to current

**Answer:** (b) Maximum force
**Solution:** Force F = BIL sin θ. When θ = 90° (perpendicular), sin θ = 1, so F is maximum. When θ = 0° (parallel), F = 0 (no force).
**Shortcut:** Force is max when current ⊥ field; zero when parallel.
**Common Mistake:** Saying minimum or no force (confusing parallel vs perpendicular).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q8. Faraday's law (Medium — Trap)
According to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, the induced EMF is:
(a) Directly proportional to magnetic flux (b) Inversely proportional to rate of change of flux (c) Directly proportional to rate of change of magnetic flux (d) Independent of magnetic flux

**Answer:** (c) Directly proportional to rate of change of magnetic flux
**Solution:** Faraday's second law: ε = -dΦ_B/dt. The induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the coil. The negative sign (Lenz's law) shows the EMF opposes the change.
**Shortcut:** EMF ∝ dΦ/dt. Faster change → bigger EMF.
**Common Mistake:** Saying proportional to flux (it's the rate of change that matters).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q9. AC vs DC generator (Medium)
An AC generator uses:
(a) Split-ring commutator (b) Slip rings (c) Rectifier (d) Transformer

**Answer:** (b) Slip rings
**Solution:** AC generator (alternator) uses slip rings to maintain continuous contact without changing the direction of output current. DC generator (dynamo) uses split-ring commutator to convert AC to DC by reversing connections every half-cycle.
**Shortcut:** AC = slip rings (continuous); DC = split rings (commutator).
**Common Mistake:** Reversing AC and DC generator parts.
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q10. Domestic AC supply (Medium)
The frequency of AC supplied in Indian homes is:
(a) 50 Hz (b) 60 Hz (c) 100 Hz (d) 220 Hz

**Answer:** (a) 50 Hz
**Solution:** India uses 220 V AC at 50 Hz. USA uses 110 V at 60 Hz. The 50 Hz frequency means the current changes direction 100 times per second (50 complete cycles).
**Shortcut:** India = 50 Hz; USA = 60 Hz.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with 60 Hz (USA standard).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q11. Earth wire (Medium — Trap)
The earth wire in a household circuit is identified by its insulation colour:
(a) Red (b) Black (c) Green (d) Blue

**Answer:** (c) Green
**Solution:** Earth wire = green insulation (safety). Live wire = red insulation (220 V). Neutral wire = black insulation. Earth wire provides a low-resistance path for leakage current.
**Shortcut:** Earth = Green (nature/safety). Live = Red. Neutral = Black.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing green with another colour (green = ground/safety globally).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q12. Electromagnet (Medium)
An electromagnet is made by:
(a) Wrapping a coil around a permanent magnet (b) Passing current through a coil with a soft iron core (c) Rubbing iron with a magnet (d) Heating iron in a magnetic field

**Answer:** (b) Passing current through a coil with a soft iron core
**Solution:** An electromagnet consists of a solenoid (coil of insulated copper wire) with a soft iron core. When current flows, the soft iron becomes magnetised. Stops being a magnet when current stops.
**Shortcut:** Electromagnet = coil + current + soft iron core (NOT steel — steel retains magnetism).
**Common Mistake:** Choosing permanent magnet or steel core (would make a permanent magnet).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q13. Force on conductor calculation (Hard)
A 0.5 m long wire carrying 2 A is placed perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.4 T. The force on the wire is:
(a) 0.4 N (b) 0.8 N (c) 4 N (d) 0.04 N

**Answer:** (a) 0.4 N
**Solution:** F = BIL sin θ = 0.4 × 2 × 0.5 × sin 90° = 0.4 × 1 × 1 = 0.4 N.
**Shortcut:** F = BIL (when perpendicular). Plug in: 0.4 × 2 × 0.5 = 0.4 N.
**Common Mistake:** Multiplying in wrong order or omitting sin θ (forgetting it's perpendicular).
**Time target:** 60 sec

---

## Q14. Lenz's law (Hard)
Lenz's law is in accordance with the law of conservation of:
(a) Charge (b) Mass (c) Momentum (d) Energy

**Answer:** (d) Energy
**Solution:** Lenz's law (induced current opposes the change that caused it) ensures energy conservation. If induced current aided the change, we'd get free energy — violating conservation. The work done against the opposing force = electrical energy generated.
**Shortcut:** Lenz's law = energy conservation (in EM induction).
**Common Mistake:** Saying charge (that would be Kirchhoff's law).
**Time target:** 60 sec

---

## Q15. Induced current direction (Hard)
A magnet is moved towards a coil. The induced current in the coil:
(a) Aids the motion (b) Opposes the motion (c) Is zero (d) Flows only once

**Answer:** (b) Opposes the motion
**Solution:** By Lenz's law, induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux. As magnet approaches, induced current creates a magnetic field opposing the approaching magnet (acts like a repelling magnet).
**Shortcut:** Lenz's law: induced current always opposes the cause.
**Common Mistake:** Saying it aids motion (that would violate energy conservation).
**Time target:** 60 sec
