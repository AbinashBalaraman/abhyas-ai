### Q1. The Imperial Bank of India was nationalized in 1955 and renamed as:
(a) RBI (b) SBI (c) PNB (d) BOB

**Answer: (b) SBI**

**Solution:** The Imperial Bank of India was formed in 1921 by amalgamating the three Presidency Banks (Bank of Calcutta/Bengal 1806, Bank of Bombay 1840, Bank of Madras 1843). It was nationalized on 1 July 1955 and renamed as the State Bank of India (SBI). The first SBI Chairman was John Mathai. SBI is the largest Public Sector Bank in India. Nationalization of 14 major commercial banks happened on 19 July 1969 under PM Indira Gandhi, and another 6 banks were nationalized on 15 April 1980.

### Q2. The 14 major commercial banks were nationalized in India on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 19 July 1969 (c) 1 April 1976 (d) 15 April 1980

**Answer: (b) 19 July 1969**

**Solution:** 14 major commercial banks were nationalized on 19 July 1969 under the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Ordinance, 1969 by PM Indira Gandhi. Banks with deposits exceeding ₹50 crore were nationalized. A second round of nationalization followed on 15 April 1980, bringing 6 more banks under government control. After the 2020 mega merger, India has 12 Public Sector Banks. Nationalization was aimed at expanding banking access, especially in rural areas.

### Q3. Scheduled Banks are listed in which Schedule of the RBI Act?
(a) 1st (b) 2nd (c) 7th (d) 8th

**Answer: (b) 2nd**

**Solution:** Scheduled Banks are banks listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934 (under Section 2(e)). They are eligible to borrow from RBI for normal banking business. Non-Scheduled Banks are not listed in this schedule and are not eligible for RBI refinance facilities. India has 12 Public Sector Banks, 20+ Private Sector Banks (HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, etc.), 40+ Foreign Banks, 43 RRBs, 10 Small Finance Banks, and 6 Payments Banks.

### Q4. NCLT stands for:
(a) National Company Law Tribunal (b) National Civil Law Tribunal (c) National Company Legal Tribunal (d) New Company Law Tribunal

**Answer: (a) National Company Law Tribunal**

**Solution:** NCLT stands for National Company Law Tribunal. It is the adjudicating authority under the IBC, 2016. NCLAT (National Company Law Appellate Tribunal) is the appellate authority. The NCLT was established under the Companies Act, 2013. The IBC, 2016 established the IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) as the regulator. NCLT also deals with company law matters, mergers, demergers, and other corporate affairs. NCLT benches are in various cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, etc.).

### Q5. PMMY was launched in:
(a) 2014 (b) 2015 (c) 2016 (d) 2018

**Answer: (b) 2015**

**Solution:** PMMY (Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana) was launched on 8 April 2015 by PM Narendra Modi. The administrator is MUDRA Ltd (Micro Units Development & Refinance Agency). It targets non-corporate, non-farm small/micro enterprises. The scheme has three categories: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (₹50,001 to ₹5 lakh), and Tarun (₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh). Loans are collateral-free and available from banks, NBFCs, and MFIs. The MUDRA card was also introduced for working capital.

### Q6. Under PMMY, Tarun loans are from:
(a) Up to ₹50,000 (b) ₹50,001 to ₹5 lakh (c) ₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh (d) Above ₹10 lakh

**Answer: (c) ₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh**

**Solution:** Under PMMY, the three categories are: Shishu (up to ₹50,000) for small businesses, Kishore (₹50,001 to ₹5 lakh) for medium-sized enterprises, and Tarun (₹5,00,001 to ₹10 lakh) for larger micro enterprises. The scheme was launched on 8 April 2015. Some categories of Tarun have been extended to ₹20 lakh under Tarun Plus. MUDRA Ltd provides refinance to banks and NBFCs. The PMMY loans are collateral-free and have helped millions of small entrepreneurs in India.

### Q7. KCC was launched based on the recommendation of:
(a) R.V. Gupta Committee (b) Narasimham Committee (c) Chakravarty Committee (d) Rangarajan Committee

**Answer: (a) R.V. Gupta Committee**

**Solution:** The KCC (Kisan Credit Card) was launched in 1998 based on the recommendations of the R.V. Gupta Committee. It provides short-term credit to farmers. The credit limit is based on land holding, cropping pattern, and other factors. The interest rate is 7% per annum, with a 3% subsidy for timely repayment (effective 4%). KCC has been expanded to include animal husbandry, fisheries, and other allied activities. It is a major success in agricultural credit delivery in India.

### Q8. The BRBNM subsidiary of RBI prints banknotes in:
(a) Nashik and Dewas (b) Mysore and Salboni (c) Kolkata and Chennai (d) Mumbai and Delhi

**Answer: (b) Mysore and Salboni**

**Solution:** BRBNM (Bharatiya Reserve Bank Note Mudran) is a wholly-owned subsidiary of RBI that prints banknotes. It has two presses: one in Mysore (Karnataka) and another in Salboni (West Bengal). BRBNM was set up in 1995. The Security Printing and Minting Corporation of India Ltd (SPMCIL) has presses in Nashik (Maharashtra) and Dewas (Madhya Pradesh) for printing currency notes and minting coins. SPMCIL is a Government of India undertaking. RBI manages currency while the Government of India issues coins.

### Q9. PACS are regulated by:
(a) RBI (b) NABARD (c) State Government (d) None of the above

**Answer: (d) None of the above**

**Solution:** PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies) are NOT regulated by RBI. They are regulated by the State Government. PACS operate at the village level. The cooperative banking structure is: PACS (village) → CCBs (Central Cooperative Banks at district level) → StCBs (State Cooperative Banks at state level). CCBs and StCBs are regulated by RBI. The duality of control: Banking functions are under RBI, while management and audit are under the State/Central Government. NABARD is the apex agricultural credit institution.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.83-90 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. The IBA (Indian Banks' Association) was established in:
(a) 1946 (b) 1947 (c) 1951 (d) 1969

**Answer: (a) 1946**

**Solution:** The Indian Banks' Association (IBA) was established in 1946. It is a representative body of Indian banks (both PSBs and private banks). IBA is the platform for negotiation of wage settlements for bank employees (5-yearly bipartite settlements). It is headquartered in Mumbai. IBA plays a key role in coordinating between banks, RBI, and the Government on policy matters. IBA also handles training of bank officers and is involved in industrial relations in the banking sector.
