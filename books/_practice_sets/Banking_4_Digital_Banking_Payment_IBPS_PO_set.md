## Questions

### Q1. NEFT was launched in:
(a) 2002 (b) 2004 (c) 2008 (d) 2010

**Answer: (b) 2004**

**Solution:** NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) was launched in 2004. It is operated by RBI. NEFT settlement happens in half-hourly batches (48 batches per day since December 2019). NEFT has no minimum or maximum amount. Charges are mostly NIL for inward; nominal for outward. NEFT is available 24x7 since December 2019. NEFT is used for routine transfers like salary, EMI, and recurring payments. It is different from RTGS (real-time, minimum ₹2 lakh) and UPI (instant, mobile-based, max ₹1 lakh generally).

### Q2. UPI is regulated by:
(a) NPCI (b) RBI (c) SEBI (d) PFRDA

**Answer: (b) RBI**

**Solution:** UPI is regulated by RBI (Reserve Bank of India). RBI is the regulator of all payment systems in India under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. RBI sets the rules, approves new products (e.g., Credit Line on UPI), and issues Master Directions. NPCI is the operator/developer of UPI. SEBI regulates the securities market. PFRDA regulates pension funds. RBI issues the regulatory framework for digital lending, e-mandates, and other payment-related products. NPCI implements these on the technical side.

### Q3. The CBDC wholesale pilot started on:
(a) 1 November 2022 (b) 1 December 2022 (c) 1 April 2023 (d) 1 January 2023

**Answer: (a) 1 November 2022**

**Solution:** The CBDC wholesale pilot (e₹-W) started on 1 November 2022, a month before the retail pilot (1 December 2022). The wholesale CBDC is for inter-bank settlements (settlement of secondary market transactions in G-Secs). The retail CBDC is for the public. CBDC is the digital form of fiat currency issued by RBI. India is one of the first major economies to pilot CBDC. CBDC uses token-based and account-based variants. It can be transacted via UPI QR codes. CBDC has no interest (similar to physical cash).

### Q4. NPCI stands for:
(a) National Payment and Cash Infrastructure (b) National Payments Corporation of India (c) New Payment Corporation of India (d) National Payment Clearing Institute

**Answer: (b) National Payments Corporation of India**

**Solution:** NPCI stands for National Payments Corporation of India. It was established in 2008 under Section 8 of the Companies Act. NPCI is the umbrella organization for retail payments in India. It is promoted by 10 banks (each holding 10% stake). NPCI operates UPI, IMPS, NACH, AePS, RuPay, CTS, e-RUPI, and Bharat Bill Payment System (BBPS). NPCI is headquartered in Mumbai. NPCI is regulated by RBI. NPCI plays a key role in India's digital payment revolution.

### Q5. UPI was launched by:
(a) RBI (b) NPCI (c) SEBI (d) Government of India

**Answer: (b) NPCI**

**Solution:** UPI was launched by NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) on 11 April 2016. NPCI developed UPI and operates it. UPI was inaugurated by Dr. Raghuram G. Rajan (then RBI Governor) in Mumbai with 21 member banks. UPI is built on IMPS infrastructure. UPI enables real-time inter-bank transactions through mobile phones. UPI is regulated by RBI. As of August 2025, UPI handles ~640 million transactions daily. NPCI was established in 2008 as the umbrella organization for retail payments.

### Q6. RuPay card network is operated by:
(a) RBI (b) Visa (c) NPCI (d) MasterCard

**Answer: (c) NPCI**

**Solution:** RuPay is operated by NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India). RuPay is India's domestic card payment network launched in 2012 by NPCI. It was created to reduce dependence on international networks like Visa and Mastercard. RuPay cards are accepted at ATMs, POS, and e-commerce. India has 5 card networks: Visa, Mastercard, RuPay (NPCI), American Express (Amex), and Diners Club. RuPay has lower transaction fees than international networks. NPCI is promoted by 10 banks (each holding 10% stake).

### Q7. BHIM app is based on which technology?
(a) NEFT (b) UPI (c) RTGS (d) SWIFT

**Answer: (b) UPI**

**Solution:** BHIM (Bharat Interface for Money) app is based on UPI technology. It was launched on 30 December 2016 by PM Modi and developed by NPCI. BHIM allows users to send/receive money, scan QR codes, and check balance. It supports multiple bank accounts. The app is available in multiple languages. BHIM 2.0 was launched in 2018 with additional features. BHIM uses UPI infrastructure. NPCI is the developer while the Government of India launched it. BHIM is one of the most popular UPI apps.

### Q9. Digital Lending Apps must partner with:
(a) Banks (b) Regulated Entities (c) NBFCs (d) Fintech companies

**Answer: (b) Regulated Entities**

**Solution:** Under RBI's Master Directions on Digital Lending (September 2022, revised December 2022), all Digital Lending Apps (DLAs) must be part of Regulated Entities (REs) or partner with REs. REs include banks, NBFCs, and other financial institutions regulated by RBI. FLDG (First Loss Default Guarantee) guidelines have been issued. Data privacy: No collection beyond necessity. This was to prevent unauthorized digital lending practices. Many Chinese-loan apps were shut down due to predatory practices. The framework brought digital lending under RBI's regulatory ambit.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/04_Digital_Banking_Payment.md, p.228-233 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. India's first digital payment system launched in 2004 was:
(a) UPI (b) IMPS (c) NEFT (d) RTGS

**Answer: (c) NEFT**

**Solution:** NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) was launched in 2004. RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement) was also launched in 2004. NEFT is operated by RBI and uses half-hourly batched settlement. RTGS is also operated by RBI and uses real-time gross settlement. NEFT has no minimum/maximum; RTGS has a minimum of ₹2 lakh. Both are available 24x7 since December 2019. NEFT is for routine transfers; RTGS is for large-value transactions. IMPS was launched later in 2010. UPI was launched in 2016. NEFT and RTGS are RBI's foundational electronic payment systems.
