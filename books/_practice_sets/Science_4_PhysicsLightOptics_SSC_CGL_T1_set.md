## Q1. Laws of reflection (Easy)
According to the laws of reflection:
(a) Angle of incidence = angle of reflection (b) Angle of incidence = 2 × angle of reflection (c) Angle of incidence = 90° (d) Angle of incidence = angle of refraction

**Answer:** (a) Angle of incidence = angle of reflection
**Solution:** Two laws of reflection: (1) ∠i = ∠r (angle of incidence equals angle of reflection); (2) incident ray, normal, and reflected ray all lie in the same plane. The angle is always measured from the normal.
**Shortcut:** Reflection = bounce back; ∠i = ∠r (mirror rule).
**Common Mistake:** Saying ∠i = ∠r with respect to the mirror surface (must be from normal).
**Time target:** 15 sec

## Q2. Concave mirror image at F (Easy)
When an object is placed at the focus of a concave mirror, the image is formed at:
(a) Focus (b) Centre of curvature (c) Infinity (d) No image formed

**Answer:** (c) Infinity
**Solution:** At focus F: image is formed at infinity, is highly enlarged, real, and inverted. This is the principle behind headlights and searchlights — placing the bulb at F gives a parallel beam.
**Shortcut:** Object at F → image at infinity (parallel beam).
**Common Mistake:** Saying image is at F (that's when object is at infinity).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q3. Refractive index definition (Easy)
The absolute refractive index of a medium is defined as:
(a) v/c (b) c/v (c) c × v (d) c − v

**Answer:** (b) c/v
**Solution:** n_m = c/v_m, where c = speed of light in vacuum (~3 × 10⁸ m/s), v_m = speed of light in the medium. Diamond has n = 2.42 (highest natural); air n ≈ 1.0003. Refractive index is dimensionless.
**Shortcut:** n = c/v_m; n > 1 means light slows in medium.
**Common Mistake:** Inverting (v/c instead of c/v).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q4. Convex lens image (Easy)
A convex lens forms a real, inverted image of the same size as the object when the object is placed at:
(a) F (b) 2F (c) Between F and 2F (d) Infinity

**Answer:** (b) 2F
**Solution:** When object is at 2F₁ of a convex lens, the image is formed at 2F₂ on the other side, is real, inverted, and same size as object (m = −1).
**Shortcut:** Object at 2F → image at 2F, same size (m = −1).
**Common Mistake:** Saying at F (then image is at infinity).
**Time target:** 20 sec

## Q5. Concave lens image (Medium)
A concave lens always produces an image that is:
(a) Real and inverted (b) Virtual and erect (c) Real and at infinity (d) Same size as object

**Answer:** (b) Virtual and erect
**Solution:** A concave (diverging) lens always produces a virtual, erect, and diminished image, regardless of object position. The image is located between the optical centre O and the principal focus F.
**Shortcut:** Concave lens = "CVD": Concave, Virtual, Diminished.
**Common Mistake:** Saying real and inverted (only convex lens forms real images for some positions).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q6. Hypermetropia correction (Medium)
Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) is corrected by using a:
(a) Concave lens (b) Convex lens (c) Bifocal lens (d) Cylindrical lens

**Answer:** (b) Convex lens
**Solution:** In hypermetropia, the image of a nearby object forms behind the retina (eyeball too short or lens too flat). A convex (converging) lens brings the rays to focus sooner, forming the image on the retina.
**Shortcut:** Hypermetropia = "HYPO-far" → can't see near → need converging (convex).
**Common Mistake:** Choosing concave (used for myopia/near-sightedness).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q7. Power of lens (Medium)
A lens has a focal length of −50 cm. What is its power?
(a) +2 D (b) −2 D (c) +0.5 D (d) −0.5 D

**Answer:** (b) −2 D
**Solution:** P = 1/f (f in metres). f = −50 cm = −0.5 m. P = 1/(−0.5) = −2 D. Negative power indicates a concave (diverging) lens.
**Shortcut:** Convert cm → m by dividing by 100. P = 100/f(cm). For f = −50 cm: P = 100/(−50) = −2 D.
**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to convert cm to m, or missing the negative sign.
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q8. Twinkling of stars (Medium — Trap)
The twinkling of stars is caused by:
(a) Reflection from stars (b) Atmospheric refraction (c) Dispersion of light (d) Earth's rotation

**Answer:** (b) Atmospheric refraction
**Solution:** Stars are point-sized sources. Their light passes through atmospheric layers of varying refractive index (and turbulence), so the apparent position of the star fluctuates rapidly → twinkling. Planets are extended sources, so variations average out → no twinkling.
**Shortcut:** Atmospheric refraction = star twinkle + advanced sunrise + delayed sunset.
**Common Mistake:** Saying reflection (stars don't reflect light; they emit it).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q9. Dispersion of light (Medium)
The phenomenon of splitting of white light into seven colours is called:
(a) Reflection (b) Refraction (c) Dispersion (d) Scattering

**Answer:** (c) Dispersion
**Solution:** When white light passes through a prism, different colours bend through different angles (different refractive indices). Red bends least, violet bends most. The seven colours are remembered as VIBGYOR (Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red).
**Shortcut:** Prism = VIBGYOR; Red bends least, Violet most.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with scattering (Tyndall effect).
**Time target:** 30 sec

## Q10. Sky appears blue (Medium)
The blue colour of the sky is due to:
(a) Reflection from oceans (b) Refraction by air (c) Scattering of light by air molecules (d) Polarisation by atmosphere

**Answer:** (c) Scattering of light by air molecules
**Solution:** Air molecules are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light (Rayleigh scattering condition). They scatter shorter wavelengths (blue, violet) more than longer wavelengths (red). Since human eyes are more sensitive to blue, the sky appears blue.
**Shortcut:** Scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴ → blue (short λ) scatters most.
**Common Mistake:** Saying reflection from oceans (the ocean reflects sky; doesn't cause sky's colour).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q11. Why red for danger signals (Medium — Trap)
Red colour is used for danger signals because:
(a) Red is the brightest colour (b) Red scatters the least in fog/smoke (c) Red has the longest wavelength (d) Red is the primary colour

**Answer:** (b) Red scatters the least in fog/smoke
**Solution:** Red has the longest wavelength among visible colours, so it scatters the least in fog and smoke. This makes it visible from a greater distance, even in poor visibility — perfect for danger signals.
**Shortcut:** Long λ → less scattering → visible from far.
**Common Mistake:** Saying "brightest colour" (red isn't the brightest; green appears brightest to the eye).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q12. Critical angle for TIR (Medium)
Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from:
(a) Rarer to denser medium (b) Denser to rarer medium above critical angle (c) Any two media (d) Vacuum to any medium

**Answer:** (b) Denser to rarer medium above critical angle
**Solution:** Two conditions for TIR: (1) light must travel from a denser (higher n) to a rarer (lower n) medium; (2) angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle (the angle at which refracted ray grazes the interface).
**Shortcut:** TIR = denser → rarer + i > critical angle.
**Common Mistake:** Saying rarer to denser (this always bends towards normal, never reflects totally).
**Time target:** 45 sec

## Q13. Mirror formula application (Hard)
An object is placed at 30 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. The image distance is:
(a) 30 cm (b) 15 cm (c) 10 cm (d) 60 cm

**Answer:** (a) 30 cm
**Solution:** Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. With u = −30 cm (object in front of mirror) and f = −15 cm (concave). 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/(−15) − 1/(−30) = −2/30 + 1/30 = −1/30. v = −30 cm. Image is at 30 cm (same distance as object), real, inverted, same size.
**Shortcut:** Object at 2F → image at 2F, same size. Here, object is at 2f (= 30 cm), so image is also at 30 cm.
**Common Mistake:** Sign confusion (forgetting u is negative for object in front of mirror).
**Time target:** 90 sec

## Q14. Lens power combination (Hard)
A convex lens of power +2 D is in contact with a concave lens of power −1.5 D. The combined power is:
(a) +3.5 D (b) +0.5 D (c) −0.5 D (d) −3.5 D

**Answer:** (b) +0.5 D
**Solution:** Power of lenses in contact: P = P₁ + P₂ = (+2) + (−1.5) = +0.5 D. Combined focal length f = 1/P = 1/0.5 = 2 m.
**Shortcut:** Powers add directly (with signs). P₁ + P₂ = combined P.
**Common Mistake:** Subtracting the magnitudes (|+2 − 1.5| = 0.5, by luck this works — but the formula is additive with signs).
**Time target:** 60 sec

## Q15. Image formation by convex lens (Hard)
A 4 cm tall object is placed 20 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. The image is:
(a) Real, inverted, 4 cm tall at 20 cm (b) Virtual, erect, 4 cm at 20 cm (c) Real, inverted, 8 cm tall at 20 cm (d) At infinity

**Answer:** (a) Real, inverted, 4 cm tall at 20 cm
**Solution:** Object at 2F₁ (since 2F₁ = 2 × 10 = 20 cm). At 2F₁, image forms at 2F₂ = 20 cm, is real, inverted, same size as object. So image height = 4 cm. Use lens formula: 1/v − 1/(−20) = 1/10 → 1/v = 1/10 − 1/20 = 1/20 → v = +20 cm. m = v/u = 20/(−20) = −1 → h' = m × h = −4 cm (inverted, same size).
**Shortcut:** Object at 2F → image at 2F, real, inverted, m = −1 (same size).
**Common Mistake:** Saying image is enlarged (only between F and 2F).
**Time target:** 90 sec
