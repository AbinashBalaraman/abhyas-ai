### Q1. The Harappan site famous for the world's earliest known dockyard is:
(a) Harappa (b) Mohenjo-Daro (c) Lothal (d) Kalibangan

**Answer: (c) Lothal**

**Solution:** Lothal (Gujarat, India) had the world's earliest known dockyard, a remarkable feat of Harappan engineering. Other key sites: Mohenjo-Daro (Great Bath), Harappa (Granaries), Dholavira (Gujarat), Kalibangan (Rajasthan, ploughed field), Rakhigarhi (Haryana — largest in India). Discovered by S.R. Rao in 1957.

### Q2. The Battle of Buxar was fought in:
(a) 1757 (b) 1764 (c) 1767 (d) 1774

**Answer: (b) 1764**

**Solution:** The Battle of Buxar (1764) was fought between Hector Munro (British) and the combined forces of Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh), Shah Alam II (Mughal Emperor), and Mir Qasim (Bengal). The British victory resulted in the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) which gave Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha to the East India Company.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.24 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q3. "Din-i-Ilahi" was founded by:
(a) Babur (b) Akbar (c) Jahangir (d) Shah Jahan

**Answer: (b) Akbar**

**Solution:** Akbar (1556-1605), the greatest Mughal emperor, founded Din-i-Ilahi in 1582, a syncretic religion drawing from Islam, Hinduism, Zoroastrianism, and Christianity. He also practiced Sulh-i-Kul (universal tolerance), married Jodha Bai (Mariam-uz-Zamani), and appointed the Navaratnas (Nine Jewels) including Birbal, Tansen, and Todarmal. He introduced the Mansabdari system with 33 grades.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.18 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q4. The Maratha Empire's first capital under Shivaji was:
(a) Pune (b) Raigad (c) Torna (d) Pratapgad

**Answer: (b) Raigad**

**Solution:** Raigad Fort was the capital of the Maratha Empire and the site of Shivaji's coronation in 1674. Shivaji used guerrilla warfare tactics (Ganimi Kava) and built a strong forts system. The Peshwas (starting 1713) later became the actual rulers, with famous Peshwas being Balaji Vishwanath, Bajirao I, Balaji Bajirao, and Madhavrao Peshwa.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.20 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. The First Census of India was conducted in 1872 during the time of:
(a) Lord Cornwallis (b) Lord Hastings (c) Lord Mayo (d) Lord Lytton

**Answer: (c) Lord Mayo**

**Solution:** Lord Mayo (1869-1872) conducted the First Census of India in 1872. He also established the State Railway. Other notable Governor-Generals/Viceroys: Lord Cornwallis (Permanent Settlement 1793), Lord Wellesley (Subsidiary Alliance), Lord Dalhousie (Railways, Telegraph, Doctrine of Lapse), Lord Curzon (Partition of Bengal 1905), Lord Ripon (Local Self-Government 1882, Ilbert Bill 1883).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.25 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919) was ordered by:
(a) Lord Curzon (b) Lord Chelmsford (c) General Dyer (d) Lord Irwin

**Answer: (c) General Dyer**

**Solution:** The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on 13 April 1919 at Amritsar, ordered by General Reginald Dyer. It was in response to the Rowlatt Act (1919). The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22) was launched by Gandhi and withdrawn after the Chauri Chaura incident (Feb 1922). Lord Chelmsford was the Viceroy at the time.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.34 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. Who is known as the "Father of the Indian Unrest"?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (c) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (d) Dadabhai Naoroji

**Answer: (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak**

**Solution:** Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856-1920) was called the "Father of Indian Unrest" by the British. He was a leading Extremist, founded the Ganesh Chaturthi festival, and authored Gita Rahasya in prison. Lala Lajpat Rai was "Punjab Kesari"/"Sher-e-Punjab". Gopal Krishna Gokhale was Gandhi's political guru. Dadabhai Naoroji was the "Grand Old Man of India" who wrote "Poverty of India".

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.32 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted because:
(a) It had no Indian members (b) It supported the British rule (c) It proposed partition (d) It imposed new taxes

**Answer: (a) It had no Indian members**

**Solution:** The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted by all Indian political parties because it had no Indian members. Lala Lajpat Rai died after being hit during a lathi charge at a protest against the Commission, leading to the famous slogan "Simon Go Back". The commission was led by Sir John Simon.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.34 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The Dandi March was associated with which movement?
(a) Non-Cooperation Movement (b) Civil Disobedience Movement (c) Quit India Movement (d) Khilafat Movement

**Answer: (b) Civil Disobedience Movement**

**Solution:** The Dandi March (12 March 1930) was led by Mahatma Gandhi from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi (coastal Gujarat) to break the salt law by producing salt from seawater. This marked the beginning of the Civil Disobedience Movement. The Lahore Session (1929) under Jawaharlal Nehru's presidency declared Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence). January 26 was first celebrated as Independence Day in 1930.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.34 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. Subhas Chandra Bose organized the Indian National Army (INA) in:
(a) 1915 (b) 1925 (c) 1942 (d) 1945

**Answer: (c) 1942**

**Solution:** Subhas Chandra Bose reorganized the Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj) in 1942, with its headquarters in Singapore. The INA's motto was "Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom" (Netaji's famous slogan). Ras Behari Bose originally founded the INA; Mohan Singh took over after Ras Behari. The INA fought alongside the Japanese against the British in Burma. Bose is believed to have died in a Taipei air crash in 1945.
