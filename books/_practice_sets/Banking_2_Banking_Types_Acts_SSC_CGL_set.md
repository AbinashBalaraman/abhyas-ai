### Q1. The Banking Regulation Act came into force on:
(a) 1 April 1935 (b) 16 March 1949 (c) 1 January 1949 (d) 19 July 1969

**Answer: (b) 16 March 1949**

**Solution:** The Banking Regulation Act (BR Act) came into force on 16 March 1949. It is the principal statute for regulating banking in India. The 1966 amendment extended the Act's provisions to Cooperative Societies. Important sections: Section 22 (licensing of banks), Section 35A (RBI's supersession power — used in YES Bank 2020 case), Section 35(6) (NABARD inspection), Section 49 (Bank Rate), Section 56 (Cooperative societies inclusion). The BR Act applies to all banking companies in India.

### Q2. The SARFAESI Act was enacted in:
(a) 1999 (b) 2002 (c) 2004 (d) 2016

**Answer: (b) 2002**

**Solution:** The SARFAESI Act (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act) was enacted in 2002. It provides three mechanisms for NPA recovery: Securitisation, Asset Reconstruction (via ARCs), and Enforcement of Security Interest without court intervention. The borrower is given a 60-day notice before the bank takes possession. The minimum loan threshold is ₹2 lakh (raised to ₹20 lakh in some cases). SARFAESI does not apply to agricultural land below specified limits.

### Q3. The number of Public Sector Banks after the 1 April 2020 merger is:
(a) 27 (b) 21 (c) 12 (d) 8

**Answer: (c) 12**

**Solution:** After the mega merger of 10 Public Sector Banks (PSBs) into 4 entities effective 1 April 2020, the total number of PSBs is 12. The 12 PSBs are: SBI, PNB, BOB, BOI, Canara Bank, Union Bank of India, Indian Bank, IOB, Central Bank of India, UCO Bank, Bank of Maharashtra, and Punjab & Sind Bank. Before the merger, India had 27 PSBs. The merger aimed to create globally-sized banks. Notable mergers: PNB + OBC + United Bank = PNB; BOB + Vijaya + Dena = BOB; Canara + Syndicate = Canara.

### Q4. Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act deals with:
(a) Cheque bounce (b) Promissory note dishonour (c) Bill of exchange (d) Forgery

**Answer: (a) Cheque bounce**

**Solution:** Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act (NI Act) 1881 deals with dishonour of cheques due to insufficient funds. The penalty can be up to 2× the cheque amount or ₹50,000 (whichever is less). Section 141 deals with offences by companies — the directors/partners are liable if the offence is committed with their consent. The NI Act defines and regulates promissory notes, bills of exchange, and cheques. Sections 118-138 cover various NI aspects including presentation, acceptance, etc.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.112-117 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. The IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code) came into force on:
(a) 1 December 2015 (b) 1 December 2016 (c) 1 April 2017 (d) 1 January 2018

**Answer: (b) 1 December 2016**

**Solution:** The IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code), 2016 came into force on 1 December 2016. It created three key institutions: NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal) as Adjudicating Authority, NCLAT (National Company Law Appellate Tribunal) as Appellate Authority, and IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) as the Regulator. The CIRP (Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process) has a maximum period of 180 + 90 = 270 days (extendable up to 330 days). Major resolved cases: Bhushan Steel (Tata Steel), Videocon (first IBC resolution by NCLAT), DHFL (Piramal-Capital First merger).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.128-140 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. Payments Banks can accept deposits up to:
(a) ₹50,000 (b) ₹1 lakh (c) ₹2 lakh (d) ₹5 lakh

**Answer: (c) ₹2 lakh**

**Solution:** Payments Banks can accept deposits up to ₹2 lakh per account. They cannot lend but can accept deposits, issue debit cards, and provide mobile banking. The 6 active Payments Banks (as of 2024) are: Airtel Payments Bank, Fino Payments Bank, India Post Payments Bank, Jio Payments Bank, Paytm Payments Bank, and NSDL Payments Bank. Payments Banks can accept remittances, utility bill payments, and other small banking services. Aditya Birla Payments Bank surrendered its license. Maximum initial capital: ₹100 crore.

### Q7. Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) were established on:
(a) 2 October 1975 (b) 1 April 1976 (c) 15 April 1980 (d) 19 July 1969

**Answer: (a) 2 October 1975**

**Solution:** RRBs were established on 2 October 1975 under the RRB Act, 1976. Their ownership structure is: Central Government 50%, Sponsor Bank 35%, State Government 15%. RRBs provide rural credit and financial inclusion. As of 2024, there are 43 RRBs in India after consolidation. Examples: Assam Gramin Vikash Bank, Kerala Gramin Bank, Paschim Banga Gramin Bank, Uttar Bihar Gramin Bank. The first RRB was the Prathama Bank (1975).

### Q8. PMLA's Adjudicating Authority is:
(a) NCLT (b) PMLA Adjudicating Authority (c) ED (d) FIU-IND

**Answer: (b) PMLA Adjudicating Authority**

**Solution:** The PMLA (Prevention of Money Laundering Act) Adjudicating Authority handles the cases of money laundering. The Appellate Tribunal is the PMLA Appellate Tribunal. Section 3 defines the offence of money laundering; Section 4 provides punishment (rigorous imprisonment 3-7 years + fine); Section 5 deals with attachment of property. The FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit-India) was set up in 2004 under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. The ED (Enforcement Directorate) investigates PMLA cases.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.143-150 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The maximum Priority Sector Lending (PSL) target for PSBs is:
(a) 25% (b) 32% (c) 40% (d) 50%

**Answer: (c) 40%**

**Solution:** The maximum Priority Sector Lending (PSL) target for Public Sector Banks is 40% of ANBC (Adjusted Net Bank Credit) or CEOB (Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure), whichever is higher. Foreign banks with 20+ branches also have a 40% target; those with <20 branches have 32%. The 40% target covers: Agriculture (18%), MSME/Micro Enterprises (7.5%), Export Credit (2%), Weaker Sections (10%), and other priority sectors. RRBs have a 75% PSL target.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.60-69 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. FIU-IND operates under:
(a) RBI (b) Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance (c) MHA (d) CBI

**Answer: (b) Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance**

**Solution:** FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit-India) operates under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. It was set up in 2004 as the central national agency responsible for receiving, processing, analyzing, and disseminating information relating to suspect financial transactions. FIU-IND is also responsible for coordinating and strengthening the collection of financial intelligence. It is not under RBI or MHA. ED (Enforcement Directorate) is under the Department of Revenue.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.144-145 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q11. DICGC deposit insurance cover per depositor per bank is:
(a) ₹1 lakh (b) ₹2 lakh (c) ₹5 lakh (d) ₹10 lakh

**Answer: (c) ₹5 lakh**

**Solution:** The DICGC deposit insurance cover per depositor per bank is ₹5 lakh (raised from ₹1 lakh in February 2020). The premium paid by banks to DICGC is 0.10% of the deposits. The cover applies to all commercial banks, RRBs, UCBs, and some cooperative banks. If a bank fails, depositors get up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank. The DICGC was established in 1961 as a wholly-owned subsidiary of RBI. The cover is now ₹5 lakh.

### Q12. The maximum initial capital for a Payments Bank is:
(a) ₹50 crore (b) ₹100 crore (c) ₹200 crore (d) ₹500 crore

**Answer: (b) ₹100 crore**

**Solution:** The maximum initial capital requirement for a Payments Bank is ₹100 crore. The minimum capital is also ₹100 crore (paid-up equity capital). The promoter should contribute at least 40% of the paid-up equity for the first 5 years. For Small Finance Banks, the minimum capital is ₹200 crore. For a Universal Bank (new), the minimum capital is ₹500 crore (raised to ₹1,000 crore for Payments Banks by RBI's recent draft guidelines). Currently there are 6 Payments Banks operating in India.

### Q13. An Insolvency Professional is regulated by:
(a) RBI (b) NCLT (c) IBBI (d) SEBI

**Answer: (c) IBBI**

**Solution:** Insolvency Professionals (IPs) are regulated by the IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India). IBBI was established in 2016 under the IBC, 2016. It regulates Insolvency Professionals, Information Utilities, and Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs). It writes rules for the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) and liquidation. The NCLT is the adjudicating authority for insolvency cases. NCLAT is the appellate authority. SEBI regulates the securities market.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.135-140 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q14. FEMA 1999 replaced which earlier act?
(a) FERA 1973 (b) RBI Act 1934 (c) BR Act 1949 (d) IBC 2016

**Answer: (a) FERA 1973**

**Solution:** FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act) 1999 replaced FERA (Foreign Exchange Regulation Act) 1973. FEMA is administered by RBI (Department of External Investments and Operations) and applies to all foreign exchange transactions in India. The penalty for contravention can be up to 3x the sum involved. FEMA is more liberal than FERA — FERA was very restrictive and had criminal penalties, while FEMA focuses on management of foreign exchange with civil penalties. The Adjudicating Authority for serious cases is the Enforcement Directorate.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** Banking/02_Banking_Types_Acts.md, p.151-160 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q15. Small Finance Banks must lend minimum to Priority Sector:
(a) 50% (b) 60% (c) 75% (d) 90%

**Answer: (c) 75%**

**Solution:** Small Finance Banks (SFBs) must lend a minimum of 75% of their loans to the Priority Sector. Also, 50% of their loans should be ≤ ₹25 lakh. There are 10 active SFBs (as of 2024): AU SFB, Capital SFB, ESAF SFB, Equitas SFB, Fincare SFB, Jana SFB, North East SFB, Suryoday SFB, Ujjivan SFB, and Utkarsh SFB. SFBs target financial inclusion — small/marginal farmers, micro industries, and unorganized sector entities. Initial capital requirement: ₹200 crore.
