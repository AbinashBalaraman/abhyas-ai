### Q1.
**Spot the error:** "**Neetu have been** waiting for me since 10 o'clock."
- (a) Neetu have been (b) in the morning (c) since 10 o'clock (d) waiting for me

**Answer:** (a) Neetu have been
**Solution:** "Neetu" is singular → "has been" (not "have been").
**Grammar Rule:** Singular proper noun + singular verb (has, not have).
**Common Mistake:** Mistaking -u ending names for plural.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q2.
**Spot the error:** "**I were** writing this poem last evening."
- (a) I were (b) writing (c) This poem (d) Last evening

**Answer:** (a) I were
**Solution:** "I" + Past Continuous → "I was" (subjunctive "were" only in hypotheticals like "If I were").
**Grammar Rule:** "I" + was (indicative); "were" only for subjunctive ("If I were rich").
**Common Mistake:** Using "I were" outside hypothetical clauses.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q3.
**Spot the error:** "Jiten and Hiru **loves** to eat french fries for lunch."
- (a) Jiten and Hiru (b) loves (c) to eat french fries (d) for lunch

**Answer:** (b) loves
**Solution:** Two subjects joined by "and" → plural verb. Correct: "Jiten and Hiru **love**."
**Grammar Rule:** "A and B" = plural subject → plural verb.
**Common Mistake:** Singular verb with "A and B."
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q4.
**Spot the error:** "Rapid climate warming in the tundra biome **have been linked** to increasing shrub dominance."
- (a) Rapid climate warming in (b) No error (c) the tundra biome have been linked (d) to increasing shrub dominance

**Answer:** (c) the tundra biome have been linked
**Solution:** "Tundra biome" is the subject (singular) → "has been linked."
**Grammar Rule:** Subject-verb agreement with long subjects (find the head noun).
**Common Mistake:** Picking verb form that matches a nearer plural noun.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q5.
**Spot the error:** "India's claim to be a world leader in pharmaceuticals **are** heavily compromised."
- (a) world leader in pharmaceuticals (b) No error (c) are heavily compromised (d) India's claim to be a

**Answer:** (c) are heavily compromised
**Solution:** "India's claim" is singular → "is" (not "are").
**Grammar Rule:** Possessive + singular noun = singular subject.
**Common Mistake:** Mistaking the long subject for plural.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q6.
**Spot the error:** "She always **invite** me to dinner."
- (a) She always (b) invite me (c) to dinner (d) No error

**Answer:** (b) invite me
**Solution:** "She" is singular → "invites" (with -s).
**Grammar Rule:** 3rd person singular Simple Present → V + s/es.
**Common Mistake:** Using base form with 3rd person singular.
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q7.
**Spot the error:** "The photo session included **between he and I**."
- (a) The photo session (b) included (c) between he and I (d) No error

**Answer:** (c) between he and I
**Solution:** "Between" is a preposition → objective case "him and me." Correct: "between **him and me**."
**Grammar Rule:** Preposition + objective case pronouns (me, him, her, us, them).
**Common Mistake:** Defaulting to "I" after "and."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q8.
**Spot the error:** "Either he or his friends **is** coming."
- (a) Either he (b) or his friends (c) is coming (d) No error

**Answer:** (c) is coming
**Solution:** "Either...or / Neither...nor" → verb agrees with the **NEAREST subject** ("friends" is plural → "are"). Correct: "Either he or his friends **are** coming."
**Grammar Rule:** "Either...or / Neither...nor" → verb agrees with nearest subject.
**Common Mistake:** Defaulting to singular with "either."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q9.
**Spot the error:** "The child along with his parents **are** coming."
- (a) The child (b) along with his parents (c) are coming (d) No error

**Answer:** (c) are coming
**Solution:** "Along with / as well as / with" → verb agrees with the **FIRST** subject ("child" is singular → "is"). Correct: "The child along with his parents **is** coming."
**Grammar Rule:** "A along with B" → verb with A (not B).
**Common Mistake:** Using plural verb because of the phrase after "along with."
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q10.
**Spot the error:** "He behaves as if he **was** a king."
- (a) He behaves (b) as if (c) he was (d) a king

**Answer:** (c) he was
**Solution:** Subjunctive mood for hypothetical "as if" → "he **were**." Correct: "He behaves as if he **were** a king."
**Grammar Rule:** Subjunctive "were" in hypothetical clauses (as if, as though, wish, if only).
**Common Mistake:** Using indicative "was" with hypothetical "as if."
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q11.
**Spot the error:** "I prefer tea **than** coffee."
- (a) I prefer (b) tea (c) than (d) coffee

**Answer:** (c) than
**Solution:** "Prefer" takes "to," not "than." Correct: "I prefer tea **to** coffee."
**Grammar Rule:** "Prefer A to B" (NOT "prefer A than B").
**Common Mistake:** Using "than" with "prefer."
**Time Target:** 20 sec

---

### Q12.
**Sentence Improvement:** "I **am** working here since 2010."
- (a) am working (b) have been working (c) was working (d) No improvement

**Answer:** (b) have been working
**Solution:** "Since 2010" = point of time → Present Perfect Continuous. Correct: "I **have been working** here since 2010."
**Grammar Rule:** "Since" + point in time → Present Perfect (have been V-ing).
**Common Mistake:** Using Present Continuous with "since."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q13.
**Spot the error:** "The teacher **asked** the students to open their books."
- (a) The teacher (b) asked (c) the students (d) to open their books

**Answer:** (d) No error
**Solution:** This sentence is correct. "Asked + obj + to + V1" is the standard construction for reporting imperative/request.
**Grammar Rule:** "Asked/ordered/told + object + to-infinitive" is standard.
**Common Mistake:** Trying to "improve" a sentence that is already correct.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q14.
**Spot the error:** "**Between you and I**, this is a secret."
- (a) Between (b) you and I (c) this is a secret (d) No error

**Answer:** (b) you and I
**Solution:** "Between" is a preposition → "between you and **me**" (objective case).
**Grammar Rule:** Preposition + objective case (me, him, her, us, them).
**Common Mistake:** Using subjective case after preposition.
**Time Target:** 30 sec

---

### Q15.
**Spot the error:** "He **does not know** to swim."
- (a) He (b) does not know (c) to swim (d) No error

**Answer:** (c) to swim
**Solution:** "Know" is followed by "how + to-infinitive." Correct: "He does not know **how to** swim."
**Grammar Rule:** "Know + how to + V1" (NOT "know to V1").
**Common Mistake:** Writing "know to" without "how."
**Time Target:** 30 sec

## Tier 2 Specific Strategies

1. **Long subject trap:** Find the head noun first, then check singular/plural.
2. **"As if / as though" → subjunctive were** (NOT was).
3. **"Either...or / Neither...nor" → nearest subject.**
4. **"A along with B" → verb with A.**
5. **"No improvement" is a valid answer** — don't over-edit correct sentences.
