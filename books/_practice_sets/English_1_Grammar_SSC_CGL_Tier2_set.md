### Q1.
**Convert to Passive:** "I **bade him go**."
- (a) He was bade to go. (b) He was bidden to go. (c) He bade to go. (d) He bid to go by me.

**Answer:** (b) He was bidden to go.
**Solution:** Per Rule XIV, "bid" takes bare infinitive in active, "to-infinitive" in passive. "Bidden" is the V3 form of "bid."
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule XIV — Verbs (let, bid, make, help, feel, see, watch, hear) take bare infinitive in active, to-infinitive in passive.
**Common Mistake:** Writing "was bade" instead of "was bidden"; forgetting the bare-infinitive → to-infinitive shift.
**Time Target:** 60 sec

### Q2.
**Convert to Passive:** "She **let me go**."
- (a) I was let go by her. (b) I was let to go by her. (c) I let go by her. (d) I am let go by her.

**Answer:** (a) I was let go by her.
**Solution:** "Let" is the EXCEPTION to Rule XIV — both active and passive use **bare infinitive** (no "to").
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule XIV — Exception: "Let" is used with bare infinitive in BOTH active and passive.
**Common Mistake:** Adding "to" before "go" (treating "let" like other Rule XIV verbs).
**Time Target:** 45 sec

### Q3.
**Convert to Passive:** "She was seen crossing the road."
- (a) She crossed the road. (b) People saw her crossing the road. (c) She was seen to cross the road. (d) She has been seen crossing the road.

**Answer:** (b) People saw her crossing the road.
**Solution:** This sentence is ALREADY passive. Convert to active: "People saw her crossing the road." Note: "see + bare infinitive" active; "seen + to-infinitive" passive is also acceptable. The cleanest active form is (b).
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule XIV — "see + bare infinitive" active; "seen + to-infinitive" passive.
**Common Mistake:** Missing that the sentence is already in passive and converting to another passive.
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q4.
**Spot the error:** "He behaves as if he **was** a king."
- (a) He behaves (b) as if (c) he was (d) a king

**Answer:** (c) he was
**Solution:** Subjunctive mood for hypothetical/unreal conditions → "he **were**" (even with present/future reference). Correct: "He behaves as if he **were** a king."
**Grammar Rule:** Subjunctive "were" is used in hypothetical clauses with "as if / as though / if / wish."
**Common Mistake:** Using indicative "was" instead of subjunctive "were."
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q5.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** She said, "I wish I were a bird."
- (a) She said that she wished she was a bird. (b) She said that she wished she were a bird. (c) She said that she wishes she were a bird. (d) She said that I wished I were a bird.

**Answer:** (b) She said that she wished she were a bird.
**Solution:** Back-shift "wish" (Present) → "wished" (Past). But "were" (subjunctive) is preserved.
**Grammar Rule:** Subjunctive "were" remains unchanged even after back-shift; the reporting verb itself back-shifts.
**Common Mistake:** Changing "were" to "was" in the indirect form.
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q6.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** She said, "If I were a bird, I would fly."
- (a) She said that if she were a bird, she would fly. (b) She said that if she was a bird, she would fly. (c) She said that if she had been a bird, she would have flown. (d) She said that if she is a bird, she will fly.

**Answer:** (a) She said that if she were a bird, she would fly.
**Solution:** Unreal conditional (subjunctive "were" + "would") → **NO back-shift** of "were" or "would." Pronoun "I" → "she."
**Grammar Rule:** Unreal/hypothetical conditionals (Type 2) are NOT back-shifted because the unreality remains.
**Common Mistake:** Back-shifting "were" → "was" or "would" → "should/would have."
**Time Target:** 60 sec

### Q7.
**Convert to Passive:** "They made him king."
- (a) He was made king by them. (b) He was made to king by them. (c) He is made king by them. (d) He has been made king by them.

**Answer:** (a) He was made king by them.
**Solution:** Rule XI — Two objects + complement. The complement "king" is retained in the passive construction.
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule XI — "Made + object + complement" → "Object + was/were + made + complement."
**Common Mistake:** Adding "to" before the complement ("made to king").
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q8.
**Convert to Passive:** "I have finished the work."
- (a) The work has been finished by me. (b) The work is finished by me. (c) The work was finished by me. (d) The work had been finished by me.

**Answer:** (a) The work has been finished by me.
**Solution:** Present Perfect Active → "has/have + been + V3" Passive. "I" → "by me"; "the work" becomes subject.
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule V — Perfect tenses → has/have/had + been + V3.
**Common Mistake:** Dropping "been" (writing "has finished" instead of "has been finished").
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q9.
**Convert to Passive:** "He is writing a letter."
- (a) A letter is written by him. (b) A letter is being written by him. (c) A letter was being written by him. (d) A letter has been written by him.

**Answer:** (b) A letter is being written by him.
**Solution:** Present Continuous → "is/am/are + being + V3."
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule IV — Continuous tenses → is/am/are/was/were + being + V3.
**Common Mistake:** Confusing with Simple Present passive ("is written") which would lose the progressive meaning.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q10.
**Spot the error:** "The sceneries of Kashmir **are** beautiful."
- (a) The sceneries of Kashmir (b) are (c) beautiful (d) No error

**Answer:** (a) The sceneries of Kashmir
**Solution:** "Scenery" is an **uncountable noun** — no plural form. Correct: "The **scenery** of Kashmir is beautiful." (Also: singular verb "is.")
**Grammar Rule:** Uncountable nouns (scenery, furniture, luggage, information, advice) take no -s and take singular verbs.
**Common Mistake:** Treating "scenery" as countable.
**Time Target:** 45 sec

---

### Q11.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** He said, "Let us go for a walk."
- (a) He suggested that they should go for a walk. (b) He said that they should let them go for a walk. (c) He told them to go for a walk. (d) He said let us go for a walk.

**Answer:** (a) He suggested that they should go for a walk.
**Solution:** "Let us" in direct = suggestion/proposal. Reporting verb → "suggested" + "should + V1." Pronoun "us" → "they."
**Grammar Rule:** "Let us" in indirect = suggestion → "suggested + that + subject + should + V1."
**Common Mistake:** Treating "let us" literally (writing "let them go").
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q12.
**Convert to Passive:** "I made her sing a song."
- (a) She was made sing a song by me. (b) She was made to sing a song by me. (c) She was made singing a song by me. (d) She is made to sing a song by me.

**Answer:** (b) She was made to sing a song by me.
**Solution:** Rule XIV — "make + bare infinitive" active → "made + to-infinitive" passive. V3 of "make" is "made."
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule XIV — "Make" shifts bare infinitive → to-infinitive in passive.
**Common Mistake:** Retaining bare infinitive ("made sing" instead of "made to sing").
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q13.
**Convert to Indirect Speech:** He said, "I have been waiting since morning."
- (a) He said that he had been waiting since morning. (b) He said that he has been waiting since morning. (c) He said that he had been waiting from morning. (d) He said that he had waited since morning.

**Answer:** (a) He said that he had been waiting since morning.
**Solution:** Back-shift Present Perfect Continuous "have been waiting" → Past Perfect Continuous "had been waiting." "Since" stays as is.
**Grammar Rule:** Present Perfect Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous.
**Common Mistake:** Back-shifting to Past Perfect Continuous but missing the "been" form.
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q14.
**Convert to Passive:** "One should respect one's elders."
- (a) One's elders should be respected. (b) One's elders should be respected by one. (c) Elders should be respected. (d) No change possible.

**Answer:** (b) One's elders should be respected by one.
**Solution:** Modal passive = "modal + be + V3." "Should respect" → "should be respected." Subject becomes "One's elders"; agent "by one."
**Grammar Rule:** Voice Rule III — Modal + be + V3.
**Common Mistake:** Dropping the agent or using wrong verb form.
**Time Target:** 60 sec

---

### Q15.
**Spot the error:** "He **is having** no house to live in."
- (a) He (b) is having (c) no house (d) to live in

**Answer:** (b) is having
**Solution:** "Have" (possession) is stative → no continuous. Correct: "He **has** no house to live in."
**Grammar Rule:** Stative verb "have" (possession) → Simple Present, not Present Continuous.
**Common Mistake:** Using continuous form with possession; reserve "is having" for meals (e.g., "is having dinner").
**Time Target:** 30 sec

## Tier 2 Specific Strategies

1. **Rule XIV trap:** Always check for let/bid/make/help/feel/see/watch/hear → bare infinitive in active, to-infinitive in passive (except "let").
2. **Subjunctive preservation:** "were" (not "was") in "as if / if / wish" clauses survives back-shift.
3. **Type 2 conditionals:** NO back-shift of "were" or "would."
4. **Uncountable nouns:** Scenery, furniture, luggage, advice, information → no -s, singular verb.
