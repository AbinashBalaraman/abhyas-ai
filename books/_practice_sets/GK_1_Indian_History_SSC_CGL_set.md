## Questions

### Q1. The Great Bath of the Indus Valley Civilization is located at:
(a) Harappa (b) Mohenjo-Daro (c) Lothal (d) Dholavira

**Answer: (b) Mohenjo-Daro**

**Solution:** The Great Bath is the most famous structure of Mohenjo-Daro (Sindh, Pakistan). It measures 11.88 × 7.01 metres with steps on both sides. Mohenjo-Daro was discovered by R.D. Banerji in 1922. Other key Harappan features: Dockyard at Lothal (world's earliest), Granaries at Harappa, Pashupati seal from Mohenjo-Daro.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.1-3 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q2. The 24th Tirthankara of Jainism was:
(a) Rishabhanatha (b) Parshvanatha (c) Mahavira (d) Neminatha

**Answer: (c) Mahavira**

**Solution:** The 24th and last Tirthankara of Jainism was Mahavira (540–468 BCE), born in Vaishali. The first Tirthankara was Rishabhanatha. Jain teachings include five vows: Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha, Brahmacharya; and doctrines of Anekantavada (multiple viewpoints) and Syadvada. Symbol: Wheel with 24 spokes.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.5-6 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q3. Who wrote the ancient Indian treatise 'Arthashastra'?
(a) Megasthenes (b) Kautilya (c) Kalidasa (d) Banabhatta

**Answer: (b) Kautilya**

**Solution:** Kautilya (also known as Chanakya) wrote Arthashastra, the famous treatise on statecraft, economics, and military strategy, during the Mauryan period. Megasthenes was the Greek ambassador who wrote Indica. Kalidasa wrote Shakuntalam and Meghdoot (Gupta period). Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita (Harsha's court).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.10 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q4. The Kalinga War was fought by which Mauryan emperor?
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Bindusara (c) Ashoka (d) Samudragupta

**Answer: (c) Ashoka**

**Solution:** Emperor Ashoka fought the Kalinga War in 261 BCE, after which he converted to Buddhism and followed a policy of non-violence. The war caused massive casualties which deeply moved Ashoka. He sent Buddhist missions to Sri Lanka (Mahinda), Egypt, and Syria; and erected Rock and Pillar edicts throughout his empire.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.10 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q5. The Gupta Empire is known as the Golden Age of India primarily because of:
(a) Military conquests (b) Cultural and scientific achievements (c) Trade with Rome (d) Religious uniformity

**Answer: (b) Cultural and scientific achievements**

**Solution:** The Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE) under Chandragupta I, Samudragupta, and Chandragupta II witnessed unprecedented achievements in literature (Kalidasa - Shakuntalam), science (Aryabhata - value of π, Earth's rotation), mathematics (decimal system, zero, chess - chaturanga), and art. Samudragupta was called the "Indian Napoleon" while Chandragupta II's court had the Navaratnas including Kalidasa and Varahamihira.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.11-12 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. The first Muslim ruler of Delhi was:
(a) Muhammad Ghori (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (c) Iltutmish (d) Alauddin Khilji

**Answer: (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak**

**Solution:** Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206) founded the Mamluk/Slave dynasty and became the first Muslim ruler of Delhi. He started the construction of Qutub Minar (completed by Iltutmish). He was a slave of Muhammad Ghori. Iltutmish was his successor and is regarded as the real consolidator of the Sultanate.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.16 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. The Taj Mahal was built by which Mughal emperor?
(a) Akbar (b) Jahangir (c) Shah Jahan (d) Aurangzeb

**Answer: (c) Shah Jahan**

**Solution:** Shah Jahan (1628-1658), known as the "Architect of Mughal architecture", built the Taj Mahal in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. He also built the Red Fort, Jama Masjid in Delhi, and the Peacock Throne. Akbar built Fatehpur Sikri; Jahangir is known for the "Chain of Justice"; Aurangzeb was the last great Mughal.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.18 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. Shivaji's coronation ceremony took place in:
(a) Pune (b) Raigad (c) Torna (d) Sinhagad

**Answer: (b) Raigad**

**Solution:** Chhatrapati Shivaji's coronation took place at Raigad Fort in 1674. He founded the Maratha Empire, used guerrilla warfare (Ganimi Kava), and built a strong forts system. After his death, the Peshwas became the actual rulers; the Third Battle of Panipat (1761) was a major setback when Marathas were defeated by Ahmad Shah Abdali.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.20 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in which year?
(a) 1492 (b) 1498 (c) 1500 (d) 1505

**Answer: (b) 1498**

**Solution:** Vasco da Gama, a Portuguese explorer, reached Calicut (Kozhikode) in 1498, opening the sea route from Europe to India. Albuquerque captured Goa for the Portuguese in 1510. The English East India Company was founded in 1600, and the French East India Company in 1664. The Battle of Plassey (1757) marked the beginning of British political dominance in India.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.23 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between:
(a) Clive and Tipu Sultan (b) Clive and Siraj-ud-Daulah (c) Dalhousie and Hyder Ali (d) Wellesley and Marathas

**Answer: (b) Clive and Siraj-ud-Daulah**

**Solution:** The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between Robert Clive (British) and Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal). The British victory marked the beginning of British political dominance in India. The Battle of Buxar (1764) was fought by Hector Munro against the combined forces of Shuja-ud-Daulah, Shah Alam II, and Mir Qasim. Treaty of Allahabad (1765) gave Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar, Odisha to East India Company.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.24 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q11. The Permanent Settlement of Bengal was introduced by:
(a) Lord Dalhousie (b) Lord Cornwallis (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Hastings

**Answer: (b) Lord Cornwallis**

**Solution:** Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement in Bengal in 1793, fixing land revenue permanently with zamindars. Lord Dalhousie is associated with the Doctrine of Lapse and railways/telegraph. Lord Wellesley introduced the Subsidiary Alliance system. Lord Hastings fought the Third Anglo-Maratha War. Other notable reforms: Lord William Bentinck abolished Sati (1829); Lord Lytton introduced the Vernacular Press Act (1878).

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.24-26 | **VERIFY:** No

---

### Q12. The Doctrine of Lapse was associated with:
(a) Lord Cornwallis (b) Lord Wellesley (c) Lord Dalhousie (d) Lord Curzon

**Answer: (c) Lord Dalhousie**

**Solution:** The Doctrine of Lapse was the annexation policy of Lord Dalhousie (1848-1856) under which states without natural heirs (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, Awadh) were annexed. Dalhousie also introduced railways, telegraph, postal system in India. Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal in 1905. Lord Ripon is known for the Ilbert Bill (1883) and Local Self-Government (1882).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.24 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q13. The Indian National Congress was founded in:
(a) 1875 (b) 1885 (c) 1905 (d) 1915

**Answer: (b) 1885**

**Solution:** The Indian National Congress (INC) was founded in 1885 in Bombay by A.O. Hume, a retired British civil servant. W.C. Bonnerjee was the first president. Dadabhai Naoroji is called the "Grand Old Man of India" and authored "Poverty of India". The Moderate Phase (1885-1905) used constitutional methods; Extremist Phase (1905-1919) led by Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal used Boycott, Swadeshi, and National Education.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.32 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q14. The Quit India Movement was launched in:
(a) 1920 (b) 1930 (c) 1942 (d) 1946

**Answer: (c) 1942**

**Solution:** The Quit India Movement was launched on 8 August 1942 at the Gowalia Tank Maidan in Mumbai by Mahatma Gandhi with the slogan "Do or Die". Top leaders were arrested immediately; parallel governments emerged at Tamluk (Bengal), Satara (Maharashtra), etc. The Civil Disobedience Movement (1930) started with the famous Dandi March (12 March 1930) from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi by Gandhi to break the salt law. Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) was declared on 26 January 1930.

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.34 | **VERIFY:** No

---

### Q15. India gained independence on:
(a) 15 August 1946 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 November 1949

**Answer: (c) 15 August 1947**

**Solution:** India gained independence on 15 August 1947 (Pakistan on 14 August 1947). The last Viceroy was Lord Mountbatten. The Indian Independence Act 1947 was passed by the British Parliament. The first PM was Jawaharlal Nehru; first President (after Republic) was Dr. Rajendra Prasad; first Home Minister was Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel. The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/01_Indian_History.md, p.36 | **VERIFY:** No
