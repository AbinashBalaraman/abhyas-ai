### Q1. The Constituent Assembly of India first met on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 9 December 1946 (d) 26 November 1949

**Answer: (c) 9 December 1946**

**Solution:** The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was the temporary (provisional) Chairman; Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946. H.C. Mukherjee was Vice-President. B.N. Rau was the Constitutional Adviser. The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

### Q2. The Indian Constitution came into force on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 26 November 1949 (d) 2 October 1950

**Answer: (b) 26 January 1950**

**Solution:** The Indian Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 but came into force on 26 January 1950, chosen to commemorate the Purna Swaraj declaration of 1930. This date is celebrated as Republic Day. The Constituent Assembly had 299 members (after partition) and met for 165 days over 2 years, 11 months, 18 days. The original Constitution had 395 Articles + 8 Schedules + 22 Parts.

### Q3. Who is called the "Father of the Indian Constitution"?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (d) Dr. Rajendra Prasad

**Answer: (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**

**Solution:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar is called the "Father of the Indian Constitution" for chairing the Drafting Committee. The original Drafting Committee had 7 members including N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadulla, B.L. Mitter, D.P. Khaitan (replaced by N. Madhava Rau). Dr. Ambedkar also called Article 32 the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution.

### Q4. The Objective Resolution of the Constituent Assembly was moved by:
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Sardar Patel

**Answer: (c) Jawaharlal Nehru**

**Solution:** The Objective Resolution was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946 and adopted on 22 January 1947. It outlined the fundamental principles of the Constitution — Sovereign, Democratic Republic guaranteeing justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity to all citizens. It later became the basis of the Preamble.

### Q5. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) is known as:
(a) Right to Property Amendment (b) Mini-Constitution (c) Anti-Defection Amendment (d) RTE Amendment

**Answer: (b) Mini-Constitution**

**Solution:** The 42nd Amendment (1976) is called the "Mini-Constitution" because of the wide-ranging changes it made. It added three words to the Preamble — "Socialist", "Secular", and "Integrity". It added Fundamental Duties (Article 51A), transferred 5 subjects from State to Concurrent List, and reduced the power of the Supreme Court and High Courts. Many of its changes were reversed by the 44th Amendment (1978).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.30 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q6. Which Article is called the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 19 (c) Article 21 (d) Article 32

**Answer: (d) Article 32**

**Solution:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution because it allows citizens to move the Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. It provides writ jurisdiction to the Supreme Court. Article 226 provides similar writ jurisdiction to the High Courts. Article 21 (Right to Life) is the most litigated article.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.18 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q7. The Right to Education is enshrined in which Article?
(a) Article 21 (b) Article 21A (c) Article 30 (d) Article 45

**Answer: (b) Article 21A**

**Solution:** Article 21A (Right to Education for children aged 6-14 years) was added by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002. It makes elementary education a Fundamental Right. Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty) has been expanded through judicial interpretation to include livelihood, privacy, education, and dignity. Article 30 provides minorities the right to establish educational institutions.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.17 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q8. National Emergency is declared under Article:
(a) Article 352 (b) Article 356 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 365

**Answer: (a) Article 352**

**Solution:** Article 352 deals with National Emergency (during war, external aggression, or armed rebellion). It has been declared three times: 1962 (Chinese aggression), 1971 (Bangladesh war), 1975 (Internal - Indira Gandhi). Article 356 deals with President's Rule (state emergency) when constitutional machinery fails. Article 360 deals with Financial Emergency (never declared). The 44th Amendment (1978) limited emergency provisions to only two grounds (external aggression and armed rebellion).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.21-22 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q9. The Panchayati Raj system was introduced by which Constitutional Amendment?
(a) 71st (b) 72nd (c) 73rd (d) 74th

**Answer: (c) 73rd**

**Solution:** The 73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992) gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj. It established a 3-tier system (Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad) and added the 11th Schedule (29 subjects). The 74th Amendment (1992) gave constitutional status to Municipalities and added the 12th Schedule (18 subjects). Article 40 (DPSP) had earlier directed the state to organize village panchayats.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.30 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q10. GST was introduced by which Constitutional Amendment?
(a) 100th (b) 101st (c) 102nd (d) 103rd

**Answer: (b) 101st**

**Solution:** The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 enabled GST (Goods and Services Tax). GST was implemented on 1 July 2017. The 102nd Amendment (2018) gave constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC). The 103rd Amendment (2019) provided 10% reservation to Economically Weaker Sections (EWS). The 104th Amendment (2020) extended SC/ST reservation in LS/Assemblies by 10 years. The 106th Amendment (2023) - Women's Reservation (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam).

**Time Target:** 45s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.30-31 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q11. The Anti-defection law is included in which Schedule?
(a) 8th Schedule (b) 9th Schedule (c) 10th Schedule (d) 11th Schedule

**Answer: (c) 10th Schedule**

**Solution:** The Anti-defection Law is in the 10th Schedule, added by the 52nd Constitutional Amendment (1985). It provides for disqualification of members of Parliament/State Legislatures who defect from their party. The 8th Schedule contains 22 languages (originally 14, expanded by 92nd Amendment 2003). The 9th Schedule deals with land reform laws protected from judicial review. The 11th Schedule lists Panchayati Raj subjects (29 items).

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.25-26 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q12. The Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of:
(a) Lok Sabha (b) Rajya Sabha (c) Both Houses (d) Neither

**Answer: (b) Rajya Sabha**

**Solution:** The Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Article 64). The Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is elected by its members. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha is elected by its members. The Vice-President acts as acting President in case of vacancy. The first Vice-President of India was Dr. S. Radhakrishnan.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.34 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q13. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) Rajya Sabha (b) Lok Sabha (c) Joint Sitting (d) Either House

**Answer: (b) Lok Sabha**

**Solution:** A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha (as per Article 109). It can be introduced only by a Minister (not a private member). The Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes to a Money Bill within 14 days; the Lok Sabha has the final say. Joint Sitting of Parliament is presided over by the Speaker (not Chairman of Rajya Sabha) and is summoned by the President under Article 108 for ordinary bills.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.33 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q14. The first Speaker of Lok Sabha was:
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) G.V. Mavalankar (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Sardar Patel

**Answer: (b) G.V. Mavalankar**

**Solution:** G.V. Mavalankar was the first Speaker of the Lok Sabha (1952-1956). He is also called the "Father of Lok Sabha". The current Speaker (as of 2026) is Om Birla. The first Prime Minister was Jawaharlal Nehru (longest serving PM). The first President was Dr. Rajendra Prasad (longest serving, 12 years). The first Chief Justice of India was H.J. Kania.

**Time Target:** 30s | **Chapter Reference:** GK/02_Indian_Polity_Constitution.md, p.34 | **VERIFY:** No

### Q15. The Planning Commission was replaced by which body?
(a) Finance Commission (b) NITI Aayog (c) National Development Council (d) None

**Answer: (b) NITI Aayog**

**Solution:** The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) on 1 January 2015. NITI Aayog is chaired by the Prime Minister and serves as a policy think tank (advisory only). Unlike the Planning Commission, it does not allocate funds. The Finance Commission (Article 280) is constituted every 5 years to recommend tax devolution; the 16th FC is chaired by Arvind Panagariya.
