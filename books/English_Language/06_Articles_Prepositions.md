## 2. Articles (A, An, The)

### 2.1 Definition

An **article** is a word that comes before a noun to specify whether the noun is **definite** (specific) or **indefinite** (general). English has three articles: **A, An** (indefinite), **The** (definite).

### 2.2 The Three Articles

| Article | Type | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| **A** | Indefinite (consonant sound) | a book, a car, a university |
| **An** | Indefinite (vowel sound) | an apple, an hour, an honest man |
| **The** | Definite (specific) | the book, the car, the sun |

### 2.3 The "Sound Rule" for A vs An

The choice between **A** and **An** is based on the **SOUND**, not the letter:
- **A** before consonant **sounds**: a book, a car, a dog, a university (yu-ni-VER-si-ty starts with "yu" sound).
- **An** before vowel **sounds**: an apple, an egg, an hour (silent h), an honest man (silent h), an umbrella.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard rule]

### 2.4 Examples of A vs An

| Word | Article | Reason |
|------|---------|--------|
| a book | A | "b" is consonant |
| an apple | AN | "a" is vowel |
| a university | A | "yu" sound (consonant) |
| an honest man | AN | "h" is silent |
| an hour | AN | "h" is silent |
| a European | A | "yu" sound |
| a one-rupee note | A | "w" sound (one → wun) |
| an MLA | AN | "M" starts with "em" (vowel) |

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 2.5 When to Use "A/An"

Use **A/An** when:
1. **First mention** of a singular countable noun.
2. Referring to **any one** of a class.
3. With **profession, nationality, religion** (singular).
4. With **rate, ratio, frequency** (e.g., "twice a day", "₹100 a kg").
5. In exclamations ("What a beautiful flower!").

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard rules]

### 2.6 When to Use "The"

Use **The** when:
1. **Specific** noun already mentioned or implied.
2. **Unique** nouns (the sun, the moon, the President).
3. **Superlative** adjectives (the best, the tallest).
4. **Ordinal** numbers (the first, the second).
5. **Musical instruments** (play the piano).
6. With **"of"** phrases (the capital of India).
7. With **geographical features** (the Himalayas, the Pacific).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard rules]

### 2.7 When to Use NO Article (Zero Article)

Use **no article** when:
1. **Plural countable** nouns in general (Books are useful).
2. **Uncountable** nouns in general (Water is essential).
3. **Proper nouns** in most cases (India, not The India).
4. **Meals, days, months** (have lunch, on Monday).
5. **Languages** (English is spoken widely).
6. With **"at home", "at work", "at school", "in bed"**.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 2.8 Common Errors with Articles

**Error 1:** "I saw a elephant" (wrong) → "I saw an elephant" (correct).
**Error 2:** "He is the best player" (correct — superlative).
**Error 3:** "Sun rises in east" (wrong) → "The sun rises in the east" (correct — unique noun, specific direction).
**Error 4:** "She is a MBA" (wrong) → "She is an MBA" (correct — M is "em" sound).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — common error patterns]

### 2.9 Common Test Patterns (Articles)

1. "Honest man is respected" → "An honest man is respected."
2. "I bought a apple" → "I bought an apple."
3. "Sun is shining brightly" → "The sun is shining brightly."
4. "She is doctor" → "She is a doctor."

## 3. Prepositions

### 3.1 Definition

A **preposition** is a word that shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and other words in a sentence. Common prepositions: in, on, at, of, for, to, from, with, by, about, under, over, between, among.

### 3.2 Categories of Prepositions

**1. Prepositions of Time:** in, on, at, since, for, during, before, after.
**2. Prepositions of Place:** in, on, at, above, below, between, among, under, over.
**3. Prepositions of Direction:** to, from, into, onto, toward, through.
**4. Prepositions of Manner/Means:** with, by, in, on.
**5. Miscellaneous:** of, about, for, with, against.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard classification]

### 3.3 The Big Three: In, On, At (Time)

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **IN** | Months, years, seasons, centuries, parts of day | in March, in 2024, in summer, in the morning |
| **ON** | Days, dates, specific days | on Monday, on 5th May, on my birthday |
| **AT** | Specific time, night, weekend | at 5 PM, at night, at noon |

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.4 The Big Three: In, On, At (Place)

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **IN** | Enclosed space, countries, cities | in India, in Mumbai, in a box |
| **ON** | Surface, public transport | on the table, on the bus, on a page |
| **AT** | Specific point, address | at the door, at 10 MG Road |

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.5 Common Preposition Errors

| Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|-------|---------|------|
| in Monday | on Monday | days use "on" |
| at the morning | in the morning | parts of day use "in" |
| on 5 PM | at 5 PM | specific time uses "at" |
| in the bus | on the bus | public transport uses "on" |
| since 5 years | for 5 years | "since" + point in time; "for" + duration |
| discuss about | discuss | "discuss" doesn't take "about" |
| enter into | enter | "enter" doesn't take "into" |
| comprised of | composed of | "comprise" means "include"; "compose" means "make up" |

### 3.6 Prepositions After Adjectives/Verbs

Many adjectives and verbs are followed by specific prepositions:

**Adjective + Preposition:**

**Verb + Preposition:**
- depend **on**
- consist **of**
- believe **in**
- succeed **in**
- apologize **for**
- accuse **of**
- blame **for**
- congratulate **on**
- insist **on**
- object **to**

- the reason **for**
- the solution **to**
- the answer **to**
- the key **to**
- the approach **to**
- the damage **to**

## 4. Conjunctions

### 4.1 Definition

A **conjunction** is a word that connects words, phrases, or clauses.

### 4.2 Categories of Conjunctions

**1. Coordinating Conjunctions:** and, but, or, nor, for, yet, so (FANBOYS).
- Connect words/phrases/clauses of equal rank.

**2. Subordinating Conjunctions:** because, although, since, when, if, while, after, before, until.
- Connect a dependent clause to an independent clause.

**3. Correlative Conjunctions:** either...or, neither...nor, both...and, not only...but also, whether...or.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.3 Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

| Conjunction | Use | Example |
|------------|-----|---------|
| **and** | Addition | He is tall **and** handsome. |
| **but** | Contrast | She is poor **but** honest. |
| **or** | Choice | Tea **or** coffee? |
| **nor** | Negative addition | Neither he **nor** I was there. |
| **for** | Reason | He left, **for** he was tired. |
| **yet** | Contrast | Small **yet** powerful. |
| **so** | Result | He studied hard, **so** he passed. |

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.4 Subordinating Conjunctions (Common)

| Type | Conjunctions |
|------|--------------|
| Time | when, while, before, after, until, since, as |
| Cause | because, since, as, for |
| Contrast | although, though, even though, while, whereas |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that |
| Purpose | so that, in order that |
| Result | so...that, such...that |
| Comparison | as...as, than |

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.5 Correlative Conjunctions

- **either...or**: Either A or B.
- **neither...nor**: Neither A nor B.
- **both...and**: Both A and B.
- **not only...but also**: Not only A but also B.
- **whether...or**: Whether A or B.
- **no sooner...than**: No sooner did A than B.
- **hardly...when**: Hardly had A when B.
- **scarcely...when**: Scarcely had A when B.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.6 Common Errors with Conjunctions

**Error 1:** "He is both intelligent but lazy" (wrong) → "He is both intelligent **and** lazy" (correct).
**Error 2:** "Although he is rich, but he is unhappy" (wrong) → "**Although** he is rich, **he is** unhappy" (correct — "but" and "although" together is redundant).
**Error 3:** "Since he was tired, so he went home" (wrong) → "**Since** he was tired, **he went** home" or "He was tired, **so** he went home" (correct).
**Error 4:** "Neither A or B" (wrong) → "**Neither A nor B**" (correct).
**Error 5:** "Either A nor B" (wrong) → "**Either A or B**" (correct).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — common error patterns]

### 4.7 Subject-Verb Agreement with Correlative Conjunctions

With **either...or / neither...nor**, the verb agrees with the **closest subject**:
- Either he **or** they **are** coming.
- Neither he **nor** I **am** going.

## 5. Modals

### 5.1 Definition

**Modals** (or **modal auxiliaries**) are helping verbs that express necessity, possibility, permission, ability, or obligation.

### 5.2 The Modal Auxiliaries

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **can** | ability, possibility | I **can** swim. |
| **could** | past ability, polite request | I **could** help. |
| **may** | permission, possibility | I **may** go. |
| **might** | less certain possibility | I **might** go. |
| **will** | future, willingness | I **will** go. |
| **would** | polite request, past of will | I **would** like tea. |
| **shall** | future (with I/we) | I **shall** go. |
| **should** | advice, obligation | You **should** study. |
| **must** | strong obligation, deduction | You **must** study. |
| **ought to** | moral obligation | You **ought to** help. |
| **need to** | necessity | You **need to** study. |
| **dare** | courage | He **dare** not come. |
| **used to** | past habit | I **used to** play. |

### 5.3 Modal Characteristics

Modals share these properties:
1. **No -s in third person**: He **can** go (NOT cans).
2. **Followed by base form**: I **can** swim (NOT swimming).
3. **No "to"**: I **can** go (NOT can to go).
4. **Form negatives with "not"**: I **cannot** go / I **can't** go.
5. **Form questions by inversion**: **Can** you swim?

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard modal rules]

### 5.4 Modal Meanings in Detail

**Can/Could:**
- Ability (present/past): I can swim. / I could swim when young.
- Possibility: It can rain today.
- Permission (informal): Can I go?

**May/Might:**
- Permission (formal): May I come in?
- Possibility: It may rain. / It might rain (less certain).

**Will/Would:**
- Future: I will go tomorrow.
- Willingness: I will help you.
- Polite request: Would you help me?

**Shall/Should:**
- Future (with I/we): I shall return.
- Advice: You should see a doctor.
- Obligation: You should be on time.

**Must:**
- Strong obligation: You must complete this.
- Deduction: He must be tired (deduction from evidence).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 5.5 Common Errors with Modals

**Error 1:** "He cans swim" → "He **can** swim" (no -s).
**Error 2:** "I may to go" → "I **may** go" (no "to").
**Error 3:** "He musts go" → "He **must** go" (no -s).
**Error 4:** "She cans sing" → "She **can** sing" (no -s).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 5.6 Modals in Passive Voice

When the active voice has a modal, the passive retains the modal:
- Active: He **can** complete the work.
- Passive: The work **can be** completed by him.

- Active: She **must** submit the report.
- Passive: The report **must be** submitted by her.

## 6. Speed-Solving Shortcuts

### 6.1 The "Sound, Not Letter" Trick for A/An

Listen for the **sound** of the first letter, not the letter itself.
- "university" → "yu-ni..." sound → "a university"
- "hour" → silent h → "an hour"
- "honest" → silent h → "an honest"

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 6.2 The "Specific vs General" Trick for Articles

- **Specific/Unique** (the sun, the President) → **the**.
- **General** (any book, any car) → **a/an** (singular) or **no article** (plural).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 6.3 The "Time Trick" for Prepositions

- **Years/Months/Seasons** → **in** (in 2024, in March, in winter).
- **Days/Dates** → **on** (on Monday, on 5 May).
- **Specific time** → **at** (at 5 PM, at noon).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 6.4 The "Since vs For" Trick

- **Since** + point in time (since 2020, since Monday).
- **For** + duration (for 5 years, for a long time).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 6.5 The "FANBOYS" Trick for Coordinating Conjunctions

Memorize FANBOYS = For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 6.6 The "Although-But" Trick

Don't use "although" and "but" together. Use one or the other.
- ✗ "Although he is rich, but he is unhappy."
- ✓ "Although he is rich, he is unhappy."
- ✓ "He is rich, but he is unhappy."

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 6.7 The "No -s on Modals" Trick

Modals never take -s in third person: He can, He must, He will (not cans, musts, wills).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

## 7. Classification of Question Types

### 7.1 Type 1 — Article Error

**Example:** "I saw elephant" → "I saw **an** elephant."
**Solution:** Singular countable noun needs article. *Time: 10 sec.* [See §2]

### 7.2 Type 2 — Preposition Error

**Example:** "He is good in mathematics" → "He is good **at** mathematics."
**Solution:** "Good at" is the correct collocation. *Time: 10 sec.* [See §3]

### 7.3 Type 3 — Conjunction Error

**Example:** "Although he worked hard, but he failed" → "Although he worked hard, **he failed**."
**Solution:** Remove "but" — redundant with "although". *Time: 15 sec.* [See §4]

### 7.4 Type 4 — Modal Error

**Example:** "He cans speak English" → "He **can** speak English."
**Solution:** Modals don't take -s. *Time: 10 sec.* [See §5]

### 7.5 Type 5 — Multiple Errors

**Example:** "I have been working here since 5 years and I cans speak 3 languages."
**Solution:** "since" → "for" (5 years is duration); "cans" → "can". *Time: 30 sec.* [Combined]

### 7.6 Type 6 — Fill in the Blank

**Example:** "She is ___ honest woman."
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Solution:** "honest" has silent h → vowel sound → "an". **Answer: (b).** *Time: 10 sec.*

### 7.7 Type 7 — Sentence Improvement

**Example:** Improve: "He is good in mathematics."
(a) good at (b) good in (c) good with (d) good of
**Solution:** "Good at" is correct. **Answer: (a).** *Time: 15 sec.*
