## 1. Conceptual Foundation

### 1.1 What is Subject-Verb Agreement?

**Subject-Verb Agreement (SVA)** is the grammatical rule that the **verb must agree with its subject in number (singular/plural) and person (first/second/third)**.

### 1.2 What are Tenses?

**Tenses** indicate the **time** of an action (present, past, future) and its **state** (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). There are **12 tenses** in English (3 times × 4 states).

### 1.3 Why These Topics Matter

- **Every sentence** has a subject and a verb, and uses a tense.
- **SVA errors** are among the most common error-spotting questions.
- **Tense errors** appear in sentence improvement, narration, voice.
- **Mastery here** improves overall English accuracy.

## 2. Subject-Verb Agreement — Core Rules

### 2.1 The Basic Rule

A **singular subject** takes a **singular verb**; a **plural subject** takes a **plural verb**.

- The boy **runs**. (singular subject + singular verb)
- The boys **run**. (plural subject + plural verb)

### 2.2 Singular Verb Formation

In simple present tense, **add -s/-es** to the verb for singular third-person subjects:
- He **runs**. (run + s)
- She **watches**. (watch + es)
- The box **contains**. (contain + s)
- The bus **stops**. (stop + s)

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard rule]

### 2.3 Plural Verb Formation

**Remove -s** for plural subjects:
- They **run**. (no -s)
- The boxes **contain**. (no -s)
- The buses **stop**. (no -s)

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 2.4 The 24 Key SVA Rules

**Rule 1: Two subjects joined by "and"** → plural verb.
- Ram **and** Shyam **are** coming. (plural)

**Rule 2: Two subjects joined by "with, along with, together with, as well as"** → verb agrees with the **first subject**.
- Ram, along with his friends, **is** going. (Ram is singular, so singular verb)
- The teacher, as well as the students, **was** present. (teacher is singular)

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard rule; also seen in Competitive Exam Question Bank Spotting Error examples]

**Rule 3: "Either...or / Neither...nor"** → verb agrees with the **nearest subject**.
- Either he **or** they **are** going. (they is plural → plural verb)
- Neither he **nor** I **am** guilty. (I is singular → singular verb)

**Rule 4: "Each, Every, Either, Neither"** as subject → **singular** verb.
- **Each** student **has** a book.
- **Every** boy **was** present.

**Rule 5: "Anyone, Everyone, Someone, No one, Nobody, Somebody"** → **singular** verb.
- **Everyone** is here.
- **Nobody** knows the answer.

**Rule 6: Collective nouns (team, family, group, jury)** → **singular** verb (usually).
- The team **is** playing well.
- The family **has** arrived.

**Rule 7: News, Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Athletics** → **singular** verb.
- The news **is** good.
- Mathematics **is** a difficult subject.

**Rule 8: "A number of"** → **plural** verb. **"The number of"** → **singular** verb.
- A number of students **are** absent.
- The number of students **is** increasing.

**Rule 9: "Many a / Many an"** → **singular** verb.
- Many a student **has** failed. (NOT "have")
- Many a man **has** tried.

**Rule 10: Subjects separated by "or, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also"** → verb agrees with the **nearest subject**.

**Rule 11: "All, Some, Most, Half, Part"** → verb agrees with the **noun** they refer to.
- All of the water **is** gone. (water is uncountable → singular)
- All of the books **are** new. (books is plural → plural)

**Rule 12: "A lot of, Lots of, Plenty of"** → verb agrees with the **noun** that follows.
- A lot of books **are** missing.
- A lot of water **is** wasted.

**Rule 13: "None"** → **singular** (in formal) or **plural** (informal).
- None of the students **is** (formal) / **are** (informal) absent.

**Rule 14: "One of + plural noun + who/which/that"** → verb agrees with the **plural noun** (the antecedent of who/which/that).
- He is one of the students who **have** passed. (students is plural)

**Rule 15: "The only one of + plural noun"** → **singular** verb.
- She is the only one of the girls who **has** passed. (only one is singular)

**Rule 16: "Each of + plural noun"** → **singular** verb.
- Each of the boys **is** present. (each is singular)

**Rule 17: Fractions and percentages** → verb agrees with the **noun** they modify.
- Two-thirds of the water **is** gone.
- Two-thirds of the students **are** absent.

**Rule 18: "The + adjective" (referring to a class)** → **singular** or **plural** depending on context.
- The rich **are** not always happy. (plural)
- The beautiful **is** not always good. (abstract — singular)

**Rule 19: Titles of books, films** → **singular** verb.
- "The Lord of the Rings" **is** a famous movie.

**Rule 20: "More than one"** → **singular** verb.
- More than one student **has** failed.

**Rule 21: Units of measurement, time, money, distance** → **singular** verb.
- Five rupees **is** not enough.
- Ten kilometers **is** a long distance.

**Rule 22: "There + be"** → verb agrees with the **subject** (after "be").
- There **is** a book on the table.
- There **are** two books on the table.

**Rule 23: "It is I / It is you / It is they"** → "It is I" (formal, technically correct) but "It's me" (informal).
- Strictly: It is I who am responsible. (who refers to I)

**Rule 24: Countries, cities, organizations as single units** → **singular** verb.
- The United States **is** a large country.
- The Philippines **is** an archipelago.

## 3. Tenses — The 12 English Tenses

### 3.1 The 3 Times

- **Present**: action happens now.
- **Past**: action happened before.
- **Future**: action will happen.

### 3.2 The 4 States (Aspects)

- **Simple (Indefinite)**: action happens/happened/will happen.
- **Continuous (Progressive)**: action is/was/will be ongoing.
- **Perfect**: action completed by a certain time.
- **Perfect Continuous**: action ongoing from past to a point.

### 3.3 The 12 Tenses

| # | Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|-------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | Present Simple | S + V1/V1+s | He **plays**. |
| 2 | Present Continuous | S + is/am/are + V1+ing | He **is playing**. |
| 3 | Present Perfect | S + has/have + V3 | He **has played**. |
| 4 | Present Perfect Continuous | S + has/have + been + V1+ing | He **has been playing**. |
| 5 | Past Simple | S + V2 | He **played**. |
| 6 | Past Continuous | S + was/were + V1+ing | He **was playing**. |
| 7 | Past Perfect | S + had + V3 | He **had played**. |
| 8 | Past Perfect Continuous | S + had + been + V1+ing | He **had been playing**. |
| 9 | Future Simple | S + will/shall + V1 | He **will play**. |
| 10 | Future Continuous | S + will + be + V1+ing | He **will be playing**. |
| 11 | Future Perfect | S + will + have + V3 | He **will have played**. |
| 12 | Future Perfect Continuous | S + will + have + been + V1+ing | He **will have been playing**. |

### 3.4 Present Tenses — Detailed

**Present Simple (Indefinite):**
- **Habitual action**: I go to school daily.
- **General truth**: The sun rises in the east.
- **Permanent state**: He lives in Delhi.
- **Historical present**: Now Netaji enters the hall.
- **Future arrangement**: The train leaves at 5 PM tomorrow.
- **Time signals**: often, usually, always, never, sometimes, daily, every day.

**Present Continuous:**
- **Action in progress at the moment of speaking**: She is reading a book.
- **Temporary action**: He is working in Chennai this month.
- **Future plan (with time reference)**: I am meeting him tomorrow.
- **Time signals**: now, at present, currently, at the moment.

**Present Perfect:**
- **Action completed at some unspecified past time** (just, recently, already, yet).
- **Action with present relevance**: I have finished my work.
- **Action begun in past and continuing to present**: He has lived here for 10 years.
- **Time signals**: just, already, yet, ever, never, recently, so far, since, for.

**Present Perfect Continuous:**
- **Action started in past, continuing to present, and may continue**: I have been waiting for 2 hours.
- **Time signals**: for, since, all day, all morning, recently, lately.

### 3.5 Past Tenses — Detailed

**Past Simple:**
- **Completed action at definite past time**: I went to school yesterday.
- **Sequence of past events**: He came, saw, and conquered.
- **Time signals**: yesterday, last week, ago, in 1990, when.

**Past Continuous:**
- **Action in progress at a specific past time**: I was reading at 8 PM.
- **Background action**: While I was cooking, he was watching TV.
- **Time signals**: while, when, at that time.

**Past Perfect:**
- **Action completed before another past action**: I had finished my work before he came.
- **The "past of the past"**.
- **Time signals**: before, after, by the time, when (in past context).

**Past Perfect Continuous:**
- **Action ongoing before another past action**: I had been working for 2 hours when he called.
- **Time signals**: for, since, before (in past context).

### 3.6 Future Tenses — Detailed

**Future Simple (will/shall):**
- **Prediction**: It will rain tomorrow.
- **Spontaneous decision**: I'll help you.
- **Promise**: I will be there.
- **Time signals**: tomorrow, next week, in the future.

**Future Continuous:**
- **Action in progress at a specific future time**: I will be sleeping at 11 PM.
- **Time signals**: at this time tomorrow, when (future).

**Future Perfect:**
- **Action completed before a specific future time**: I will have finished by 5 PM.
- **Time signals**: by, by the time, before (future).

**Future Perfect Continuous:**
- **Action ongoing until a specific future time**: By next year, I will have been working here for 10 years.
- **Time signals**: by, by the time (future).

### 3.7 Sequence of Tenses

When reporting speech or combining sentences, tenses follow a logical sequence:

**Direct → Indirect (Tense Backshift):**
- Present Simple → Past Simple: "I am happy" → He said he **was** happy.
- Present Continuous → Past Continuous: "I am working" → He said he **was working**.
- Present Perfect → Past Perfect: "I have finished" → He said he **had finished**.
- Past Simple → Past Perfect: "I went" → He said he **had gone**.
- Will → Would: "I will go" → He said he **would go**.

### 3.8 Time Signal Table

| Time Signal | Common Tense |
|-------------|--------------|
| yesterday, ago, last week | Past Simple |
| now, at the moment | Present Continuous |
| just, already, yet, ever, never | Present Perfect |
| for, since, recently, lately | Present Perfect Continuous |
| while, when (in past) | Past Continuous |
| before, after, by the time (past) | Past Perfect |
| tomorrow, next week, in future | Future Simple |
| by, by the time (future) | Future Perfect |

## 4. Speed-Solving Shortcuts

### 4.1 The "Singular Subject = Singular Verb" Rule

If the subject is **clearly singular** (he, she, it, name), the verb gets **-s/-es** in present tense.
- He **goes**, not "he go".
- She **plays**, not "she play".

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.2 The "Find the Subject First" Trick

For complex SVA, **find the actual subject** first. Ignore prepositional phrases and other modifiers.
- "The box of chocolates **is** on the table." (Subject = "box", not "chocolates")

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.3 The "Closest Subject Wins" Trick

For "either...or / neither...nor", verb agrees with the **nearest subject**.
- Either he **or** they **are** coming. (they → are)

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.4 The "Time Signal → Tense" Trick

Match the **time signal** to the appropriate tense (see §3.8 table).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.5 The "Perfect Tense + Since/For" Trick

Use **Present Perfect** (not Past Simple) with "since/for" when action continues to present.
- "I **have lived** here for 5 years" (not "I lived").

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.6 The "Past of Past = Past Perfect" Trick

When a past action is reported in past context, use **Past Perfect**.
- He said he **had** finished.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.7 The "Future = Will + V1" Trick

For future tense, use **will + base form** (V1), not V2.
- "I will **go**" (not "I will went").

## 5. Classification of Question Types

### 5.1 Type 1 — Simple SVA Error

**Example:** "He don't know" → "He **doesn't** know."
**Solution:** Singular subject → singular verb (doesn't). *Time: 10 sec.* [See §2]

### 5.2 Type 2 — Compound Subject with "And"

**Example:** "Ram and Shyam is coming" → "Ram and Shyam **are** coming."
**Solution:** Two subjects joined by "and" → plural verb. *Time: 10 sec.* [Rule 1]

### 5.3 Type 3 — "Either/Or" SVA

**Example:** "Either he or I are wrong" → "Either he or I **am** wrong."
**Solution:** Nearest subject "I" → singular verb. *Time: 15 sec.* [Rule 3]

### 5.4 Type 4 — Tense Error

**Example:** "I am working here since 2020" → "I **have been working** here since 2020."
**Solution:** "Since 2020" with continuing action → Present Perfect Continuous. *Time: 15 sec.* [See §3]

### 5.5 Type 5 — Time Signal Mismatch

**Example:** "I have went to school yesterday" → "I **went** to school yesterday."
**Solution:** "Yesterday" → Past Simple (V2), not Present Perfect. *Time: 10 sec.* [See §3.4]

### 5.6 Type 6 — Subject Identification

**Example:** "The list of items are on the table" → "The list of items **is** on the table."
**Solution:** Subject = "list" (singular), not "items". *Time: 15 sec.* [See §4.2]

### 5.7 Type 7 — Collective Noun

**Example:** "The team are playing well" → "The team **is** playing well."
**Solution:** "Team" is a singular collective noun. *Time: 10 sec.* [Rule 6]

### 5.8 Type 8 — Sequence of Tenses

**Example:** "He said that he is happy" → "He said that he **was** happy."
**Solution:** Past context → Past Simple. *Time: 15 sec.* [See §3.7]

## 7. Comprehensive Practice Set (20 Questions)

### Set A — Foundation (Q1–Q5)

**Q1.** "He ___ to school daily." (go/goes/going)
**Solution:** Singular third person → "goes". **Answer: goes.** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q2.** "They ___ in the park." (is/are)
**Solution:** Plural subject → "are". **Answer: are.** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q3.** "I ___ a book now." (read/am reading)
**Solution:** Action in progress → "am reading". **Answer: am reading.** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q4.** "She ___ already ___ lunch." (has/have/had/eat/eats/eaten/ate)
**Solution:** "Already" → Present Perfect. "She has already eaten." *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q5.** "He ___ TV when I called." (watch/watched/was watching)
**Solution:** Background action in past → "was watching". **Answer: was watching.** *Time: 10 sec.*

### Set B — Intermediate (Q6–Q13)

**Q6.** "Neither he nor I ___ guilty." (am/is/are)
**Solution:** Nearest subject "I" → "am". **Answer: am.** *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q7.** "The list of items ___ on the table." (is/are)
**Solution:** Subject = "list" (singular). **Answer: is.** *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q8.** "I ___ here since 2020." (live/lived/have lived/am living)
**Solution:** Action continuing from 2020 → "have lived". **Answer: have lived.** *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q9.** "He ___ before she arrived." (left/had left/has left)
**Solution:** Past of past → "had left". **Answer: had left.** *Time: 15 sec.*

**Q10.** "By next year, I ___ here for 10 years." (will work/will have worked/will be working)
**Solution:** Future Perfect with "by" + duration → "will have worked". **Answer: will have worked.** *Time: 30 sec.*

**Q11.** "Each student ___ a book." (have/has)
**Solution:** "Each" is singular → "has". **Answer: has.** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q12.** "The team ___ well." (is/are)
**Solution:** Collective singular → "is". **Answer: is.** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q13.** "Mathematics ___ a difficult subject." (is/are)
**Solution:** "Mathematics" as subject → "is". **Answer: is.** *Time: 5 sec.*

### Set C — Advanced (Q14–Q18)

**Q14.** "He said that he ___ happy." (is/was/has been)
**Solution:** Past context → "was". **Answer: was.** *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q15.** "Many a student ___ failed." (have/has)
**Solution:** "Many a" → singular → "has". **Answer: has.** *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q16.** "A number of students ___ absent." (is/are)
**Solution:** "A number of" → plural → "are". **Answer: are.** *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q17.** "The number of students ___ increasing." (is/are)
**Solution:** "The number of" → singular → "is". **Answer: is.** *Time: 10 sec.*

**Q18.** "I ___ to the market yesterday." (go/went/have gone/had gone)
**Solution:** "Yesterday" → Past Simple → "went". **Answer: went.** *Time: 10 sec.*

### Set D — Challenge (Q19–Q20)

**Q19.** "He is one of the students who ___ passed." (have/has)
**Solution:** "Who" refers to "students" (plural) → "have". **Answer: have.** *Time: 30 sec.* [Rule 14]

**Q20.** "I ___ for 2 hours when he called." (waited/was waiting/had been waiting)
**Solution:** Past action ongoing before another past → Past Perfect Continuous. **Answer: had been waiting.** *Time: 60 sec.* [Rule 8 — Past Perfect Continuous]

2. **Closest subject wins** — for either/or.
3. **Time signal → tense** — match the signal.
4. **Past of past = Past Perfect** — for sequence of tenses.

### 8.4 5 Common Mistakes

1. **Each/Everyone + plural verb** — they're singular.
2. **"A number of" treated as singular** — it's plural.
3. **"Many a" + plural verb** — it's singular.
4. **Past time with Present Perfect** — "yesterday I have gone" is wrong.
5. **"Since 5 years"** — "since" needs point in time.
