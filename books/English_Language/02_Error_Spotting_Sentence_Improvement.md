## 1. Conceptual Foundation

### 1.1 Error Spotting — Question Format

A typical SSC Error Spotting question presents a sentence split into **four segments** (a, b, c, d). One segment contains a grammatical error. The candidate must identify it:

> **"Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. If there is no error, select 'No error'."**
>
> Example:
> Your eldest sister / lives in / a big city, / does she?
> (a) Your eldest sister (b) lives in (c) a big city (d) does she

The 4 sub-framing variations observed in the Competitive Exam Question Bank corpus:
1. "Identify the segment..."
2. "The following sentence has been split into four segments. Identify the segment..."
3. "Find the part of the given sentence that has an error..."
4. "In the given sentence, identify the segment..."

### 1.3 The 7 Most Common Error Categories

From the 443 PYQs analyzed in the Competitive Exam Question Bank corpus, errors cluster into 7 categories:

1. **Subject-Verb Agreement** (most common, ~40% of all errors)
2. **Tense** (~20%)
3. **Preposition** (~15%)
4. **Article** (~8%)
5. **Pronoun/Adjective/Adverb** (~7%)
6. **Question tag** (~5%)
7. **Voice/Narration** (~3%)

### 1.4 Sentence Improvement — Question Format

Sentence Improvement presents a sentence with one part **underlined** and asks the candidate to replace it with the best alternative. Typical options:

> "He is **one of those who never** admits mistakes."
> (a) never admits (b) never admit (c) doesn't admit (d) No improvement

The standard 4-option format: (a) original, (b) correct rewrite, (c) alternate, (d) "No improvement".

### 1.5 Sentence Rearrangement (Para Jumbles)

A jumbled paragraph is presented with sentences labelled A, B, C, D, E. The candidate must find the **logical sequence** that makes a coherent paragraph.

**Standard 4-option format:**
- (a) ABCD (b) ABDC (c) BACD (d) DBAC

### 1.6 The 5 Para-Jumble Solving Heuristics

1. **Topic sentence first.** Often sentence A or the first option starts with an introduction.
2. **Pronouns refer to earlier nouns.** "He", "she", "it", "they" must refer to nouns in earlier sentences.
3. **Cause before effect.** Sentences with "because", "as a result", "therefore" follow their cause.
4. **Chronological order.** Time-marker sentences usually follow time order.
5. **Conclusion last.** Sentences with "thus", "hence", "in conclusion" come last.

### 1.7 Subject-Verb Agreement Master Rules

The **#1 error category** (40% of all errors). Master rules:

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Singular subject + singular verb | "He goes" (not "go") |
| Plural subject + plural verb | "They go" (not "goes") |
| "A number of" + plural noun + plural verb | "A number of students **are** present." |
| "The number of" + singular noun + singular verb | "The number of students **is** increasing." |
| "Each of" / "Either of" / "Neither of" + plural noun + singular verb | "Each of the boys **is** here." |
| "One of" + plural noun + singular verb | "One of the boxes **has** the portrait." |
| "Many a" / "More than one" + singular noun + singular verb | "Many a student **was** absent." |
| Collective noun + singular verb (British) | "The team **is** playing." |
| "As well as / along with / with" — verb agrees with FIRST subject | "Ram, as well as his friends, **is** coming." |
| "Either...or / Neither...nor" — verb agrees with NEAREST subject | "Either he or his friends **are** wrong." (friends is plural → are) |
| Indefinite pronouns (everyone, someone, nobody, each) + singular verb | "Everyone **is** here." |
| "There is" / "There are" — verb agrees with what follows | "There are a number of..." (plural noun → are) |
| Titles, countries, currencies + singular verb | "The United States **is**..." |
| News, mathematics, physics + singular verb | "Mathematics **is** my favorite." |
| Means, wages, news, innings + singular verb | "The means **is** available." |

## 2. Step-by-Step Solving Algorithm

### 2.1 For Error Spotting

```
Step 1: Read the entire sentence for meaning.

Step 2: Identify the SUBJECT (the noun/pronoun doing the action).

Step 3: Identify the VERB (the action or state).

Step 4: Check agreement: Singular subject ↔ Singular verb?

Step 5: Check tense consistency across clauses.

Step 6: Check articles (a/an/the) before nouns.

Step 7: Check prepositions (in/on/at/by/for/with/to/of) after verbs/adjectives.

Step 8: Check pronoun case (subject: I/he/we; object: me/him/us; possessive: my/his/our).

Step 9: Check adjective vs. adverb usage.

Step 10: Eliminate segments that look correct; pick the one that's wrong.
```

### 2.2 For Sentence Improvement

```
Step 1: Read the underlined segment.
Step 2: Identify the grammatical issue:
        - Wrong tense?
        - Wrong preposition?
        - Wrong word form (adjective vs. adverb)?
        - Redundancy / wordiness?
        - Wrong voice?
        - Idiomatic error?

Step 3: Look for the option that fixes the issue WITHOUT changing meaning.

Step 4: If the underlined part is already correct → answer is "(d) No improvement".

Step 5: Be wary of options that change meaning even if grammatically cleaner.
```

### 2.3 For Sentence Rearrangement (Para Jumbles)

```
Step 1: Read all sentences to understand the topic.

Step 2: Identify the topic sentence (usually a general statement).

Step 3: Find sentences that MUST be adjacent (pronoun references, cause-effect).

Step 4: Identify the conclusion sentence (summary, "thus", "hence").

Step 5: Test your sequence against the 4 options; eliminate wrong ones.

Step 6: If two options differ by one sentence's position, decide which fits.
```

### 2.4 The Competitive Exam Question Bank "Find the Error" 4-Step Method

Competitive Exam Question Bank's recommended method:

1. **Read the question tag note first.** Each topic in Competitive Exam Question Bank opens with a "Note" giving the rule.
2. **Read the entire sentence.** Don't skip context.
3. **Check each segment for the specific rule.**
4. **Pick the option that violates the rule.**

### 2.5 The "One of + plural + singular" Pattern

One of the most-tested rules in SSC:

> *"One of + plural subject + singular verb"*

**Example:**
> "Rapid climate warming in the tundra biome **have been** linked to increasing shrub dominance."
> **Error:** "have been" should be "has been" (singular subject: tundra biome).
>

**Worked Example [Competitive Exam Question Bank Q#13]:**
> "One of these / boxes **have** / the portrait / of the heiress."
> **Error:** "boxes have" → "boxes has" (one of + plural = singular verb).
>

## 3. Trap Detection & Error Prevention Guide

### 3.1 Top 15 Error-Spotting Traps

1. **"My sister are" (Subject-Verb).** Singular subject + plural verb. **Trap:** When the subject is far from the verb, students miss it.

2. **"There is a number of medications".** "A number of" → plural verb. "The number of" → singular verb. **Trap:** Students confuse "the number" with "a number".

3. **"Neetu have been waiting".** Singular subject "Neetu" requires "has been". **Trap:** When subject ends in -u or unusual spelling, students mistake it for plural.

4. **"Jiten and Hiru loves".** Two subjects joined by "and" → plural verb. **Trap:** When the conjunction is far from the verb.

5. **"I were writing".** "I" + "were" is SUBJUNCTIVE only ("If I were"). For Past Continuous, "I was". **Trap:** Mixing subjunctive and indicative.

6. **"Each of the boys are"** → "Each of the boys is". **Trap:** "Each of" takes singular.

7. **"A significant number of students volunteers"** → "volunteer" (plural). **Trap:** "Number of" attracts singular.

8. **"The child along with parents are coming"** → "is coming". **Trap:** "Along with" doesn't change singular.

9. **"Either he or his friends is"** → "are" (verb agrees with nearest, which is plural). **Trap:** Defaulting to singular.

10. **"More fast"** → "much faster" or "faster". **Trap:** Using adjective form for adverb (and vice versa).

11. **"He very fast runs"** → "He runs very fast" (adverb before verb). **Trap:** Word order.

12. **"Between he and I"** → "between him and me" (objective case). **Trap:** Always defaulting to "I".

13. **"Theirselves"** → "themselves". **Trap:** Confusing reflexive forms.

14. **"Who" vs. "that".** "Who" for persons; "that" for things/groups. **Trap:** Using "who" for "the community" (use "which" or "that").

15. **"Yours house"** → "your house". **Trap:** Confusing possessive pronoun "yours" with possessive adjective "your".

### 3.2 Top 10 Sentence Improvement Traps

1. **Idiom change.** "Make haste" → "Make hurry" (WRONG; correct is still "Make haste" or "Hurry up"). **Trap:** Tempting to "correct" idioms that are actually correct.

2. **Voice change.** Sometimes the original Active is fine; the options "improve" by switching to Passive (which may or may not be an improvement). **Trap:** Choosing the more complex option.

3. **Word-form change.** "He **runs quick**" → "He runs quickly" (adverb needed). **Trap:** Choosing "He runs quick" → "He runs quicklier" (non-existent form).

4. **Article insertion.** "He is **best** player" → "He is **the best** player". **Trap:** Missing article.

5. **Preposition swap.** "Good **in** mathematics" → "Good **at** mathematics". **Trap:** Random preposition choice.

6. **Tense shift consistency.** "I have finished the work and **will go** home" → "I have finished the work and **am going** home" (closer to logical sequence). **Trap:** Changing tense inconsistently.

7. **Pronoun case.** "Between you and **I**" → "Between you and **me**". **Trap:** Defaulting to "I".

8. **Negative transfer.** "He **did not** go" → "He **didn't** go" (no improvement in formality; usually no improvement needed).

9. **Redundancy removal.** "He **returned back**" → "He returned". **Trap:** "Returned" already implies "back".

10. **"No improvement" trap.** Many students don't pick (d) "No improvement" when the original is already correct.

### 3.3 Top 8 Para Jumble Traps

1. **Pronoun mismatch.** Sentences 2 and 4 both start with "He" → one must come right after the sentence introducing "He".

2. **Time-marker confusion.** "Yesterday" can appear in middle sentences, not just the start.

3. **First-sentence bias.** The option starting with A is often the answer, but not always.

4. **Conclusion marker.** Sentences with "Thus", "Hence", "Therefore" are usually last but sometimes are mid-paragraph transitions.

5. **Plural vs. singular pronouns.** If sentence 1 says "they", the next sentence must continue with "they", not "he/she".

6. **Definite article in continuation.** "The president" (referring back to "a president" introduced earlier) — must follow the introduction.

7. **Inversion in options.** Options ABDC vs. ABCD differ only by the last two sentences; check which sentence is the proper conclusion.

8. **Filler sentences.** Some sentences are decoys that don't fit anywhere in the paragraph.

## 4. Type-Wise Question Bank with Solutions

### Type 1: Question Tag Error

**Solved Example 1:**
> Every Saturday, your mother prepares a pudding, **isn't she?**
> (a) isn't she (b) Every Saturday, your mother (c) Prepares a pudding (d) No error
>
> **Solution:** "Isn't she" should be "doesn't she" (present indefinite, singular, positive sentence → negative tag).
>
> **Answer: (c) Prepares a pudding** (the question tag "isn't she" is wrong)

**Solved Example 2:**
> My friend **never tells** a lie, doesn't he?
> (a) My friend (b) never tells (c) doesn't he (d) a lie
>
> **Solution:** "Never" makes the sentence negative. Question tag must be positive. "Doesn't he" → "does he".
>
> **Answer: (c) doesn't he**

**Solved Example 3:**
> I am suitably dressed for the occasion, **am I?**
> (a) am I (b) I am (c) for the occasion (d) suitably dressed
>
> **Solution:** "am" in positive → tag is "aren't I" (not "am I not").
>
> **Answer: (a) am I**

### Type 2: Subject-Verb Agreement

**Solved Example 4:**
> To treat nightmare disorder, **there is a number of** medications and therapies.
> (a) of medications and therapies (b) To treat nightmare disorder (c) there is a number (d) No error
>
> **Solution:** "A number of" + plural noun → "there are a number of".
>
> **Answer: (c) there is a number**

**Solved Example 5:**
> I am thirty-two years old and **my sister are** fifteen.
> (a) I am thirty-two (b) years old and (c) my sister are fifteen (d) No error
>
> **Solution:** "My sister" is singular; verb should be "is".
>
> **Answer: (c) my sister are fifteen**

**Solved Example 6:**
> Rapid climate warming in the tundra biome **have been linked** to increasing shrub dominance.
> (a) Rapid climate warming in (b) No error (c) the tundra biome have been linked (d) to increasing shrub dominance
>
> **Solution:** "Tundra biome" is singular; verb should be "has been linked".
>
> **Answer: (c) the tundra biome have been linked**

**Solved Example 7:**
> India's claim to be a world leader in pharmaceuticals **are** heavily compromised.
> (a) world leader in pharmaceuticals (b) No error (c) are heavily compromised (d) India's claim to be a
>
> **Solution:** "India's claim" is singular; verb should be "is".
>
> **Answer: (c) are heavily compromised**

**Solved Example 8:**
> One of these boxes **have** the portrait of the heiress.
> (a) of the heiress (b) One of these (c) the portrait (d) boxes have
>
> **Solution:** "One of" + plural noun → singular verb "has".
>
> **Answer: (d) boxes have**

**Solved Example 9:**
> **Neetu have been** waiting for me since 10 o'clock in the morning.
> (a) Neetu have been (b) in the morning (c) since 10 o'clock (d) waiting for me
>
> **Solution:** "Neetu" is singular; verb should be "has been".
>
> **Answer: (a) Neetu have been**

### Type 3: Tense Error

**Solved Example 10:**
> Jiten and Hiru **loves** to eat french fries for lunch.
> (a) Jiten and Hiru (b) loves (c) to eat french fries (d) for lunch
>
> **Solution:** Two subjects joined by "and" → plural verb "love".
>
> **Answer: (b) loves**

**Solved Example 11:**
> **I were** writing this poem last evening.
> (a) I were (b) writing (c) This poem (d) Last evening
>
> **Solution:** "I" + Past Continuous → "I was" (subjunctive "were" only for hypothetical).
>
> **Answer: (a) I were**

### Type 4: Pronoun/Adjective/Adverb Error

**Solved Example 12:**
> She always **invite** me to dinner.
> (a) She always (b) invite me (c) to dinner (d) No error
>
> **Solution:** Singular subject "She" → "invites".
>
> **Answer: (b) invite me**

**Solved Example 13:**
> The photo session included **between he and I**.
> (a) The photo session (b) included (c) between he and I (d) No error
>
> **Solution:** Preposition "between" + objective case "him and me".
>
> **Answer: (c) between he and I**

### Type 5: Sentence Improvement

**Solved Example 14:**
> "He is **one of those who never** admits mistakes."
> (a) never admits (b) never admit (c) doesn't admit (d) No improvement
>
> **Solution:** "Those" is plural; verb must be "admit". "One of those who admit" — the relative pronoun "who" refers to "those" (plural), so verb is plural.
> **Answer: (b) never admit**

### Type 6: Sentence Rearrangement

**Solved Example 15:**
> A. The Indian cricket team won the match.
> B. This was their first victory in the tournament.
> C. The team celebrated with great joy.
> D. The captain credited the bowlers.
>
> Find the correct order.
>
> **Solution:** A (event) → B (significance) → C (consequence) → D (attribution). Order: ABCD.
>

## 5. Timed Practice Sets

### Set A — Warm-Up (5 questions, 30-45 sec each)

**Q1.** "She **don't** like apples."
(a) She (b) don't (c) like apples (d) No error
**Solution:** "She" requires "doesn't".
**Answer: (b) don't** → "doesn't"

**Q2.** "There are **less** students today."
(a) There are (b) less (c) students today (d) No error
**Solution:** Countable noun "students" requires "fewer", not "less".
**Answer: (b) less** → "fewer"

**Q3.** "He **has went** to the market."
(a) He (b) has went (c) to the market (d) No error
**Solution:** "has gone" (Past Participle), not "has went".
**Answer: (b) has went** → "has gone"

**Q4.** "Me and him are friends."
(a) Me (b) and him (c) are friends (d) No error
**Solution:** Subject case required: "He and I".
**Answer: (a) Me** → "He"

**Q5.** "She is **taller than me**."
(a) She is (b) taller than (c) me (d) No error
**Solution:** Subject case: "taller than I".
**Answer: (c) me** → "I" (formal) or keep "me" (informal)

### Set B — Exam Simulation (SSC CGL Tier-II Standard)

**Q6.** "Neither of the candidates **are** eligible."
(a) Neither of the candidates (b) are (c) eligible (d) No error
**Solution:** "Neither of" + plural noun → singular verb "is".
**Answer: (b) are** → "is"

**Q7.** "The sceneries of Kashmir **are** beautiful."
(a) The sceneries of Kashmir (b) are (c) beautiful (d) No error
**Solution:** "Scenery" is uncountable; no plural form.
**Answer: (a) The sceneries** → "The scenery"

**Q8.** "He **has** no any money."
(a) He (b) has (c) no any (d) money
**Solution:** "No" and "any" are both negatives; double negative is wrong. Use "no" or "any".
**Answer: (c) no any** → "no" or "any"

**Q9.** "I **am** working here since 2010."
(a) I (b) am working (c) here (d) since 2010
**Solution:** "Since" + point in time requires Present Perfect, not Continuous.
**Answer: (b) am working** → "have been working"

**Q10.** "He is **more taller** than his brother."
(a) He is (b) more taller (c) than his brother (d) No error
**Solution:** "Taller" is comparative; "more" is redundant.
**Answer: (b) more taller** → "taller"

### Set C — Advanced (IBPS PO Mains Level)

**Q11.** "Each of the students **were given** a book."
(a) Each of the students (b) were given (c) a book (d) No error
**Solution:** "Each of" + plural noun → singular verb "was".
**Answer: (b) were given** → "was given"

**Q12.** "The furniture **are** expensive."
(a) The (b) furniture (c) are (d) expensive
**Solution:** "Furniture" is uncountable; singular verb "is".
**Answer: (c) are** → "is"

**Q13.** "He behaves as if he **was** a king."
(a) He behaves (b) as if (c) he was (d) a king
**Solution:** Subjunctive mood → "he were" (even in present/future hypothetical).
**Answer: (c) he was** → "he were"

**Q14.** "I prefer tea **than** coffee."
(a) I prefer (b) tea (c) than (d) coffee
**Solution:** "Prefer" takes "to", not "than".
**Answer: (c) than** → "to"

**Q15.** "The teacher **asked** the students to open their books."
(a) The teacher (b) asked (c) the students (d) to open their books
**Solution:** Correct. (No improvement)
**Answer: (d) No improvement**

## 6. Quick Revision Sheet

### 6.1 Core Rules in Bullet Form

**Subject-Verb Agreement:**
1. **Singular subject ↔ Singular verb.** "He goes" not "go".
2. **Plural subject ↔ Plural verb.** "They go" not "goes".
3. **"A number of" + plural noun + plural verb.**
4. **"The number of" + singular verb.**
5. **"Each of / Either of / Neither of" + singular verb.**
6. **"One of" + plural noun + singular verb.**
7. **"As well as / along with" → verb agrees with FIRST subject.**
8. **"Either...or / Neither...nor" → verb agrees with NEAREST subject.**
9. **Indefinite pronouns (everyone, each, somebody) + singular verb.**
10. **Collective nouns (team, family) → usually singular.**

**Question Tag:**
1. **Positive sentence → negative tag.**
2. **Negative sentence → positive tag.**
3. **"I am" → "aren't I"** (NOT "amn't I" or "am I not" in standard usage).
4. **Modal "should" → "shouldn't" in tag.**
5. **"Never/None/Nothing" → positive tag.**

**Sentence Improvement:**
1. **Check word form** (adjective vs. adverb).
2. **Check preposition.**
3. **Check article.**
4. **Check tense consistency.**
5. **Check idiom preservation.**

**Para Jumbles:**
1. **Topic sentence first.**
2. **Pronoun references must match.**
3. **Cause before effect.**
4. **Time order (chronology).**
5. **Conclusion last.**

### 6.2 Decision Tree

**Error Spotting:**

| If you see... | Check... |
|---------------|----------|
| "There is/are" | Subject after "there" |
| Subject far from verb | Singular/plural match |
| "Each/Every/Neither" | Singular verb |
| "A number of / The number of" | "A number" = plural; "The number" = singular |
| "Either...or" | Nearest subject |
| Question tag | Positive/negative match |
| "Since/for" | Perfect tense |
| "Was/were" | Subjunctive vs. indicative |

**Sentence Improvement:**

| If you see... | Improve... |
|---------------|-----------|
| Adjective instead of adverb | Change to adverb |
| Missing article | Add a/an/the |
| Wrong preposition | Use correct preposition |
| Wrong tense | Match time marker |
| "No improvement" | Check carefully — original might be correct |

**Para Jumbles:**

| If you see... | Order... |
|---------------|----------|
| "He/She/They" without antecedent | Must follow introduction |
| "Therefore/Thus" | Usually conclusion |
| "Because/Since" | Usually follows cause |
| General statement | Usually first |
| Specific detail | Usually mid-paragraph |

### 6.4 The 14 Subject-Verb Agreement Rules (Quick Reference)

1. Singular subject + singular verb.
2. Plural subject + plural verb.
3. "A number of" → plural verb.
4. "The number of" → singular verb.
5. "Each of / Either of / Neither of" → singular verb.
6. "One of" + plural noun → singular verb.
7. "Many a / More than one" → singular noun + singular verb.
8. Collective noun (British) → singular verb.
9. "As well as / along with / with" → first subject.
10. "Either...or / Neither...nor" → nearest subject.
11. Indefinite pronouns → singular verb.
12. "There is / There are" → verb agrees with what follows.
13. Titles, countries, currencies → singular verb.
14. Means, news, mathematics → singular verb.

### 6.5 The 5 Para-Jumble Connecting Devices

| Device | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Pronoun reference | "He" / "She" / "It" → must refer to earlier noun |
| Definite article | "The president" → refers back to "a president" |
| Time marker | "Yesterday", "Now", "Then" → chronological |
| Cause-Effect | "Because", "Therefore", "Thus" → cause before effect |
| Continuation | "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In addition" → continue previous point |
