## 1. Conceptual Foundation

### 1.1 What is Spelling?

**Spelling** is the correct sequence of letters in a word. SSC/Banking tests commonly misspelled words.

### 1.2 What is Punctuation?

**Punctuation** uses marks (.,;:!?'-—) to clarify meaning.

### 1.3 What are Abbreviations?

An **abbreviation** is a shortened form of a word/phrase. Types: Acronym (NATO), Initialism (FBI), Contraction (can't), Short forms (Mr.).

### 1.4 Why This Topic Matters

- **Spelling**: 1-2 Qs per Tier-I.
- **Punctuation**: tested in sentence improvement.
- **Abbreviations**: tested in GK section as well.
- **Combined**: 3-5 Qs per exam.

## 2. Spelling Rules (Detailed)

### 2.1 The "I Before E" Rule

**Rule:** "I before E, except after C, or when sounded as 'ay' as in 'neighbor' and 'weigh'."

**Examples (I before E):** believe, ceiling, brief, chief, field, shield, priest, niece, fierce, piece.
**Examples (after C):** receive, deceive, conceive, perceive.
**Examples (ay sound):** neighbor, weigh, vein, reign, eight, freight, sleigh.

**Exceptions (memorize):** weird, foreign, seize, leisure, neither, either, height, heir, foreign, sovereign.

### 2.3 Doubling the Final Consonant

**Rule:** CVC pattern (single consonant + single vowel + single consonant) — double the consonant when adding a vowel-starting suffix.

**Examples:**
- run → runn**ing**, runn**er**
- big → bigg**er**, bigg**est**
- stop → stopp**ed**, stopp**ing**
- get → gett**ing**
- begin → beginn**ing**
- prefer → preferr**ed**, preferr**ing**
- admit → admitt**ed**
- occur → occurr**ed**, occurr**ing**
- occur → occurr**ence** (after double r)

**Exceptions:**
- Stress not on last syllable: visit → visiting, happen → happening, open → opening.
- Words ending in w, x, y: show → showing, fix → fixed, play → playing.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 2.4 Dropping the Final 'E'

**Rule:** When adding a vowel-starting suffix, drop the silent 'e'.

**Examples:** make → making, hope → hoped, live → liver, create → creation, decide → decided, move → moving.

**Exceptions (KEEP the e):**
- Words ending in **ce, ge, se** (soft sound): notice → noticeable, change → changeable, please → pleasant.
- Words ending in **oe, ee, ye**: see → seeing, agree → agreement, dye → dyeing.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 2.5 Changing 'Y' to 'I'

**Rule:** Consonant + y, then add a non-i suffix → y becomes i.

**Examples:** happy → happiness, easy → easier, beauty → beautiful, busy → busily, carry → carried.

**Exceptions (KEEP y):**
- Adding -ing: study → studying, carry → carrying.
- Vowel + y: play → played, enjoy → enjoyed.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 2.6 Forming Plurals

**Standard:** Add -s (cat → cats).

**Special cases:**
- **-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z**: Add -es (bus → buses, box → boxes, church → churches, buzz → buzzes).
- **Consonant + y**: y → i + -es (city → cities, baby → babies).
- **-f or -fe**: Sometimes -ves (leaf → leaves, knife → knives), sometimes -s (roof → roofs, chief → chiefs).
- **Irregular plurals:** man → men, woman → women, child → children, mouse → mice, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, ox → oxen.
- **Unchanged plurals:** sheep → sheep, deer → deer, fish → fish, aircraft → aircraft, series → series, species → species.
- **Foreign plurals:** analysis → analyses, criterion → criteria, phenomenon → phenomena, nucleus → nuclei, cactus → cacti, fungus → fungi, syllabus → syllabi (or syllabuses), cactus → cacti.

## 3. Punctuation Rules

### 3.1 Period (.)

**Uses:**
- End of a declarative sentence: "I went home."
- End of an imperative (mild): "Please sit down."
- After abbreviations: "Mr.", "Dr.", "etc.", "e.g.", "i.e."
- Decimal point: "3.14"

### 3.2 Comma (,)

**Uses:**
- Between items in a list: "I bought apples, oranges, and bananas."
- After introductory phrases: "After the meeting, we left."
- Around appositives: "My friend, the doctor, helped me."
- Before coordinating conjunctions in compound sentences: "I went, but he stayed."
- After "Yes", "No", "However", "Therefore" at sentence start.
- In dates: "March 5, 2024" (American) or "5 March 2024" (British).
- In numbers: "1,000,000" (with commas every 3 digits from right).
- In salutations: "Dear John," / "Sincerely,".

### 3.3 Question Mark (?)

**Use:** At the end of a direct question.
- "Where are you going?"
- "Did you finish?"
- NOT used in indirect questions: "He asked where I was going." (no ?)

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.4 Exclamation Mark (!)

**Uses:**
- End of exclamatory sentence: "What a beautiful day!"
- End of interjection: "Oh! Wow!"
- After emphatic statement: "I can't believe it!"

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.5 Colon (:)

**Uses:**
- Before a list: "I need: milk, eggs, and bread."
- After salutation in formal letters: "Dear Sir:"
- To introduce explanation: "He had one goal: to win."
- In time notation: "3:30 PM".

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.6 Semicolon (;)

**Uses:**
- Between independent clauses without conjunction: "I went home; he stayed."
- Between items in a complex list: "Speakers were: John, the manager; Mary, the engineer; and Bob, the designer."

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.7 Apostrophe (')

**Uses:**
- Possessive: "John's book" (singular), "the students' books" (plural).
- Contractions: "don't" (do not), "won't" (will not), "it's" (it is), "they're" (they are).
- NOT used for plurals: "the 1990s" (not 1990's).
- Exception: Single letters or numbers as plurals: "Mind your p's and q's."

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.8 Quotation Marks (" " or ' ')

**Uses:**
- Direct speech: "He said, 'I am happy.'"
- Titles of short works: articles, poems, short stories, songs.
- Special terms or emphasis: "The 'free' offer wasn't really free."

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.9 Hyphen (-)

**Uses:**
- Compound words: "mother-in-law", "well-known", "high-speed".
- Between prefixes and proper nouns: "anti-American", "pre-1947".
- In numbers 21-99: "twenty-one", "ninety-nine".
- To avoid confusion: "re-cover" vs "recover", "reform" vs "re-form".

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.10 Dash (— or --)

**Uses:**
- To indicate a break in thought: "I was thinking — well, never mind."
- To emphasize or set off: "He had one quality — courage."

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.11 Parentheses ( )

**Uses:**
- Additional information: "He (the manager) signed the document."
- Acronyms: "The United Nations (UN) was founded in 1945."

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 3.12 Capitalization Rules

- First word of a sentence.
- Proper nouns (names, places).
- Days, months, holidays.
- Languages, nationalities, religions.
- Titles before names: "President Lincoln".
- "I" (the pronoun).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

## 5. Speed-Solving Shortcuts

"I before E, except after C, or when sounded as 'ay' as in 'neighbor' and 'weigh'."

### 5.4 The "Homophone" Test

For confusable pairs, memorize the meaning difference (accept/except, affect/effect, principal/principle).

Capitalize: First word, proper nouns, "I", days, months, languages, titles.

Apostrophe = possessive (John's) or contraction (don't). NOT for plurals (1990s, not 1990's).

## 6. Question Types

### 6.1 Type 1 — Spelling Identification

**Example:** Find correct spelling:
(a) Recieve (b) Receive (c) Reciece (d) Receeve
**Solution:** "Receive" — I before E except after C. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 10 sec.* [§2.1]

### 6.2 Type 2 — Spelling Correction

**Example:** Find misspelled:
(a) Definitely (b) Seperate (c) Embarrass (d) Accomodate
**Solution:** All except (a) are misspelled. **Answer: (b) or (d).** *Time: 10 sec.* [§2.8]

### 6.3 Type 3 — Punctuation

**Example:** "John said he is happy." Should be:
(a) John said, "he is happy." (b) John said "he is happy." (c) John, said he is happy. (d) No change
**Solution:** Direct quote needs comma and quotes. **Answer: (a).** *Time: 15 sec.* [§3.8]

### 6.4 Type 4 — Capitalization

**Example:** "i went to delhi on monday." Corrected:
(a) I went to delhi on Monday. (b) I went to Delhi on Monday. (c) i went to Delhi on monday. (d) I went to Delhi on monday.
**Solution:** "I" capitalized, "Delhi" capitalized, "Monday" capitalized. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 15 sec.* [§3.12]

### 6.5 Type 5 — Abbreviation

**Example:** "Dr." stands for:
(a) Direct (b) Doctor (c) Director (d) Drive
**Solution:** "Dr." = Doctor. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 5 sec.* [§4.1]

### 6.6 Type 6 — Contraction

**Example:** "It's" stands for:
(a) Its (b) It is / It has (c) Itself (d) Itself
**Solution:** "It's" = It is / It has. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 5 sec.* [§4.5]

### 6.7 Type 7 — Multiple Errors

**Example:** "i havent recieved the letter." Errors:
(a) i (b) havent (c) recieved (d) All of these
**Solution:** "i" → "I", "havent" → "haven't", "recieved" → "received". **Answer: (d).** *Time: 30 sec.* [Combined]

**Solution:** United Nations. **Answer: (a).** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q17.** "i.e." means:
(a) For example (b) That is (c) And so on (d) Namely
**Solution:** "id est" = That is. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q18.** "e.g." means:
(a) For example (b) That is (c) And so on (d) Namely
**Solution:** "exempli gratia" = For example. **Answer: (a).** *Time: 5 sec.*

### Set D — Contractions (Q19–Q20)

**Q19.** "It's" is a contraction of:
(a) Its (b) It is / It has (c) Itself (d) Its
**Solution:** It is / It has. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 5 sec.*

**Q20.** "Can't" is a contraction of:

**Solution:** Cannot (one word, but "can not" also correct in two). **Answer: (c).** *Time: 5 sec.*
