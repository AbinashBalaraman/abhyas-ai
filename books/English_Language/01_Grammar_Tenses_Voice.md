# Chapter 1: Grammar — Tenses, Voice, Narration

> **Chapter Overview:** Tenses, Voice (Active-Passive), and Narration (Direct-Indirect Speech) are the **three pillars of English grammar** tested in every SSC/Banking exam. Together they account for **15-25 questions per SSC CGL Tier-II**, **10-15 questions in SSC CHSL Tier-I**, **8-12 in IBPS PO/SO Mains**, and **5-8 in SSC MTS**. This chapter provides the Objective English rule-framework, the 12-tense system, the 14-rule Voice transformation system, and the Narration conversion rules.
>
> **Comprehensive exam-aligned study material covering standard syllabi for SSC, RRB, Banking, and State PSC examinations.

## 1. Conceptual Foundation

### 1.1 The Tense System: Time × State

A verb in English operates on **two axes**:

1. **Time of action** (Tense): Past / Present / Future
2. **State of action** (Function): Indefinite / Continuous / Perfect / Perfect Continuous

This gives the canonical **12 tenses**:

| Time | Indefinite | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
|------|------------|------------|---------|---------------------|
| **Present** | Simple Present | Present Continuous | Present Perfect | Present Perfect Continuous |
| **Past** | Simple Past | Past Continuous | Past Perfect | Past Perfect Continuous |
| **Future** | Simple Future | Future Continuous | Future Perfect | Future Perfect Continuous |

### 1.2 The Function of Tenses (Objective English's Frame)

Objective English explicitly distinguishes **Time** from **Function**:

> *"A tense may be defined as that form of a verb which indicates the time and the state of an action or event."*

**Worked example:**

> (i) I **write** letters regularly. — Present Indefinite, **Habitual function**
> (ii) I **am writing** a letter. — Present Continuous, **Progressive function**
> (iii) I **have just written** a letter. — Present Perfect, **Preceding function**
> (iv) I **have been writing** a letter for some time. — Present Perfect Continuous, **Time-expression function**

### 1.3 The 4 Functions (Indefinite / Continuous / Perfect / Perfect Continuous)

| Function | Aspect | Use | Adverbs |
|----------|--------|-----|---------|
| **Indefinite** (Simple) | No aspect | Habit, fact, permanent | often, usually, never, always, sometimes, generally, daily |
| **Continuous** | Imperfective (-ing) | Action in progress at the time | now, at present, still, currently |
| **Perfect** | Perfective (have + V3) | Action completed before reference time | just, already, yet, recently, ever, never |
| **Perfect Continuous** | Both | Action started in past, continues | for, since, how long, all day |

### 1.4 Stative (Non-Progressive) Verbs

Some verbs **do not admit progressive action**. These are called **stative** or **non-progressive** verbs:

| Category | Verbs |
|----------|-------|
| **Perception** | see, taste, smell, hear, prefer, please |
| **Thinking** | think, know, mean, mind |
| **Possession** | own, have, belong, comprise, possess, contain |
| **Feelings / State of Mind** | believe, like, love, want, wish, desire, hate |
| **General** | look, seem, appear, affect, resemble, cost, require, stand, face, become |

**Comparative examples (Incorrect vs. Correct):**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| He **is owning** a car. | He **owns** a car. |
| We **are hearing** the bell. | We **hear** the bell. |
| This house **is belonging** to me. | This house **belongs** to me. |
| I **am not hating** him. | I **don't hate** him. |
| Are you **forgetting** my name? | Have you **forgotten** my name? |
| I **am not meaning** this. | I **don't mean** this. |
| I **am having** no house to live in. | I **have** no house to live in. |
| The temple **is standing** in the heart of the city. | The temple **stands** in the heart of the city. |
| The book **is containing** good subject-matter. | The book **contains** good subject-matter. |

### 1.5 Voice — Overview

**Voice** indicates whether the **subject performs** the action (Active) or **receives** it (Passive):

- **Active voice:** Subject does the action. "Ram **kicked** the ball."
- **Passive voice:** Subject is acted upon. "The ball **was kicked** by Ram."

**The 14 Rules of Voice transformation** are Objective English's signature treatment.

### 1.6 The 14 Rules of Voice (Objective English)

| Rule | Description | Active | Passive |
|------|-------------|--------|---------|
| **I** | Simple Present / Past | He **writes** a letter. | A letter **is written** by him. |
| **II** | Future | He **will write** a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |
| **III** | Modals | He **can write** a letter. | A letter **can be written** by him. |
| **IV** | Present/Past Continuous | He **is writing** a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| **V** | Perfect Tenses | He **has written** a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| **VI** | Infinitive | To **write** a letter is his duty. | Writing a letter is his duty. |
| **VII** | Participle / Gerund | Writing a letter, he went. | (no clean passive) |
| **VIII** | Imperative | Write a letter. | Let a letter be written. |
| **IX** | "Let" | Let him write a letter. | Let him write a letter. (let = exception) |
| **X** | Two objects (give, send, etc.) | He gave me a book. | I was given a book by him. / A book was given to me by him. |
| **XI** | Two objects + complement | They made him king. | He was made king by them. |
| **XII** | By/at/with/to swap | The room is filled with smoke. | Smoke fills the room. |
| **XIII** | Infinitive with preposition | He insisted on helping her. | Helping her was insisted on by him. |
| **XIV** | Bare infinitive → to-infinitive (let, bid, make, help, feel, see, watch, hear) | I made her sing. | She was made to sing. |

### 1.7 The Critical Rule XIV — Bare Infinitive → To-Infinitive

The verbs **let, bid, make, help, feel, see, watch, hear** take a **bare infinitive** (without "to") in active voice. In passive, they take the **to-infinitive**:

> *"The Verbs, 'Let, bid, make, help, feel, see, watch, hear', are used with direct infinitive (without to) in active voice. In passive voice these verbs are used with Infinitive (to + verb). 'Let' is an exception. 'Let' is followed by direct infinitive both in active & passive voice sentences."*

**Examples:**

| Active | Passive |
|--------|---------|
| I **bade him go**. | He **was bidden to go**. |
| I have made her sing a song. | She has been made to sing a song. |
| She let me go. | I was let go by her. |
| I saw him cross the road. | He was seen to cross the road. |

**Note:** "Let" is an exception — both active and passive use bare infinitive.

### 1.8 Narration — Direct vs. Indirect Speech

**Direct Speech** quotes the speaker's exact words. **Indirect Speech** reports what was said without quoting.

**Example:**
- Direct: Ram said, "I am tired."
- Indirect: Ram said (that) he was tired.

**The 4-step Narration conversion:**

1. **Remove comma and quotation marks; add "that".**
2. **Change pronouns** (I → he/she, we → they, my → his/her, our → their).
3. **Back-shift tense** (if reporting verb is past):
   - Present → Past
   - Past → Past Perfect
   - Present Perfect → Past Perfect
   - Future → Present Conditional (would/should)
   - Present Continuous → Past Continuous
4. **Change time/place expressions** (today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, now → then).

### 1.9 The 12-Tense Back-Shift Table

| Direct | Indirect (back-shifted) |
|--------|-------------------------|
| Simple Present "goes" | Simple Past "went" |
| Present Continuous "is going" | Past Continuous "was going" |
| Present Perfect "has gone" | Past Perfect "had gone" |
| Present Perfect Continuous "has been going" | Past Perfect Continuous "had been going" |
| Simple Past "went" | Past Perfect "had gone" |
| Past Continuous "was going" | Past Perfect Continuous "had been going" |
| Past Perfect "had gone" | Past Perfect "had gone" (no change) |
| Future "will go" | Conditional "would go" |
| Modal "can" | "could" |
| Modal "may" | "might" |
| Modal "must" | "had to" / "must" |
| Modal "should" | "should" (sometimes) |

## 2. Step-by-Step Solving Algorithm

### 2.1 For Tense Correction

```
Step 1: Identify the time marker in the sentence.
        (today, yesterday, ago, since, for, tomorrow, etc.)

Step 2: Match the time marker to the correct tense:
        - "now" / "currently" → Present Continuous / Present Perfect
        - "yesterday" / "ago" → Simple Past
        - "since" / "for" → Perfect tenses
        - "tomorrow" / "next week" → Future
        - "always" / "usually" → Simple Present

Step 3: Check function (state of action):
        - Habit / Fact → Simple Present
        - In progress → Continuous
        - Completed → Perfect

Step 4: If the verb is stative (own, know, like...), use Simple, not Continuous.
```

### 2.2 For Voice Conversion

```
Step 1: Identify the object of the active sentence (will become the subject of passive).

Step 2: Determine the tense and verb form:
        - Simple Present → am/is/are + V3
        - Simple Past → was/were + V3
        - Future → will be + V3
        - Modal → modal + be + V3
        - Continuous → is being / was being + V3
        - Perfect → has/have/had been + V3

Step 3: Apply auxiliary verb matching the new subject (singular/plural).

Step 4: Add "by + agent" if the agent is important.

Step 5: For Rule XIV (let, make, etc.), convert bare infinitive to "to-infinitive" in passive.
```

### 2.3 For Narration Conversion

```
Step 1: Change the reporting verb's tense (typically "said" stays "said"; past-tense reporting verb triggers back-shift).

Step 2: Add "that" (optional but recommended).

Step 3: Change pronouns to third person (matching the subject of reporting verb).

Step 4: Back-shift the tense (use the 12-tense back-shift table).

Step 5: Convert time/place:
        today → that day
        tonight → that night
        tomorrow → the next day / the following day
        yesterday → the previous day / the day before
        now → then
        here → there
        this → that
        these → those
        ago → before

Step 6: Handle interrogatives (questions) and imperatives separately.
        - Questions: Use "if" / "whether" for yes/no; "asked + question word" for wh-.
        - Imperatives: Use "to + V1" (told/asked/ordered + object + to + V1).
```

### 2.4 The Modals Cheat Sheet (Objective English)

| Modal | Meaning | Past Form |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| **may** | permission / possibility | might |
| **can** | ability / permission | could |
| **should** | obligation / advice | — |
| **must** | necessity | had to |
| **ought to** | moral duty | — |
| **used to** | past habit | — |
| **would** | past habit / polite request | — |
| **need** | necessity (negative/question) | — |
| **dare** | courage (negative/question) | — |

**The "have" forms (modal + have + V3) — past time:**

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **may have V3** | past possibility |
| **might have V3** | unrealised past possibility |
| **could have V3** | past ability (unrealised) |
| **should have V3** | past obligation (unfulfilled) |
| **must have V3** | past deduction |
| **ought to have V3** | past neglected duty |
| **need not have V3** | unnecessary action done |

## 3. Trap Detection & Error Prevention Guide

### 3.1 Top 12 Tense Traps

1. **Stative verb in continuous.** "I am owning a car" is WRONG. Stative verbs don't take -ing.

2. **"Since" vs. "For".** "Since" + point in time (since 2010). "For" + duration (for 5 years). Mixing these is the most common error.

3. **Present Perfect vs. Simple Past with "yesterday".** "I have seen him yesterday" is WRONG. Use "I saw him yesterday" (Simple Past with point-of-time adverb).

4. **Future arrangement vs. Present.** "I am leaving tomorrow" (Present Continuous for future arrangement) is correct. "I leave tomorrow" (Simple Present for future) is also correct. But "I will leave tomorrow" (will-future) is also correct. Multiple correct forms exist for future.

5. **"Always" with continuous.** "She is always coming late" implies annoyance (different from "She always comes late" which is neutral).

6. **Perfect Continuous vs. Perfect.** "I have been waiting for 2 hours" (still waiting). "I have waited for 2 hours" (still waiting OR completed). Continuous emphasizes continuity.

7. **Past Continuous + "while".** "While I was reading, the phone rang" — both actions in past, one in progress.

8. **"Will" vs. "Shall".** In modern English, "will" is used for all persons in statements. "Shall" is reserved for questions ("Shall I help?") and formal invitations.

9. **"Used to" vs. "Would".** Both indicate past habit. "Used to" for states ("I used to know him"). "Would" for actions only.

10. **Tense after "as if / as though".** Use Past tense or Past Perfect for unreal conditions. "He behaves as if he **were** rich" (Subjunctive).

11. **"Did + V1" for emphasis.** "I **did** go" (emphatic Simple Past).

12. **Historical Present.** "Now Netaji enters and addresses..." — present tense for dramatic narrative (literary).

### 3.2 Top 10 Voice Traps

1. **Intransitive verbs cannot be passivized.** "He **slept**" — no passive. "He **ran**" — no passive. Passive requires a transitive verb (with object).

2. **Stative verb in continuous passive.** "The house is being belonged to" — WRONG. "Belong" is stative.

3. **Imperative Rule VIII wrong transformation.** "Open the door" → "Let the door be opened" (NOT "The door is opened").

4. **Rule IX (Let) wrong transformation.** "Let him go" → "Let him go" (same in passive).

5. **Rule X — Two-object ambiguity.** "He gave me a book" → "I was given a book" (person-object as subject) OR "A book was given to me" (thing-object as subject). Both are correct.

6. **Rule X — "Say" takes only one object; "Tell" takes two.** "He said me a story" WRONG (correct: "He told me a story"). But in passive: "A story was told to me" is correct.

7. **Modal passive without "be".** "He can write" → "It can be written" (NOT "It can written").

8. **Perfect Continuous has no passive.** "He has been writing" → "Writing has been going on" (cumbersome; often not used).

9. **"Be" as main verb.** "He is a teacher" → "A teacher is he" (archaic; modern English often avoids this passive).

10. **Rule XIV — bare infinitive trap.** "Make" takes bare infinitive in active but "to-infinitive" in passive. "I made her sing" → "She was made to sing" (NOT "She was made sing").

### 3.3 Top 10 Narration Traps

1. **No back-shift for universal truths.** "He said, 'The earth revolves around the sun'" → "He said that the earth revolves around the sun" (no back-shift).

2. **No back-shift for present/past habitual.** "He said, 'I go to school daily'" → "He said that he went to school daily" — back-shift because habitual is shifted. BUT "He said, 'I am a doctor'" → "He said that he is a doctor" (if still true).

3. **"Said" vs. "Told".** "He said me" WRONG. "He told me" CORRECT. "Said" takes only indirect object (no "to me"); "Told" takes direct object (told me).

4. **Interrogative conversion.** "He said, 'Are you coming?'" → "He asked me if I was coming." (NOT "He asked me that...").

5. **Imperative conversion.** "He said, 'Open the door'" → "He told/ordered me to open the door" (NOT "He said me to open the door").

6. **Exclamatory conversion.** "He said, 'What a beautiful flower!'" → "He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful flower" (or "He exclaimed with admiration that...").

7. **"Let" in indirect speech.** "He said, 'Let us go'" → "He suggested that they should go" (NOT "He said that they should let them go").

8. **"Should/must" in indirect.** "He said, 'You should study'" → "He advised me to study" (or "He said that I should study"). Both correct.

9. **Time expressions — "ago" vs. "before".** "He said, 'I came a week ago'" → "He said that he had come a week before".

10. **"Today / Tonight" conversion.** "He said, 'I will come today'" → "He said that he would come that day" (NOT "today").

## 4. Type-Wise Question Bank with Solutions

### Type 1: Tense Correction

**Solved Example 1:**
> Spot the error: "She **is often coming** to me on Sundays."
>
> **Solution:** "Often comes" (Simple Present for habitual) is correct.
> **Correct:** She often comes to me on Sundays.
>

**Solved Example 2:**
> Spot the error: "I **have met** your brother **yesterday**."
>
> **Solution:** "Have met" (Present Perfect) cannot combine with "yesterday" (point of time in past). Use Simple Past.
> **Correct:** I met your brother yesterday.
>

### Type 2: Voice Conversion (Rule I — Simple)

**Solved Example 3:**
> Active: "The cat killed the mouse." → Passive?
>
> **Solution:** Object = "the mouse". Tense = Simple Past. Passive: "The mouse was killed by the cat."
> **Answer: The mouse was killed by the cat.**
>

### Type 3: Voice Conversion (Rule XIV — Bare Infinitive)

**Solved Example 4:**
> Active: "I **bade him go**." → Passive?
>
> **Solution:** Per Rule XIV, "bid" takes bare infinitive in active, "to-infinitive" in passive.
> **Answer: He was bidden to go.**
>

**Solved Example 5:**
> Active: "She **let me go**." → Passive?
>
> **Solution:** "Let" is the exception — bare infinitive in BOTH active and passive.
> **Answer: I was let go by her.**
>

### Type 4: Voice Conversion (Imperative)

**Solved Example 6:**
> Active: "**Open the door**." → Passive?
>
> **Solution:** Per Rule VIII, imperative → "Let + object + be + V3".
> **Answer: Let the door be opened.**
>

### Type 5: Voice Conversion (Modal)

**Solved Example 7:**
> Active: "He **can write** a letter." → Passive?
>
> **Solution:** Per Rule III, modal passive = modal + be + V3.
> **Answer: A letter can be written by him.**
>

### Type 6: Voice Conversion (Two-Object)

**Solved Example 8:**
> Active: "He **gave me** a book." → Passive?
>
> **Solution:** Per Rule X, two objects → two possible passives:
> - "I was given a book by him." (person-object as subject)
> - "A book was given to me by him." (thing-object as subject)
>

### Type 7: Narration (Statement)

**Solved Example 9:**
> Direct: Ram said, "**I am tired**." → Indirect?
>
> **Solution:**
> 1. Remove comma, add "that".
> 2. Pronoun: I → he (subject of "said" is Ram, 3rd person).
> 3. Back-shift: Present Continuous → Past Continuous.
> 4. No time/place to change.
> **Answer: Ram said that he was tired.**

### Type 8: Narration (Question)

**Solved Example 10:**
> Direct: He said to me, "**Are you coming**?" → Indirect?
>
> **Solution:**
> 1. Reporting verb "said" → "asked" (for question).
> 2. Yes/no question → "if" / "whether".
> 3. Pronoun: you → I (me is indirect object).
> 4. Back-shift: Present Continuous → Past Continuous.
> **Answer: He asked me if I was coming.**

### Type 9: Narration (Imperative)

**Solved Example 11:**
> Direct: He said to me, "**Please help** me." → Indirect?
>
> **Solution:**
> 1. Reporting verb → "requested" / "asked".
> 2. Imperative → "to + V1".
> 3. Pronoun: me → him.
> **Answer: He requested me to help him.**

### Type 10: Narration (Exclamatory)

**Solved Example 12:**
> Direct: He said, "**What a beautiful flower!**" → Indirect?
>
> **Solution:**
> 1. Reporting verb → "exclaimed".
> 2. Exclamatory → "that + it was a very + adjective + noun".
> **Answer: He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful flower.**

## 5. Timed Practice Sets

### Set A — Warm-Up (5 questions, 30-45 sec each)

**Q1.** Correct form: "He ___ (own) a car."
(a) is owning (b) owns (c) has owning (d) own
**Solution:** "Own" is stative; use Simple Present.
**Answer: (b) owns**

**Q2.** Passive: "She writes a letter."
(a) A letter is written by her. (b) A letter is being written by her. (c) A letter was written by her. (d) A letter has been written by her.
**Solution:** Simple Present → am/is/are + V3.
**Answer: (a) A letter is written by her.**

**Q3.** Indirect: He said, "I am happy."
(a) He said that he is happy. (b) He said that he was happy. (c) He says that he is happy. (d) He said that I was happy.
**Solution:** Back-shift Present Continuous to Past Continuous; pronoun I → he.
**Answer: (b) He said that he was happy.**

**Q4.** Passive: "They made him king."
(a) He was made king by them. (b) He was made to king by them. (c) He is made king by them. (d) He has been made king.
**Solution:** Rule XI — Two objects + complement. "Made" + complement → keep complement in passive.
**Answer: (a) He was made king by them.**

**Q5.** Indirect: He said, "The sun rises in the East."
(a) He said that the sun rose in the East. (b) He said that the sun rises in the East. (c) He says the sun rises in the East. (d) He said that the sun will rise in the East.
**Solution:** Universal truth — NO back-shift.
**Answer: (b) He said that the sun rises in the East.**

### Set B — Exam Simulation (SSC CGL Tier-II Standard)

**Q6.** Spot the error: "I **am knowing** him for ten years."
(a) am knowing (b) him (c) for ten years (d) No error
**Solution:** "Know" is stative; use Present Perfect.
**Answer: (a) am knowing** → "have known"

**Q7.** Passive: "I have finished the work."
(a) The work has been finished by me. (b) The work is finished by me. (c) The work was finished by me. (d) The work had been finished by me.
**Solution:** Present Perfect → has/have + been + V3.
**Answer: (a) The work has been finished by me.**

**Q8.** Indirect: She said, "I will come tomorrow."
(a) She said that she will come tomorrow. (b) She said that she would come the next day. (c) She said that she would come tomorrow. (d) She said that she will come the next day.
**Solution:** Back-shift will → would; tomorrow → the next day.
**Answer: (b) She said that she would come the next day.**

**Q9.** Passive: "Make hay while the sun shines."
(a) Let the hay be made while the sun shines. (b) Let hay be made while the sun shines. (c) Hay is made while the sun shines. (d) Hay was made while the sun shines.
**Solution:** Imperative → "Let + object + be + V3".
**Answer: (b) Let hay be made while the sun shines.**

**Q10.** Spot the error: "He **is having** two cars."
(a) is having (b) two (c) cars (d) No error
**Solution:** "Have" (possession) is stative; use Simple Present.
**Answer: (a) is having** → "has"

### Set C — Advanced (IBPS PO Mains Level)

**Q11.** Passive: "He has been writing a letter since morning."
(a) A letter has been being written by him since morning. (b) A letter has been written by him since morning. (c) A letter is being written by him since morning. (d) Awkward; avoid.
**Solution:** Perfect Continuous passive is awkward. Standard answer is to recast as Simple Perfect passive.
**Answer: (d) Awkward; avoid (or recast)**

**Q12.** Indirect: He said, "I wish I were a bird."
(a) He said that he wished he was a bird. (b) He said that he wished he were a bird. (c) He said that he wishes he were a bird. (d) He said that I wished I were a bird.
**Solution:** "Wish + Subjunctive were" — back-shift wish to wished but keep were (Subjunctive).
**Answer: (b) He said that he wished he were a bird.**

**Q13.** Passive: "She was seen crossing the road."
(a) She crossed the road. (b) People saw her crossing the road. (c) She was seen to cross the road. (d) She has been seen crossing the road.
**Solution:** Rule XIV — "see + bare infinitive" active; "seen + to infinitive" passive.
**Answer: (c) She was seen to cross the road.**

**Q14.** Indirect: She said, "If I were a bird, I would fly."
(a) She said that if she were a bird, she would fly. (b) She said that if she was a bird, she would fly. (c) She said that if she had been a bird, she would have flown. (d) She said that if she is a bird, she will fly.
**Solution:** Unreal conditional (Subjunctive + would) — no back-shift of "were" or "would".
**Answer: (a) She said that if she were a bird, she would fly.**

**Q15.** Spot the error: "He **does not know** to swim."
(a) does not know (b) to swim (c) He (d) No error
**Solution:** "Know + how to" or "know how to" with infinitive.
**Answer: (b) to swim** → "how to swim"

## 6. Quick Revision Sheet

### 6.1 Core Rules in Bullet Form

**Tenses:**
1. **Simple Present:** He goes (habit / fact).
2. **Present Continuous:** He is going (in progress).
3. **Present Perfect:** He has gone (completed; connection to present).
4. **Present Perfect Continuous:** He has been going (started past, continues).
5. **Simple Past:** He went (completed past action).
6. **Past Continuous:** He was going (in progress in past).
7. **Past Perfect:** He had gone (completed before another past).
8. **Stative verbs:** own, know, like, believe → NO continuous.
9. **"Since" + point in time.** "For" + duration.
10. **"Yesterday" → Simple Past** (not Present Perfect).

**Voice:**
1. **Active → Passive:** Object of active = subject of passive.
2. **Simple Present → am/is/are + V3.**
3. **Future → will be + V3.**
4. **Modal → modal + be + V3.**
5. **Continuous → is/was being + V3.**
6. **Perfect → has/have/had been + V3.**
7. **Imperative → Let + obj + be + V3.**
8. **Two-object → two possible passives** (person or thing as subject).
9. **Rule XIV:** let/make/help/feel/see/watch/hear/bid — bare infinitive active, to-infinitive passive.
10. **Intransitive verbs have NO passive.**

**Narration:**
1. **Remove quotes; add "that".**
2. **Change pronouns to 3rd person matching subject.**
3. **Back-shift tense** (if reporting verb is past).
4. **Universal truths — NO back-shift.**
5. **Yes/no question → "asked + if/whether".**
6. **Imperative → "told/asked + obj + to + V1".**
7. **Exclamatory → "exclaimed + that +..."**
8. **Today → that day; tomorrow → the next day; here → there.**
9. **"Said" → no object.** "Told" → takes object.
10. **Past perfect stays past perfect** (no back-shift).

### 6.2 Decision Tree

**Tense:**
| If you see... | Use... |
|---------------|--------|
| "yesterday", "ago", "last week" | Simple Past |
| "now", "currently", "at present" | Present Continuous |
| "since", "for", "just", "already" | Present Perfect |
| "while + action" | Past Continuous |
| "always" + stative verb | Simple Present (NOT Continuous) |

**Voice:**
| If you see... | Then... |
|---------------|---------|
| "make/let/help/see" + bare infinitive | Active = bare; Passive = to-infinitive |
| Imperative | Passive = "Let + obj + be + V3" |
| Two objects | Two passive forms possible |
| Intransitive verb | NO passive possible |

**Narration:**
| If you see... | Then... |
|---------------|---------|
| Statement | "said + that + back-shifted" |
| Question | "asked + if/wh- + back-shifted" |
| Imperative | "told/asked + obj + to + V1" |
| Exclamatory | "exclaimed + that +..." |
| Universal truth | NO back-shift |
