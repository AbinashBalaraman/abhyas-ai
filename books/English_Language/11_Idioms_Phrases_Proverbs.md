# Chapter 11: Idioms, Phrases & Proverbs

> **Chapter Overview:** This chapter covers **Idioms & Phrases** and **Proverbs**. **Idioms** are expressions whose meaning cannot be deduced from individual words (e.g., "kick the bucket" = die). **Phrases** are groups of words with a specific meaning. **Proverbs** are short, well-known sayings. Together, they contribute **3-5 questions per SSC CGL Tier-I**, **2-3 in Banking**, and **2-3 in RRB NTPC**.
>
> **Comprehensive exam-aligned study material covering standard syllabi for SSC, RRB, Banking, and State PSC examinations.

## 1. Conceptual Foundation

### 1.1 What are Idioms?

An **idiom** is a group of words whose meaning is **different from the literal meaning** of the individual words. Idioms are culturally specific and must be memorized.

### 1.2 What are Phrases?

A **phrase** is a group of words that **function as a single unit** in a sentence. Phrases are often used in **prepositional** (in, on, at + noun) or **verb** (give up, look after) forms.

### 1.3 What are Proverbs?

A **proverb** is a **short, well-known saying** that expresses a general truth or piece of advice. They are often based on cultural wisdom.

## 4. Speed-Solving Shortcuts

### 4.1 The "Context Clue" Trick

In a sentence, the **surrounding words** often hint at the idiom's meaning. Use context to guess.

### 4.2 The "Common Idioms" Memorization Trick

Memorize the **100 most common** idioms (death, success, money, time). They cover 80% of questions.

### 4.3 The "Visualize" Trick

For some idioms, **visualizing** the literal action helps remember the meaning. "Kick the bucket" → picture a bucket being kicked → "die".

## 5. Question Types

### 5.1 Type 1 — Choose Correct Meaning

**Example:** "Kick the bucket" means:
(a) To kick (b) To die (c) To fall (d) To fight
**Solution:** "Kick the bucket" = die. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 10 sec.* [§2.1]

### 5.2 Type 2 — Choose Correct Idiom for Meaning

**Example:** "Very easy" — which idiom?
(a) A piece of cake (b) A walk in the park (c) Child's play (d) All of these
**Solution:** All mean "very easy". **Answer: (d).** *Time: 15 sec.* [§2.3]

### 5.3 Type 3 — Fill in the Blank

**Example:** "I was ___ when I got the gift." (over the moon, in hot water, at my wit's end, etc.)
**Solution:** "Over the moon" = very happy. **Answer: over the moon.** *Time: 15 sec.* [§2.3]

### 5.4 Type 4 — Foreign Phrase Meaning

**Example:** "Bona fide" means:
(a) Bad faith (b) Good faith (c) In doubt (d) After the fact
**Solution:** Bona fide = genuine, in good faith. **Answer: (b).** *Time: 10 sec.* [§3]

### 5.5 Type 5 — Sentence Replacement

**Example:** "He was extremely happy" — replace with idiom:
(a) On cloud nine (b) In hot water (c) Bite the dust (d) Tighten his belt
**Solution:** "On cloud nine" = very happy. **Answer: (a).** *Time: 15 sec.* [§2.3]

### 5.6 Type 6 — Idiom in Context

**Example:** "After losing his job, he is in ___" (deep water / at the eleventh hour / in seventh heaven / over the moon)
**Solution:** "In deep water" = in trouble. **Answer: deep water.** *Time: 15 sec.* [§2.4]
