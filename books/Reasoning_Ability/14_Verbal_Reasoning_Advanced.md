## 2. Logical Venn Diagrams

### 2.1 Definition

A **Venn diagram** represents the relationship between two or three sets using overlapping circles. The position of circles shows the relationship.

### 2.2 The Five Venn Diagram Relationships

For TWO sets, there are 5 possible relationships:

1. **Disjoint** (A and B have no common elements): Two separate circles.
2. **Subset** (A is inside B): A's circle inside B's circle.
3. **Superset** (B is inside A): B's circle inside A's circle.
4. **Intersecting** (A and B share some elements): Two overlapping circles.
5. **Equal** (A = B): Same circle.

### 2.3 For THREE Sets

For three sets A, B, C, the relationships become more complex:
- All three disjoint
- Two intersect, third disjoint
- All three mutually intersect
- A inside B inside C
- etc.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — standard extension]

### 2.4 Example: Venn for Doctors, Females, Mothers

Sets:
- **D** = Doctors
- **F** = Females
- **M** = Mothers

Possible Venn:
- D and F intersect (some doctors are female).
- M is subset of F (all mothers are female).
- M may or may not intersect D (some mothers are doctors).

Representation:
```
        ________
       |  D     |
       |  __    |
       | |  |   |
       | | M|---|
       | |__|   |
       |________|
       |        |
       |   F    |
       |________|
```

All mothers are inside both F (female) and possibly inside D (doctor) if some mothers are doctors.

### 2.5 Common Venn Question Types

**Type 1: Identify the correct Venn for given sets.**
- "Which Venn represents: men, fathers, doctors?"

**Type 2: Identify the shaded region meaning.**
- "What does the shaded region represent?"

**Type 3: Find the relationship.**
- "If A, B, C are three sets, which Venn correctly shows their relationship?"

### 2.6 Examples

**Example 1:** "Vegetables, Tomatoes, Fruits"
- Tomato is a fruit biologically, but commonly classified as vegetable. 
- Venn: Fruits and Vegetables are disjoint (in common usage), Tomato can be in either (depends on classification).
- **Correct Venn:** Tomato inside Vegetables (vegetable in common usage), separate from Fruits.

## 3. Word Formation

### 3.1 Definition

In **word formation** questions, the student is given a word and asked to form **meaningful words** using a subset of its letters (or rearrange all letters).

### 3.2 Question Types

**Type 1:** "How many meaningful words can be formed from the letters of 'TRIANGLE'?"
- TRIANGLE, TRIANGEL? No. Actually TRIANGLE: TRI, ANGLE, RING, RANG, RAG, TAG, TAN, RAT, etc. Many words.
- Count = ?

**Type 2:** "Which word cannot be formed from the letters of 'COMPUTER'?"
- COMPUTE, COMP, COM, PUT, PUTR? No, no R. COMPUTER, COMPUTE (R? COMPUTER has R), COMPUTER = C-O-M-P-U-T-E-R. Words: COMPUTE, COMPUTER, MUTE, CORE, PORE, etc.
- Some word from options cannot be formed.

**Type 3:** "What is the highest-scoring word in Scrabble using these letters?"
- Less common in SSC.

### 3.3 Strategy for Word Formation

1. **List all letters** in the given word.
2. **For each option**, check if it can be formed.
3. **Discard** options that need letters NOT in the original word.
4. **Verify** spelling.

**Example 1:** Which word cannot be formed from "EDUCATION"?
- E, D, U, C, A, T, I, O, N

## 4. Dictionary Order (Alphabetical Order)

### 4.1 Definition

**Dictionary order** is the alphabetical arrangement of words. The student must arrange words in the order they would appear in a dictionary.

### 4.2 Rules for Dictionary Order

1. Compare the **first letter** of each word.
2. If same, compare the **second letter**, and so on.
3. The word that has a **letter earlier in the alphabet** at the first point of difference comes first.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.3 Example

**Arrange: CAT, CART, CAR, CASTLE, COAT**
- C-A-R: comes before C-A-S (R < S).
- C-A-R-T: C-A-R then T. C-A-R-T < C-A-S-T (R < S).
- C-A-S-T: comes after C-A-R-T.
- C-A-S-T-L-E: comes after C-A-S-T.
- C-O-A-T: comes after all C-A- words (O > A).
- Order: CAR, CART, CAST, CASTLE, CAT? Hmm, let me redo.

Words: CAR, CART, CASTLE, CAT, COAT.
- CAR: C-A-R
- CART: C-A-R-T
- CASTLE: C-A-S-T-L-E
- CAT: C-A-T
- COAT: C-O-A-T

Comparing CAR, CART, CAT, CASTLE, COAT:
- All start with C.
- Second letter: A, A, A, A, O. The four C-A- words come before C-O-AT.
- Within C-A-: third letter: R, R, T, S.
- CAR, CART (third letter R): come before CAT (T) and CASTLE (S).
- Between CAR and CART: CART has 4 letters, CAR has 3. The shorter word (CAR) comes first if it's a prefix of the longer.
- So CAR < CART.
- Between CAT and CASTLE: third letter T vs S. S < T, so CASTLE < CAT.
- Final order: CAR, CART, CASTLE, CAT, COAT.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 4.4 Common Dictionary Order Questions

**Type 1:** "Which word comes first in the dictionary?"
- Compare letter by letter.

**Type 2:** "Which word comes last in the dictionary?"
- Compare letter by letter from the end.

**Type 3:** "Find the position of a word in the dictionary."
- Count words before it.

### 4.5 Examples

**Example 1:** Which comes first: ZEBRA, ZEAL, ZEST, ZOO?
- All start with Z.
- Z-E (ZEAL, ZEBRA, ZEST) come before Z-O (ZOO).
- Among ZE-: E is same, next is A (ZEAL), B (ZEBRA), S (ZEST). A < B < S.
- **Order: ZEAL, ZEBRA, ZEST, ZOO.** *Time: 30 sec.* [NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

**Example 2:** Which comes last: APPLE, ANT, AIR, APE?
- All start with A.
- AIR, ANT, APE, APPLE.
- I < N < P (for second letter).
- AIR < ANT < APE.
- APPLE comes after APE (P-P vs P-E at third position; APE < APPLE).
- **Order: AIR, ANT, APE, APPLE.** *Time: 30 sec.* [NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

## 5. Calendar (Brief Recap from Chapter 8)

### 5.1 Key Concepts

- **Odd days**: Days left over after dividing by 7.
- **1 year = 1 odd day; 1 leap year = 2 odd days.**
- **100 years = 5 odd days.**
- **200 years = 3 odd days.**
- **300 years = 1 odd day.**
- **400 years = 0 odd days.**

### 5.2 Leap Year Rule

- Divisible by 4 → leap.
- Divisible by 100 → NOT leap.
- Divisible by 400 → IS leap.

### 5.3 Example

**Q:** What day of the week was January 1, 2000?
- 2000 is divisible by 400 → leap year.
- Need to compute backward, which is complex.
- Standard answer: Saturday (in 2000, Jan 1 was Saturday).

## 6. Clocks (Brief Recap from Chapter 8)

### 6.1 Key Concepts

- Minute hand: 6°/min. Hour hand: 0.5°/min.
- Minute hand gains 5.5°/min on hour hand.
- Hands coincide 11 times in 12 hours.
- In 65 min 5/11, minute hand gains 360°.

### 6.2 Angle Formulas

- **Angle between hands at H:M** = |30H − 5.5M| (or 360° minus that).
- **Hands coincide**: 30H = 5.5M → M = 60H/11.
- **Hands opposite (180°)**: 30H − 5.5M = 180.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS — formulas]

### 6.3 Examples

**Example 1:** Angle between hands at 3:00?
- 30(3) − 5.5(0) = 90°. **Answer: 90°.** *Time: 15 sec.* [NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

**Example 2:** At what time between 2 and 3 do the hands coincide?
- 30(2) = 5.5M → 60 = 5.5M → M = 60/5.5 = 120/11 = 10 min 10/11.
- **Answer: 2:10 10/11.** *Time: 60 sec.* [NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

## 8. Speed-Solving Shortcuts

### 8.1 The "Letter-by-Letter" Trick for Dictionary Order

Compare words one letter at a time. The first difference determines the order.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 8.2 The "Set Theory Intuition" Trick for Venn

Think about real-world examples. Doctors and Mothers: are all mothers doctors? No. So Mothers is a separate set. Some doctors are mothers (overlap).

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 8.3 The "Letter Inventory" Trick for Word Formation

List all letters of the given word. For each option, check if all its letters are in the inventory.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 8.4 The "Prefix Rule" for Dictionary Order

Shorter word that's a prefix of a longer word comes first. E.g., CAT < CATS.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 8.5 The "Odd Day Calculation" Trick for Calendar

Memorize: 1y=1, 1LY=2, 100y=5, 200y=3, 300y=1, 400y=0.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 8.6 The "5.5° Gain per Minute" Trick for Clocks

Minute hand gains 5.5° per minute over hour hand. Apply to find coincidence times.

[NOT IN EXTRACTED BOOKS]

### 12.2 Top 5 Shortcuts

1. **Venn: think real-world** — Dogs and Cats are both Mammals but disjoint.
2. **Word formation: list letters, check inventory**.
3. **Dictionary: prefix rule** — shorter prefix comes first.
4. **Calendar: 1y=1, 1LY=2, 100y=5, 400y=0**.
5. **Clock: 5.5° gain per minute**.

### 12.3 5 Common Mistakes

1. **Venn: wrong relationship** — e.g., marking disjoint when actually subset.
2. **Word formation: missing duplicate letters** — word needs two T's, original has only one.
3. **Dictionary: comparing wrong position** — start from first letter, not from the end.
4. **Calendar: forgetting leap year** — affects 29 Feb.
5. **Clock: not considering both formulas** — 30H−5.5M and 5.5M−30H.
