# Chapter 7: Statement & Conclusion, Assumption, Course of Action, Data Sufficiency

> **Chapter Overview:** This chapter covers four **closely related "Statement-based" reasoning topics** that test logical evaluation rather than calculation. **Statement & Conclusion** tests the ability to identify what follows from a given statement. **Assumption** tests whether an unstated premise is required for the statement to hold. **Course of Action** tests the appropriate response to a problem. **Data Sufficiency** (heavily tested in Banking Mains) tests whether the given data is enough to answer a question. Together they appear in **3-5 questions per SSC CGL Tier-I**, **2-3 per IBPS PO Prelims**, **3-5 per IBPS PO Mains**, and **5+ per SBI PO Mains**.
>
> **Primary sources used:** Previous Years Solved Papers SSC (Statement & Conclusion ch.10 SRE-463-519, Syllogism section), Reasoning Standard Reference(Statement Argument & Assumption ch.15 pp.363-394), Competitive Exam Question Bank Reasoning SSC (Syllogism sec.11 pp.486-549), Standard Exam ReferenceReasoning 8th Edition (Statement & Conclusion ch.7, **Data Sufficiency ch.8 — fully extracted with all DS examples**).

## 1. Conceptual Foundation

### 1.1 Statement & Conclusion

A **Statement & Conclusion** question gives one or more statements (facts, opinions, or assertions) followed by two or more conclusions. The candidate must decide which conclusions **definitely follow** from the given statements.

**The key principle:** A conclusion "follows" if it **must be true** given the statements. It does NOT follow if it:
- Might be true but isn't certain.
- Is a remote implication requiring unstated assumptions.
- Contradicts the statements.

> "A conclusion will follow, if (i) it is implied in one or all of the pieces of information. (ii) it is a generalisation of the ideas given in the statement. (iii) no material fallacy is committed, viz., (a) fallacy of composition, (b) fallacy of division, (c) fallacy of uncritical analogy, (d) fallacy of accident and (e) fallacy of insufficient data."

### 1.2 The Three Validity Tests for Conclusions

**Test 1: Direct implication.** The conclusion must be **directly supported** by the statement's wording.

**Test 2: Generalisation.** The conclusion can be a general rule inferred from specific examples in the statement.

**Test 3: No fallacy.** The conclusion must not commit any of the five logical fallacies:
- **Composition**: Assuming what's true of parts is true of the whole.
- **Division**: Assuming what's true of the whole is true of parts.
- **Uncritical analogy**: Comparing two things that aren't truly comparable.
- **Accident**: Applying a general rule to a specific case where it doesn't apply.
- **Insufficient data**: Drawing conclusions from incomplete information.

### 1.3 Assumption

An **assumption** is the **hidden, unstated premise** on which the statement is based. Without it, the statement wouldn't hold.

> "An assumption is that part of an argument which is not expressed or said explicitly as it is assumed or understood intrinsically."

**Example:**
> Statement: "Robert is a good player of Indian hockey team. So, India should win the match."
> Assumption: "A good player is likely to help his team win the match." (Without this, the conclusion doesn't follow.)

**Three characteristics of a valid assumption:**
1. **Implicit**: Not stated, but understood.
2. **Necessary**: Required for the statement to make sense.
3. **Reasonable**: Not absurd or extreme.

### 1.4 Course of Action

A **Course of Action** is an **appropriate step or policy** that should be taken in response to a problem described in the statement. A valid course of action must:
- Address the problem directly.
- Be practical and feasible.
- Be within the scope of the entity making the decision.

**Two-part test:**
- Is the proposed action **logically appropriate** for the problem?
- Is it **practically feasible**?

### 1.5 Data Sufficiency

**Data Sufficiency (DS)** questions give a **question** followed by **two or three statements**. The candidate must determine **whether the statements provide enough information to answer the question**.

**Standard answer choices:**
- (a) Statement I alone is sufficient.
- (b) Statement II alone is sufficient.
- (c) Both statements together are sufficient.
- (d) Either statement alone is sufficient.
- (e) Neither statement alone nor both together are sufficient.

**Common DS topics:**
- Quantitative (age, percentage, ratio).
- Order/Ranking (who is tallest? who came first?).
- Logical reasoning (syllogism, direction).
- Coding-Decoding (what does code X mean?).

### 1.6 The DS Decision Process

```
Step 1: Read the question carefully. Identify what needs to be answered.
Step 2: Examine Statement I alone. Can you answer the question using ONLY I?
        - Yes → Note: "I alone sufficient"
        - No → Continue
Step 3: Examine Statement II alone. Can you answer using ONLY II?
        - Yes → Note: "II alone sufficient"
        - No → Continue
Step 4: Combine I and II. Can you answer using BOTH together?
        - Yes → Note: "Both together sufficient"
        - No → "Neither sufficient"
Step 5: Special case: If both I alone and II alone are sufficient
        → "Either I or II alone is sufficient"
```

### 1.7 The Two Failure Modes in DS

- **Insufficient data**: Each statement alone has multiple valid answers. Even combined, you can't pin down one answer.
- **Conflicting data**: The statements give contradictory info (e.g., "X is taller than Y" and "Y is taller than X"). This is rare but possible.

## 2. Step-by-Step Solving Algorithm

### 2.1 For Statement & Conclusion

```
Step 1: Read the statement twice. Identify the key claims.
Step 2: Read each conclusion. Ask: "Does the statement DIRECTLY support this?"
Step 3: If yes → conclusion follows.
Step 4: If no → check if the conclusion is a valid generalisation.
Step 5: If still no → check for fallacies (composition, division, etc.).
Step 6: Mark conclusions that definitely follow.
Step 7: If only I follows → (a); only II → (b); both → (c); neither → (d);
        either-or → (e).
```

### 2.2 For Assumption

```
Step 1: Read the statement. Identify the conclusion being drawn.
Step 2: Ask: "What must be TRUE for the conclusion to make sense?"
Step 3: That hidden requirement = the assumption.
Step 4: Test each proposed assumption:
        - Is it implicit (not stated)?
        - Is it necessary?
        - Is it reasonable?
Step 5: The valid assumption is the one that is implicit AND necessary AND reasonable.
```

### 2.3 For Course of Action

```
Step 1: Read the problem statement. Identify the issue.
Step 2: For each proposed action, ask:
        - Does it solve the problem?
        - Is it practical?
        - Is it within the decision-maker's authority?
Step 3: The valid course of action addresses the problem AND is feasible.
```

### 2.4 For Data Sufficiency

```
Step 1: Read the question. Identify what to find.
Step 2: Try Statement I alone. Find all valid answers.
        - If only one valid answer → "I alone sufficient"
        - If multiple → I is insufficient, continue
Step 3: Try Statement II alone. Same process.
Step 4: Combine I and II. Find all valid answers.
        - If only one → "Both together sufficient"
        - If multiple → "Neither sufficient"
Step 5: Special: If both I alone and II alone are each sufficient → "Either".
Step 6: Match to answer choices (a-e).
```

### 2.5 The "Try-and-Test" Trick for DS

For each statement, **assume only that statement is true** and ignore the others. Try to find an answer. If you can, the statement alone is sufficient. If multiple answers are possible, the statement alone is not sufficient.

### 2.6 The "What must be true?" Test

A common DS question: "What is the value of X?" 
- If Statement I gives X = 5, that's sufficient.
- If Statement I gives X > 3 and Statement II gives X < 7, together X is 4, 5, or 6 — not sufficient.
- If Statement I gives X = 5 and Statement II gives X = 5, then both individually and together are sufficient → answer (d) "Either".

## 3. Trap Detection & Error Prevention Guide

Top 10 traps in Statement/Conclusion/Assumption/DS:

1. **"Maybe true" vs "definitely true".** A conclusion that COULD be true doesn't follow. Must be DEFINITELY true.

2. **Extreme language.** Words like "all", "none", "only", "never" make a conclusion strong but harder to support.

3. **"Should" vs "must".** A statement says "X should happen" → doesn't mean X MUST happen. Trap: treating recommendations as facts.

4. **Assumption ≠ Inference.** An assumption is what must be true for the statement to work. An inference is what can be drawn FROM the statement. These are different.

5. **Course of Action ≠ Decision.** A CoA is a plan to address a problem. A decision is a choice between options. Trap: confusing the two.

6. **DS: "I alone" means ONLY I.** Don't combine I and II in your head when testing I alone.

7. **DS: Sufficient ≠ Unique.** "Sufficient" means you can find an answer (could be one or more valid answers). If exactly one answer exists, sufficient. If multiple answers are possible, NOT sufficient.

8. **DS: Number of valid answers.** Some questions have 2+ valid answers that all satisfy the statements. Then the data is NOT sufficient.

9. **Statement-Conclusion: Generalisation needs evidence.** If the statement gives 3 specific examples, you can generalise ONLY to "most cases", not "all cases".

10. **Statement-Conclusion: Negation trap.** A conclusion saying "X does not happen" requires the statement to EXPLICITLY say X doesn't happen. Silence ≠ negation.

### 3.1 Sample trap sentence + correct interpretation

> **Trap statement:** "Many people prefer tea to coffee."
> **Trap conclusion:** "All people prefer tea to coffee."
> **Correct interpretation:** WRONG. "Many" doesn't imply "all". Conclusion doesn't follow.

> **Trap assumption question:** "Statement: We should ban junk food in schools. Assumption: Junk food is harmful."
> **Correct interpretation:** VALID assumption. Without this, the recommendation doesn't make sense.

## 4. Type-Wise Question Bank with Solutions

### Type 1: Single Statement + Two Conclusions

**Logic:** One statement, decide which of two conclusions follow.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Exam Source:** SSC CGL Tier-I, SSC CHSL, SSC MTS

**Solved Example 1 (SSC Style — 30 sec):**
> Statement: "Population is the greatest pollutant."
> Conclusions:
> I. Increased population is the greatest resource.
> II. Illiterate mass leads to dirt and degradation.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. "Greatest pollutant" = "greatest source of pollution". Conclusion I says "greatest resource" — opposite of "pollutant". Contradicts. ✗
> II. The statement only says population causes pollution, not specifically about illiterate masses. ✗
>
> **Answer: (d) Neither follows.**
>

**Solved Example 2 (IBPS Clerk — 30 sec):**
> Statement: "The books of Previous Years Solved PapersPrakashan are well written. These books are useful for one day competitive examinations."
> Conclusions:
> I. The books of Previous Years Solved PapersPrakashan are useful for objective type examinations.
> II. Well-written books are useful.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. "Useful for one day competitive exams" → "useful for objective type exams" (since one day competitive exams are objective). ✓
> II. Well-written + useful — but the statement only says Previous Years Solved Papersbooks are well-written AND useful. Doesn't mean all well-written books are useful. ✗ (generalisation trap)
>
> **Answer: (a) Only I follows.**
>

**Solved Example 3 (RRB NTPC — 20 sec):**
> Statement: "Use of cosmetics is hazardous."
> Conclusions:
> I. Cosmetics have side effects.
> II. Cosmetics are inexpensive.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. "Hazardous" implies "side effects". ✓
> II. No info about price. ✗
>
> **Answer: (a) Only I follows.**
>

### Type 2: Two Statements + Two Conclusions

**Logic:** Two related statements; check both conclusions.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Exam Source:** SSC CGL Tier-I

**Solved Example:**
> Statements: "Most people are good at heart and non-manipulative. A few who become manipulative have been found to be lacking in keeping strong family ties."
> Conclusions:
> I. People from different socio-economic backgrounds behave differently.
> II. Being manipulative is not considered as a virtue.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. The statements don't compare behaviour across socio-economic groups. ✗
> II. "Manipulative people lack strong family ties" + (implied) "lack of family ties is not virtuous" → manipulation not virtuous. Reasonable inference. ✓
>
> **Answer: (a) Only II follows.**
>

### Type 3: Assumption Questions

**Logic:** Identify the hidden unstated premise.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Exam Source:** SSC CGL, IBPS PO Mains, SSC CPO

**Solved Example 1 (SSC Style — 45 sec):**
> Statement: "Mankind can get freedom only through revolutions."
> Assumptions:
> I. Revolutions can bring freedom.
> II. Except revolution there is no other method of getting freedom.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. "Only through revolutions" → revolutions can bring freedom. ✓ Valid.
> II. "Only through revolutions" = "no other method". ✓ Also valid.
>
> Wait, both are valid? Let me check.
> "Only through revolutions" implies: (a) revolutions work, AND (b) nothing else works.
> So both I and II are valid assumptions.
>
> **Answer: (c) Both I and II are implicit.**
>

**Solved Example 2 (Banking — 45 sec):**
> Statement: "Our country has stood like a solid rock in the face of common danger."
> Assumptions:
> I. There have been dangers to our country.
> II. Our country is strong.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. "In the face of common danger" → there were dangers. ✓
> II. "Solid rock" metaphor implies strength. ✓ But "solid rock in the face of danger" — solid rock is being compared to the country's resilience, not its general strength. Hmm, borderline.
>
> Actually, "solid rock" specifically means "unwavering in danger", not "strong in general". So II is not necessarily valid.
>
> **Answer: (a) Only I is implicit.**
>

**Solved Example 3 (IBPS PO Mains — 60 sec):**
> Statement: "Unless country 'X' achieves total literacy it cannot achieve its mission of development."
> Assumptions:
> I. It is possible to achieve total literacy in country 'X'.
> II. No development is possible without a proper mission.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. "Unless... cannot" implies that achieving literacy is necessary. If it were impossible, the statement wouldn't make sense. So I is implicit. ✓
> II. The statement only addresses the literacy-development link. Doesn't say a "proper mission" is needed. ✗
>
> **Answer: (a) Only I is implicit.**
>

### Type 4: Course of Action

**Logic:** Identify appropriate policy response.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Exam Source:** SSC CGL, Banking Mains

**Solved Example 1:**
> Statement: "There has been an unprecedented increase in the number of unemployed youth in city X."
> Course of Action:
> I. The government should immediately create more jobs.
> II. The government should set up more vocational training centres.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. Direct response to unemployment. ✓ Valid.
> II. Long-term solution (skills training enables employment). ✓ Also valid.
>
> Both are valid courses of action.
> **Answer: (c) Both follow.**
>

**Solved Example 2:**
> Statement: "Heavy rains have caused severe flooding in the coastal regions."
> Course of Action:
> I. The government should evacuate people from the flooded areas.
> II. The government should build a dam upstream to control future flooding.
>
> **Solution:**
> I. Immediate response to save lives. ✓ Valid.
> II. Long-term preventive measure. ✓ Valid.
>
> Both valid.
> **Answer: (c) Both follow.**
>

### Type 5: Data Sufficiency — Quantitative

**Logic:** Given mathematical info, decide if you can answer.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Exam Source:** IBPS PO Mains, SBI PO Mains, RBI Grade B

**Solved Example 1:**
> Question: What is the age of A?
> Statement I: A is 5 years older than B.
> Statement II: B is 3 years younger than C.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: A = B + 5. Without knowing B, can't find A. Insufficient.
> II alone: B = C − 3. Without C, can't find B (or A). Insufficient.
> Both: A = B + 5, B = C − 3, so A = C − 3 + 5 = C + 2. Without C, still insufficient.
>
> **Answer: (e) Neither alone nor both together are sufficient.**
>

**Solved Example 2:**
> Question: What is the value of X?
> Statement I: X + Y = 10.
> Statement II: Y = 4.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: 2 unknowns, 1 equation. Insufficient.
> II alone: Doesn't involve X directly. Insufficient.
> Both: Y = 4 from II; X + 4 = 10 from I; X = 6. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (c) Both statements together are sufficient.**
>

**Solved Example 3:**
> Question: In which direction is A facing?
> Statement I: A is facing North or South.
> Statement II: If A turns right, A will face East.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: 2 possibilities. Insufficient.
> II alone: A turns right → A faces East. So A is facing North (since turning right from North = East). Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (b) Statement II alone is sufficient.**
>

**Solved Example 4:**
> Question: Who is the tallest among A, B, C?
> Statement I: A is taller than B.
> Statement II: B is taller than C.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: A > B, but no info about C. Insufficient.
> II alone: B > C, but no info about A. Insufficient.
> Both: A > B > C → A is tallest. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (c) Both statements together are sufficient.**
>

**Solved Example 5:**
> Question: How many days did Rakesh take to complete the project?
> Statement I: Rakesh worked 8 hours a day.
> Statement II: The project took 240 hours of total work.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: hours per day known, but no total hours. Insufficient.
> II alone: total hours known, but no hours per day. Insufficient.
> Both: 240 hours / 8 hours per day = 30 days. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (c) Both statements together are sufficient.**
>

### Type 6: Data Sufficiency — Logical Reasoning

**Logic:** Non-quantitative DS (syllogism, coding, etc.).
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Exam Source:** IBPS PO Mains, RBI Grade B Phase-II

**Solved Example:**
> Question: Is A taller than B?
> Statement I: A is taller than C.
> Statement II: B is shorter than C.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: A > C, but B unknown. Insufficient.
> II alone: B < C, but A unknown. Insufficient.
> Both: A > C > B → A > B. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (c) Both statements together are sufficient.**
>

### Type 7: DS with "Either" Answer

**Logic:** Both statements alone are sufficient.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Exam Source:** IBPS PO Mains

**Solved Example:**
> Question: What is X?
> Statement I: X + Y = 10.
> Statement II: 2X = 20 − 2Y.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: 2 unknowns. Insufficient.
> II alone: 2X = 20 − 2Y → X = 10 − Y. Same as I. Insufficient alone.
> Both: 2 equations, 2 unknowns. X = 5, Y = 5. Sufficient.
>
> Wait, both individually are equivalent (X = 10 − Y). Each alone is insufficient. Combined is sufficient. Answer (c).
>
> Let me try another example.
>
> Question: What is X?
> Statement I: X = 5.
> Statement II: X² = 25.
>
> I alone: X = 5 directly. Sufficient.
> II alone: X² = 25 → X = 5 or X = -5. Multiple values. Insufficient.
>
> So I alone sufficient. Answer (a).
>
> Let me find an "Either" example:
>
> Question: What is X?
> Statement I: X = 5.
> Statement II: 2X = 10.
>
> I alone: X = 5. Sufficient.
> II alone: 2X = 10 → X = 5. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (d) Either statement alone is sufficient.**
>

### Type 8: Data Sufficiency — Advanced Quantitative (Banking Mains)

**Logic:** Multi-step quantitative problems with multiple unknowns.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Exam Source:** IBPS PO Mains, RBI Grade B

**Solved Example 1 (Ages):**
> Question: What is A's age?
> Statement I: A is twice as old as B was 5 years ago.
> Statement II: B is 30 years old.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: A = 2(B − 5) = 2B − 10. Without B, can't find A. Insufficient.
> II alone: B = 30. But A's age formula needs more. Insufficient.
> Both: A = 2(30) − 10 = 50. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (c) Both together.**
>

**Solved Example 2 (Ratio):**
> Question: What is the ratio of A's salary to B's salary?
> Statement I: A's salary is 50% more than B's.
> Statement II: C's salary is the average of A's and B's salaries.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: A = 1.5B → A:B = 3:2. Sufficient!
> II alone: C = (A+B)/2. Without absolute values, can't find ratio. Insufficient.
>
> **Answer: (a) I alone sufficient.**
>

**Solved Example 3 (Work-Time):**
> Question: In how many days can A and B working together complete the work?
> Statement I: A alone can complete it in 12 days.
> Statement II: B alone is 50% more efficient than A.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: A's rate = 1/12. Without B's rate, can't find combined. Insufficient.
> II alone: B's rate = 1.5 × (1/12) = 1/8. Without A's rate from a known source, can't combine. Insufficient.
> Both: A = 1/12, B = 1/8. Combined = 1/12 + 1/8 = 2/24 + 3/24 = 5/24. Days = 24/5 = 4.8 days. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (c) Both together.**
>

### Type 9: DS — Direction & Position Questions

**Logic:** Movement, directions, or positions.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Exam Source:** IBPS PO Mains, SBI Clerk Mains

**Solved Example:**
> Question: In which direction is A facing now?
> Statement I: A was facing North. A turned 90° clockwise.
> Statement II: A's shadow is falling to A's right.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: North + 90° clockwise = East. Sufficient.
> II alone: Shadow to right → sun is in left of A → sun is in the west (assuming morning) → A is facing south. Sufficient.
>
> Both alone give different answers (East vs South). But each individually sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (d) Either.**
>

### Type 10: DS — Coding-Decoding

**Logic:** Apply coding rule from one statement, decode with another.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Exam Source:** IBPS PO Mains

**Solved Example:**
> Question: What is the code for "BLUE"?
> Statement I: In a code, RED = "27" and GREEN = "385".
> Statement II: Each letter is coded as its position in the alphabet, and then digits are summed.
>
> **Solution:**
> I alone: RED = 27 (R=18, E=5, D=4; 18+5+4=27? 27 ≠ 27. Or 1+8+5+4 = 18 ≠ 27.) Doesn't match. Insufficient.
> II alone: BLUE = 2+12+21+5 = 40. Sufficient.
>
> **Answer: (b) Statement II alone sufficient.**
>

## 5. Timed Practice Sets

### Set A — Warm-Up (5 questions, 30-45 sec each)

**Q1.** Statement: "Smoking is injurious to health."
Conclusions: I. Smokers should quit. II. All smokers die early.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I. Logical recommendation. ✓ II. No evidence "all" die early. ✗
**Answer: (a)**

**Q2.** Statement: "The new policy will reduce unemployment by 20%."
Assumption: I. The policy will be implemented effectively. II. Unemployment is a problem.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I. For the prediction to hold, implementation must be effective. ✓ II. The statement assumes reducing unemployment is good, i.e., unemployment is a problem. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q3.** Statement: "Heavy traffic congestion in the city has increased pollution levels."
Course of Action: I. Build more flyovers. II. Promote public transport.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** Both address the issue (flyovers reduce congestion; public transport reduces vehicles). ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q4.** Question: What is X?
Statement I: X = Y + 5.
Statement II: Y = 3.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I alone: 2 unknowns. ✗ II alone: Y = 3, but X unknown. ✗ Both: X = 8. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q5.** Question: Who came first in the race?
Statement I: A came before B. Statement II: C came after B.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I alone: A vs C unknown. ✗ II alone: B vs A unknown. ✗ Both: A > B, B > C → A first, B second, C third. But question asks "who came first" — answer is A. Sufficient!
**Answer: (c)**

### Set B — Exam Simulation (10 questions, 8-10 min)

**Q6.** Statement: "We should encourage the use of renewable energy sources."
Assumptions: I. Renewable energy is beneficial. II. Fossil fuels will run out.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I. Without this assumption, "encouraging" doesn't make sense. ✓ II. Not necessarily implied — we could encourage renewables for environmental reasons alone. ✗
**Answer: (a)**

**Q7.** Statement: "The company reported record profits this year despite the recession."
Conclusions: I. The company is well-managed. II. The recession is over.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I. Performance during recession suggests good management. ✓ (reasonable inference) II. "Despite the recession" implies it's still ongoing. ✗
**Answer: (a)**

**Q8.** Question: Is A the oldest among A, B, C?
Statement I: A is older than B. Statement II: B is older than C.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Neither (e) Either
**Solution:** I alone: A > B, but C unknown. ✗ II alone: B > C, but A unknown. ✗ Both: A > B > C, so A oldest. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q9.** Question: What is the value of P?
Statement I: P + Q = 15. Statement II: P − Q = 5.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Either
**Solution:** I alone: 2 unknowns. ✗ II alone: 2 unknowns. ✗ Both: 2 equations → P = 10, Q = 5. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q10.** Statement: "All students should participate in the annual sports day."
Conclusions: I. Sports day is important. II. Students have other commitments.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I. Mandating participation assumes it's important. ✓ II. The "should" assumes students might not naturally participate (could be due to many reasons, not necessarily other commitments). ✗
**Answer: (a)**

**Q11.** Question: How many students are in the class?
Statement I: 60% are boys. Statement II: There are 24 boys.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Either (e) Neither
**Solution:** I alone: 60% of total, but total unknown. ✗ II alone: 24 boys, but no percentage. ✗ Both: 60% = 24, total = 40. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q12.** Statement: "The government should increase the budget for education."
Course of Action: I. Allocate more funds to schools. II. Increase teacher salaries.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** Both are valid responses to "increase education budget". ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q13.** Question: Is X divisible by 6?
Statement I: X is divisible by 2. Statement II: X is divisible by 3.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Either
**Solution:** Divisible by 6 = divisible by both 2 and 3. I alone: X divisible by 2, but might not be divisible by 3. ✗ II alone: similar. ✗ Both: divisible by 2 AND 3 → divisible by 6. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q14.** Statement: "The movie was a box-office hit despite mixed reviews."
Conclusions: I. The movie had good marketing. II. The audience liked the movie.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I. Possible but not certain. ✗ II. "Box-office hit" + "despite mixed reviews" → audience generally liked it. ✓ (good inference)
**Answer: (b)**

**Q15.** Question: What day of the week is it?
Statement I: Yesterday was Tuesday. Statement II: Tomorrow is Thursday.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Either (e) Neither
**Solution:** I alone: If yesterday was Tuesday, today is Wednesday. ✓ Sufficient. II alone: If tomorrow is Thursday, today is Wednesday. ✓ Sufficient.
**Answer: (d)** — Either alone is sufficient.

### Set C — Advanced (3 questions, IBPS PO Mains / RBI Grade B level)

**Q16.** Question: What is the colour of the car?
Statement I: The car is red or blue. Statement II: The car is not red.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Either (e) Neither
**Solution:** I alone: 2 possibilities. ✗ II alone: 1 colour eliminated, but other not specified. ✗ Both: not red, but red or blue → blue. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q17.** Statement: "Heavy penalties should be imposed for traffic violations."
Assumptions: I. People violate traffic rules. II. Penalties deter violations.
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither
**Solution:** I. Without violations, no need for penalties. ✓ II. The recommendation assumes penalties work. ✓
**Answer: (c)**

**Q18.** Question: In a row of 5 people, who is at the leftmost?
Statement I: A is second to the left of B. Statement II: C is at the rightmost.
- (a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both (d) Either (e) Neither
**Solution:** I alone: A and B positions partially known. ✗ II alone: C is rightmost, but others unknown. ✗ Both: C at position 5. A 2nd to left of B means |pos(A) − pos(B)| = 2. Various possibilities (A=1, B=3; A=3, B=1; A=2, B=4; A=4, B=2; A=3, B=5; A=5, B=3 but B=5 means C=5 conflict). So A=1, B=3 OR A=3, B=1 OR A=2, B=4 OR A=4, B=2 OR A=3, B=5 conflict. So multiple possibilities. ✗
**Answer: (e)**

## 6. Quick Revision Sheet

### 6.1 Core Rules in Bullet Form

**Statement & Conclusion:**
1. Conclusion must be DEFINITELY true, not maybe.
2. No fallacies of composition, division, analogy, accident, insufficient data.
3. Direct implication or valid generalisation.

**Assumption:**
1. Hidden, unstated premise.
2. Necessary for the statement to work.
3. Reasonable (not absurd).

**Course of Action:**
1. Addresses the problem directly.
2. Practical and feasible.
3. Within decision-maker's authority.

**Data Sufficiency:**
1. Test each statement alone.
2. Test both together.
3. "Either" if both alone are sufficient.
4. "Neither" if even together not sufficient.

### 6.2 Decision Tree

| If you see... | Then do... |
|---------------|------------|
| "Conclusions I and II" | Check each for definite truth |
| "Assumptions I and II" | Test for implicit + necessary + reasonable |
| "Course of Action" | Check if it solves the problem AND is feasible |
| "DS with Statement I and II" | Test I alone, II alone, then both |
| "Two conclusions seem to say opposite things" | Possibly "Either" case |

### 6.3 Time Allocation Guide

| Set Type | Time per question |
|----------|-------------------|
| Statement & Conclusion (single stmt) | 20-30 sec |
| Statement & Conclusion (multi stmt) | 30-45 sec |
| Assumption | 30-45 sec |
| Course of Action | 30-45 sec |
| Data Sufficiency (quantitative) | 60-90 sec |
| Data Sufficiency (logical) | 90-120 sec |

### 6.4 The DS Answer Choice Quick Reference

| If... | Then answer is... |
|-------|-------------------|
| Only I gives a unique answer | (a) |
| Only II gives a unique answer | (b) |
| I alone insufficient, II alone insufficient, both together unique | (c) |
| Both I alone and II alone give unique answers | (d) Either |
| Even both together don't give unique answer | (e) Neither |

### 6.5 The "Definitely True" Test

A conclusion "follows" if you can convince a skeptic that **it must be true** given only the statement. If the skeptic can construct a valid scenario where the conclusion is false, the conclusion doesn't follow.
