## 1. Physics — Units, Motion, and Mechanics

### 1.1 Units and Measurements

**SI (International System of Units)** — Adopted in 1960; modern metric system. Used in 195+ countries.

**7 SI Base Units:**
| Quantity | SI Unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

**SI Derived Units:**
- Force: **Newton (N)** = kg·m/s²
- Energy/Work: **Joule (J)** = N·m
- Power: **Watt (W)** = J/s
- Pressure: **Pascal (Pa)** = N/m²
- Frequency: **Hertz (Hz)** = 1/s
- Electric charge: **Coulomb (C)** = A·s
- Voltage: **Volt (V)** = J/C
- Resistance: **Ohm (Ω)** = V/A
- Capacitance: **Farad (F)** = C/V
- Magnetic flux: **Weber (Wb)** = V·s
- Inductance: **Henry (H)** = Wb/A
- Luminous flux: **Lumen (lm)**
- Illuminance: **Lux (lx)** = lm/m²

**Fundamental & Supplementary Units:**
- Plane angle: radian (rad)
- Solid angle: steradian (sr)

**Prefixes (Decimal Multiples/Submultiples):**
- Tera (10¹²), Giga (10⁹), Mega (10⁶), kilo (10³), hecto (10²), deca (10¹)
- deci (10⁻¹), centi (10⁻²), milli (10⁻³), micro (10⁻⁶), nano (10⁻⁹), pico (10⁻¹²), femto (10⁻¹⁵)

**Dimensional Analysis:**
- [L] = L, [M] = M, [T] = T
- Velocity [v] = LT⁻¹
- Acceleration [a] = LT⁻²
- Force [F] = MLT⁻²
- Energy [E] = ML²T⁻²
- Power [P] = ML²T⁻³
- Pressure [P] = ML⁻¹T⁻²
- Momentum [p] = MLT⁻¹

### 1.2 Motion

**Rest and Motion**: A body is at rest if its position doesn't change with respect to surroundings. Motion = change of position.

**Types of Motion:**
- Translatory (linear, curvilinear, random)
- Rotatory (spinning)
- Vibratory (oscillatory)

**Distance vs Displacement:**
- Distance: Total path length (scalar)
- Displacement: Shortest distance between initial and final positions (vector)

**Speed**: Distance/Time (scalar); unit m/s
- Average speed = Total distance / Total time
- **Uniform speed**: Equal distances in equal intervals
- **Variable speed**: Different distances in same time

**Velocity**: Displacement/Time (vector)
- Average velocity = Total displacement / Total time
- Uniform velocity: constant magnitude & direction
- Instantaneous velocity: v = dx/dt

**Acceleration**: Rate of change of velocity; vector
- a = (v - u)/t; unit m/s²
- Uniform acceleration: equal changes in equal intervals

**Equations of Motion (Uniform Acceleration):**
$$v = u + at$$
$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$
$$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$

Where u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, t = time, a = acceleration, s = displacement

**Newton's Laws of Motion:**
1. **First Law (Law of Inertia)**: Body at rest stays at rest, body in motion stays in motion, unless acted on by external force. Inertia is the tendency.
2. **Second Law**: F = m·a; Force is proportional to rate of change of momentum. $F = \frac{dp}{dt} = m \cdot a$
3. **Third Law**: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

**Momentum**: p = m·v; vector; unit kg·m/s
**Impulse**: J = F·t = Δp; vector

**Mass vs Weight:**
- Mass: amount of matter (kg); constant
- Weight: gravitational force (N) = m·g; varies with location

### 1.3 Work, Energy, Power

**Work**: W = F·d·cos(θ); scalar; unit Joule
- If θ = 0°: W = F·d (max)
- If θ = 90°: W = 0
- If θ = 180°: W = -F·d (negative)

**Energy**: Capacity to do work; scalar; Joule
- **Kinetic Energy (KE)**: $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$
- **Potential Energy (PE)**: PE = m·g·h (gravitational); $PE = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$ (spring, k=spring constant)
- **Mechanical Energy**: ME = KE + PE

**Conservation of Energy**: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; only converted. (First Law of Thermodynamics)

**Power**: Rate of doing work; P = W/t; unit Watt
- 1 horsepower (HP) = 746 W
- 1 kW = 1000 W
- Electrical power: P = V·I = I²R = V²/R

**Commercial Unit of Energy**: kilowatt-hour (kWh) = 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 3.6 MJ
- 1 unit of electricity = 1 kWh

### 1.4 Gravitation

**Newton's Law of Gravitation:**
$$F = G \cdot \frac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^2}$$
Where G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (Gravitational constant)

**Acceleration due to gravity (g):**
$$g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2 \text{ (on Earth surface)}$$
- g varies: ~9.78 at equator, ~9.83 at poles
- g decreases with altitude; g decreases inside Earth (zero at center)
- g on Moon = 1.63 m/s² (~1/6 of Earth)

**Kepler's Laws:**
1. **First (Law of Orbits)**: Planets move in ellipses with Sun at one focus
2. **Second (Law of Areas)**: Equal areas swept in equal times
3. **Third (Law of Periods)**: T² ∝ a³ (T = orbital period, a = semi-major axis)

**Mass vs Weight (Key Distinction):**
- Mass (m): remains constant; measured by beam balance
- Weight (W = mg): varies; measured by spring balance

**Escape Velocity**: Minimum velocity to escape gravitational field
$$v_e = \sqrt{2gR} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}$$
- For Earth: ~11.2 km/s
- For Moon: 2.4 km/s
- For Sun: 618 km/s

**Satellite Orbits:**
- Geostationary satellite: T = 24 hours; orbit at ~36,000 km altitude
- Polar satellite: passes over poles
- Low Earth Orbit (LEO): 200-2000 km

### 1.5 Pressure, Floatation, and Surface Tension

**Pressure**: P = F/A; unit Pascal (Pa) = N/m²
- Atmospheric pressure at sea level: ~101,325 Pa = 101.325 kPa = 1 atm = 760 mm Hg = 760 torr
- 1 bar = 100,000 Pa = 100 kPa

**Pascal's Law**: Pressure applied to enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions (basis of hydraulic lift)

**Archimedes' Principle**: Buoyant force on immersed object = weight of fluid displaced

**Law of Floatation**: Object floats if its weight = weight of fluid displaced
- Density of object < density of fluid → floats
- Density of object > density of fluid → sinks
- Density of object = density of fluid → neutral buoyancy

**Density**: ρ = m/V; unit kg/m³
- Water: 1000 kg/m³
- Mercury: 13,600 kg/m³
- Air: 1.225 kg/m³
- Iron: 7870 kg/m³

**Surface Tension**: Tendency of liquid surface to contract; caused by cohesive forces
- Surface tension of water: 0.0728 N/m (at 20°C)
- Decreases with temperature; zero at critical point

**Viscosity**: Resistance to flow; depends on intermolecular forces
- Honey is more viscous than water
- Viscosity decreases with temperature (liquids); increases with temperature (gases)

**Capillary Action**: Spontaneous flow of liquid in narrow tube
- Water rises in glass capillary (concave meniscus)
- Mercury falls in glass capillary (convex meniscus)

### 1.6 Sound

**Sound**: Longitudinal mechanical wave; requires medium (cannot travel in vacuum)
- Speed: ~343 m/s in air (at 20°C); ~1480 m/s in water; ~5120 m/s in iron
- In air: speed increases with temperature

**Frequency**: Number of oscillations per second; unit Hertz (Hz)
- 1 kHz = 1000 Hz; 1 MHz = 10⁶ Hz
- Human hearing: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz
- Infrasound: <20 Hz (e.g., earthquakes, whales)
- Ultrasound: >20,000 Hz (e.g., bats, dolphins, medical imaging)

**Properties of Sound:**
- Pitch: depends on frequency
- Loudness: depends on amplitude
- Quality/Timbre: distinguishes instruments
- Wavelength: λ = v/f

**Applications of Ultrasound:**
- SONAR (Sound Navigation and Ranging) for underwater detection
- Medical imaging (sonography)
- Cleaning jewelry, dental instruments
- Detection of cracks in metals

**Applications of Infrasound:**
- Detecting earthquakes, volcanic eruptions
- Communication by elephants, whales
- Studying atmospheric phenomena

**Doppler Effect**: Apparent change in frequency due to relative motion
- Approaching: higher frequency; receding: lower frequency
- Applications: radar guns, sirens, redshift/blue shift in astronomy

### 1.7 Light and Optics

**Light**: Electromagnetic wave; speed c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (in vacuum)
- Visible spectrum: ~380-750 nm
- Wavelength: violet (shortest) to red (longest)
- VIBGYOR: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red

**Reflection**: Light bouncing off a surface
- Laws: angle of incidence = angle of reflection
- Plane mirror: same size, virtual, laterally inverted, behind mirror

**Refraction**: Bending of light as it passes between media
- Snell's Law: $n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2$
- Refractive index: n = c/v (c = speed in vacuum, v = speed in medium)

**Total Internal Reflection (TIR):**
- Occurs when light goes from denser to rarer medium at angle > critical angle
- Applications: optical fibres, mirage, sparkling diamond, prism

**Lens and Mirrors:**
- Convex lens: converges light; forms real image (when u > f)
- Concave lens: diverges light; virtual image
- Concave mirror: converges light; can form real, virtual, or no image
- Convex mirror: diverges; virtual image (used in vehicles - rearview)

**Lens Formula:** $\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$ (sign convention: real is positive, virtual negative)

**Mirror Formula:** $\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$

**Magnification**: m = v/u = h_image/h_object

**Dispersion**: Splitting of white light into 7 colors (VIBGYOR)
- Cause: different wavelengths refract differently
- Prism made of glass

**Rainbow**: Caused by dispersion, refraction, and internal reflection of light in raindrops

**Scattering of Light**: Tyndall effect; Rayleigh scattering (blue sky, red sunset)

### 1.8 Electricity and Magnetism

**Electric Charge**: Property due to which body experiences force in electric field
- Types: Positive (+, protons) and Negative (-, electrons)
- Like charges repel; unlike charges attract
- Unit: Coulomb (C)
- Charge is conserved and quantized: q = ne (n = integer, e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)

**Coulomb's Law:** $F = k \cdot \frac{q_1 \cdot q_2}{r^2}$
- k = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²

**Electric Current**: I = Q/t; unit Ampere (A)
- 1 A = 1 C/s
- Conventional current: + to -
- Electron flow: - to +

**Ohm's Law:** V = I·R (at constant temperature)
- R = ρL/A (resistance depends on length, area, material)
- ρ = resistivity (Ω·m)

**Resistors in Series:** R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
**Resistors in Parallel:** $\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$

**Joule's Law of Heating:** H = I²Rt = VIt (Heat produced in resistor)
- Applications: heater, electric iron, fuse, toaster

**Electric Power:** P = VI = I²R = V²/R
- Energy consumed: E = P × t
- Unit: Watt-second (Joule); commercial unit: kWh

**Magnetism:**
- **Bar magnet** has North (N) and South (S) poles
- Like poles repel; unlike poles attract
- Magnetic field lines emerge from N and enter S
- Earth's geographic North is magnetic South

**Electromagnet**: Current-carrying coil; magnetic field depends on current, turns
- Right-Hand Thumb Rule: Grip conductor with right hand; thumb points in current direction, fingers point in field direction

**Electromagnetic Induction (Faraday's Law):** Induced EMF = -N(dΦ/dt)
- Lenz's Law: Induced current opposes the change
- Applications: generator, transformer, electric motor

**Alternating Current (AC) vs Direct Current (DC):**
- AC: 220V, 50 Hz in India
- DC: flows in one direction

**Transformer**: Step-up (increases voltage) or step-down (decreases voltage)
- $\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} = \frac{I_p}{I_s}$

## 2. Chemistry — Matter, Atoms, Reactions

### 2.1 Matter and its States

**Matter**: Anything that has mass and occupies space

**States of Matter:**
1. **Solid**: Fixed shape & volume; particles tightly packed
2. **Liquid**: Fixed volume, variable shape; particles loosely packed
3. **Gas**: Variable shape & volume; particles far apart
4. **Plasma**: Ionized gas (e.g., stars, lightning, fluorescent tubes)
5. **Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC)**: Ultra-cold state (5th state, 1995)
6. **Fermionic Condensate**: 6th state (2004)

**Changes:**
- **Physical change**: Reversible; no new substance (e.g., ice melting)
- **Chemical change**: New substance formed; usually irreversible

**Properties:**
- Extensive (depend on amount): mass, volume, length
- Intensive (don't depend on amount): density, temperature, color

### 2.2 Atomic Structure

**Atom**: Smallest unit of element; composed of protons, neutrons, electrons
- **Proton**: +1 charge; ~1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg; in nucleus
- **Neutron**: 0 charge; ~1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg; in nucleus
- **Electron**: -1 charge; ~9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; in orbitals (1/1836 of proton mass)

**Atomic Number (Z)**: Number of protons
**Mass Number (A)**: Protons + Neutrons
**Isotopes**: Same Z, different A (e.g., ¹H, ²H, ³H)
**Isobars**: Same A, different Z
**Isotones**: Same number of neutrons

**Bohr's Model (1913):**
- Electrons orbit in fixed energy levels (shells)
- Shells: K (n=1), L (n=2), M (n=3), N (n=4), O (n=5), P (n=6), Q (n=7)
- Maximum electrons: 2n²
- Electrons fill innermost shell first

**Quantum Numbers:**
- Principal (n): shell number
- Azimuthal (l): subshell (0 to n-1); s, p, d, f
- Magnetic (m): orbital orientation
- Spin (s): electron spin (+1/2 or -1/2)

### 2.3 Periodic Table

**Father of Periodic Table**: Dmitri Mendeleev (1869) — arranged by atomic mass
**Modern Periodic Law**: Henry Moseley (1913) — arranged by atomic number
**Long Form of Periodic Table**: Based on Aufbau principle (Bohr-Bury scheme)

**18 Groups (vertical) and 7 Periods (horizontal)**

**Block Classification:**
- **s-block**: Groups 1, 2 (alkali metals, alkaline earth metals)
- **p-block**: Groups 13-18 (boron to noble gases)
- **d-block**: Groups 3-12 (transition metals)
- **f-block**: Lanthanides & Actinides (inner transition)

**Key Elements & Symbols:**
- H (Hydrogen, 1) - lightest
- He (Helium, 2) - lightest noble gas
- C (Carbon, 6) - basis of organic life
- N (Nitrogen, 7) - 78% of atmosphere
- O (Oxygen, 8) - 21% of atmosphere
- Fe (Iron, 26) - most abundant metal in Earth's crust
- Cu (Copper, 29) - good conductor
- Ag (Silver, 47) - best conductor
- Au (Gold, 79) - most malleable
- Hg (Mercury, 80) - only metal liquid at room temperature
- Pb (Lead, 82) - heaviest stable element
- U (Uranium, 92) - heaviest naturally occurring
- Pu (Plutonium, 94) - used in nuclear weapons

**Periodic Trends:**
- Atomic radius: decreases left to right; increases top to bottom
- Ionization energy: increases left to right; decreases top to bottom
- Electronegativity: increases left to right; decreases top to bottom
- Metallic character: decreases left to right; increases top to bottom

### 2.4 Chemical Bonding

**Types of Bonds:**
1. **Ionic (Electrovalent)**: Transfer of electrons; metal + non-metal (e.g., NaCl)
2. **Covalent**: Sharing of electrons; non-metal + non-metal (e.g., H₂O, CH₄)
3. **Metallic**: Metal atoms sharing electrons in "sea"
4. **Coordinate (Dative)**: Both electrons from one atom (e.g., NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺)
5. **Hydrogen Bond**: Special dipole-dipole; in H₂O, DNA, proteins
6. **Van der Waals**: Weak intermolecular forces

**Octet Rule**: Atoms tend to have 8 electrons in outer shell (2 for H, He)

**Exceptions**: H, He, B, Al, transition elements, lanthanides

### 2.5 Acids, Bases, and Salts

**Acid**: Sour taste; turns blue litmus red; pH < 7
- Examples: HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, CH₃COOH (acetic acid in vinegar)
- Strong acids: HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃
- Weak acids: CH₃COOH, H₂CO₃, H₃BO₃

**Base**: Bitter taste; turns red litmus blue; pH > 7
- Examples: NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂, NH₄OH
- Soluble bases = alkalis (NaOH, KOH)

**Indicator**: Substance that shows acidic/basic nature by color change
- Litmus (red/blue), Methyl orange, Phenolphthalein
- Natural: turmeric, red cabbage, Hydrangea flowers

**pH Scale**: 0 (strongly acidic) to 14 (strongly alkaline); 7 = neutral
- pH = -log[H⁺]

**Chemical Reactions:**
- Acid + Metal → Salt + H₂ (e.g., Zn + HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂)
- Acid + Base → Salt + Water (Neutralization)
- Acid + Carbonate/Bicarbonate → Salt + H₂O + CO₂
- Acid + Metal Oxide → Salt + Water
- Base + Metal → Salt + H₂ (for amphoteric metals like Al, Zn)

**Important Salts:**
- **NaCl (Common Salt)**: From sea water, rock salt
- **Na₂CO₃ (Washing Soda)**: Used in glass, soap, paper
- **NaHCO₃ (Baking Soda)**: Cooking, antacid, fire extinguisher
- **CaSO₄·2H₂O (Gypsum)**: Used in fertilizers, plaster
- **CaSO₄·½H₂O (Plaster of Paris)**: Used in setting bones, casts
- **CaOCl₂ (Bleaching Powder)**: Water purification, bleaching
- **K₂SO₄·Al₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O (Potash Alum)**: Purification of water

**Plaster of Paris:**
- Made by heating gypsum at 373K
- CaSO₄·2H₂O →(373K)→ CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O
- Sets with water: CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O

### 2.6 Metals and Non-Metals

**Properties of Metals:**
- Lustrous (shiny)
- Malleable (beaten into sheets)
- Ductile (drawn into wires)
- Conduct heat and electricity
- Sonorous (produce sound)
- High melting/boiling points
- Solid at room temperature (except Hg)
- Form basic oxides

**Properties of Non-Metals:**
- Non-lustrous (dull)
- Non-malleable, non-ductile (brittle)
- Poor conductors (insulators)
- Low melting/boiling points
- Solid, liquid, or gas at room temperature
- Form acidic oxides

**Exceptions:**
- Iodine (non-metal but lustrous)
- Sodium (soft metal)
- Mercury (liquid metal)
- Graphite (non-metal but conducts electricity)
- Hydrogen (non-metal but placed with alkali metals)

**Reactivity Series (from most to least reactive):**
K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au

**Extraction of Metals:**
1. **Roasting**: Heating ore in presence of oxygen (for sulfide ores)
2. **Calcination**: Heating ore in absence of oxygen (for carbonate ores)
3. **Reduction**: Reducing metal oxide to metal
4. **Electrolytic Reduction**: For highly reactive metals (Al, Na, K)

**Alloys:**
- **Brass**: Cu + Zn
- **Bronze**: Cu + Sn
- **Steel**: Fe + C (plus other elements)
- **Stainless Steel**: Fe + Cr + Ni
- **Solder**: Pb + Sn
- **Alnico**: Al + Ni + Co
- **Duralumin**: Al + Cu + Mn + Mg

**Corrosion:**
- Iron rusting: Fe + O₂ + H₂O → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust)
- Prevention: painting, oiling, galvanizing (Zn coating), electroplating, alloying
- **Rancidity**: Oxidation of fats/oils; prevented by antioxidants, nitrogen flushing

### 2.7 Carbon Compounds

**Organic Chemistry**: Chemistry of carbon compounds
- Carbon forms covalent bonds (4 valence electrons)
- Can form long chains, branches, rings
- Tetravalent, catenation (self-linking)

**Functional Groups:**
- -OH: Alcohol
- -CHO: Aldehyde
- -COOH: Carboxylic Acid
- -C=O: Ketone
- -NH₂: Amine

**Hydrocarbons:**
- **Saturated (Single bonds)**: Alkanes (Methane CH₄, Ethane C₂H₆, Propane C₃H₈, Butane C₄H₁₀)
- **Unsaturated**: Alkenes (C=C, Ethene C₂H₄), Alkynes (C≡C, Ethyne C₂H₂)
- **Aromatic**: Benzene (C₆H₆) — 6 carbon ring

**Important Compounds:**
- **Methane (CH₄)**: Simplest hydrocarbon; biogas, natural gas
- **Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)**: Alcohol in beverages; fuel
- **Acetic Acid (CH₃COOH)**: Vinegar
- **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)**: Simple sugar
- **Sucrose (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁)**: Common sugar

**Polymers:**
- **Natural**: Cotton (cellulose), Silk, Wool, Rubber, Starch, Proteins, DNA
- **Synthetic**: PVC, Nylon, Polyester, Teflon, Polythene, Bakelite

### 2.8 Chemical Reactions (Quick Recap)

1. **Combination (Synthesis)**: A + B → AB
   - 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
   - CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂

2. **Decomposition**: AB → A + B
   - 2FeSO₄ →(Heat)→ Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃
   - CaCO₃ →(Heat)→ CaO + CO₂

3. **Displacement (Single Replacement)**: A + BC → AC + B
   - Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
   - More reactive displaces less reactive

4. **Double Displacement (Metathesis)**: AB + CD → AD + CB
   - Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl
   - Forms precipitate, gas, or water

5. **Oxidation-Reduction (Redox)**:
   - Oxidation: gain of O, loss of H, loss of e⁻
   - Reduction: loss of O, gain of H, gain of e⁻
   - Oxidizing agent: causes oxidation; itself reduced
   - Reducing agent: causes reduction; itself oxidized

6. **Exothermic**: Releases heat
   - Combustion, respiration, neutralization
   - C + O₂ → CO₂ + heat

7. **Endothermic**: Absorbs heat
   - Photosynthesis, melting, evaporation
   - CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (heat absorbed)

## 3. Biology — Cell, Plants, Animals, Human Body

### 3.1 Cell — The Basic Unit of Life

**Cell Theory** (Schleiden, Schwann, 1839):
1. All living organisms are composed of cells
2. Cell is the basic unit of life
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells (Virchow)

**Cell Types:**
- **Prokaryotic**: No nucleus (bacteria, archaea); smaller; no membrane-bound organelles
- **Eukaryotic**: True nucleus (animals, plants, fungi, protists); larger; membrane-bound organelles

**Cell Organelles:**
- **Nucleus**: Control center; contains DNA
- **Mitochondria**: "Powerhouse of cell"; ATP production; cellular respiration
- **Ribosomes**: Protein synthesis
- **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)**: 
  - Rough ER: protein synthesis
  - Smooth ER: lipid synthesis
- **Golgi Apparatus**: Packaging, modification
- **Lysosomes**: Digestion (suicide bags)
- **Vacuoles**: Storage
- **Chloroplasts**: Photosynthesis (only in plants)
- **Cell Wall**: Cellulose (plants); Peptidoglycan (bacteria)
- **Plasma Membrane**: Phospholipid bilayer; selectively permeable

**Cell Division:**
- **Mitosis**: One cell divides into 2 identical daughter cells; for growth, repair
  - Phases: Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase
- **Meiosis**: One cell divides into 4 haploid cells; for gamete formation
  - 2 divisions: Meiosis I (reduction) + Meiosis II (equational)

**Chromosomes**: 46 in humans (23 pairs); 22 pairs autosomes + 1 pair sex chromosomes
- Female: XX
- Male: XY

### 3.2 Plant Kingdom

**Major Groups:**
1. **Thallophyta** (Algae): No root, stem, leaf; no vascular tissue
2. **Bryophyta** (Mosses, Liverworts): No vascular tissue; need water for reproduction
3. **Pteridophyta** (Ferns): Vascular tissue; no seeds
4. **Phanerogams** (Seed plants):
   - **Gymnospermae**: Naked seeds; no flowers (conifers)
   - **Angiospermae**: Flowering plants; seeds enclosed in fruit
     - **Monocots**: 1 cotyledon (rice, wheat, maize, sugarcane)
     - **Dicots**: 2 cotyledons (gram, pea, bean, mango)

**Plant Parts & Functions:**
- **Root**: Anchorage, absorption of water & minerals
  - Tap root (dicot), Fibrous root (monocot)
- **Stem**: Support, transport (xylem, phloem)
- **Leaf**: Photosynthesis
  - Stomata: gas exchange
- **Flower**: Reproduction
  - Male: stamen (anther + filament)
  - Female: pistil (stigma, style, ovary)
- **Fruit**: Mature ovary
- **Seed**: Embryo + food storage

**Photosynthesis:**
$$6CO_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{light, chlorophyll}} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2$$
- Takes place in chloroplasts
- Requires sunlight, chlorophyll, water, CO₂
- Produces glucose and oxygen

**Plant Hormones:**
- Auxin: Cell elongation, apical dominance
- Gibberellin: Stem elongation, germination
- Cytokinin: Cell division
- Abscisic Acid (ABA): Dormancy, stress response
- Ethylene: Fruit ripening, senescence

### 3.3 Animal Kingdom

**Classification:**
1. **Porifera** (Sponges): No tissues; pores
2. **Cnidaria** (Jellyfish, Corals): Radial symmetry; stinging cells
3. **Platyhelminthes** (Flatworms): Bilateral; flat
4. **Nematoda** (Roundworms): Bilateral; cylindrical
5. **Annelida** (Earthworms): Segmented body
6. **Arthropoda** (Insects, Crustaceans): Exoskeleton; jointed legs; largest phylum
7. **Mollusca** (Snails, Octopus): Soft body, often with shell
8. **Echinodermata** (Starfish): Spiny skin; radial symmetry
9. **Chordata** (Vertebrates): Notochord, dorsal nerve cord
   - **Pisces** (Fish): Gills; cold-blooded; 2-chambered heart
   - **Amphibia** (Frogs): Dual life; 3-chambered heart
   - **Reptilia** (Lizards, Snakes): 3-chambered heart (4 in crocodile); cold-blooded
   - **Aves** (Birds): 4-chambered heart; warm-blooded; feathers
   - **Mammalia** (Mammals): 4-chambered heart; warm-blooded; mammary glands

**Vertebrate Heart Comparison:**
- Fish: 2 chambers (1 auricle + 1 ventricle)
- Amphibians: 3 chambers (2 auricles + 1 ventricle)
- Reptiles: 3 chambers (4 in crocodile)
- Birds & Mammals: 4 chambers (2 auricles + 2 ventricles)

### 3.4 Human Body Systems

**Digestive System:** Mouth → Esophagus → Stomach → Small Intestine → Large Intestine → Rectum
- Enzymes: Amylase (saliva), Pepsin (stomach), Trypsin (pancreas), Bile (liver)
- Absorption: Mainly in small intestine (villi increase surface area)
- Liver: largest gland; produces bile; stores glycogen; detoxifies

**Respiratory System:** Nose → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Lungs → Alveoli
- Gas exchange in alveoli
- Inhalation: O₂ in; Exhalation: CO₂ out
- **Hemoglobin** carries oxygen in RBCs

**Circulatory System:** Heart (4 chambers) → Arteries → Capillaries → Veins → Heart
- Pulmonary circulation: Heart ↔ Lungs
- Systemic circulation: Heart ↔ Rest of body
- Blood groups: A, B, AB, O; ABO and Rh systems
- Universal donor: O negative; Universal recipient: AB positive

**Nervous System:** Brain + Spinal Cord + Nerves
- Brain: Cerebrum (thinking), Cerebellum (balance), Medulla (vital functions)
- Neuron: Functional unit; dendrites, cell body, axon
- Reflex arc: Receptor → Sensory neuron → Spinal cord → Motor neuron → Effector

**Endocrine System:** Glands producing hormones
- Pituitary: "Master gland"
- Thyroid: Thyroxine (metabolism)
- Pancreas: Insulin & Glucagon (blood sugar)
- Adrenal: Adrenaline (fight or flight)
- Reproductive glands: Testosterone, Estrogen, Progesterone

**Skeletal System:** 206 bones in adult human
- Longest: Femur (thigh)
- Smallest: Stapes (ear)
- Joints: Hinge (knee), Ball-and-socket (shoulder), Pivot (neck)

**Excretory System:** Kidneys → Ureters → Bladder → Urethra
- Filter blood; produce urine
- Nephrons are functional units

**Reproductive System:**
- Male: Testes (produce sperm + testosterone)
- Female: Ovaries (produce ova + estrogen/progesterone)
- Fertilization: Sperm + Ovum → Zygote (in fallopian tube)
- Implantation: Uterus

### 3.5 Nutrition & Health

**Nutrients:**
- **Carbohydrates**: Energy (4 kcal/g)
- **Proteins**: Building blocks; 20 amino acids (9 essential)
- **Fats**: Energy (9 kcal/g)
- **Vitamins**: Organic; A, B-complex, C, D, E, K
- **Minerals**: Inorganic; Fe, Ca, P, K, Na, I, Zn
- **Water**: 60-70% of body weight

**Vitamin Deficiency Diseases:**
- Vitamin A: Night blindness
- Vitamin B1 (Thiamine): Beriberi
- Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin): Cheilosis
- Vitamin B3 (Niacin): Pellagra
- Vitamin B6: Anemia
- Vitamin B9 (Folic acid): Anemia, neural tube defects
- Vitamin B12: Pernicious anemia
- Vitamin C: Scurvy
- Vitamin D: Rickets (children), Osteomalacia (adults)
- Vitamin E: Sterility
- Vitamin K: Slow blood clotting

**Mineral Deficiency Diseases:**
- Iron: Anemia
- Iodine: Goiter, cretinism
- Calcium: Osteoporosis, rickets
- Phosphorus: Bone weakness

**Common Diseases:**
- **Communicable**: TB, Malaria, Cholera, Typhoid, AIDS, COVID-19, Dengue
- **Non-communicable**: Diabetes, Hypertension, Cancer, Heart disease
- **Vector-borne**: Malaria (Anopheles mosquito), Dengue (Aedes), Filariasis (Culex)
- **Airborne**: TB, Influenza, COVID-19

**Pathogens:**
- Bacteria: TB, Cholera, Typhoid, Plague
- Viruses: AIDS, COVID-19, Influenza, Dengue
- Protozoa: Malaria (Plasmodium), Amoebiasis
- Fungi: Ringworm, Athlete's foot

**Antibiotics**: Inhibit/kill bacteria; not effective against viruses
- Discovered by Alexander Fleming (1928) — Penicillin

### 3.6 Genetics & Evolution

**Mendel's Laws:**
- **Law of Dominance**: One allele dominates the other
- **Law of Segregation**: Two alleles separate during gamete formation
- **Law of Independent Assortment**: Genes on different chromosomes are inherited independently

**Genotype vs Phenotype:**
- Genotype: Genetic makeup (e.g., Rr, RR, rr)
- Phenotype: Physical expression
- Dominant: Expresses in both homozygous and heterozygous
- Recessive: Expresses only in homozygous

**DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid):**
- Double helix (Watson & Crick, 1953)
- Bases: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Cytosine (C), Guanine (G)
- A=T; C≡G (complementary base pairing)
- RNA: Single-stranded; U (Uracil) replaces T

**Blood Group Inheritance:**
- ABO system: Multiple alleles (I^A, I^B, i)
- Rh factor: + or -

**Evolution:**
- Darwin's theory: Natural selection; "Origin of Species" (1859)
- Lamarck: Inheritance of acquired characters (disproved)

### 3.7 Environment & Ecology (Brief)

**Ecosystem**: Biotic + Abiotic components interacting
- **Producers**: Plants (autotrophs)
- **Consumers**: Animals (heterotrophs) — Primary (herbivores), Secondary (carnivores), Tertiary (top carnivores)
- **Decomposers**: Bacteria, fungi

**Food Chain**: Producers → Primary consumers → Secondary → Tertiary
- Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle
- Each level = **Trophic level**
- 10% energy transfer (Lindeman's law)

**Food Web**: Interconnected food chains

**Ecological Pyramids:**
- Pyramid of Numbers (in grassland: grass > insects > frogs > snakes)
- Pyramid of Biomass
- Pyramid of Energy (always upright)

**Biogeochemical Cycles:** Water, Carbon, Oxygen, Nitrogen, Phosphorus

**Biodiversity**: Variety of life
- Hotspots: 4 in India — Western Ghats, Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Sundaland
- India: 12 biodiversity hotspots? (Verify - India is in 4 of 36 global hotspots)

**Pollution:**
- Air: Particulate matter, SO₂, NOₓ, CO, CO₂, Ozone
- Water: BOD, COD, pathogens, heavy metals
- Soil: Pesticides, industrial waste
- Noise: >85 dB harmful
- Thermal: Heated water discharge
- Nuclear: Radioactive waste

**Greenhouse Effect**: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs trap heat
- Global warming: Average temperature rise
- **Kyoto Protocol (1997)**: Reduce GHG emissions
- **Paris Agreement (2015)**: Limit warming to 1.5-2°C above pre-industrial
- **Montreal Protocol (1987)**: Protect ozone layer (CFC phase-out)

**Ozone Layer**: Stratosphere; absorbs UV radiation
- Ozone hole: Antarctica (largest)

**Acid Rain**: pH < 5.6; SO₂ + NOₓ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ + HNO₃
