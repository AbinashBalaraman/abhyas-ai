## 1. Prehistoric Period (Stone Age)

### 1.1 Three Ages of Prehistory

| Age | Time Period | Tool Type | Key Sites |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paleolithic (Old Stone)** | 5,00,000 BCE – 10,000 BCE | Hand axes, cleavers, choppers | Bhimbetka (MP), Kurnool (AP), Attirampakkam (TN) |
| **Mesolithic (Middle Stone)** | 10,000 BCE – 8,000 BCE | Microliths (small flint tools) | Bagor (Rajasthan), Langhnaj (Gujarat), Adamgarh (MP) |
| **Neolithic (New Stone)** | 8,000 BCE – 4,000 BCE | Polished stone tools, pottery, agriculture | Mehrgarh (Balochistan), Burzahom (Kashmir), Lahuradewa (UP), Koldihwa (UP), Gufkral (Kashmir), Imlidih (MP) |
| **Chalcolithic (Copper-Stone)** | 4,000 BCE – 1,500 BCE | Copper + stone tools | Ahar (Rajasthan), Jorwe (Maharashtra), Malwa (MP) |

### 1.2 Key Findings

- **Bhimbetka** (MP) — Discovered by **V.S. Wakankar** (1957-58); UNESCO World Heritage Site (2003); 7,000+ years of human activity; rock shelters with paintings depicting hunting, dancing, animals
- **Mehrgarh** (Balochistan, Pakistan) — Earliest agricultural community (7000 BCE); cotton traces; transition from hunting to farming
- **Koldihwa** (UP) — Earliest evidence of rice cultivation (c. 7000 BCE)
- **Burzahom** (Kashmir) — Pit dwellings; dog burials; unique Neolithic culture
- **Lahuradewa** (UP) — Early rice agriculture; transition to settled life

## 2. Indus Valley Civilization (Harappan Civilization)

### 2.1 Overview

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Time Period** | Early Phase: 6000-2600 BCE; Mature Phase: 2600-1900 BCE; Declining Phase: 1900-1300 BCE |
| **Type** | Protohistoric; Bronze Age (Copper + Tin); Planned Urban Civilization |
| **Area** | 1,299,000 sq km (largest of 4 ancient civilizations); triangular shape |
| **First Information** | Charles Mason (1826) |
| **Father of Indian Archaeology** | Alexander Cunningham (1861) |
| **Harappa Discovery** | Rai Bahadur Daya Ram Sahni (1921) under John Marshall |

| **Official Declaration** | John Marshall (1924), Director General of ASI |
| **Naming** | "Indus Valley Civilization" (John Marshall); "Harappan" (first excavated site); "Indus-Saraswati" (Saraswati valley sites); "Meluha" (Mesopotamian name) |

### 2.2 Major Sites — Complete Table

| Site | River | Location | Excavator | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Harappa** | Ravi | Sahiwal, Punjab (PK) | Daya Ram Sahni (1921) | First excavated site; "Semi-industrial city"; Earth Goddess evidence; Granary (45.71m × 15.23m); Nataraja; bronze cart; Cemetery R-37 |
| **Mohenjo-Daro** | Indus | Larkana, Sindh (PK) | R.D. Banerjee (1922) | "Mound of Dead"; Great Bath; Bronze Dancing Girl (Tribhanga pose); Pashupati Seal; Priest-King statue (steatite, 17.5 cm); "City of Stupas" |
| **Lothal** | Bhogava | Ahmedabad, Gujarat | S.R. Rao (1954-55) | "Mini Harappa"; Dockyard (world's earliest tidal port); Fire altars; Bead-making factories; 3 paired burials (Sati evidence); Rice evidence |
| **Dholavira** | Manhar/Mansar | Kutch, Gujarat | Jagpati Joshi & R.S. Bisht | India's 40th World Heritage Site (2021); 3-part division (citadel, middle town, lower town); 16 rock-cut reservoirs; Advanced water management; Signboard |
| **Kalibangan** | Ghaggar/Saraswati | Hanumangarh, Rajasthan | L.P. Tessitori, A. Ghosh, B.B. Lal | "Black bangles"; Ploughed fields (mixed cropping); Fire altars (Agnikund); Decorated bricks; Paired burials; "Impoverished city" |
| **Rakhigarhi** | Ghaggar-Hakra | Hisar, Haryana | Suraj Bhan, Amrendra Nath, Vasant Shinde | **Largest Harappan site**; Cylindrical seal; Terracotta figurine |
| **Banawali** | Saraswati | Hisar, Haryana | R.S. Bisht | Clay plough evidence; Barley; 6 granaries; Pre-Harappan + Harappan phases |

| **Chanhudaro** | Indus | Sindh (PK) | N.G. Majumdar (1931) | "Industrial city"; Bead-making factories; **Only city without a citadel**; Beauty products (lipstick, perfume, eyeliner) |
| **Ropar** | Sutlej | Rupnagar, Punjab | Yajnadatta Sharma (1953) | First excavation after independence; Pit grave (human + dog) |
| **Sanauli** | — | Baghpat, UP | ASI | Post-Harappan cemetery; 3 chariots found |
| **Shortughai** | — | Afghanistan | — | Major trading colony; Lapis lazuli source |
| **Kot Diji** | Indus | Khairpur, Sindh (PK) | John Marshall & F.A. Khan | Pre-Harappan site |

### 2.3 Town Planning

- **Grid system**: Chessboard pattern; right-angle intersections; main road width ~10 meters
- **Mohenjo-daro intersection**: Called "Oxford Circus"
- **Two-part city**: Citadel (West, elite class) + Lower Town (East, common people)
- **Exceptions**: Dholavira and Rakhigarhi have 3 parts; absence of fortification in some areas
- **Drainage**: Unique feature; parallel to roads; baked brick with gypsum and lime; manhole covers
- **Houses**: 4-5 rooms + courtyard + kitchen + bathroom; wells in most houses; 2-story houses; doors not facing main road (exception: Lothal)
- **Bricks**: Standardized ratio 4:2:1; baked brick for houses/drains; unbaked for roads/fortification

### 2.4 Major Structures

| Structure | Location | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Great Bath** | Mohenjo-Daro | 39 ft × 23 ft × 8 ft; baked brick with gypsum and bitumen; used for religious rituals |
| **Granary** | Harappa | Largest building of IVC; 45.71m × 15.23m; traces of wheat and barley; 2 rows of 6 rooms |
| **Dockyard** | Lothal | On Bhogava river; trade center with West Asia and Africa; National Maritime Heritage Complex approved (2019) |

### 2.5 Script, Seals & Art

- **Script**: Pictorial; boustrophedon style (left-to-right then right-to-left); ~400 signs; **undeciphered**; inscribed on seals, pottery, tools, tablets
- **Longest inscription**: 26-34 symbols
- **Seals**: Steatite material; square/cylindrical/circular; most common: Unicorn and humped bull; Pashupati seal (Mohenjo-Daro)
- **Sculptures**: Bronze Dancing Girl (Lost Wax technique, Tribhanga pose); Priest-King (steatite, 17.5 cm); Yaksha/Yakshi figures
- **Pottery**: Red ware with black design; plain pottery prominent; polychrome rare

### 2.6 Economy & Trade

- **Agriculture**: Wheat, barley, cotton (first producer in world; Greeks called it "Sindon"), rice (rare, Lothal), lentil, chickpea, sesame, millets
- **Animals**: Oxen, bulls, camels, dogs, goats, buffaloes; horse remains at Surkotada, Kalibangan, Lothal
- **Trade**: Mesopotamia (Sumer, Akkad); Dilmun (Bahrain); Magan (Oman); barter system
- **Weights**: Cubical weights (coefficient of 16); ivory scales at Lothal and Mohenjo-Daro
- **Metals**: Copper (Khetri, Rajasthan; Balochistan; Oman); Gold (South India/Karnataka); Silver (Rajasthan, Afghanistan); Tin (Iran, Afghanistan); Lapis Lazuli (Badakhshan, Afghanistan)

### 2.7 Religious Beliefs

- **Mother Goddess**: Worshipped as goddess of fertility
- **Pashupati/Shiva**: Proto-Shiva on Pashupati seal (Mohenjo-Daro)
- **Nature worship**: Peepal tree, humped bull
- **Fire altars**: Lothal, Kalibangan, Banawali
- **Burial practices**: Cremation (Mohenjo-Daro); coffin burial (Harappa); extended burial (Sanauli); joint/paired burial (Lothal, Kalibangan); urn burial (Mohenjo-Daro); North-South orientation

### 2.8 Decline (1900 BCE)

| Theory | Historian/Scholar |
|---|---|
| Floods | Marshall, Mackay, S.R. Rao |
| Aryan invasion | Gordon Childe, Mortimer Wheeler, Stuart Piggott |
| Climate change | Aurel Stein, Amalananda Ghosh |
| Geological change | M.R. Sahni, H.T. Lambrick, G.F. Dales |
| Pandemic | K.U.R. Kennedy |
| Continuous low rainfall (900 years) | IIT Kharagpur study (Tso Moriri lake, Ladakh) |

### 3.2 Rig Vedic / Early Vedic Period (1500-1000 BCE)

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Region** | Sapta Sindhu (7 rivers: Sindhu, Saraswati, Shutudri/Sutlej, Vipasha/Beas, Parushni/Ravi, Vitasta/Jhelum, Asikni/Chenab) |
| **Society** | Tribal; jana → janata; patriarchal |
| **Polity** | Chief (rajan) elected by sabha/samiti; no standing army |
| **Economy** | Pastoral (cattle central); limited agriculture; no iron |
| **Women** | Relatively higher status; attended sabha/samiti; education; no purdah |
| **Varna** | 4-fold: Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra (not rigid yet; based on occupation) |
| **Gods** | Indra (most invoked, 250+ hymns), Agni, Varuna, Surya, Soma |
| **Important rivers** | Saraswati = "Naditama" (most sacred); Sindhu = most mentioned |
| **Battles** | Battle of Ten Kings (Dasarajna) — King Sudas (Bharata tribe) vs. 10 tribes, on Parushni (Ravi) river |

### 3.3 Vedic Literature — Complete Structure

| Text | Description |
|---|---|
| **Rigveda** | Oldest Veda; 10 Mandalas, 85 Anuvakas, 1028 Suktas, 10,552 Richas; Reciter: Hotri; Compared to Persian Zend Avesta |
| **Samaveda** | "Singing Veda"; smallest; 1,895 mantras; oldest text of Indian music; Reciter: Udgata |
| **Yajurveda** | "Sacrifice Veda"; prose + verse; Reciter: Adhvaryu; Shukla (Shatapatha Brahmana — longest) & Krishna branches |
| **Atharvaveda** | Newest Veda; magic, medicine, charms; 20 chapters, 730 hymns, 5,987 mantras; Reciter: Brahma; mentions Magadha and Anga |
| **Brahmanas** | Explain rituals; Shatapatha Brahmana (longest, 14 cantos, 100 chapters) |
| **Aranyakas** | "Forest texts"; for Vanaprasthas |
| **Upanishads** | Philosophical; 108 total; "Vedanta"; oldest: Chandogya; largest: Brihadaranyaka; smallest: Mandukya |

### 3.4 Key Upanishads

### 3.6 Later Vedic Period (1000-600 BCE)

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Region** | Expansion to Ganga Valley; iron & silver mentioned; Ganga, Yamuna, Narmada mentioned |
| **Society** | Territorial (janapada); varna rigidified; 4 ashramas: Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sannyasa |
| **Polity** | Kingdoms; hereditary monarchy; taxation; large armies |
| **Economy** | Agriculture dominant; iron use (c. 1000 BCE); trade guilds |
| **Women** | Status declined; early marriage; Upanayana restricted; no access to sabha |
| **Gods** | Prajapati (creator); Vishnu, Shiva gain prominence; Rudra → Shiva |
| **Important texts** | Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads; Shatapatha Brahmana; Aitareya |
| **Cults** | Vaishnavism and Shaivism emerge |

### 3.7 Smritis (Hindu Law Books)

| Smriti | Period | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Manu Smriti** | Shunga Age | Oldest; 12 chapters; Commentator: Medhatithi |
| **Yajnavalkya Smriti** | Post-Mauryan | Commentator: Vijnaneshwara (Mitakshara) |
| **Narada Smriti** | Gupta Age | Commentator: Asahaya |
| **Devala Smriti** | Pre-Medieval | — |

### 3.8 Puranas

- 18 Puranas; compiler: Lomaharsha or son Ugrasrava
- Only 5 contain genealogies of kings: Matsya, Vayu, Vishnu, Brahmanda, Bhagavata
- **Matsya Purana** — most ancient and authentic
- Vishnu Purana → Maurya Dynasty; Matsya Purana → Satavahana Dynasty; Vayu Purana → Gupta Dynasty
- Agni Purana → Agriculture + Monarchy

## 5. Jainism (6th Century BCE)

### 5.1 Overview

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founder** | Rishabhadeva (1st Tirthankara); Mahavira (24th, real founder) |
| **Tirthankaras** | 24 total; 1st: Rishabhadeva; 23rd: Parshvanath (c. 872 BCE); 24th: Vardhaman Mahavira (540-468 BCE) |
| **Mahavira's birth** | 540 BCE at Kundagrama near Vaishali (Bihar); father: Siddhartha; mother: Trishala |
| **Renunciation** | Left home at age 30 |
| **Kaivalya (Enlightenment)** | At age 42, under a Sal tree on banks of Rijupalika river near Jrimbhik village |
| **Death (Nirvana)** | 468 BCE at Pavapuri (Bihar) |
| **Wife** | Yashoda; daughter: Priyadarshana |
| **Followers** | Called "Nirgrantha" |
| **Sacred texts** | **Angas** (14 texts compiled from Mahavira's teachings); written in semi-Magadhi Prakrit; written down at **Valabhi, Gujarat** (~1500 years ago) |
| **Key concepts** | Karma (cause of birth/death); Kaivalya (enlightenment); Sallekhana/Santhara (ritual fast unto death); Rebirth |
| **Festivals** | Paryushan Parva (main festival; self-purification) |

### 5.2 Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Jina** | "The Conqueror" — one who conquered inner enemies (desires) |
| **Tirthankara** | "Ford-maker" — enlightened teacher who shows path to liberation |
| **Kaivalya** | Supreme knowledge/enlightenment in Jainism |
| **Sallekhana/Santhara** | Voluntary fasting unto death (Shwetambara: Santhara; Digambara: Sallekhana) |
| **Nirgrantha** | "Without bonds" — follower of Jainism |

### 5.3 Important Jain Sites

| Site | Location | Significance |

## 6. Buddhism (6th Century BCE)

### 6.1 Overview

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|

### 6.2 Four Noble Truths (Arya Satya)

1. **Dukkha**: The world is full of suffering
2. **Samudaya**: The cause of suffering is desire
3. **Nirodha**: Suffering can be ended by giving up desire
4. **Magga**: The path to end suffering (Eightfold Path)

### 6.3 Eightfold Path (Astangika Marg)

1. Right Understanding
2. Right Thought
3. Right Speech
4. Right Action
5. Right Livelihood
6. Right Effort
7. Right Mindfulness
8. Right Concentration

- Father: Chanak
- **Discovery**: R. Shamasastry (1905)
- **Subject**: Book on Political Principles (Statecraft & Governance)
- **Comparison**: "The Prince" by Niccolò Machiavelli
- **Mandala Theory**: Theory of Foreign Policy
- **Saptanga Theory**: 7 components of state — King (Svami), Ministers (Amatya), Territory (Janapada), Fort (Durga), Treasury (Kosha), Army/Force (Danda), Ally (Mitra)

### 7.3 Indica (Megasthenes)

- Megasthenes: Ambassador of Seleucus Nicator to Chandragupta's court
- **Pataliputra**: Surrounded by massive walls with 64 gates; 2-3 story wooden houses; king's palace (wood + stone); gardens
- Emperor's procession; female bodyguards
- Divided Indian society into **7 classes**: Philosophers, Farmers, Hunters/Herders, Merchants/Artisans, Warriors, Spies, Ministers

#### Central Administration

- Absolute centralized monarchical; welfare state

| Tirtha | Function |
|---|---|
| Mantri | Minister |
| Samaharta | Revenue collector (tax assessment) |
| Sannidhata | Treasurer |
| Senapati | War department |
| Yuvaraja | King's successor |
| Pradeshta | Judge of criminal court (Kantakashodhana) |
| Purohita | Priest |

Key Adhyakshas:

| Adhyaksha | Function |
|---|---|
| Lakshanadhyaksha | Head of currency and mint |
| Sitadhyaksha | Head of royal agriculture |
| Panyadhyaksha | Head of trade and commerce |
| Shulkadhyaksha | Head of toll collection |
| Mudradhyaksha | Passport officer |
| Akaradhyaksha | Head of mines |
| Navadhyaksha | Head of navy |

#### Provincial Administration

| Province | Capital | Governor |
|---|---|---|
| Uttarapatha | Taxila | Prince (Kumara) |
| Dakshinapatha | Suvarnagiri | Prince |
| Avanti Rashtra | Ujjayini | Prince |
| Eastern (Prachi) | Pataliputra | Prince |
| Kalinga | Tosali | Prince |

- **Yukta**: Link between central, provincial, and local administration
- **Rajuka**: District-level revenue and justice officer

#### Urban Administration (per Megasthenes)

- Council of 30 members; 6 committees of 5 members each:
  1. Industries and crafts
  2. Supervision of foreigners
  3. Records of births and deaths
  4. Trade and commerce
  5. Supervision of sale of manufactured goods
  6. Sales tax

#### Judicial Administration

- Supreme: King
- **Dharmasthiya** (Civil court): Vyavaharika/Dharmastha
- **Kantaka Shodhana** (Criminal court): Pradeshta — "removal of thorns"
- Death penalty for tax evasion

#### Military Administration

- Four-fold army: Infantry, Cavalry, Chariots, Elephants
- Per Megasthenes: 6 committees managing army
- Per Justin: 600,000 soldiers, 50,000 cavalry, 9,000 elephants, 8,000 chariots
- Per Pliny: 600,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry, 9,000 elephants
- Emblem: Peacock; Army Chief: Commander (Senapati)

### 7.7 Mauryan Economy

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Best crop** | Rice |
| **Villages** | Pariharika (tax-free), Ayudhika (military supplies), Kupya (raw materials) |

### 7.8 Mauryan Art

- **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)**: Pottery type; bright black; centers: Kaushambi, Pataliputra
- **Lomas Rishi Cave** (Barabar hills): Built by Dasaratha (grandson of Ashoka) for Ajivikas
- **Sanchi Pillar**: Four Lions; sandstone; ancient names: Kakanaya, Kakanava
- **Didarganj Yakshi**: Chamaragrahini Yakshi found at Didarganj, Patna — symbol of prosperity
- **Yaksha Manibhadra**: Parkham, Mathura (UP)
- **Ashoka Pillar (Sarnath)**: Lion Capital; Red sandstone; National Emblem of India (1950); 24-spoke Ashoka Chakra
- **Sanchi Stupa**: Built by Ashoka with bricks; discovered by Tyler (1818); 3 stupas
- **Bharhut Stupa**: Laid by Ashoka, completed by Pushyamitra Shunga; discovered by Alexander Cunningham (1873); red sandstone; Jataka tales

## 8. Post-Mauryan Period (185 BCE – 320 CE)

### 8.1 Shunga Dynasty (185-75 BCE)

|---|---|---|

### 8.2 Kanva Dynasty (75-28 BCE)

- Founded by Vasudeva Kanva
- Last ruler: Susharma
- Ended by Simuka (Satavahana founder)

### 8.3 Satavahana Dynasty (~1st century BCE – 3rd century CE)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Also known as** | Andhra Bhratya, Andhrajati |
| **Duration** | ~400 years of rule |
| **Founder** | Simuk/Sindhuk/Shiprak (killed Susharma, last Kanva ruler) |
| **Territory** | Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh |
| **Capitals** | Initial: Dhanyakattak (Amaravati, AP); Later: Pratishthan/Paithan (Godavari river, Maharashtra) |
| **Language** | Prakrit; Script: Brahmi |
| **Symbol** | Ujjain symbol (cross with 4 circles) |
| **Source** | Matsya Purana |
| **Significance** | Bridge between North and South India |

#### Major Rulers

| Ruler | Key Details |
|---|---|
| **Satakarni I** | Nanaghat inscription of Nayanika (wife); 2 Ashvamedha + 1 Rajasuya; titles: Dakshinapathapati, Pratishthanapati |
| **Hala** | 17th ruler; poet; Gatha Saptashati (Prakrit, love stories); court scholars: Gunadhya (Brihatkatha), Sarvavarman (Kaatantra grammar) |
| **Gautamiputra Satakarni** | 23rd and greatest ruler; Nasik inscription of Gautami Balashri; titles: "Eka Brahmin" (only Brahmin), "Khatiyapamanamadanam" (crushed Kshatriya pride); defeated Nahapana (Kshaharata dynasty); destroyed Kshaharata dynasty; protector of caste system |
| **Vashishthiputra Pulumavi** | Titles: Dakshinapatheshwar, Andhra Samrat; Kanheri inscription; defeated by Shaka ruler Rudradaman; married Rudradaman's daughter; ships with two rudders on coins |

#### Satavahana Features

- **Matriarchal influence**: Kings named after mothers (Gautamiputra, Vashishthiputra), but succession was patriarchal
- **Coins**: Lead (most), potin, copper, bronze; **no gold coins**; large quantities of Roman coins
- **Economy**: Cotton production; important port: Bharuch (Gujarat)
- **Buddhist centers**: Nagarjunakonda and Amaravati (AP)

### 8.4 Chedi Dynasty (Kalinga)

- King **Kharavela** of Mahameghavahana dynasty (also called Chedi)
- Follower of Jainism; built cave at Udayagiri hill for Jain sages
- **Hathigumpha Inscription** (Elephant Cave): Main source; mentions Mahapadma Nanda's canal in Kalinga

### 8.5 Indo-Greek Rulers

| Ruler | Key Details |
|---|---|
| **Demetrius** | 190 BCE; conquered Afghanistan, Punjab, Sindh; bilingual coins (Greek + Kharoshthi); capital: Sakala/Sialkot |
| **Menander (Milinda)** | Most renowned; converted to Buddhism by monk Nagasena; "Milindapanha" (dialogue between Milinda and Nagasena); Hinayana sect; Dharmachakra on coins |

**Indo-Greek Contributions**: First gold coins in India; names of rulers on coins; Hellenistic art; Yavanika (curtain trend)

### 8.6 Shakas (Scythians)

Five branches from Central Asia:

| Branch | Location | Key Rulers |
|---|---|---|
| **Northern Satraps** | Taxila | Maues; Rajul/Rajbul (defeated Indo-Greeks); Shodash |
| **Western Satraps (Nashik)** | Nashik | Nahapana (Kshaharata dynasty) |
| **Western Satraps (Ujjain)** | Ujjain | Chashtan (founder, Kardamaka dynasty); Rudradaman; Rudrasimha III (last) |

#### Rudradaman I (130-150 CE)

- Greatest Saka ruler; territory: Sindh, Konkan, Narmada Valley, Malwa, Gujarat
- Title: Mahakshatrapa ("Restorer of the Fallen Kingdom")
- **Junagadh (Girnar) Inscription**: First long inscription in pure **Sanskrit**; Brahmi script; poetic style; 20 lines; mentions repair of Sudarshan Lake
- Defeated Satavahana ruler Vashishthiputra Pulumavi twice
- Sakas issued first dated currency in India
- **Vikrama Era** (57 BCE): Local ruler of Ujjain defeated Shakas; started Vikram Samvat

### 8.7 Parthians (Pahlavas)

- Origin: Parthia/Iran; first ruler: Arsaces I
- Greatest ruler: **Gondophernes**; capital: Taxila
- **St. Thomas** came to India during his reign to preach Christianity; later assassinated at Mylapore (near Madras)
- Coins bear title "Dharmiyya" (religious)

| **Rudrasena I** | 335-360 CE | Main branch |

### 8.9 Ikshvaku Dynasty

| Style | Features |

### 9.5 Post-Mauryan Trade

- **Monsoon winds**: Discovered by Greek sailor Hippolus (46 CE) in Arabian Sea

- **Roman trade**: Huge gold exports from Rome; Pliny: "Rome spends 10 million sesterces annually on Indian luxury goods"
- **Industries**: Cotton textiles (Uraiyur, Arikamedu); bead-making (Ujjain)
- **Guilds (Shreni)**: Chief: Jethtak; head: Shresthi; Akshaynivi (permanent investment); judicial authority; own coins

## 10. Gupta Empire (320-550 CE) — Golden Age of India

### 10.1 Overview

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Period** | 275-550 CE (Gupta ruling period) |
| **Founder** | Maharaja Shri Gupta (progenitor); son: Ghatotkacha |
| **Real founder** | Chandragupta I |
| **Golden Age** | Art, Literature, Science, Technology; "India's Periclean Age"; "Renaissance of Literature" (Max Müller) |
| **Emblem** | Garuda |
| **Feudatories of** | Kushanas; based at Kaushambi (UP) |

### 10.2 Rulers

#### 10.2.1 Chandragupta I (319-335 CE)

- Actual founder of Gupta dynasty
- Title: **Maharajadhiraja** (first among Gupta rulers)
- Married Princess **Kumaradevi** of Lichchhavi dynasty; issued Chandragupta-Kumaradevi gold coins
- Started **Gupta Era** (Gupta Samvat, 319-320 CE)
- Capital: Pataliputra
- Introduced gold coins (Dinars)

#### 10.2.2 Samudragupta (335-375 CE)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Most powerful ruler** | Called "Napoleon of India" by V.A. Smith |
| **Never defeated** | "Hero of hundred battles" |
| **Source** | **Prayag Prasasti** (Allahabad Pillar Inscription); composed by court poet **Harisena**; Sanskrit, Champu style, Brahmi script; originally Ashoka's at Kaushambi; brought to Allahabad by Akbar |
| **Titles** | Paramabhagavata, Ashvamedha performer, Vikramanka, Kaviraja (King of Poets), "Peerless Warrior on Earth", Lichchhavi-Dauhitra, Sarvarajocchetta |
| **Conquest policies** | 9 Aryavarta states: Prasaboddharana (complete annexation); 12 Southern states: Grahana-Mokshanugraha (conquest & restoration); 18 Atavika states: Paricharakikrita (enslavement); Frontier states: tribute + homage |
| **Coins** | "Victor of hundred battles"; Ashvamedha Parakrama; depicted playing veena |

#### 10.2.3 Chandragupta II Vikramaditya (375-415 CE)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Accession** | Killed brother Ramagupta (sources: Devichandraguptam, Harshacharita, Kavyamimamsa) |
| **Marriages** | Dhruvadevi (wife of Ramagupta; son: Kumaragupta); Kuberanaga (Naga dynasty; daughter: Prabhavati Gupta — married to Vakataka king Rudrasena II) |
| **Titles** | Vikramaditya, Shakari (defeated Shaka ruler Rudrasimha III), Paramabhagavata, Devagupta, Devaraja, Rajadhirajarshi |
| **Capitals** | Pataliputra and Ujjayini (after Shaka victory) |
| **Silver coins** | First Gupta ruler to issue silver coins (Rupaka/Rupyaka, Tiger type) |
| **Chinese traveler** | **Faxian** (Fa-Hien, 399-414 CE); described happy people, hospitals in Pataliputra; never mentions emperor's name |
| **Mehrauli Iron Pillar** | Sanskrit; Vishnupada/Garuda Pillar; rust-resistant; king "Chandra" identified with Chandragupta II |

#### Navaratnas (9 Gems) of Chandragupta II's Court

| Gem | Field |
|---|---|
| **Kalidasa** | Poetry/Drama (Abhijnanasakuntalam, Vikramorvashiyam, Malavikagnimitram, Raghuvamsam, Kumarasambhavam, Meghadutam, Ritusamhara) |
| **Dhanvantari** | Medicine |
| **Kshapanaka** | Astrology |
| **Amarasimha** | Lexicography (Amarkosh — Sanskrit encyclopedia) |
| **Shanku** | Scholar/Astrologer |
| **Vetalabhatta** | Magician/Scholar (Niti-Pradipa, Vetala-Panchavimshatika) |
| **Ghatakarpara** | Scholar/Diplomat (Ghatakarpara Kavya) |
| **Varahamihira** | Astronomy (Brihajjataka, Brihatsamhita, Panchasiddhantika) |
| **Vararuchi Katyayana** | Grammar (commentator on Panini's Sutras) |

#### 10.2.4 Kumaragupta I Mahendraditya (415-455 CE)

- Longest reign; son of Chandragupta II and Dhruvadevi
- Titles: Mahendraditya, Shri Mahendra, Shukraditya, Parambhagavat
- **Founded Nalanda University** (Bihar) — "Oxford of Mahayana Buddhism"
- Maximum 18 inscriptions and 14 types of coins
- Peacock (Kartikeya) coins instead of Garuda
- Bilsad Inscription (Etah, UP) — Gupta genealogy
- Mandsaur Prashasti — world's first advertising inscription (silk weavers' guild, Sun Temple)

#### 10.2.5 Skandagupta (455-467 CE)

- Last great ruler; capital: Ayodhya
- Titles: Kramaditya, Shri Skandagupta, Paramabhagavata
- **First Huna invasion** (455 CE): Defeated Huns; Bhitari Inscription (Saidpur, UP)
- **Junagadh Inscription**: Sudarshan Lake repair; Lakshmi chose Skandagupta; Hun invader: Khushnevaz
- After his death, Gupta decline began
- **Eran Pillar Inscription** (510 CE, Sagar, MP): First epigraphic evidence of **Sati** practice (Goparaja's wife self-immolated)

### 10.3 Gupta Administration

| Level | Unit | Head |
|---|---|---|
| **Province** | Bhukti/Avani/Desha | Uparika (internal); Gopta/Gopna (border) |
| **District** | Vishaya | Vishayapati (appointed by Uparika) |
| **City** | — | Nagar Shresthi (merchants), Sarthavaha (trade), Prathama Kayastha (scribes) |
| **Village** | Grama | Gramika |

- Decentralized (contrary to Mauryans)
- Land grant tradition expanded
- Judicial: Puga, Kula, Village Panchayat; judges: Pradvivaka, Dandanayaka
- Police: Dandapashika (head); Chata and Bhata (officers)

### 10.4 Gupta Economy

- Main income: Land revenue (Bhaga, 1/6 of produce)
- Other taxes: Vishti (forced labor), Bhutavataprtyaya (import duty)
- Land types: Kshetra (cultivable), Sila (non-cultivable), Vastu (habitation), Aprahata (wild)
- Land measurement: Nal (metal rod); Angula (smallest unit); Kulyavapa/Dronavapa
- Textiles: Gujarat, Bengal, Deccan, Tamil Nadu
- Silver coin: Rupaka; Copper: Mashaka
- Main western port: Bharuch; Ujjain = most important trading center
- Port cities: Ghantasala, Kadura, Chaul

### 10.5 Gupta Art & Architecture

- **Temple architecture originated**: Nagara style, Panchayatana style
- Famous temples: Vishnu Temple (Tigawa, MP), Shiva Temple (Bhumara), Parvati Temple (Nachna Kuthara), Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh, UP), Lakshman Temple/Bhitargaon (bricks, Kanpur)
- **Sculpture centers**: Mathura, Sarnath, Pataliputra
- **Varaha Avatar**: Udayagiri (13.5 ft × 8.5 ft — largest in India); Eran Garuda Pillar (47 ft, single stone)
- **Sun worship**: Oldest Surya sculpture from Bharhut (3rd century BCE); oldest Sun temple at Taxila; Multan center

### 10.6 Gupta Literature

| Book | Author |
|---|---|
| Ramayana & Mahabharata (final form) | — |
| Narada, Katyayana, Parashara, Brihaspati Smritis | — |
| Panchatantra | Vishnu Sharma |
| Kamasutra | Vatsyayana |
| Amarkosh | Amarasimha |
| Mrichchakatikam | Shudraka |
| Kiratarjuniyam | Bharavi |
| Dashakumarcharita | Dandin |
| Nyayavatara | Siddhasena |
| Visuddhimagga | Buddhaghosa |

### 10.7 Gupta Science

| Scientist | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Aryabhata** | Mathematics & astronomy; Aryabhatiya, Arya Siddhanta; value of π; length of solar year; Earth rotates on axis; concept of zero & decimal system |
| **Varahamihira** | Brihajjataka, Brihatsamhita, Panchasiddhantika |
| **Brahmagupta** | Brahmasphutasiddhanta; gravity; zero |

### 10.8 Gupta Period Religion

- Idol worship in temples began
- Vaishnavism and Shaivism developed
- Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh) — Panchayatana style
- Gupta emperors: followers of Vaishnavism; Garuda = royal emblem
- Earliest Ardhanarishvara image: Bhita (Allahabad)
- Trimurti concept developed
- King Meghavarmma of Sri Lanka requested permission from Samudragupta to build Buddhist temple in Gaya

## 11. Post-Gupta Period (550-750 CE)

### 11.1 Huna Invasion

- Nomadic tribe of Central Asia; two branches (Western → Roman Empire; Eastern → India)
- **First invasion**: 455 CE (defeated by Skandagupta)
- **Toramana** and son **Mihirakula**
- Mihirakula: follower of Shaivism; opponent of Buddhism and Jainism; defeated by Yashodharman and Narasimhagupta
- Official language: Sanskrit; peak of feudalism

### 11.2 Regional Dynasties

| Dynasty | Founder | Key Rulers |
|---|---|---|
| **Maitrakas of Vallabhi** | Bhattarka | Dhruvasena II (visited by Hiuen Tsang) |
| **Maukharis of Kannauj** | Harivarma | Ishnavarma, Sarvavarma, Grahavarma (last; killed by Devagupta of Malwa) |
| **Gaudas of Bengal** | — | Shashanka (follower of Shaivism; enemy of Harsha; cut down Bodhi Tree at Gaya; capital: Karna-Suvarna) |
| **Later Guptas** | — | Feudatories of Kushanas; based at Kaushambi |

### 11.3 Pushyabhuti (Vardhana) Dynasty

| Ruler | Key Details |
|---|---|
| **Pushyabhuti** | Founder; location: Thanesar (Haryana); devotee of Shiva and Sun |
| **Prabhakaravardhana** | "Huna-Harina-Kesari" (Lion among Huns); real founder; mother Yashomati committed self-immolation |
| **Rajyavardhana II** | Killed by Shashanka of Bengal |
| **Harshavardhana** | 606-647 CE |

### 11.4 Harshavardhana (606-647 CE)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Coronation** | Age 16, 606 CE → **Harsha Era** |
| **Capital** | Kannauj (shifted from Thanesar) |
| **Titles** | "Akbar of the Hindu Period", Shiladitya, Kavindra, Sakalottarapathanatha |
| **Religion** | Initially Shaiva, later Buddhist |
| **Sources** | Banabhatta (Harshacharita, Kadambari); Kalhana (Rajatarangini); Hiuen Tsang (Si-Yu-Ki); Harsha himself (Priyadarshika, Ratnavali, Nagananda); Bhartrihari (Shringara Shataka) |
| **Chinese traveler** | **Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)**: visited 629-645 CE; studied at Nalanda; chancellor: Acharya Shilabhadra; described sales tax, fire/water/poison ordeals |
| **Administration** | Autocratic monarchy; Maharaja/Mahasamanta (subordinate rulers); Sachiva/Amatya (ministers); Chata/Bhata (police); Bhukti → Vishaya → Pathaka → Village |
| **Council** | Bhandi (Chief Secretary), Sinhanada (Commander-in-Chief), Kuntala (Cavalry head), Skandagupta (Elephant Corps) |

#### Key Events

- **Prayaga Mahamoksha Parishad**: Religious assembly every 5th year; "Indian Hatim" (excessive charity); Xuanzang attended 6th assembly; 18 kings participated
- **Kannauj Assembly** (643 CE): Mahayana branch; Xuanzang given titles Mokshadeva and Mahayanadeva
- Sent envoys to China (641 CE)
- Defeated Shashanka with help of King Bhaskar Varma of Kamarupa (Assam)
- **Defeated by Pulakeshin II** (Chalukya) at Narmada bank; source: Aihole Prashasti

### 11.5 Tripartite Struggle

- After Harsha's death, **Yashovarman** occupied Kannauj
- 150-year struggle among **Gurjara-Pratiharas**, **Palas**, and **Rashtrakutas**
- Gurjara-Pratiharas ultimately successful; Nagabhata II made Kannauj his capital
- Poet Vakpati wrote "Gaudavaho" (Prakrit); Bhavabhuti wrote Malati-Madhava, Uttara-Ramacharita, Mahavira-Charita

## 13. Practice Questions (30 PYQs)

**Q1.** Indus Valley Civilization was discovered first at:
(a) Mohenjo-Daro (b) Harappa (c) Lothal (d) Kalibangan
**Answer**: (b) Harappa (1921 by Rai Bahadur Daya Ram Sahni; Mohenjo-Daro by R.D. Banerji 1922)

**Q2.** The Great Bath is found at:
(a) Harappa (b) Mohenjo-Daro (c) Lothal (d) Dholavira
**Answer**: (b) Mohenjo-Daro

**Q3.** Lothal is located in which state?
(a) Gujarat (b) Rajasthan (c) Punjab (d) Sindh
**Answer**: (a) Gujarat

**Q4.** The "Pillars of Ashoka" were erected by which emperor?
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Bindusara (c) Ashoka (d) Kanishka
**Answer**: (c) Ashoka the Great (Devanampiya Piyadasi)

**Q5.** Which ruler started the Saka era in 78 CE?
(a) Kanishka (b) Vikramaditya (c) Samudragupta (d) Ashoka
**Answer**: (a) Kanishka (Saka era started 78 CE; Vikrama era is 57 BCE)

**Q6.** The "Navratnas" (9 gems) were in the court of:
(a) Kanishka (b) Chandragupta II Vikramaditya (c) Samudragupta (d) Harsha
**Answer**: (b) Chandragupta II (included Kalidasa, Varahamihira, Dhanvantari, etc.)

**Q7.** Which Chinese pilgrim visited Harsha's court?
(a) Fa-Hien (b) Hiuen Tsang (c) I-tsing (d) Sung Yun
**Answer**: (b) Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang); Fa-Hien visited Chandragupta II's court

**Q8.** The author of Arthashastra is:
(a) Panini (b) Kautilya (Chanakya) (c) Vishnu Sharma (d) Banabhatta
**Answer**: (b) Kautilya/Chanakya (Mauryan advisor to Chandragupta)

**Q9.** Mehrgarh is located in:
(a) Gujarat (b) Balochistan (c) Rajasthan (d) Haryana
**Answer**: (b) Balochistan (earliest agricultural community)

**Q10.** The Fourth Buddhist Council was held during the reign of:
(a) Ashoka (b) Kanishka (c) Harsha (d) Samudragupta
**Answer**: (b) Kanishka (at Kundalvan, Kashmir; resulted in Hinayana/Mahayana split)

**Q11.** The capital of Magadha during Mahajanapada period was:
(a) Rajagriha (b) Pataliputra (c) Vaishali (d) Champa
**Answer**: (a) Rajagriha (Pataliputra became capital later under Udayin)

**Q12.** The "Iron Pillar of Delhi" is associated with which dynasty?
(a) Maurya (b) Gupta (c) Kushan (d) Mughal
**Answer**: (b) Gupta (likely Chandragupta II; non-rusting iron technology)

**Q13.** "Harshacharita" was written by:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Banabhatta (c) Dhanvantari (d) Varahamihira
**Answer**: (b) Banabhatta (biography of Harsha); Kalidasa wrote plays

**Q14.** The Saka era started by Kanishka corresponds to:
(a) 57 BCE (b) 78 CE (c) 320 CE (d) 606 CE
**Answer**: (b) 78 CE (Saka era); (a) 57 BCE is Vikrama era (different system)

**Q15.** The "Ashoka Chakra" in the Indian flag was adopted from:
(a) Sarnath Lion Capital (b) Amaravati Stupa (c) Ajanta Cave (d) Nalanda
**Answer**: (a) Sarnath Lion Capital (Ashoka Pillar capital)

**Q16.** Who was the last Tirthankara of Jains?
(a) Chandraprabhu (b) Rishabhnath (c) Vardhaman Mahavira (d) Padmaprabha
**Answer**: (c) Mahavira Swami (540-468 BCE); 24th Tirthankara; attained Kaivalya at age 42 under Sal tree

**Q17.** Buddha attained enlightenment at:
(a) Sarnath (b) Bodh Gaya (c) Kushinagar (d) Lumbini
**Answer**: (b) Bodh Gaya (under Peepal/Bodhi tree at Uruvela, age 35, Vaishakha Purnima)

**Q18.** The first Buddhist Council was held at:
(a) Vaishali (b) Rajgriha (c) Pataliputra (d) Kashmir
**Answer**: (b) Rajgriha (Saptaparni Cave, 483 BCE, patron: Ajatashatru, chairman: Mahakashyapa)

**Q19.** Bimbisara was the king of which dynasty?
(a) Haryanka (b) Maurya (c) Shunga (d) Nanda
**Answer**: (a) Haryanka (544-492 BCE; founded Haryanka dynasty; also called "Shronika")

**Q20.** Who was called "Agrammes" or "Xandrames" by Greek writers?
(a) Ajatashatru (b) Kalashoka (c) Mahapadma Nanda (d) Dhanananda
**Answer**: (d) Dhanananda (last Nanda ruler; contemporary of Alexander's invasion)

**Q21.** The Vikrama Era began in:
(a) 57 BCE (b) 78 CE (c) 319 CE (d) 606 CE
**Answer**: (a) 57 BCE (started by Vikramaditya after defeating Shakas)

**Q22.** Who was the founder of the Shunga Dynasty?
(a) Pushyamitra (b) Jayadratha (c) Kunal (d) Brihadratha
**Answer**: (a) Pushyamitra Shunga (185 BCE; killed Brihadratha; performed 2 Ashvamedha yajnas)

**Q23.** Gautamiputra Satakarni was the greatest ruler of which dynasty?
(a) Shunga (b) Kanva (c) Satavahana (d) Kushan
**Answer**: (c) Satavahana (23rd ruler; called "Eka Brahmin"; defeated Nahapana)

**Q24.** The Junagadh inscription in pure Sanskrit was issued by:
(a) Ashoka (b) Rudradaman (c) Samudragupta (d) Kanishka
**Answer**: (b) Rudradaman I (130-150 CE; first long Sanskrit inscription; mentions Sudarshan Lake repair)

**Q25.** Nalanda University was established by:
(a) Samudragupta (b) Chandragupta II (c) Kumaragupta (d) Skandagupta
**Answer**: (c) Kumaragupta I (5th century CE; "Oxford of Mahayana Buddhism")

**Q26.** Who is called the "Napoleon of India"?
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Samudragupta (c) Ashoka (d) Harsha
**Answer**: (b) Samudragupta (by V.A. Smith; never defeated in battle)

**Q27.** The first Huna invasion of India took place in:
(a) 358 CE (b) 458 CE (c) 558 CE (d) 658 CE
**Answer**: (b) 458 CE (defeated by Skandagupta; Toramana and Mihirakula)

**Q28.** Harshavardhana was defeated by which ruler?
(a) Pulakeshin II (b) Shashanka (c) Narasimhavarman (d) Bhaskar Varma
**Answer**: (a) Pulakeshin II (Chalukya; at Narmada bank; source: Aihole Prashasti)

**Q29.** The term "Stupa" is associated with which event of Buddha's life?
(a) Birth (b) First Sermon (c) Renunciation (d) Death
**Answer**: (d) Death (relics like teeth, ashes kept in stupas)

**Q30.** Which Mauryan ruler was an ardent follower of Jainism?
(a) Bindusara (b) Ashoka (c) Chandragupta Maurya (d) Brihadratha
**Answer**: (c) Chandragupta Maurya (adopted Jainism from Bhadrabahu; Sallekhana at Shravanabelagola, 298 BCE)
