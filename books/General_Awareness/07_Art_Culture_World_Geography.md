## 1. Indian Classical Dance Forms

India officially recognizes **8 classical dance forms** (declared by Sangeet Natak Akademi). Each has distinct style, origin, and tradition.

### 1.1 The 8 Classical Dances

| Dance | Origin State | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Bharatanatyam** | Tamil Nadu | Oldest classical dance; temple dance; sculptures at Chidambaram; balanced between Nritta, Nritya, Natya; items: Alarippu, Jatiswaram, Varnam, Padam, Thillana |
| **Kathak** | Uttar Pradesh | North Indian; ghungroo bells; spins (chakkars); themes from Radha-Krishna; court dance during Mughal period; gharanas: Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras, Raigarh |
| **Kathakali** | Kerala | "Story play"; elaborate makeup (vilasini, kathi, pacha, thadi, kari, minukku); facial expressions; green/red face; themes from Mahabharata, Ramayana |
| **Kuchipudi** | Andhra Pradesh | "Yakshagana" style; includes dialogue; Tarangam (on brass plate); originated in Kuchipudi village; founded by Tirtha Narayana Yati |
| **Odissi** | Odisha | "Maharis" tradition; tribhanga posture; Konark dance sculptures; guru Kelucharan Mohapatra |
| **Mohiniyattam** | Kerala | "Dance of the enchantress"; swaying body; based on Lasya style; Vishnu/Krishna themes; popularized by Vallathol Narayana Menon |
| **Manipuri** | Manipur | Ras Lila (Radha-Krishna); cylindrical costume; graceful; not jumps; themes of Vaishnavism |
| **Sattriya** | Assam | Added 2000; founded by Sankaradeva; Vaishnavite themes; ankiya nat; apsara dance |

**Note:** Earlier 5 were recognized; **Mohiniyattam** added in 2000; **Kuchipudi** added in 2000; **Kathakali** declared classical in 2000; **Manipuri** recognized in 1972; **Sattriya** added in 2000 (most recent addition).

**Body Movements in Indian Dance:**
- **Nritta**: Pure dance (rhythmic movements)
- **Nritya**: Expressive dance (with emotions/abhinaya)
- **Natya**: Dance drama (with dialogue)

**Key Terms:**
- **Abhinaya**: Expression through face, body
- **Mudra**: Hand gestures (~108)
- **Asamyukta**: Single hand mudra
- **Samyukta**: Combined hands
- **Tala**: Rhythm cycle
- **Bhav**: Mood/emotion

**Famous Dancers:**
- **Balasaraswati** (Bharatanatyam)
- **Rukmini Devi Arundale** (revived Bharatanatyam; founder of Kalakshetra, Chennai)
- **Birju Maharaj** (Kathak)
- **Sonal Mansingh** (Odissi/Bharatanatyam)
- **Mrinalini Sarabhai** (Bharatanatyam/Kuchipudi)
- **Mallika Sarabhai** (Kuchipudi/Bharatanatyam)
- **Kelucharan Mohapatra** (Odissi)
- **Sanjukta Panigrahi** (Odissi)
- **Guru Vempati Chinna Satyam** (Kuchipudi)
- **Padma Subrahmanyam** (Bharatanatyam)
- **Alarmel Valli** (Bharatanatyam)
- **Srinivasa Acharya** (Kathak)

## 2. Indian Classical Music

### 2.1 Two Traditions: Hindustani & Carnatic

| Feature | Hindustani (North) | Carnatic (South) |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Vedic + Persian | Ancient Tamil/Sanskrit |
| Region | North India | South India |
| Main deities | Krishna, Rama | Shiva, Vishnu, Devi |
| Major centers | Varanasi, Delhi, Agra | Chennai, Thanjavur |
| Improvisation | High (raga development) | Less (kriti-based) |
| Instruments | Sitar, Sarod, Tabla, Bansuri | Veena, Mridangam, Nadaswaram |
| Text language | Brijbhasha, Hindi, Sanskrit | Telugu, Tamil, Sanskrit |
| Compositions | Bandish, Dhrupad, Khayal, Tarana | Kriti, Varnam, Padam |
| Notation system | Not strictly followed | Followed |
| Number of basic Ragas | ~6 main (Bhairav, Kalyan, etc.) | 72 Melakarta system |

**Important Concepts:**
- **Raga**: Melodic framework; 5-7 notes
- **Tala**: Rhythmic cycle
- **Svara**: Note (Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni)
- **Aarohi (Ascending)**, **Avarohi (Descending)**
- **Pakad**: Signature phrase
- **Sthayi**: Fixed phrase
- **Bandish**: Compositions in Hindustani
- **Kriti**: Compositions in Carnatic
- **Alap, Jor, Jhala**: Slow to fast progression
- **Gat**: Instrumental composition (tabla accompaniment)

## 3. Indian Painting & Sculpture

### 3.1 Painting Traditions

**Mural Paintings:**
- **Ajanta Caves** (Maharashtra): 5th-6th century CE; Buddhist themes; "Padmapani" Bodhisattva; finest examples of ancient Indian painting
- **Ellora Caves** (Maharashtra)
- **Bagh Caves** (Madhya Pradesh)
- **Sittanavasal** (Tamil Nadu): Jain themes

**Miniature Paintings:**
- **Mughal Miniature**: Babur, Humayun, Akbar (Hamzanama); Jahangir
- **Rajput Miniature**: Rajasthani schools — Mewar, Bundi, Kota, Kishangarh, Jaipur
- **Pahari Miniature**: Painted in hill states — Kangra, Guler, Basohli, Chamba
- **Deccan Miniature**: Bijapur, Golconda, Hyderabad

**Famous Art Styles & Schools:**
- **Madhubani/Mithila Painting**: Bihar; geometric patterns; no empty space; themes from Ramayana, Mahabharata, Krishna; traditionally by women
- **Warli Painting**: Maharashtra; tribal art; geometric patterns; daily life
- **Pattachitra**: Odisha; cloth-based; Jagannath themes
- **Tanjore Painting**: Tamil Nadu; gold foil; gods/royal figures
- **Kalamkari**: Andhra Pradesh; pen (kalam) used; natural dyes; themes from Hindu epics
- **Kalighat Painting**: Bengal (19th century); satirical; simple lines
- **Phad Painting**: Rajasthan; scroll painting; religious narratives
- **Patna Painting**: Bihar; Mughal style with local themes
- **Chittara Painting**: Karnataka
- **Saora Painting**: Odisha
- **Gond Painting**: Madhya Pradesh

**Modern Indian Painters:**
- **Raja Ravi Varma** (1848-1906): "Father of Modern Indian Art"; famous for "Lady with Lamp" and Indian mythological paintings
- **Abanindranath Tagore**: Founder of Bengal School; "Bharat Mata" painting
- **Nandalal Bose**: Father of modern Indian art
- **Amrita Sher-Gil**: "Indian Frida Kahlo"
- **Jamini Roy**: Folk art style
- **M.F. Husain**: "Picasso of India"
- **S.H. Raza**: Modern abstract
- **Tyeb Mehta**
- **F.N. Souza**
- **K.K. Hebbar**

**Famous Sculptures:**
- **Ashoka Pillars** (Mauryan): Lion Capital at Sarnath (National Emblem)
- **Khajuraho Group of Monuments** (Madhya Pradesh): UNESCO; Chandela dynasty
- **Ellora Caves** (Maharashtra): Kailasa Temple
- **Elephanta Caves** (Maharashtra): Shiva sculptures; UNESCO
- **Ajanta Caves** (Maharashtra): Buddhist; UNESCO
- **Mahabalipuram** (Tamil Nadu): Shore Temple, Pancha Rathas; UNESCO
- **Hampi** (Karnataka): Vijayanagara; UNESCO
- **Konark Sun Temple** (Odisha): Chariot-shaped; UNESCO
- **Modhera Sun Temple** (Gujarat)
- **Dilwara Temples** (Rajasthan): Jain

### 3.2 Architecture Styles

**Ancient Indian Architecture:**
- **Indus Valley**: Grid-pattern cities, drainage
- **Buddhist**: Stupas (Sanchi, Sarnath, Amaravati), rock-cut caves (Ajanta, Ellora)
- **Hindu**: Nagara (North), Dravida (South), Vesara (hybrid)
- **Jain**: Dilwara (Mount Abu), Ranakpur
- **Indo-Islamic**: Mughal (Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Fatehpur Sikri), Dravidian-Islamic (Gol Gumbaz)

**Medieval Indian Architecture:**
- **Delhi Sultanate**: Qutub Minar (started by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, completed by Iltutmish); Alai Darwaza; Hauz Khas
- **Mughal**: Taj Mahal (Shah Jahan), Red Fort (Shah Jahan), Jama Masjid (Shah Jahan), Humayun's Tomb, Fatehpur Sikri (Akbar), Buland Darwaza
- **Vijayanagara**: Vittala Temple (musical pillars), Hampi
- **Hoysala**: Belur, Halebidu, Somanathapura
- **Chola**: Brihadeeswarar Temple (Thanjavur — tallest temple tower, ~216 feet)

**Modern Indian Architecture:**
- **Indo-Saracenic**: Mixing Hindu, Islamic, Western; e.g., Victoria Memorial (Kolkata), Gateway of India (Mumbai)
- **Le Corbusier**: Chandigarh (planned city)
- **Charles Correa**: Belapur Housing
- **B.V. Doshi**: Aranya Low Cost Housing
- **Hafeez Contractor**: Many modern buildings
