## 1. Early Medieval Period (750-1206 CE)

### 1.1 Overview

The medieval period in India begins from the **8th century** and ends in the **18th century**. It is divided into:
- **Early Medieval Period** (8th-11th century)
- **Late Medieval Period** (12th-18th century)

### 1.2 Major Dynasties

| Dynasty | Period | Region | Capital | Notable Rulers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pala** | 750-1161 CE | Bengal, Bihar | Pataliputra, Vikramapura | Gopala (founder), Dharmapala, Devapala |
| **Pratihara** | 730-1036 CE | Western/Central India | Kannauj | Nagabhata II, Mihir Bhoja |
| **Rashtrakuta** | 753-982 CE | Deccan | Manyakheta | Dantidurga, Krishna III |
| **Chola** | 848-1279 CE | Tamil Nadu | Thanjavur/Tanjore | Rajaraja I, Rajendra I |

### 1.3 Key Details

- **Tripartite Struggle**: Pala, Pratihara, and Rashtrakuta dynasties fought for control of **Kannauj** (symbolic of sovereignty over northern India)
- **Dharmapala** (Pala dynasty): assumed title "Uttarapatha Swamin"; founded **Vikramshila University** in Bhagalpur, Bihar; succeeded Gopala (founder of Pala empire)
- **Tomar Rajput Dynasty**: Anangapala Tomar founded **Delhi** (originally named "Dhillika") in **736 CE** (some sources say 1052)
- **Chahamana/Chauhan Dynasty**: Prithviraj III (1168-1192 CE); Bijolia Inscription reveals Chauhan genealogy (Vats Gotriya Brahman)
- **Kakatiya Dynasty**: Rudrama Devi (1263-1289 CE, daughter of Ganapatideva); ended by Delhi Sultanate (Ghiyath-al-Din Tughlaq, 1323 CE); Warangal; Golconda Fort originally built by Kakatiyas in 13th century (1143 as Mankal)
- **Hoysala Dynasty**: Hoysaleshvara Temple built 1150 CE with **dark schist stone**; capital Dorasamudra/Dwarasamudra
- **Paramara Dynasty**: Bhojshala (Saraswati temple, Dhar, MP); founded by Upendra/Krishnaraja; ruled Malwa 9th-14th century
- **Suryavamsi Gajapati Dynasty**: founded by **Kapilendra Deva** (1434-66 CE); ruled Orissa and Andhra Pradesh
- **Maharana Kumbha** (1433-1468 CE): Sisodia clan; composed "Sangeet Raj" (book on music); titles: Abhinav Bhattacharya, Rajguru, Daanguru, Halguru, Rano Raso

## 2. Foreign Invasions (Pre-Sultanate)

### 2.1 Mahmud of Ghazni (998-1030 CE)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Dynasty** | Ghaznavid (Turkish origin) |
| **Reign** | 998-1030 CE |
| **Invasions** | 17 invasions of India |
| **Objective** | Plunder (not territorial conquest) |
| **Most Famous** | Attack on **Somnath Temple** (1025 CE) — Gujarat coast |
| **Companion Scholar** | **Al-Biruni** (Abu Rayhan, 973-1048 CE) — came with Mahmud in 1017; wrote **Kitab-ul-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind** (study of Indian culture); known as **"Father of Indology"** |
| **Court Poet** | **Ferdowsi** — wrote **Shahnameh** (Book of Kings, about Iran history, NOT about Mughal India); relationship with Mahmud was strained |
| **Other Notable** | Hafez — 14th century Persian poet known for ghazals |

### 2.2 Muhammad Ghori (1173-1206 CE)

| Battle | Year | Combatants | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Battle of Tarain** | 1191 CE | Prithviraj III (Chauhan) vs Muhammad Ghori | **Prithviraj won**; Ghori escaped |
| **Second Battle of Tarain** | 1192 CE | Prithviraj III vs Muhammad Ghori | **Ghori won**; Prithviraj captured and killed; opened North India to Turkish rule |

- **Prithviraj III** (1168-1192 CE): Chahamana dynasty; court poet **Chand Bardai** wrote **Prithviraj Raso** (Braj language epic about his life, love story, and heroic deeds)
- Ghori's conquests laid the foundation for the Delhi Sultanate

### 2.3 Other Invasions

- **Timur (Tamerlane)**: Invaded Delhi in **1398 CE** during the reign of Nasir-ud-din Mahmud Tughlaq; massive plunder and massacre; led to the decline of the Tughlaq dynasty
- **Genghis Khan**: Mongol ruler; died **1227 CE**; Mongols inhabited Central Asia; skilled in hunting, archery, and horse riding

## 3. Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526 CE)

### 3.1 Five Dynasties Overview

| Dynasty | Period | Founder | Capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mamluk (Slave)** | 1206-1290 | Qutbuddin Aibak | Delhi/Lahore |
| **Khilji** | 1290-1320 | Jalaluddin Firoz Khilji | Delhi |
| **Tughlaq** | 1320-1414 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq | Delhi (also Daulatabad) |
| **Sayyid** | 1414-1451 | Khizr Khan | Delhi |
| **Lodi** | 1451-1526 | Bahlul Lodi | Delhi/Agra |

### 3.2 Slave/Mamluk Dynasty (1206-1290 CE)

#### Qutbuddin Aibak (1206-1210)
- Slave of Muhammad Ghori; founded the Slave/Mamluk dynasty
- Title: **"Lakh Baksh"** (giver of lakhs — generous)
- Capital: **Lahore**
- Died in 1210 while playing **Chaugan** (polo)
- **Started construction of Qutb Minar** in **1199 CE** (foundation laid)
- Built **Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque** (1193-1197 CE) — first mosque in Delhi
- Built **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra** at Ajmer (1192-1199 CE; originally a Sanskrit college; walls inscribed with Harikeli drama by Vigraharaj IV)
- Occupied Badaun (1197-98 CE)

#### Iltutmish (1210-1236)
- Called the **"real founder"** of the Delhi Sultanate
- Introduced **silver tanka** and **copper jital** currency
- Introduced the **Iqta system** (land grants to nobles for revenue collection)
- Created the **Group of Forty (Chalisa/Chahalgani)** — elite nobles
- **Completed 3 storeys of Qutb Minar** (~1220 CE)
- Received patent/charter of sovereignty from the **Abbasid Caliph** of Baghdad
- His Wazir was **Nizam ul-Mulk Junaidi**
- His tomb was built in **1235 CE**

#### Razia Sultan (1236-1240)
- **Only female ruler** of the Delhi Sultanate
- Yaqut was her close associate, titled **"Amir-al-Khayal"**
- Imprisoned at **Qila Mubarak, Bathinda** by Malik Ikhtiyar-ud-din Altunia
- Buried at **Kaithal**

#### Balban (1266-1287)
- 9th Sultan of Delhi; previously served as **Naib** (deputy) to Nasiruddin Mahmud (1246-1266)
- Known for his **theory of kingship** — believed in divine right
- Introduced Persian customs: **Sijda** (prostration), **Paibos** (kissing the Sultan's feet), **Nauroz** (Persian New Year festival)
- Created **Diwan-i-Arz** (military department)
- Policy of **"blood and iron"** to suppress rebellions
- Title: **"Zil-i-Ilahi"** (Shadow of God)
- **Abolished the Chahalgani/Chalisa** (Group of Forty)
- Sent his son Bughra Khan as governor of Bengal
- Suppressed Tughril Beg revolt in Bengal (1279 CE)
- Associated with **Mameluke style** architecture

#### Nasiruddin Mahmud (1246-1266)
- 8th ruler of Delhi Sultanate
- Balban served as his Naib (deputy)

### 3.3 Khilji Dynasty (1290-1320 CE)

#### Jalaluddin Firoz Khilji (1290-1296)
- Founder of the Khilji dynasty
- Was **70 years old** at the time of accession

#### Alauddin Khilji (1296-1316)
- Most powerful ruler of the Khilji dynasty

**Market Control Reforms:**
- Established **3 markets** at Delhi: (1) Grain market, (2) Cloth market, (3) Horses/slaves/cattle market
- Markets located near **Kushke Sabz** within **Badaun gate**
- **Sarai-Adl** = cloth market ("Place of Justice")
- **Shahna-i-Mandi** = superintendent maintaining merchant register
- Intelligence: **Barid** (intelligence officer) + **Munhiyans** (secret spies)

**Taxation:**
- Imposed **50% land tax** on produce
- Three additional taxes: **Ghari** (house tax), **Chari** (cattle tax), **Jaziya** (religious tax on non-Muslims)

**Military Campaigns:**
- **Malik Kafur** (Hazar Dinari) led southern campaigns:
  - Devagiri (1307-08 CE)
  - Gujarat (1299 CE)
  - Ranthambore (1301 CE)
  - Chittor (1303 CE) — first recorded mention of **Jauhar** (mass self-immolation)
  - Malwa (1305 CE)
  - Warangal, Dwarasamudra (1310-11 CE)
  - Madurai (1311 CE)
- **First Sultan to attack South India**
- Defeated Mongols **12+ times**

**Architecture:**
- **Siri Fort** (1303 CE) — second city of Delhi
- **Alai Darwaza** (1311 CE) — red sandstone gateway at Qutb complex
- **Alai Minar** — unfinished tower (24.5m height)

**Military Reforms:**
- **Dagh Pratha** — branding of horses (to prevent substitution)
- **Huliya Pratha** — maintaining descriptive records of soldiers

**Administration:**
- Weakened nobles — broke the power of **"The Forty"**
- Before becoming Sultan, held positions: **Amir-i-Tuzuk** (Master of Ceremonies) and **Arizi-i-Mumalik**
- Key generals: **Zafar Khan** (Hizabruddin), **Almas Beg** (Ulugh Khan), **Nusrat Khan**

**Amir Khusrau (1253-1325 CE):**
- Title: **"Tuti-e-Hind"** (Parrot of India)
- **Inventor of Tabla and Sitar**
- Popularized **Qawwali** music
- Witnessed the reign of **7 Sultans**
- Key works: **Tughlaq Nama**, **Nuh Sipihr**, **Khazain ul Futuh**
- Disciple of Sufi saint **Nizamuddin Auliya**

### 3.4 Tughlaq Dynasty (1320-1414 CE) — Longest-Ruling Dynasty

#### Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq / Ghazi Malik (1320-1325)
- Governor of Punjab under Alauddin Khilji
- Founded **Tughlaqabad** city
- Fought and defeated Mongols

#### Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325-1351)
- Known for his **5 ambitious but disastrous projects**:

| # | Project | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Transfer of Capital** | Delhi → Daulatabad (Devagiri) in **1327 CE** — caused immense suffering |
| 2 | **Token Currency** | Introduced cheap metal (copper/brass) coins as token currency in **1330 CE** — led to massive forgery |
| 3 | **Taxation in Doab** | Increased taxes in the fertile Doab region — led to peasant rebellion |
| 4 | **Khurasan Expedition** | Planned to conquer Khurasan (Persia) — abandoned due to cost |
| 5 | **Qarachil Expedition** | Sent army to Qarachil (Kumaon hills) — army destroyed |

- Created **Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi** (Department of Agriculture)
- Introduced **Sondhar** — agricultural loans to farmers
- Achieved the **largest territorial extent** of the Delhi Sultanate
- **Ibn Battuta** (Moroccan traveler) visited India in **1333 CE**; wrote **Rihla** (travelogue in Arabic); appointed as **Qazi of Delhi** by the Sultan
- First Sultan to visit the **Ajmer shrine** of Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti
- Built **Begumpuri Mosque** (in Jahanpanah, his new city)
- Captured Warangal in **1327 CE**
- Died in **Thatta, Sindh** while campaigning against Taghi

#### Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351-1388)
- Cousin of Muhammad bin Tughlaq
- Imposed **4 Sharia taxes**: **Kharaj** (land revenue), **Khams**, **Jizya** (tax on non-Muslims), **Zakat** (religious tax)
- Introduced **Haq-e-Sharb** — irrigation/water tax
- Founded cities: **Jaunpur**, **Firozpur**, **Firozabad**, **Hissar**, **Fatehabad**
- Wrote **Futuhat-e-Firozshahi** — documenting his administrative achievements
- Created **Diwan-i-Khairat** — department for charity and welfare of orphans
- Buried at **Hauz Khas** (Delhi)
- **Added the 5th storey to Qutb Minar** in **1368 CE**; repaired it after lightning damage

#### Decline
- **Timur invasion** (1398 CE) during the reign of Nasir-ud-din Mahmud Tughlaq → led to the decline of the Tughlaq dynasty

### 3.5 Sayyid Dynasty (1414-1451 CE)

| Ruler | Reign | Key Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Khizr Khan** | 1414-1421 | Founder; claimed descent from Prophet Muhammad; defeated Daulat Khan |
| **Mubarak Shah** | 1421-1434 | Constructed **Mubarakbad** city; appointed Hindu nobles |
| **Muhammad Shah** | 1434-1445 | — |
| **Alauddin Alam Shah** | 1445-1451 | Last ruler; surrendered Delhi to **Bahlul Lodi** in 1451 |

- **Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi** by **Yahya bin Ahmad Sirhindi** — important literary source for this period

### 3.6 Lodi Dynasty (1451-1526 CE) — Fifth and Last Dynasty (Afghan)

| Ruler | Reign | Key Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Bahlul Lodi** | 1451-1489 | Founder; annexed **Jaunpur** (1476); brought Kalpi and Dholpur under suzerainty |
| **Sikandar Lodi** | 1489-1517 | Founded **Agra** in **1504 CE**; moved capital from Delhi to Agra; abolished **Zakat** tax |
| **Ibrahim Lodi** | 1517-1526 | Last Sultan of Delhi; defeated by **Babur** at the **First Battle of Panipat (1526 CE)** |

- **Moth ki Masjid** built in **1505 CE** by Wazir Miya Bhoiya
- **Double Dome** feature introduced during Lodi period
- **Battle of Khatoli** (1517 CE) — Rana Sanga vs Ibrahim Lodi

## 6. Provincial Architecture (Delhi Sultanate Period)

### 6.1 Bengal School
- Material: **Brick and terracotta**
- Feature: **Curved roofs** (influenced by local bamboo construction)

### 6.2 Jaunpur School
- Feature: **Massive arches, austere design**
- **Atala Mosque** (1408 CE) built by **Ibrahim Shah Sharki**
- **Sharqi Dynasty**: founded 1394 CE by **Khwajah-i-Jahan Malik Sarwar** (also called Malik Sarwar)
- Jaunpur city founded by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** in **1359 CE**
- Called **"Shiraz of India"** under the Sharqi kings
- Ended 1479 CE (defeated by Bahlul Lodi)

### 6.3 Gujarat School
- Feature: **Temple-style pillars, ornamental carvings, toranas, bell motifs, lotus patterns**
- Local Hindu and Jain craftsmen applied temple-building skills to mosques
- Blended trabeate (post-lintel) systems with Islamic arches and domes
- Notable examples in **Ahmedabad** and **Champaner**

## 7. Vijayanagara Empire (1336-1646 CE)

### 7.1 Overview

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | 1336 CE |
| **Founders** | **Harihara I** (1336-1356) and **Bukka I** (1356-1377) of Sangama dynasty |
| **Origin** | Ministers of Kampili kingdom; feudatories of Kakatiya dynasty; Yadava clan of Chandravamsa lineage |
| **Capital** | **Hampi** (2nd largest city in world after Beijing; UNESCO World Heritage Site 1986; Dravidian architecture) |
| **Discovered by** | Colonel Colin Mackenzie (1800 CE) |
| **Contemporary name** | "Karnataka Samrajyamu" |

### 7.2 Four Dynasties

| Dynasty | Period | Notable Rulers |
|---|---|---|
| **Sangama** | 1336-1485 | Harihara I, Bukka I, Devaraya I, Devaraya II |
| **Saluva** | 1485-1505 | — |
| **Tuluva** | 1505-1570 | Vira Narsimha, Krishnadeva Raya |
| **Aravidu** | 1570-1646 | Aliya Rama Raya |

### 7.3 Key Rulers

**Krishnadeva Raya (1509-1529 CE)** — Greatest Ruler:
- Tuluva dynasty
- Composed **Amuktamalyada** (Telugu epic on statecraft)
- Patronized **Ashtadiggaj** (8 eminent poets)
- Founded **Nagalapuram** in memory of his mother
- Title: "Establisher of the Yavana kingdom"
- **Domingo Paes** (Portuguese traveler) visited during his reign
- **Battle of Raichur** (1520 CE) vs Ismail Adil Shah of Bijapur

**Devaraya I**: Nicolo Conti (Italian traveler) visited 1420 CE; married daughter to Firuz Shah Bahmani (1406-07)

**Devaraya II** (1422-1446 CE): **Vitthal Temple** built during his reign

**Vira Narsimha** (1505-1509 CE): First Tuluva ruler

### 7.4 Battle of Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi)

- Date: **January 23, 1565 CE**
- Vijayanagara (under **Aliya Rama Raya**) defeated by Deccan Sultanates confederation:
  - Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar, Berar
- Led to political collapse of the Vijayanagara Empire

### 7.5 Administration & Economy

- **Land revenue (Sist)**: dominant revenue source
- **Amara-Nayakas**: military commanders given territories to govern
- Administrative units: **Gramas** (villages) → **Sthalas** (sub-districts) → **Nadus** (districts) → **Mandalams/Rajyas** (provinces)
- **Gauda**: village headman
- **Shiqdar**: administrative head
- Merchant communities: **Kudirai Chettis** (horse merchants), **Pattanulkars** (silk weavers from Gujarat), **Gomashta** (commercial agents)
- **Anantraj Sagar Tank**: built on Maldevi river (1.37 km dam)

### 7.6 Art & Architecture

- **Provida style** of architecture
- **Queen's Bath**, **Lotus Mahal**, **Elephant stables**
- **Carnatic music** origin: **Purandara Dasa** = "Father of Carnatic Music"
- Regions: Tamil, Karnataka, Telugu (NOT Maratha)

## 8. Bahmani Kingdom (1347-1527 CE)

### 8.1 Overview

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | 1347 CE |
| **Founder** | **Alauddin Bahman Shah** (original name: Zafar Khan / Hasan Gangu) |
| **Cause** | Revolted against Muhammad bin Tughlaq |
| **Capital** | **Gulbarga (Ahsanabad)** 1347-1425 → **Bidar (Muhammadabad)** from 1425 |
| **Last Ruler** | Kalimullah |

### 8.2 Administration

- Divided into **4 Tarafs (provinces)**: Gulbarga, Daulatabad, Berar, Bidar
- Each governed by a **Tarafdar** (provincial governor)

### 8.3 Key Rulers

| Ruler | Reign | Key Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Alauddin Bahman Shah** | 1347-1358 | Founder; original name Zafar Khan |
| **Ahmad Shah** | 1422-1435 | Transferred capital from Gulbarga to **Bidar** |
| **Ahmad Shah Al Wali Bahmani** | 1422-1436 | Tomb at **Ashtoor** (near Bidar); 12 imposing mausoleums |
| **Muhammad Shah III** | — | Died 1482 CE; after his death, kingdom disintegrated |

### 8.4 Mahmud Gawan

- Persian trader who rose to prominence in the Bahmani kingdom
- Appointed as **Prime Minister** under **Humayun Shah**
- Title: **"Malik-ut-Tujjar"** (Chief of Merchants)
- Had complete control over military affairs

### 8.5 Breakup of Bahmani Kingdom

After the death of Muhammad Shah III (1482 CE), the kingdom disintegrated into **5 independent states**:

| State | Founder | Capital |
|---|---|---|
| **Bijapur (Adil Shahi)** | Yusuf Adil Shah | Bijapur |
| **Ahmednagar (Nizam Shahi)** | Ahmad Nizam Shah | Ahmednagar |
| **Berar (Imadshahi)** | Fathullah Imad Ul Mulk (1490) | Ellichpur |
| **Golconda (Qutb Shahi)** | Sultan Quli Qutb Shah | Golconda |
| **Bidar (Barid Shahi)** | Qasim Barid | Bidar |

### 8.6 Bidri Craftwork

- Famous inlay work from **Bidar, Karnataka**
- Uses **copper and silver** inlay
- Bidar was the capital of the Bahmani Kingdom in the 14th century

## 9. Bhakti Movement

### 9.1 Philosophical Schools

| Saint | Philosophy | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Adi Shankaracharya** | **Advaita** | Non-dualism (God and soul are one) |
| **Ramanujacharya** | **Vishishtadvaita** | Qualified non-dualism; wrote **Shri Bhasya** commentary |
| **Madhvacharya** | **Dvaita** | Dualism (God and soul are separate) |
| **Nimbarka** | **Dvaitaadvaita** | Founded **Hamsa Sampradaya** (worship Krishna and Radha) |
| **Vallabhacharya** | **Shuddhadvaita** | Founded **Pushtimarg** (Krishna-centered devotion/adoration) |

## 11. Mughal Empire (1526-1857 CE)

### 11.1 Babur (1526-1530)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Real Name** | Zahir-ud-din Muhammad |
| **Born** | February 14, 1483 CE |
| **Name Origin** | Persian "Babr" (Tiger) |
| **Lineage** | 5th descendant of **Timur** (father's side); 14th descendant of **Genghis Khan** (mother's side) |
| **Ascension** | Succeeded to throne of **Ferghana** at age 11 (1494); seized **Kabul** (1504); assumed title "Padshah" (1507) |
| **First India Invasion** | 1519 CE (against Yusufzai; captured Baazaur and Bhera) |

**Key Battles:**
- **First Battle of Panipat** (April 21, 1526): Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi; used **Tulugama** tactic (dividing army into Left, Right, Centre with Forward and Rear divisions); first use of gunpowder/cannons in North India
- **Battle of Khanwa** (March 17, 1527): Babur defeated Rana Sanga of Mewar (allied with Medini Rai of Chanderi, Hasan Khan of Mewat, Mahmud Lodi); assumed title **"Ghazi"**
- **Battle of Ghaghra** (1529 CE): Babur defeated Mahmud Lodi + Nusrat Shah of Bengal

**Literature**: Wrote **Baburnama / Tuzuk-e-Babri** in **Chagtai Turkish** language

**Architecture**: Built Aram Bagh (Agra, 1528), Panipat Mosque, Kabuli Bagh Mosque (1527, Haryana)

**Death**: December 26, 1530 at Agra; first buried at Arambagh Agra, later moved to **Kabul**

> **Note**: Mughal rulers were NOT called "Sultan" (the title "Sultan" was started by Mahmud of Ghazni)

### 11.2 Humayun (1530-1540, 1555-1556)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Real Name** | Nasir-ud-Din Muhammad |
| **Relation** | Eldest son of Babur |
| **Brothers** | Kamran (Afghanistan and Punjab), Askari, Hindal |
| **Architecture** | Built **Dinpanah** (citadel in Delhi); **Purana Quila** is part of it |

**Key Battles:**
- **Battle of Chausa** (1539 CE): Defeated by Sher Shah Suri
- **Battle of Kannauj/Bilgram** (1540 CE): Defeated by Sher Shah; fled to Iran (Persia)
- Regained territories **July 23, 1555 CE**
- Died from falling down the stairs of **Dinpanah library**

**Cultural Contributions:**
- Promoted Persian art and literature
- Invited Persian painters **Mir Sayyid Ali** and **Abdus Samad** — laid the foundation of **Mughal miniature painting**

**Humayun's Tomb:**
- Built by **Biga Begum (Haji Begum)**
- Architect: **Mirak Mirza Ghiyath**
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1993)**
- **First garden-tomb in India** (built 1570 CE)
- **Hamida Banu Begum**: wife of Humayun, mother of Akbar

### 11.3 Sher Shah Suri (1540-1545) — Sur Dynasty

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Real Name** | Farid Khan; childhood name Farid |
| **Key Victories** | Defeated Humayun at Chausa (1539) and Kannauj (1540); defeated Mahmud Shah of Bengal (1538) |

**Administrative Achievements:**
- Built **Shahi (Royal) Road** from Indus Valley to Sonar Valley (Bengal) → renamed **GT Road** by British (connecting Calcutta and Peshawar)
- **Sher Shah Suri Marg**: Delhi to Amritsar
- Constructed ~**1700 Sarais** (rest houses) every 4 miles; mosques; network of roads
- Staged **Dak-Chowkis** (relay postal system) in Sarais
- Introduced silver coin **"Rupiya"** (178 grains / 11.53 grams); continued by Mughals
- Introduced **tri-metalism** system (gold, silver, copper coins)
- His administrative model was followed by **Akbar**

**Tomb**: At **Sasaram** (Bihar)

### 11.4 Akbar (1556-1605)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Full Name** | Abulfatah Jalaluddin Muhammad Akbar |
| **Born** | October 15, 1542 at **Amarkot** (palace of Raja Virsal) |
| **Coronation** | At **Kalanaur** (Punjab) by **Bairam Khan**; **Takht-i-Akbari** platform |
| **Age at accession** | 13 years old (1556) |

**Second Battle of Panipat (November 5, 1556):**
- vs **Hemu** (Hem Chandra Vikramaditya)
- Bairam Khan and Khan Zaman led Mughal forces to victory
- Hemu was captured and executed

**Bairam Khan:**
- Regent/guardian of young Akbar
- Title: **"Khan-i-Khanan"** (King of Kings)
- His son: **Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** (Navratna; scholar of Arabic, Turkish, Persian, Sanskrit; born 1556)

**Military Campaigns:**
- **Malwa** (1562)
- **Gondwana** (1564)
- **Chittor** (1567-1568)
- **Ranthambore** (1569)
- **Gujarat** (1573)
- **Bengal** (1576)
- **Kashmir** (1586)
- **Khandesh** (1601)
- **Orissa** (—)

**Battle of Haldighati (June 18, 1576):**
- **Maharana Pratap** of Mewar vs Mughal forces led by **Man Singh I** of Amer
- Pratap's horse: **Chetak**
- Pratap's guru: **Swami Haridasa** (also guru of Tansen)
- **Battle of Dewair** (1582 CE): Pratap's counter-offensive
- **Amar Singh** (son of Pratap) submitted to Jahangir in **1615 CE**, ending Mughal-Mewar conflict

**Mansabdari System:**
- Introduced in **1571 CE** (18th year of reign)
- Lowest rank: 10; Highest rank: 10,000
- Replaced the Jagirdari system
- Grading system for rank, salary, and military responsibilities
- **Zat** rank = personal status; **Sawar** rank = cavalry

**Land Revenue System:**
- **Zabt system** + **Dahsala** (10-year survey, 1570-1580 CE)
- **Raja Todar Mal** — Finance/Revenue Minister
- Land classification: **Polaj** (best, continuously cultivated), **Chachar** (fallow 3-4 years), **Banjar** (uncultivated 5+ years)

**Religious Policy:**
- **Din-i-Ilahi** (1582 CE): "Religion of God"; belief in one God; only **18 followers**; first disciples: **Birbal**, Prince Salim, Abul-Fazl; Birbal was the **only Hindu** to adopt it
- **Sulah-e-kul**: universal peace (inspired by Sufi Saint Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti)
- **Mahzarnama** (1579 CE): declaration giving Akbar authority to change Islamic religious laws
- **Ibadat-Khana** (House of Worship): built 1575 at Fatehpur Sikri for inter-religious discussions
- Abolished **Jaziya** and **pilgrim taxes**

**Fatehpur Sikri:**
- First planned Mughal city; built 1569-1585 CE
- Capital 1571-1585 CE; abandoned due to water shortage
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1986)**
- Built after winning Chittor and Ranthambore
- **Buland Darwaza** (1575 CE) — highest gateway in world; commemorated Gujarat victory
- **Tomb of Sheikh Salim Chisti** (1571-1580 CE)
- **Panch Mahal** — 5-level palace; 84 columns on ground floor
- **Diwan-i-Khas** — hall of private audience

**Agra Fort:**
- Founded **1565 CE** by Akbar (red sandstone)
- Includes: Jahangir Mahal, Khas Mahal, Diwan-i-Khas

**Navratnas (9 Gems):**

| Gem | Role | Key Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Abul Fazl** | PM/Chief Advisor | Wrote Akbarnama + Ain-i-Akbari |
| **Faizi** | Education Minister | Poet; brother of Abul Fazl |
| **Birbal** | Foreign Minister | Real name Mahesh Das; Brahmin; only Hindu to adopt Din-i-Ilahi |
| **Raja Todar Mal** | Revenue Minister | Zabt/Dahsala system |
| **Raja Man Singh** | Chief of Army Staff | Rajput general |
| **Tansen** | Culture Minister/Singer | Real name Ramtanu Pandey; title "Mian" from Akbar; guru Swami Haridasa; court singer of King Ram Chand of Rewa; title from Raja Vikramjit of Gwalior; **Dhrupad** music style |
| **Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** | Defence Minister | Son of Bairam Khan; scholar of Arabic/Turkish/Persian/Sanskrit |
| **Mulla Do-Piyaza** | Home Minister | — |
| **Fakir Aziao-Din** | Religious Minister | — |

- **Muhammad Husain**: calligrapher; title **"Zarrin Qalam"** (Golden Pen); from Kashmir

**Literary Contributions:**
- Translated **Ramayana** and **Mahabharata** into Persian (Mahabharata = **Razmnama**, translated by Faizi and Abdul Qadir Bada'uni)
- **Dara Shikoh** translated **Upanishads** into Persian in **1657 CE**
- **Akbarnama**: 3 books by Abul Fazl — (1) Akbar's ancestors, (2) events of Akbar's reign, (3) **Ain-i-Akbari** (administration, 5 volumes)

**Death**: October 27, 1605 at Fatehpur Sikri; buried at **Sikandra** (Agra); tomb built 1605-1613 by Jahangir

**Other Details:**
- Administrative seat at Agra
- **Ajmer** became Suba headquarters under Mughals (12 original Subahs, established 1572-1580)
- First Deccan expedition to Ahmednagar under Prince Murad and Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan
- **Surat** was emporium of western trade during Mughal period

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|

**Nur Jahan:**

- Held significant political influence

**Foreign Visitors:**

**Monuments:**

### 11.6 Shah Jahan (1628-1658)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Real Name** | Mirza Shahab-ud-din Baig Muhammad Khan Khurram |

**Taj Mahal:**
- Construction: **1632-1648 CE**; Agra
- Built for wife **Mumtaz Mahal**
- White marble from **Makrana** (Nagaur, Rajasthan)
- Architects: **Ustad Ahmad Lahori** (also designed Red Fort Delhi) & **Ustad Isa**

**Red Fort (Delhi):**
- Built mid-17th century
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2007)**
- **Diwan-i-Am** contains marble canopy with Pietra Dura panels

**Jama Masjid (Delhi):**
- Built **1650-1656 CE**
- Also called **Masjid-e-Jahanuma**

**Other Monuments:**
- Agra Fort (marble additions)
- Jama Masjid (Agra)
- Moti Masjid (Agra Fort)
- **Shalimar Gardens** (Lahore, 1641-42 CE)
- **Shish Mahal** (Agra)
- **Musamman Burj**: octagonal tower in Agra Fort, built for Mumtaz Mahal

**Peacock Throne**: Built by Shah Jahan; taken by **Nadir Shah** in **1739 CE**

**War of Succession:**
- **Battle of Samugarh** (May 29, 1658 CE): Aurangzeb vs Dara Shikoh — Aurangzeb won
- **Battle of Deorai** (1659 CE): Aurangzeb defeated Dara Shikoh; Dara executed
- Shah Jahan imprisoned in **Agra Fort** until death (January 1666 CE)

**Other Details:**
- **Ahmednagar** annexed 1632 CE
- **Dara Shikoh** given title "Sultan Buland Iqbal" (1642 CE); works: **Majma-ul-Bahrain**, **Sirr-i-Akbar**
- **Padshahnama / Badshah Nama** by **Abdul Hamid Lahori** (official history)
- Foreign travelers: **Tavernier** (French jeweler, visited 6 times), **Peter Mundy**, **John Fryer**
- **Jahanara** (daughter): participated in architectural projects at Shahjahanabad
- **Roshanara** (daughter): supported Aurangzeb
- Poets: **Ali Quli Salim** and **Abu Talib**

### 11.7 Aurangzeb (1658-1707)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Real Name** | Muhi-ud-din Muhammad |
| **Titles** | **"Alamgir"** (conqueror of the world); **"Zindapir"** (living saint); Fakir; Darbesh |
| **Born** | November 3, 1618 at **Dahod, Malwa** (some sources say Ujjain) |
| **Relation** | 6th Mughal emperor; 3rd son of Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal |

**Key Events:**
- Defeated Dara Shikoh at **Samugarh** (1658 CE); imprisoned father Shah Jahan at Agra Fort
- Achieved the **largest territorial extent** of the Mughal Empire

**Religious Policy:**
- **Reimposed Jizya** and Pilgrim tax in **1679 CE**
- Ended **Navroz** celebration
- Stopped music and alcohol in court
- Stopped **jharokha** (public audience window)
- Attempted to vandalise **Kailash Temple** at Ellora

**Deccan Campaign (1680-1707):**
- Spent **27 years** in the Deccan
- Annexed **Bijapur** (1686 CE) and **Golconda** (1687 CE)
- Fought Marathas under Sambhaji, Rajaram, Tarabai
- Died at **Ahmednagar** in **1707 CE**

**Relations with Marathas:**
- Sent **Jai Singh** against Shivaji
- **Treaty of Purandar** (June 1665 CE): signed between Jai Singh I and Shivaji

**Relations with Sikhs:**
- Executed **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (9th Sikh Guru) in **1675 CE**
- Guru Tegh Bahadur founded **Anandpur Sahib**; title **"Srisht-di-Chadar"** (Protector of Humanity)

**Architecture:**
- **Bibi Ka Maqbara** (1678 CE): built by Prince **Azam Shah** in memory of mother **Dilras Banu Begum** (Rabia-ud-Daurani); Aurangabad; called "second Taj Mahal"
- **Badshahi Masjid** (Lahore, 1673 CE)
- **Mecca Masjid** (Hyderabad): started 1614 by Muhammad Qutub Shah, completed by Aurangzeb 1693 CE

**Other Details:**
- Gave French permission to build factory in Surat (1667 CE)
- Maximum number of books in Persian on classical Indian music written during his reign
- **Rathor Rajput rebellion** of Marwar due to Aurangzeb's interference in succession politics
- After death (March 3, 1707 CE): regional kingdoms declared independence — **Murshid Quli Khan** (Bengal), **Saadat Ali Khan** (Awadh), **Nizam-ul-Mulk** (Hyderabad)
