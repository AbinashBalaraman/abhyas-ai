## 1. National Awards of India

### 1.1 Civilian Awards (in order of precedence)

India's civilian awards are presented annually on Republic Day (26 January). They are recommendations of the Prime Minister and approved by the President.

**Order of Precedence (highest to lowest):**
1. **Bharat Ratna** (Jewel of India) — Highest; instituted 2 January 1954
   - Awarded for exceptional service in any field (art, literature, science, sports, public service)
   - No monetary grant
   - Medal: Peepal leaf-shaped, made of platinum
   - **First recipients (1954)**: C. Rajagopalachari, S. Radhakrishnan, C.V. Raman
   - First sportsperson to receive: **Sachin Tendulkar** (2014)
   - First non-Indian (honorary): Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987)
   - **Notable recent recipients**: Karpoori Thakur (posthumous, 2024), L.K. Advani (2024), Charan Singh (posthumous, 2024), P.V. Narasimha Rao (posthumous, 2024), M.S. Swaminathan (posthumous, 2024)
   - **2024 Recipients**: 5 awarded (Karpoori Thakur, L.K. Advani, Charan Singh, P.V. Narasimha Rao, M.S. Swaminathan)

2. **Padma Vibhushan** — 2nd highest; "exceptional and distinguished service"
   - 2024 recipients: Vyjayanthimala, Chiranjeevi, Bindeshwar Pathak, Konidela Chiranjeevi, etc.

3. **Padma Bhushan** — 3rd highest; "distinguished service of high order"

4. **Padma Shri** — 4th highest; "distinguished service"

5. **Jeevan Raksha Padak** — For life-saving acts

**Categories**:
- Padma Awards given in 3 categories: Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri
- **Total Padma Awards 2024**: 132 (5 Padma Vibhushan, 17 Padma Bhushan, 110 Padma Shri)
- **Total Padma Awards 2025**: ~130+

## 2. International Awards

### 2.1 Nobel Prizes

**Founded by**: Alfred Nobel (Swedish chemist, inventor of dynamite)
**First awarded**: 1901
**Headquarters**: Stockholm, Sweden (except Peace Prize in Oslo, Norway)
**Awarded annually on**: 10 December (Nobel Day — Alfred Nobel's death anniversary)

**Six Categories:**
1. **Physics**
2. **Chemistry**
3. **Physiology or Medicine**
4. **Literature**
5. **Peace** (awarded in Oslo)
6. **Economic Sciences** (Sveriges Riksbank Prize; added 1969; technically not a Nobel Prize but awarded alongside)

**Nobel Prize Money**: ~11 million SEK (~$1 million) per category
**Award Components**: Gold medal, diploma, monetary prize

**Indian Nobel Laureates:**
- **Rabindranath Tagore** (1913) — Literature (first non-European); Gitanjali
- **C.V. Raman** (1930) — Physics (Raman Effect)
- **Mother Teresa** (1979) — Peace (born in Skopje, Macedonia; became Indian citizen)
- **Amartya Sen** (1998) — Economics
- **Kailash Satyarthi** (2014) — Peace
- **Abhijit Banerjee** (2019) — Economics (with Esther Duflo, Michael Kremer)
- No Indian has won Nobel in Chemistry or Medicine (yet)

**Notable 2025 Nobel Laureates**:
- **Physics**: John Clarke, Michel H. Devoret, John M. Martinis — "for the discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric circuit"
- **Chemistry**: Susumu Kitagawa, Richard Robson, Omar M. Yaghi — "for the development of metal–organic frameworks"
- **Medicine**: Mary E. Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell, Shimon Sakaguchi — "for their discoveries concerning peripheral immune tolerance"
- **Literature**: László Krasznahorkai (Hungary) — "for his compelling and visionary oeuvre"
- **Peace**: María Corina Machado (Venezuela)
- **Economics**: Joel Mokyr, Philippe Aghion, Peter Howitt — "for the theory of sustained growth through creative destruction"

### 2.2 Other International Awards

  - **Indian recipients**: Mother Teresa, Vinoba Bhave, Jayaprakash Narayan, Previous Years Solved PapersBedi, Arvind Kejriwal, etc.

- **UNESCO Prizes**: Literacy Prize, Kalinga Prize (popularization of science)
- **Grammy**: For music (US)
- **Oscar (Academy Award)**: For films (US)
- **BAFTA**: For films (UK)
- **Cannes, Venice, Berlin Film Festivals**: For films (Europe)
- **Heisman Trophy**: For college football (US)
- **Ballon d'Or**: For football (Europe)
- **Man Booker International**: Earlier, for translated works
- **Wolf Prize**: For arts & sciences (Israel)
- **Sakurai Prize**: Theoretical physics
- **Templeton Prize**: For affirming life's spiritual dimension
- **Right Livelihood Award**: "Alternative Nobel" (Sweden)
- **Aga Khan Award for Architecture**
- **UNESCO World Heritage List**: For sites of outstanding universal value

## 4. Sports — Cricket & Beyond

### 4.1 Cricket

**International Cricket Council (ICC):**
- **Founded**: 15 June 1909 (as Imperial Cricket Conference); 1965 renamed ICC
- **Headquarters**: Dubai, UAE (since 2005; previously Lord's, London)
- **Current Chairman**: Jay Shah (since 1 December 2024)
- **Members**: 108 (as of 2024)
- **Permanent Members**: India, Australia, England (Test-playing nations)
- India is a founder member; BCCI (Board of Control for Cricket in India) founded 1928

**Major ICC Tournaments:**
- **Cricket World Cup (ODI)**: First held 1975 (England); held every 4 years
  - **India's ODI World Cup wins**: 1983 (Kapil Dev), 2011 (MS Dhoni)
  - **2023 World Cup**: Australia won (held in India)
- **T20 World Cup**: First held 2007 (South Africa); every 2 years
  - **India's T20 World Cup wins**: 2007 (MS Dhoni), 2024 (India), 2026 (India)
  - **2026 T20 World Cup**: Co-hosted by India and Sri Lanka (Feb 7 - Mar 8, 2026); India won 3rd title defeating New Zealand by 96 runs; first to win 3 times, 2 consecutive, and at home
- **Champions Trophy**: First held 1998; revived 2017
  - **India's Champions Trophy wins**: 2002 (shared with Sri Lanka), 2013, 2025
  - **2025 ICC Champions Trophy**: India won (3rd title) on 9 March 2025 in Dubai
- **Women's Cricket World Cup**:
  - **India's Women's CWC wins**: 2025 (first ever)
  - **2025 Women's CWC**: India won 2 November 2025
- **World Test Championship (WTC)**: Inaugurated 2019-2021
  - Current champion: Australia (verify)

**Cricket Formats:**
- **Test**: 5 days; 4 innings; oldest format
- **One-Day International (ODI)**: 50 overs per side
- **Twenty20 (T20)**: 20 overs per side
- **T10**: 10 overs (less common)
- **The Hundred**: 100 balls per side (England)

**Indian Cricket Milestones:**
- **First Test**: 1932 (vs England at Lord's)
- **First Test win**: 1952 (vs England at Madras)
- **Total Tests played**: 550+ 
- **Highest Test total**: 759/7 (Sachin Tendulkar 248* vs Bangladesh, 2004)
- **Most Test runs**: Sachin Tendulkar (15,921)
- **Most Test centuries**: Sachin Tendulkar (51)
- **Most Test wickets**: Muttiah Muralitharan (800)
- **Indian with most Test wickets**: Anil Kumble (619)
- **First triple century in Test**: Virender Sehwag (309 vs Pakistan, 2004)
- **First Indian to score 10,000 Test runs**: Sachin Tendulkar
- **First Indian to score 300+ in Test**: Virender Sehwag

**National Flag:**
- **Design**: Horizontal tricolor — saffron (top), white (middle), green (bottom)
- **Ashoka Chakra** (24-spoke navy blue wheel) in center
- **Designed by**: Pingali Venkayya (freedom fighter from Andhra Pradesh)
- **Adopted**: 22 July 1947 (constituent assembly)
- **Code**: Regulated by Flag Code of India, 2002

**National Anthem (Jana Gana Mana):**
- **Composed by**: Rabindranath Tagore
- **First sung**: 27 December 1911 (Calcutta Session of INC)
- **Adopted**: 24 January 1950
- **Duration**: ~52 seconds
- **Written in**: Sanskritized Bengali (Bengali script)
- **Awarded Nobel**: Tagore's Gitanjali (1913), not the anthem

**National Song (Vande Mataram):**
- **Composed by**: Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay (1870s, novel Anandamath)
- **Adopted**: 24 January 1950
- **Written in**: Sanskritized Bengali
- **Status**: National song (not anthem)

**National Emblem:**
- **Adopted**: 26 January 1950
- **Source**: Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath (Mauryan period, ~250 BCE)
- **Motto**: "Satyameva Jayate" (Truth alone triumphs) — from Mundaka Upanishad
- **Features**: 4 Asiatic lions standing back-to-back (only 3 visible in emblem; 4th hidden)
- **Below**: Horse and bull; dharma chakra
- **Slogan below**: "Satyameva Jayate" in Devanagari

**Indian National Calendar (Saka Era):**
- **Adopted**: 22 March 1957 (Saka Era 1879)
- **Era**: Saka (begins 78 CE)
- **Use**: Gazette, Government communications
- **Gregorian**: 1 Chaitra = 22 March (normal), 21 March (leap)

**National Pledge (in Schools):**
- Composed by: Pydimarri Venkata Subba Rao (1963)
- Adopted: under National Integration Council
