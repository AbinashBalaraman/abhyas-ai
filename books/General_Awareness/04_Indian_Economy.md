## 1. Indian Economy — Overview & Characteristics

### 1.1 Salient Features of Indian Economy

India is one of the fastest-growing major economies in the world, currently ranked **5th largest by nominal GDP** (after USA, China, Japan, Germany) and **3rd largest by PPP (Purchasing Power Parity)**. 

**Key Characteristics:**
1. **Mixed Economy**: Coexistence of public and private sectors; private property & government regulation
2. **Developing Economy**: Lower-middle-income country status; rising per capita income
3. **Agrarian Base**: ~58% population dependent on agriculture (largest private-sector enterprise)
4. **Demographic Dividend**: Median age ~28 years; large young working-age population
5. **Federal Structure**: Concurrent List (Union + State); Fiscal federalism
6. **Open Economy**: Liberalization, privatization, globalization (LPG reforms 1991)
7. **Service Sector Dominant**: Services contribute ~53% to GDP, Industry ~26%, Agriculture ~18% (FY24)
8. **Capitalist with Welfare State**: Market economy + extensive social welfare schemes

### 1.2 Economy & Economics — Basic Concepts

**Economics** = Greek "oikonomia" (household management). Adam Smith (1776) — "An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations" — considered the **Father of Modern Economics**.

**Branches of Economics:**
- **Microeconomics**: Study of individual agents (households, firms, markets)
- **Macroeconomics**: Aggregate economy (GDP, inflation, unemployment)
- **Positive Economics**: What is (factual)
- **Normative Economics**: What ought to be (value judgments)
- **Managerial Economics**: Business decision-making
- **Behavioural Economics**: Psychology + economics (Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky)

**Important Terms:**
- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**: Total monetary value of all final goods & services produced within a country in a year
  - Formula: $GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)$
    - C = Consumption, I = Investment, G = Government Spending, X = Exports, M = Imports
  - **Real GDP** (inflation-adjusted) vs **Nominal GDP** (current prices)
  - **GDP Deflator** = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100
- **GNP (Gross National Product)**: GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)
  - $GNP = GDP + NFIA$
  - If NFIA > 0: GNP > GDP (as in India in some years)
- **NDP (Net Domestic Product)**: GDP - Depreciation
- **NNP (Net National Product)**: GNP - Depreciation = National Income
- **Per Capita Income**: NNP / Population

**National Income Calculation Methods:**
1. **Product Method (Value Added Method)**: Sum of value added at each stage
2. **Income Method**: Sum of factor incomes (rent, wages, interest, profit)
3. **Expenditure Method**: Sum of final expenditures (C + I + G + NX)

**Base Year for GDP:** Currently **2011-12** (revised base; per MoSPI)

## 2. Economic Planning in India

### 2.1 History of Planning

**Planning Commission** was established on **15 March 1950** by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet, based on the recommendations of the **Advisory Planning Board** constituted in 1946 under PM Nehru.

**First Five Year Plan (1951-56):** Modeled on **Harrod-Domar Model**. Focused on agriculture; prioritized by Prof. P.C. Mahalanobis. Targeted GDP growth: 2.1%; achieved 3.6%.

**Twelve Five Year Plans (FYPs):**
| FYP | Period | Focus/Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| First | 1951-56 | Agriculture; rehabilitation |
| Second | 1956-61 | Industrialization; Mahalanobis model |
| Third | 1961-66 | Self-reliant economy; Sino-Indian war |
| Plan Holidays | 1966-69 | Drought; wars; INA; Lal Bahadur Shastri, Indira Gandhi |
| Fourth | 1969-74 | "Indira's Plan" — self-reliance + growth with stability |
| Fifth | 1974-79 | "Rolling Plan"; Garibi Hatao; ended by JP movement |
| Sixth | 1980-85 | Family planning; science & technology |
| Seventh | 1985-90 | Food, work, productivity |
| Annual Plans | 1990-92 | Balance of payments crisis |
| Eighth | 1992-97 | Liberalization; "Money, Markets, Materials" |
| Ninth | 1997-2002 | "Growth with social justice" |
| Tenth | 2002-07 | "India Shining" — 8% growth target |
| Eleventh | 2007-12 | "Faster, more inclusive growth" |
| Twelfth | 2012-17 | "Faster, sustainable, inclusive growth" |

**Plan Holidays:** 1966-69 (3 years; 3 Annual Plans) and 1990-92 (2 Annual Plans)

### 2.2 NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)

**NITI Aayog** replaced the Planning Commission on **1 January 2015** (Planning Commission dissolved on same date, 65 years after creation).

**Composition:**
- **Chairperson**: Prime Minister (ex officio)
- **Vice Chairperson**: Appointed by PM (First VC: **Arvind Panagariya**; Current: **Suman Bery** )
- **Members**: Maximum 4 Union Ministers (ex officio) — Nominated by PM
- **Special Invitees**: Domain experts
- **CEO**: Appointed by PM; senior administrative position
- **Part-time members**: Up to 5

**Functions:**
- Advisory and Think Tank — replaces directive/target-based planning
- Cooperative federalism — bottom-up approach
- 15-year Vision Document; 7-year Strategy; 3-year Action Agenda

**Key Differences — NITI Aayog vs Planning Commission:**

| Feature | Planning Commission | NITI Aayog |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Extra-constitutional body | Executive body |
| Approach | Top-down, centralization | Bottom-up, federal |
| Power | Could allocate funds | Advisory only; funds allocated by Finance Ministry |
| Role | Directive | Think tank |
| Dissolution/Setup | 15 March 1950 – 1 January 2015 | 1 January 2015 – present |

### 2.3 Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) & RBI

Already covered in detail in **Chapter 3: Monetary Policy & Rates**. Key points:
- Statutory body under RBI Act (amended 2016)
- 6 members: 3 RBI + 3 external
- Determines policy repo rate
- Inflation target: 4% CPI (band 2-6%)
- Currently chaired by RBI Governor (Sanjay Malhotra, as of December 2024)

### 2.4 Important Economic Indices & Surveys

**Human Development Index (HDI) — UNDP:**
- Composite: Life expectancy + Education (Mean & Expected years of schooling) + Per capita income
- India ranked **134/193** (HDI 0.644 — medium human development) in HDR 2023-24
- HDI ranges 0-1; categories: Very High (≥0.800), High (0.700-0.799), Medium (0.550-0.699), Low (<0.550)

**Gender Inequality Index (GII):** Measures women's reproductive health, empowerment, labour market participation
**Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI):** Health, education, living standards

**Gini Coefficient:** Measures income inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality)

**India's Important Reports:**
- **Economic Survey**: Tabled a day before Union Budget; presented by Finance Minister
- **Annual Budget**: 1 February each year (since 2017)
- **Mid-Year Economic Analysis**: Mid-year review
- **Monetary Policy Report**: Bi-annual by RBI
- **Financial Stability Report (FSR)**: Bi-annual by RBI
- **World Investment Report**: UNCTAD
- **Global Competitiveness Report**: WEF (suspended post-2021)
- **Ease of Doing Business**: World Bank (suspended)

**Ease of Doing Business (Historical, before suspension):**
- India jumped from 142nd (2014) to 63rd (2020) — major reform period under PM Modi

## 4. Tax System in India

India has a dual tax system — **Direct Taxes** (levied on income/profit) and **Indirect Taxes** (levied on consumption of goods/services).

### 4.1 Direct Taxes

**Direct taxes** are paid directly by individuals/organizations on their income or wealth.

- **Income Tax**: Levied under Income Tax Act, 1961
  - Administered by **CBDT (Central Board of Direct Taxes)** under Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance
  - **Headquarters**: CBDT — Delhi
  - PAN (Permanent Account Number) for all assesses above threshold
  - **Aadhaar-PAN linking**: Mandatory (as per amendment)

- **Corporate Tax**: On company profits
  - Reduced to **22%** (without exemptions) and **15%** (for new manufacturing companies incorporated after 1 October 2019; effective rate ~17.01% inclusive of surcharge and cess) under Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019

- **Securities Transaction Tax (STT)**: On equity, derivatives, etc.

- **Capital Gains Tax**:
  - **STCG (Short-Term Capital Gains)**: <12 months holding
  - **LTCG (Long-Term Capital Gains)**: >12 months holding
  - **Budget 2024-25**: LTCG increased from 10% to 12.5%; STCG from 15% to 20%

- **Wealth Tax**: Abolished in 2015

- **Estate Duty**: Abolished in 1985

- **Gift Tax**: Abolished (but gifts taxed under "Income from Other Sources" if exceeds ₹50,000)

- **BCTT (Basic Customs Duty)**: Not direct tax but on imports

**Income Tax Slabs (New Tax Regime — Default from FY 2024-25):**

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹3 lakh | Nil |
| ₹3-7 lakh | 5% |
| ₹7-10 lakh | 10% |
| ₹10-12 lakh | 15% |
| ₹12-15 lakh | 20% |
| Above ₹15 lakh | 30% |

- **Standard Deduction**: ₹75,000 (Budget 2025-26) 
- **Rebate u/s 87A**: Up to ₹7 lakh (effectively zero tax)
- **Cess**: 4% Health & Education Cess on tax
- **Surcharge**: 10-25% on high-income earners

**Income Tax Slabs (Old Tax Regime — Optional):**

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹2.5 lakh | Nil |
| ₹2.5-5 lakh | 5% |
| ₹5-10 lakh | 20% |
| Above ₹10 lakh | 30% |

### 4.2 Indirect Taxes

**Indirect taxes** are collected by intermediaries (sellers) from consumers and paid to the government.

- **GST (Goods and Services Tax)**: Comprehensive indirect tax from 1 July 2017
- **Customs Duty**: On imports (and some exports)
- **Excise Duty**: Mostly subsumed in GST; remaining on petroleum products, alcohol, tobacco

### 4.3 GST (Goods and Services Tax)

Implemented on **1 July 2017** under the **Constitutional (101st Amendment) Act, 2016**. Replaced multiple Central and State taxes:
- **Central taxes subsumed**: Excise Duty, Service Tax, Additional Excise Duty, CVD, SAD, CST
- **State taxes subsumed**: VAT, Entry Tax, Entertainment Tax, Luxury Tax, Octroi, Purchase Tax

**GST Council:**
- **Article 279A** of the Constitution (101st Amendment)
- Chairperson: **Union Finance Minister**
- Members: Union Minister of State for Finance + Finance Ministers of all States and UTs (with legislature)
- Quorum: 1/2 of total members
- Decisions: By 3/4 majority of weighted votes (Central: 1/3; States: 2/3)
- **33rd GST Council Meeting** (Feb 2023) and subsequent meetings rationalized rates

**GST Slabs (Effective Sept 2025):**
| Slab | Items |
|---|---|
| **0%** | Essential items, food grains, fresh fruits, milk, eggs, curd, bread, salt, education, healthcare |
| **5%** | Packaged food, transport, spices, oils, medical equipment |
| **12%** | Processed food, computers, butter, ghee, namkeen |
| **18%** | Hair oil, soap, toothpaste, capital goods, industrial intermediaries (DEFAULT rate) |
| **28%** | Luxury/demerit goods (cars, cement, tobacco, aerated drinks) |
| **28% + Cess** | Super luxury (large cars, SUVs) |

**GST Components:**
- **CGST (Central GST)**: Intra-state; goes to Central Govt
- **SGST (State GST)**: Intra-state; goes to State Govt
- **IGST (Integrated GST)**: Inter-state; shared between Centre & State
- **UTGST (Union Territory GST)**: For UTs without legislature

**Important Definitions:**
- **HSN Code**: Harmonized System of Nomenclature (for goods)
- **SAC Code**: Services Accounting Code (for services)
- **GSTN (GST Network)**: IT backbone; private company with Govt stakes; manages GST portal
- **ITC (Input Tax Credit)**: Tax paid on inputs used for further supply
- **Reverse Charge**: Recipient pays GST (in specified cases)
- **E-Way Bill**: Required for goods transport above ₹50,000

**GST Collection (Monthly):**
- Average monthly collection: ₹1.5-1.7 lakh crore
- Record collection: ₹2.10 lakh crore (April 2024)

### 4.4 Direct Tax Code (DTC) — Replaced by Income Tax Act, 1961

The original Direct Taxes Code (DTC) Bill was introduced in 2010 but lapsed. The Income Tax Act, 1961 continues with periodic amendments.

### 4.5 Other Important Tax Terms

- **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)**: Sections 192-196I; rates vary (1%-30%)
- **TCS (Tax Collected at Source)**: On specified transactions
- **MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax)**: For companies; 15% (+ cess)
- **AMT (Alternate Minimum Tax)**: For non-corporate assesses
- **DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement)**: India has DTAA with ~100 countries
- **GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rule)**: From 1 April 2017; tax avoidance
- **Transfer Pricing**: Pricing between related entities in international transactions
- **BEPS (Base Erosion and Profit Shifting)**: OECD framework

## 9. Foreign Trade & BoP

### 9.1 Balance of Payments (BoP)

**BoP**: Systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world.

**Two Main Accounts:**
1. **Current Account**: Trade in goods (visible), services (invisible), primary income, secondary income
   - Includes **Trade Balance** (Exports - Imports)
   - **Current Account Deficit (CAD)**: When imports > exports
2. **Capital Account**: Capital transfers, FDI, FPI, ECBs, banking capital

**Autonomous vs Accommodating Transactions**

### 9.2 Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) vs FPI

- **FDI**: Long-term investment; management control; ≥10% equity; less volatile
- **FPI**: Short-term portfolio investment; <10% equity; volatile; "hot money"

**FDI Inflows to India (FY25):** ~$80-90 billion (verify)
**Sectors attracting highest FDI:** Services, Computer Software & Hardware, Telecommunications, Trading, Automobile, Construction

### 9.3 Foreign Exchange Reserves

India's forex reserves comprise:
1. **Foreign Currency Assets (FCA)** — US$ (largest component)
2. **Gold**
3. **Special Drawing Rights (SDRs)** — IMF
4. **Reserve Position in IMF (RTP)**

**Current**: ~$700 billion (verify) — 4th largest in the world

### 9.4 Trade Agreements

## 10. Economic Reforms Since 1991

### 10.1 LPG Reforms 1991

**Triggered by**:
- 1990-91 BoP crisis
- Foreign exchange reserves dwindled to 2 weeks of imports
- IMF intervention; rupee devalued
- V.P. Singh government → P.V. Narasimha Rao + Manmohan Singh as FM

**Three Pillars:**
- **L**iberalization
- **P**rivatization
- **G**lobalization

**Key Reforms:**
- Industrial Policy abolished licensing (except 18 industries)
- MRTP Act diluted
- Public sector disinvestment
- Foreign investment welcomed
- FERA replaced by FEMA (1999)
- Capital market reforms; SEBI strengthened
- Banking reforms; Narasimham Committee (1991, 1998)
- Tariff reduction; trade liberalization
- Rupee devaluation (July 1991)

### 10.2 Major Economic Reform Committees

- **Narasimham Committee I (1991)**: Banking reforms
- **Narasimham Committee II (1998)**: Further banking reforms
- **R.V. Gupta Committee**: Kisan Credit Card (KCC) — 1998
- **Rangarajan Committee**: Various
- **Chakravarty Committee**: Monetary policy
- **Tarpore Committee**: Balance of Payments
- **Wadhwa Committee**: Disinvestment
- **Arthkranti / Yashwant Sinha Committee**: Expenditure
- **Kelkar Committee**: Tax reforms

### 10.3 Goods & Services Tax (GST) Implementation

Already covered. **Major milestone** of Indian indirect tax reform.

### 10.4 Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016

Effective **1 December 2016**. Created:
- **IBBI** (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) — regulator
- **NCLT** (National Company Law Tribunal) — adjudicating authority
- **NCLAT** — appellate
- **CIRP** (Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process) — 180 days + 90 days extension (max 330 days)
- **IPs** (Insolvency Professionals) and **IPAs** (IPA registered with IBBI)

### 10.5 Other Major Reforms

- **FDI Liberalization**: Progressive; 100% FDI in most sectors
- **GST**: Implemented 1 July 2017
- **RERA**: Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016
- **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act 2018**
- **Companies Act 2013**
- **Banning of Unregulated Deposit Schemes Act 2019**
- **Consumer Protection Act 2019**
- **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023**
