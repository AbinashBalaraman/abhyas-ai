## 1. Advent of Europeans in India

### 1.1 Portuguese (1498)

- **Vasco da Gama** reached Calicut (Kozhikode, Kerala) on **20 May 1498**
- Received by the **Zamorin** (Samudri Raja, ruler of Calicut)
- Vasco da Gama came via the Cape of Good Hope (Africa)
- **Pedro Alvarez Cabral** arrived in 1500 (2nd Portuguese expedition)
- **Francisco de Almeida** (1505-1509): First Portuguese Governor in India
  - Policy: "Blue Water Policy" (naval supremacy)
  - Died in battle of Diu (1509)
- **Alfonso de Albuquerque** (1509-1515): Greatest Portuguese Governor
  - Captured **Goa** from Bijapur Sultan in **1510** (capital of Portuguese India until 1961)
  - Introduced **Cartaze system** (naval trade permit/pass)
  - Encouraged Portuguese-Indian marriages
- **Nino da Cunha** (1529-1538): Transferred capital from Cochin to Goa (1530)
- Portuguese centers: Goa, Daman, Diu, Salsette, Bassein, Chaul, Bombay, Hooghly, Satgaon, Chittagong
- **St. Francis Xavier** arrived in Goa (1542) for Christian missionary work
- Portuguese lost to Dutch and English; Goa liberated by India on **17-19 December 1961**

### 1.2 Dutch (1602)

- **Dutch East India Company (VOC)** established in **1602**
- First Dutch factory at **Masulipatnam** (1605)
- Main centers: Pulicat, Surat, Bimlipatam, Karaikal, Chinsura, Kasimbazar, Patna, Balasore, Negapatnam, Cochin
- **Pulicat** was their main center (later shifted to Nagapattinam)
- Anglo-Dutch rivalry: Dutch defeated by English at the **Battle of Bedara** (1759)
- Dutch ceded all Indian settlements to the British by the **Treaty of 1824**

### 1.3 English (British East India Company)

- **English East India Company (EIC)** chartered on **31 December 1600** by **Queen Elizabeth I**
- Original name: "Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies"
- **Captain Hawkins** arrived at Jahangir's court (1608) — got permission for factory at Surat but withdrawn later
- First English factory: **Surat** (1613) — established after Sir Thomas Roe's embassy (1615-18) to Jahangir
- **Bombay** (1661): Received from Portuguese as dowry for Catherine of Braganza's marriage to Charles II; transferred to EIC in 1668
- **Madras (Fort St. George)**: Founded 1639 (Francis Day); first English fort in India
- **Calcutta (Fort William)**: Founded 1690 by **Job Charnock**
- **Bombay** became the headquarters of the West coast; Madras of the East coast
- In 1717, the EIC obtained **Farman** from Mughal Emperor **Farrukhsiyar** — exempted from customs duties in Bengal

### 1.4 French (1664)

- **French East India Company** founded in **1664** by **Colbert** (under Louis XIV)
- First French factory: **Surat** (1668)
- **Pondicherry** (now Puducherry) founded by **François Martin** in **1674**
- Other centers: Chandernagore (Bengal), Mahe, Karaikal, Yanam
- **Joseph François Dupleix** (1742-1754): Most ambitious French Governor
  - Started Anglo-French rivalry in India
  - French defeated at the **Battle of Wandiwash** (1760) by Sir Eyre Coote
- **Treaty of Paris (1763)**: Ended French political ambitions in India; French allowed to keep trading posts only

### 1.5 Anglo-French / Carnatic Wars (1746-1763)

| War | Period | Key Events | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Carnatic War** | 1746-1748 | Part of War of Austrian Succession; French captured Madras (1746) | Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748); Madras returned to English |
| **Second Carnatic War** | 1749-1754 | Dupleix supported Chanda Sahib for Carnatic Nawabship; Robert Clive captured Arcot (1751) | Treaty of Pondicherry (1754); Dupleix recalled |
| **Third Carnatic War** | 1756-1763 | Part of Seven Years' War; Battle of Wandiwash (1760) — Sir Eyre Coote defeated Count de Lally | Treaty of Paris (1763); French confined to Pondicherry |

## 2. British East India Company Rule (1757-1857)

### 2.1 Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757)

- Fought between **Siraj-ud-Daulah** (Nawab of Bengal) and **Robert Clive** (EIC)
- Siraj-ud-Daulah had captured Fort William (Calcutta) — "Black Hole Tragedy" (June 1756)
- **Mir Jafar** betrayed Siraj-ud-Daulah (secret pact with Clive)
- Siraj-ud-Daulah was captured and killed by **Miran** (son of Mir Jafar)
- **Significance**: Beginning of British political control in India; Mir Jafar made Nawab
- Mir Jafar could not pay the promised amount → replaced by **Mir Qasim** (1760)
- Mir Qasim eventually rebelled → led to Battle of Buxar

### 2.2 Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764)

- Fought between EIC (Hector Munro) and combined forces of:
  - **Mir Qasim** (deposed Nawab of Bengal)
  - **Shuja-ud-Daulah** (Nawab of Awadh)
  - **Shah Alam II** (Mughal Emperor)
- **British victory** — decisive and complete
- **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)**:
  - Clive signed with Shuja-ud-Daulah and Shah Alam II
  - Shah Alam II got Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, Odisha → transferred to EIC
  - Shuja-ud-Daulah returned to Awadh, paid indemnity
  - **Diwani rights** (right to collect revenue) of Bengal, Bihar, Odisha granted to EIC
- **Significance**: EIC became the real master of Bengal; Dual System of Government established

### 2.3 Dual System of Government (1765-1772)

- Introduced by **Robert Clive** after Treaty of Allahabad
- EIC had **Diwani** (revenue collection) but not Nizamat (police/justice)
- Nawab had **Nizamat** but no revenue power
- Both power and responsibility separated → led to corruption and exploitation
- Abolished by **Warren Hastings** in **1772** when he took over direct administration

### 2.4 Anglo-Mysore Wars (1767-1799)

| War | Period | Key Events | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Anglo-Mysore** | 1767-1769 | Hyder Ali vs EIC (Lord Carnac) | Treaty of Madras (1769); mutual return of territories; English promised help if attacked (defensive alliance) |
| **Second Anglo-Mysore** | 1780-1784 | Hyder Ali attacked; Haider died 1782; Tipu continued | Treaty of Mangalore (1784); status quo ante; both returned conquered territories |
| **Third Anglo-Mysore** | 1790-1792 | Tipu Sultan vs Lord Cornwallis; Tipu attacked Travancore (ally of British) | Treaty of Seringapatam (1792); Tipu lost half his territory; paid 3 crore rupees; two sons held as hostage |
| **Fourth Anglo-Mysore** | 1799 | Lord Wellesley; Tipu Sultan killed defending Seringapatam | End of Mysore kingdom; Wodeyar dynasty restored; Tipu died 4 May 1799 |

- **Hyder Ali**: Started as a soldier in Mysore army, rose to power; became de facto ruler of Mysore (1761)
- **Tipu Sultan**: "Tiger of Mysore"; introduced new calendar, new coinage, new weights; member of Jacobin Club; planted "Tree of Liberty" at Seringapatam; used rockets in warfare; died fighting on 4 May 1799

### 2.5 Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775-1818)

| War | Period | Key Events | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Anglo-Maratha** | 1775-1782 | Raghunath Rao sought British help; fought at multiple fronts | Treaty of Salbai (1782); status quo; Raghunath Rao pensioned |
| **Second Anglo-Maratha** | 1803-1805 | Lord Wellesley; defeated Daulat Rao Scindia and Bhonsle | Treaty of Bassein (1802) preceded; Scindia lost territories; Subsidiary Alliance imposed |
| **Third Anglo-Maratha** | 1817-1818 | Lord Hastings; Pindari War simultaneously; Baji Rao II fled | End of Maratha political power; Baji Rao II pensioned; Maratha Confederacy dissolved |

- **Treaty of Bassein (1802)**: Baji Rao II accepted Subsidiary Alliance → triggered Second Anglo-Maratha War
- After Third War: Peshwaship abolished; Maratha power ended

### 2.6 Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845-1849)

- **Maharaja Ranjit Singh** (Sher-e-Punjab): Built powerful Sikh kingdom (1799-1839)
  - Signed **Treaty of Amritsar (1809)** with British — Sutlej as boundary
  - Modernized army with European officers (Jean-François Allard, Paolo Avitabile)
  - Died in 1839 → succession crisis
- **First Anglo-Sikh War (1845-46)**:
  - Fought during Lord Hardinge's tenure
  - British victory at battles of Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal, Sobraon
  - **Treaty of Lahore (1846)**: Kashmir sold to Gulab Singh (Dogra); Sikh territory reduced; British resident at Lahore
- **Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848-49)**:
  - Fought during Lord Dalhousie's tenure
  - Multan revolt by Diwan Mulraj; Chattar Singh's rebellion
  - **Battle of Gujarat (1849)**: Decisive British victory
  - **Annexation of Punjab (1849)**: Dalhousie annexed Punjab; boy king Dalip Singh pensioned

### 2.8 Subsidiary Alliance (1798)

- Introduced by **Lord Wellesley**
- Indian ruler had to:
  - Accept permanent British military force within his territory
  - Pay for the force (or cede territory for it)
  - Accept British Resident at his court
  - Not employ any European without British permission
  - Not wage war or sign treaties without British permission
  - British promised to protect the state from external threats
- First state to accept: **Nizam of Hyderabad** (1798)
- Other states: Mysore (1799), Tanjore (1799), Awadh (1801), Bhonsle (1803), Scindia (1803), Peshwa (1802), Holkar (1818), Jodhpur, Jaipur, Bharatpur, etc.

### 2.9 Doctrine of Lapse (1848)

- Introduced by **Lord Dalhousie**
- If an Indian ruler died without a natural male heir, the kingdom would "lapse" to the British
- Adopted heirs were not recognized as legitimate successors
- States annexed under Doctrine of Lapse:
  - **Satara** (1848)
  - **Jaitpur** (1849)
  - **Sambalpur** (1849)
  - **Baghat** (1850)
  - **Udaipur** (1852)
  - **Jhansi** (1853) — Rani Lakshmibai's adopted son not recognized
  - **Nagpur** (1853)
  - **Carnatic** (1853)
  - **Tanjore** (1855)
  - **Awadh** (1856) — annexed on grounds of misgovernance (not Doctrine of Lapse technically)

## 3. Land Revenue Systems Under British Rule

### 3.1 Permanent Settlement / Zamindari System (1793)

- Introduced by **Lord Cornwallis** in **1793** in Bengal, Bihar, Odisha
- Also called **Permanent Settlement**
- **Zamindars** were recognized as owners of land
- Zamindars paid a fixed amount (89%) to the government; kept 11% as collection cost
- Revenue demand was fixed permanently — could not be increased
- Zamindars could sell, mortgage, or transfer land
- **Impact**: Zamindars became absentee landlords; peasants became tenants; exploitation increased; agriculture stagnated

### 3.2 Ryotwari System (1820)

- Introduced by **Sir Thomas Munro** in **1820** in Madras Presidency
- Later extended to Bombay Presidency, Assam, Coorg
- **Direct settlement** between government and ryot (peasant/cultivator)
- Revenue fixed per acre; revised every 30 years (later 20-30 years)
- Peasant was recognized as owner of land as long as he paid revenue
- **Impact**: High revenue demand; peasants fell into debt; moneylenders gained control

### 3.3 Mahalwari System (1822)

- Introduced by **Holt Mackenzie** in **1822**
- Applied in: Punjab, Agra, Awadh, Central Provinces, North-Western Provinces
- Revenue settlement with village community (Mahal)
- Village headman (Lambardar) collected revenue
- Revenue revised periodically (not permanent)
- **Impact**: Revenue demand was highest among all three systems

### 3.4 Comparison of Revenue Systems

| Feature | Zamindari (1793) | Ryotwari (1820) | Mahalwari (1822) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduced by** | Lord Cornwallis | Thomas Munro | Holt Mackenzie |
| **Region** | Bengal, Bihar, Odisha | Madras, Bombay | Punjab, Awadh, CP |
| **Settlement with** | Zamindars | Individual peasants | Village community |
| **Ownership** | Zamindar | Peasant (Ryot) | Village community |
| **Revision** | Permanent | Every 30 years | Periodic |
| **Collection** | Zamindar | Direct to govt | Village headman |

## 4. Colonial Economy and Drain of Wealth

### 4.1 Drain of Wealth Theory

- **Dadabhai Naoroji** was the first to propound the "Drain of Wealth" theory
  - Known as **"Grand Old Man of India"**
  - First Indian elected to British House of Commons (1892)
  - Founded **East India Association** in London (1866)
  - Book: "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India"
- **R.C. Dutt** also highlighted economic exploitation in his works
- Theory: India's wealth was being drained to England through:
  - Salaries of British officials
  - Profits of British companies
  - Interest on loans
  - Home charges (pensions, war expenses)
- This led to deindustrialization, poverty, and famines

### 4.2 Development of Railways

- First railway line: **Bombay to Thane** (16 April 1853) — 34 km
  - During Lord Dalhousie's tenure (1848-1856)
  - Known as "Father of Indian Railways"
- Second line: Howrah to Hooghly (1854)
- First passenger train: Bombay to Thane
- Purpose: To facilitate movement of raw materials and British goods
- By 1869: About 4,000 miles of railway track
- Railway gauge: Broad gauge (5'6")

### 4.4 Famines

- **Bengal Famine (1770)**: Killed one-third of Bengal's population (~10 million deaths); during EIC rule
- **Famine of 1876-78**: South India; ~5 million deaths; Lord Lytton's mismanagement
- **Famine of 1896-97**: Widespread across India
- **Bengal Famine of 1943**: During WWII; ~2-3 million deaths; Churchill's policies blamed
- **Famine Commissions**:
  - First Famine Commission (1878): Sir Richard Strachey
  - Second Famine Commission (1880): Sir Richard Strachey
  - Third Famine Commission (1898): Sir James Lyall

### 5.2 Charter Act of 1813

- First time, EIC was directed to spend **₹1 lakh annually** on education in India
- This was the first state initiative for education in India

### 5.3 Macaulay's Minute (1835)

- **Thomas Babington Macaulay** (Law Member of Governor-General's Council)
- Advocated **English medium** education
- Promoted Western literature and science
- Downplayed Indian traditional knowledge
- **English** made the official language of India (replacing Persian)
- Lord William Bentinck implemented the recommendations

### 5.4 Wood's Despatch (1854)

- Known as **"Magna Carta of English Education in India"**
- Proposed by **Sir Charles Wood**
- Key recommendations:
  - Department of Education in each province
  - Universities at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (established 1857)
  - Graded education system: primary → secondary → university
  - Vernacular medium at primary level; English at higher levels
  - Establishment of affiliated colleges
  - Training of teachers
  - Grants-in-aid to private schools

### 5.5 Hunter Commission (1882)

- Chaired by **Sir William Wilson Hunter**
- Focused on primary and secondary education
- Recommended:
  - Primary education should be given priority
  - Transfer of primary education to local bodies/municipalities
  - Secondary education should be divided into literary and vocational streams
  - Private enterprise in education should be encouraged
  - Expansion of women's education

### 5.6 Indian Universities Commission / Raleigh Commission (1902)

- Chaired by Lord Curzon
- Aimed to reform university administration
- Reduced university autonomy
- Increased government control over universities

### 5.7 Hartog Committee (1929)

- Chaired by **Sir Philip Hartog**
- Key recommendations:
  - Primary education should be qualitative, not just quantitative
  - Emphasis on improving standards rather than expansion
  - Secondary education should be diversified
  - University education should be limited to capable students
  - Recommended against rapid expansion of universities

### 5.8 Sargent Plan (1944)

- Also known as **Sargent Report on Post-War Education Development**
- Proposed free and compulsory education for children aged 6-14
- Aimed at achieving 100% literacy in 40 years
- Recommended improvement in quality of teachers and their training
- Proposed adult education programs

## 7. The Revolt of 1857 (First War of Independence)

### 7.1 Causes

**Political Causes:**
- **Doctrine of Lapse** (Lord Dalhousie): Annexed Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, etc.
- **Annexation of Awadh** (1856): On grounds of misgovernance; Nawab Wajid Ali Shah deposed
- **Subsidiary Alliance**: Eroded sovereignty of Indian rulers
- **General Service Enlistment Act (1856)**: Required sepoys to serve overseas (against Hindu/Muslim beliefs)
- **Title of Governor-General changed**: Bahadur Shah Zafar was told his successors would not use "King" title

**Economic Causes:**
- Heavy taxation on peasants and zamindars
- Ruin of artisans and craftsmen due to British manufactured goods
- Loss of patronage for traditional scholars, soldiers, and nobles
- **Drain of Wealth**: Economic exploitation of India

**Social & Religious Causes:**
- **Greased cartridges**: Enfield rifle cartridges greased with cow and pig fat (offensive to Hindus and Muslims)
- Reforms like abolition of Sati (1829), widow remarriage act (1856) seen as interference
- Rumors of forced conversion to Christianity
- **Religious Disabilities Act (1850)**: Changed Hindu law of property for converts

**Military Causes:**
- Indian sepoys paid less than British soldiers
- No promotion prospects for Indians beyond subedar
- Indian sepoys required to serve overseas (General Service Enlistment Act)
- Discrimination in treatment

### 7.2 Immediate Cause

- **29 March 1857**: **Mangal Pandey** of 34th Native Infantry at Barrackpore refused to use greased cartridges
- He fired at Sergeant Major Hudson and killed two British officers
- Mangal Pandey was hanged on **8 April 1857**
- On **24 April 1857**, 90 sepoys at Meerut refused to use greased cartridges; 85 were court-martialed and sentenced to 10 years imprisonment
- **10 May 1857**: Sepoys at Meerut broke into revolt, released prisoners, killed British officers, and marched to Delhi

### 7.4 Suppression and Causes of Failure

**Causes of Failure:**
- No unified leadership or central command
- Limited geographical spread (mainly North India — Bengal, Bihar, UP, Central India)
- No participation from South India, Punjab, and most of Bengal
- Lack of modern weapons and communication
- Many Indian rulers (Sindhia, Holkar, Nizam, Rajputs) supported the British
- No clear plan or ideology for post-revolt governance
- Educated middle class did not support the revolt

**Suppression:**
- Delhi recaptured: September 1857
- Lucknow recaptured: March 1858
- Jhansi fell: June 1858
- Tantia Tope captured and hanged: April 1859
- Kunwar Singh died: April 1858
- By end of 1859, the revolt was completely suppressed

### 7.5 Aftermath

- **Queen Victoria's Proclamation** (1 November 1858): Announced transfer of power from EIC to British Crown
- **Government of India Act 1858**: EIC dissolved; British Crown took direct control
- **Lord Canning** became the first Viceroy
- Doctrine of Lapse was abandoned
- No further annexation of Indian states
- Indian states were promised non-interference in religious matters
- **Savarkar** called it "The First War of Independence" in his book (1909)
- British called it "The Sepoy Mutiny" or "The Great Mutiny"

## 8. British Crown Rule and Constitutional Development

### 8.1 Government of India Act 1858

- Also known as **Act for the Good Government of India**
- **Key Provisions:**
  - EIC dissolved; rule transferred to British Crown
  - **Secretary of State for India** (based in London) — assisted by 15-member Council
  - **Governor-General** became **Viceroy** (representative of the Crown)
  - Lord Canning became first Viceroy
  - Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished
  - Indian administration under direct control of British Parliament

### 8.2 Indian Councils Act 1861

- Introduced by **Lord Canning**
- Key Provisions:
  - Added non-official members to legislative councils (Indians)
  - **Portfolio system** introduced by Lord Canning (first step toward cabinet system)
  - Legislative powers restored to Bombay and Madras Presidencies
  - Viceroy could issue ordinances during emergency
  - First Indian member of Viceroy's Council: **Raja of Benaras** (later others)

### 8.3 Indian Councils Act 1892

- Introduced during Lord Dufferin's tenure
- Key Provisions:
  - Increased number of non-official members in legislative councils
  - Introduced **indirect election** (nomination by specific bodies)
  - Budget could be discussed (not voted upon)
  - Right to ask questions on internal administration
  - Universities, chambers of commerce, district boards, municipalities could elect representatives

### 8.4 Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)

- Named after **Lord Morley** (Secretary of State) and **Lord Minto** (Viceroy)
- Key Provisions:
  - **Separate electorates** for Muslims (first time) — "Communal Representation"
  - Also known as **Minto-Morley Reforms**
  - Central Legislative Council size increased to 69 members
  - Provincial Legislative Councils enlarged
  - Members could ask supplementary questions
  - **Satyendra Prasad Sinha**: First Indian to join Viceroy's Executive Council
  - Muslims could vote only for Muslim candidates
  - Lord Minto known as **"Father of Communal Electorate"**

### 8.5 Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms)

- Named after **Lord Montagu** (Secretary of State) and **Lord Chelmsford** (Viceroy)
- Based on **Montagu Declaration (1917)**: "Gradual development of self-governing institutions"
- Mont-Ford Reforms report presented in **July 1918**
- Key Provisions:
  - **Dyarchy** (dual government) introduced in provinces
    - **Reserved subjects** (controlled by Governor): Law and order, finance, revenue, irrigation
    - **Transferred subjects** (controlled by Indian ministers): Education, health, agriculture, local self-government
  - **Bicameralism** at the centre (Council of State + Legislative Assembly)
  - Communal electorates extended to Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians
  - Direct elections introduced for the first time
  - Central Public Service Commission established (1926)
  - Provincial budgets separated from central budget
  - High Commissioner for India in London appointed

### 8.6 Government of India Act 1935

- Longest act of British Parliament at the time
- Key Provisions:
  - **Provincial autonomy**: Provinces got responsible government
  - **Dyarchy at centre** (proposed but never implemented)
  - **All-India Federation** proposed (never materialized — princely states didn't join)
  - Federal Court established (1937)
  - Reserve Bank of India established (1935)
  - Provincial Legislative Councils enlarged; more powers to provinces
  - Separate electorates extended to Depressed Classes, women, labor
  - **Federal list, Provincial list, Concurrent list** introduced
  - Emergency provisions (President's Rule in provinces)
  - Franchise extended to ~10% of population
  - Burma separated from India
  - Sind province created (1936); Orissa province created (1936)

### 8.7 Indian Independence Act 1947

- Based on **Mountbatten Plan** (3 June 1947)
- Passed by British Parliament on **18 July 1947**
- Key Provisions:
  - Partition of India into two dominions: **India** and **Pakistan**
  - Both dominions to get independence on **15 August 1947**
  - Princely states free to join India or Pakistan or remain independent
  - British Crown's paramountcy over princely states ended
  - Governor-General for each dominion (appointed by Crown)
  - Constituent Assemblies for both dominions
  - No appeal to Privy Council from Indian courts
  - British Parliament could no longer legislate for India
  - **Lord Mountbatten** became first Governor-General of independent India
  - **Muhammad Ali Jinnah** became first Governor-General of Pakistan

## 11. Social and Religious Reform Movements

### 11.1 Brahmo Samaj (1828)

- **Founder**: Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772-1833)
- **Founded**: 1828 in Calcutta; initially called **"Atmiya Sabha"** (1814), then **"Brahmo Sabha"** (1828), renamed **"Brahmo Samaj"** (1829)
- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**:
  - "Father of Modern India", "Father of Bengali Renaissance"
  - Born in Radhanagar, Bengal (1772)
  - Fought against Sati (abolished 1829 by Lord William Bentinck)
  - Advocated monotheism, women's rights, modern education
  - Founded **Vedanta College** (1825) — combined Eastern and Western learning
  - Published **"Sambad Kaumudi"** (Bengali newspaper)
  - Went to England in 1830 as Mughal envoy; died in Bristol (1833)
  - Translated Upanishads into Bengali and English
- **Successors**:
  - **Debendranath Tagore** (father of Rabindranath Tagore): Joined in 1842; gave organizational structure; renamed as **Adi Brahmo Samaj** (1866)
  - **Keshab Chandra Sen**: Joined in 1858; more radical; formed **Bharatvarshiya Brahmo Samaj** (1866) after split; founded **Indian Reform Association** (1870); started **Nava Vidhan** (New Dispensation) in 1880
- **Tattvabodhini Sabha** (1839): Founded by Debendranath Tagore; merged with Brahmo Samaj in 1842
- **Key Principles**: Monotheism, opposition to idol worship, caste system, Sati, child marriage; promotion of women's education, widow remarriage

### 11.2 Arya Samaj (1875)

- **Founder**: Swami Dayanand Saraswati (1824-1883)
- **Founded**: 1875 in Bombay (first center); later headquarters at Lahore
- **Swami Dayanand Saraswati**:
  - Original name: Mul Shankar
  - Born in Tankara, Gujarat (1824)
  - Left home at age 21; became sannyasi
  - Studied under Swami Virjanand at Mathura
  - "Back to the Vedas" — believed Vedas are infallible
  - Book: **"Satyarth Prakash"** (Light of Truth)
  - Opposed idol worship, caste system, child marriage, untouchability
  - Advocated widow remarriage, women's education, Shuddhi (reconversion)
  - First to give call for **"Swaraj"** (self-rule) — "India for the Indians"
  - Died in 1883 (poisoned, believed)
- **Key Activities**:
  - **Shuddhi Movement**: Reconversion of Hindus who had converted to Islam or Christianity
  - **DAV (Dayanand Anglo-Vedic) Schools**: First established in Lahore (1886)
  - **Gurukul system**: Indigenous education system
  - Opposed foreign rule; promoted Swadeshi
- **Lala Lajpat Rai**: Prominent Arya Samaj leader; "Punjab Kesari"
- **Lala Hans Raj**: First headmaster of DAV School, Lahore

### 11.3 Ramakrishna Mission (1897)

- **Founder**: Swami Vivekananda (1863-1902)
- **Founded**: 1897 in Belur (near Calcutta)
- **Swami Vivekananda**:
  - Original name: Narendranath Datta
  - Born in Calcutta (1863)
  - Disciple of **Sri Ramakrishna Paramahamsa** (1836-1886)
  - Famous **Chicago Address** at the **World's Parliament of Religions** (1893):
    - Opening: "Sisters and Brothers of America"
    - Introduced Hinduism and Vedanta to the Western world
    - Called for religious tolerance and end to fanaticism
  - Founded **Belur Math** (1898) as headquarters
  - Established **Ramakrishna Mission** (1897) — socio-religious reform organization
  - "Service to man is service to God"
  - Books: "Raja Yoga", "Karma Yoga", "Jnana Yoga", "Bhakti Yoga"
  - Published magazines: **"Prabuddha Bharata"** (English), **"Udbodhana"** (Bengali)
  - Birthday (January 12) celebrated as **National Youth Day**
  - Died on 4 July 1902 at Belur Math
- **Key Principles**: Vedanta philosophy, social service, harmony of religions, spiritual regeneration of India

### 11.4 Theosophical Society (1875)

- **Founded**: 1875 in New York by **Madame H.P. Blavatsky** (Russian) and **Colonel Henry Steel Olcott** (American)
- **Headquarters**: Moved to Adyar (Madras) in 1882
- **Annie Besant** (1847-1933):
  - Irish-born; joined Theosophical Society in 1889
  - Came to India in 1893
  - Became President of Theosophical Society (1907-1933)
  - Founded **Central Hindu College** at Banaras (1898) — later became part of BHU
  - Started **Home Rule Movement** (1916)
  - Started newspapers: **"New India"** and **"Commonweal"**
  - First woman President of INC (1917, Calcutta session)
  - Adopted Jiddu Krishnamurti as the future "World Teacher"
- **Key Principles**: Universal brotherhood, comparative study of religions, investigation of unexplained laws of nature, spiritual development

### 11.5 Prarthana Samaj (1867)

- **Founded**: 1867 in Bombay by **Dr. Atmaram Pandurang**
- **Influenced by**: Keshab Chandra Sen (Brahmo Samaj leader who visited Bombay in 1867)
- **Key Leader**: **Ranade** (Mahadev Govind Ranade) — joined in 1870
- **Key Principles**: Reform Hindu society; against caste system, child marriage, idol worship; promoted women's education, widow remarriage, inter-caste dining
- **Widow Remarriage Association** (1861): Founded by Ranade and others
- **Depressed Classes Mission** (1906): Founded by V.R. Shinde

### 11.6 Satyashodhak Samaj (1873)

- **Founder**: Jyotirao (Jyotiba) Phule (1827-1890)
- **Founded**: 1873 in Pune, Maharashtra
- **Jyotiba Phule**:
  - Born in Satara, Maharashtra (1827); belonged to Mali (gardener) caste — OBC
  - Title: **"Mahatma"** — given to him by the people (first use of Mahatma for an Indian leader)
  - Opened first school for girls in Pune (1848) — first in India
  - Wife **Savitribai Phule**: First female teacher of India; first female headmistress; born 3 January 1831 in Satara
  - Book: **"Gulamgiri"** (Slavery, 1872) — dedicated to American abolitionists
  - Book: **"Shetkaryacha Asud"** (Cultivator's Whipcord)
  - Founded **Satyashodhak Samaj** (Truth-Seeking Society) — for upliftment of lower castes
  - Opposed Brahminical supremacy, caste system, idol worship
  - Advocated education for lower castes and women
  - Started orphanage for lower-caste children
  - Awarded title "Mahatma" in 1888
- **Key Principles**: Truth-seeking, social equality, opposition to caste and Brahminical dominance, education for all

### 11.7 Aligarh Movement

- **Founder**: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817-1898)
- **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan**:
  - Born in Delhi (1817)
  - Served in EIC's judicial service
  - After 1857 revolt, worked for Muslim education and modernization
  - Founded **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) College** at Aligarh in **1875** — modeled on Oxford/Cambridge
  - MAO College became **Aligarh Muslim University (AMU)** in **1920**
  - Founded **Scientific Society** (1864) at Ghazipur — translated Western works into Urdu
  - Founded **Mohammedan Literary Society** (1863) by Nawab Abdul Latif Khan
  - Started journal **"Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq"** (Social Reformer, 1870)
  - Advocated Western education for Muslims; loyalty to British; opposed INC initially
  - Believed Hindus and Muslims were two separate nations (Two-Nation Theory precursor)
  - Knighted in 1888
- **Nawab Abdul Latif Khan** (1828-1893):
  - "Father of Muslim Renaissance in Bengal"
  - Founded Mohammedan Literary Society (1863)
  - Promoted English education among Muslims in Bengal

## 13. Partition of Bengal and Swadeshi Movement (1905)

### 13.1 Partition of Bengal (1905)

- **Announced**: 19 July 1905 by **Lord Curzon**
- **Implemented**: **16 October 1905**
- Bengal was divided into:
  - **Eastern Bengal and Assam** (Muslim-majority): Dhaka as capital; included Assam, Chittagong, Mymensingh
  - **West Bengal** (Hindu-majority): Calcutta as capital; included Bihar and Odisha
- **Official reason**: Administrative convenience (Bengal too large to govern)
- **Real reason**: **"Divide and Rule"** — to divide Hindus and Muslims; weaken the nationalist movement centered in Bengal
- **Annulment**: **December 1911** by **Lord Hardinge II** during Delhi Durbar
  - Bengal reunited; Bihar and Odisha separated; Assam made separate province
  - Capital shifted from Calcutta to Delhi

### 13.2 Swadeshi Movement (1905-1908)

- **Launched**: In protest against Partition of Bengal
- **Leaders**: Surendranath Banerjee, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai
- **Key Features**:
  - **Boycott**: Of British goods, especially Manchester cloth, Liverpool salt, sugar
  - **Swadeshi**: Use of Indian-made goods; promotion of indigenous industries
  - **National Education**: Establishment of national schools and colleges
    - **National Council of Education** (1905): Founded national university at Calcutta
    - **Bengal Technical Institute**: Founded separately
  - **Swadeshi Bandhab Samiti**: For promoting swadeshi goods
  - **Rabindranath Tagore**: Composed "Amar Sonar Bangla" (which became Bangladesh's national anthem)
  - **"Vande Mataram"**: Became the rallying cry of the movement
- **Impact**:
  - First mass movement in Indian history
  - Women and students participated for the first time
  - Rise of extremism in INC
  - Surat Split (1907) — Moderates vs Extremists
  - Rise of revolutionary terrorism
  - Foundation of Muslim League (1906) — British encouraged separate Muslim political identity

## 15. Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh Massacre

### 15.1 Rowlatt Act (1919)

- Officially: **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act (1919)**
- Also called **"Black Act"**
- Based on recommendations of **Rowlatt Committee** (headed by Sir Sidney Rowlatt)
- Passed on **6 February 1919** (enacted March 19)
- **Key Provisions**:
  - Government could arrest any person without warrant
  - Detention without trial for up to 2 years
  - No right of appeal or judicial review
  - Restrictions on press and freedom of speech
- **Opposition**: Gandhi called it "Black Act"; organized **Rowlatt Satyagraha** (April 1919)
  - **Satyagraha Sabha** formed by Gandhi (February 1919)
  - Hartal on **6 April 1919** (all-India protest)
  - Protests turned violent in some cities (Delhi, Bombay, Ahmedabad, Punjab)

### 15.2 Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)

- **Date**: **13 April 1919** (Baisakhi day — harvest festival)
- **Place**: Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, Punjab
- **Context**: People gathered for a peaceful meeting (Baisakhi fair + protest against arrest of Dr. Satyapal and Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew on 9 April)
- **Perpetrator**: **General Reginald Dyer** (acting Brigadier-General)
- **Events**:
  - Dyer blocked the only exit with armed soldiers
  - Ordered firing without warning on unarmed crowd
  - Firing continued for **10 minutes**; **1,650 rounds** fired
  - Official death toll: **379** (Congress estimate: ~1,000)
  - Many killed by stampede; some jumped into well
- **Aftermath**:
  - **Hunter Commission** (1919-20): Investigated the massacre; Dyer censured but not punished
  - **Rabindranath Tagore** renounced his **knighthood** (1919) in protest
  - **Gandhi** returned **Kaiser-i-Hind medal**
  - **Udham Singh** killed **Michael O'Dwyer** (former Lieutenant Governor of Punjab) in London on **13 March 1940** to avenge the massacre; hanged 31 July 1940
  - Dyer was assassinated by Udham Singh (O'Dwyer was the political authority who supported Dyer)
  - **Marshall Law** declared in Punjab
  - **Gandhi** launched Non-Cooperation Movement (1920) partly due to this

  - **Tolstoy Farm** (1910) — second ashram
  - Fought against: pass laws, poll tax (£3), restrictions on Indian trade and movement
  - **Book**: "Hind Swaraj" or "Indian Home Rule" (1909)
- Returned to India: **9 January 1915** (observed as **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**)
- Gandhi's political mentor: **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**

### 17.2 Gandhi's Early Satyagrahas in India

| Satyagraha | Year | Place | Issue | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Champaran Satyagraha** | 1917 | Champaran, Bihar | Tinkathia system — peasants forced to grow indigo on 3/20 of land for European planters at fixed low prices | Gandhi's first satyagraha in India; tinkathia abolished; peasants got compensation |
| **Kheda Satyagraha** | 1918 | Kheda, Gujarat | Peasants demanded remission of land revenue due to famine and plague | Revenue collection suspended for poor peasants |
| **Ahmedabad Mill Strike** | 1918 | Ahmedabad, Gujarat | Mill owners wanted to cut plague bonus by 50%; workers demanded 35% wage increase | Gandhi went on fast; 35% increase agreed; first hunger strike by Gandhi |

### 17.3 Khilafat Movement (1919-1924)

- **Background**: After WWI, Allied powers planned to dismantle the Ottoman Empire and reduce the power of the **Caliph (Khalifa)** — spiritual head of Sunni Muslims
- **Leaders**: **Ali Brothers** — Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali; also Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani
- **Gandhi's role**: Supported the Khilafat cause to unite Hindus and Muslims
- **Khilafat Committee** formed (1919)
- **All-India Khilafat Conference** (November 1919): Gandhi presided
- **Khilafat Day**: 17 October 1919
- **Non-Cooperation merged with Khilafat**: 1920
- **End**: 1924 — when **Mustafa Kemal Pasha** (Atatürk) abolished the Caliphate in Turkey (March 1924)

### 17.4 Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922)

- **Launched**: September 1920 (ratified at Nagpur session, December 1920)
- **Causes**: Khilafat wrongs, Punjab wrongs (Jallianwala Bagh), Rowlatt Act, economic exploitation
- **Methods**:
  - Surrender of titles and honorary positions
  - Boycott of British schools, colleges, courts
  - Boycott of British goods (especially cloth)
  - Refusal to pay taxes
  - Non-participation in government functions
  - Resignation from government jobs
  - Establishment of national schools and colleges
  - Promotion of spinning wheel (charkha) and khadi
- **Progress**:
  - Massive participation across India
  - Students left British schools; lawyers gave up practice
  - Foreign cloth burnt; khadi promoted
  - **Jamia Millia Islamia** founded (1920) as national university
  - **Kashi Vidyapeeth** (1921), **Gujarat Vidyapeeth** (1920) established
- **Chauri Chaura Incident** (4 February 1922):
  - At Chauri Chaura, Gorakhpur, UP
  - Protesters set fire to police station; **22 policemen** killed
  - Gandhi was shocked; called off the movement on **12 February 1922**
  - Gandhi arrested on **10 March 1922**; sentenced to 6 years imprisonment
  - Released in 1924 (on health grounds — appendicitis)
- **Significance**: First mass movement under Gandhi; brought Gandhi to center stage of Indian politics; politicized masses

### 17.5 Swaraj Party (1923)

- **Founded**: 1923 by **Chittaranjan Das** ("Deshbandhu") and **Motilal Nehru**
- **Background**: After Non-Cooperation was called off, leaders differed on strategy
  - **No-changers**: Rajendra Prasad, C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel — wanted to continue boycott
  - **Pro-changers** (Swaraj Party): C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru — wanted to enter legislative councils
- **Aim**: Enter councils to obstruct British government from within
- **Elections**: Won significant seats in Central Legislative Assembly and provincial councils
- **Vithalbhai Patel**: President of Central Legislative Assembly (August 1925 - April 1930)
- **C.R. Das died** in 1925; party weakened

### 17.6 Simon Commission (1928)

- **Official name**: Indian Statutory Commission
- **Chairman**: **Sir John Simon**
- **Appointed**: November 1927; arrived in India: **3 February 1928**
- **Purpose**: Review the Government of India Act 1919 and recommend constitutional reforms
- **Composition**: 7 members, all British — **no Indian member**
- **Opposition**:
  - All parties opposed: INC, Muslim League, Hindu Mahasabha
  - Slogan: **"Simon Go Back"**
  - Black flag demonstrations everywhere
  - **Lala Lajpat Rai** led protest in Lahore; lathi-charged by police (led by Superintendent J.A. Scott)
  - Lala Lajpat Rai died on **17 November 1928** (injuries from lathi charge)
  - Saunders (J.P. Saunders, police officer) was assassinated by Bhagat Singh to avenge Lajpat Rai's death
- **Report submitted**: 27 May 1930 (two volumes)
- **Result**: Led to **Nehru Report** (1928) and Round Table Conferences

### 17.7 Nehru Report (1928)

- **Chairman**: **Motilal Nehru**
- **Purpose**: Indian response to Simon Commission; to draft a constitution for India
- **Key Recommendations**:
  - Dominion Status for India
  - Responsible government at centre and provinces
  - Bicameral legislature
  - Fundamental rights for citizens
  - No separate electorates (unitary bias)
  - Universal adult suffrage
  - Linguistic provinces
- **Rejection**: Muslim League rejected it; Jinnah gave **14 Points** (1929) as counter
- **Congress response**: Gave one-year ultimatum to British (December 1928) — if Dominion Status not granted by December 1929, would demand complete independence

### 17.8 Lahore Session (1929) — Purna Swaraj

- **Session**: December 1929 at Lahore
- **President**: **Jawaharlal Nehru** (first presidency)
- **Key Resolutions**:
  - **Purna Swaraj** (Complete Independence) declared as the goal
  - **26 January 1930** declared as **"Independence Day"** — to be observed as Purna Swaraj Day
  - On 31 December 1929, tricolor flag hoisted on banks of Ravi river
  - Congress authorized Gandhi to launch Civil Disobedience Movement

### 17.9 Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-1934)

#### Dandi March / Salt Satyagraha

- **Started**: **12 March 1930** from **Sabarmati Ashram** (Ahmedabad)
- **Ended**: **6 April 1930** at **Dandi** (coastal village in Gujarat)
- **Distance**: 240 miles (385 km); **78 satyagrahis** accompanied Gandhi
- **Duration**: 24 days
- **Action**: Gandhi broke the **Salt Law** by making salt from sea water
- **Significance**: Salt was a basic necessity taxed by the British; breaking salt law symbolized defiance of British authority
- **Impact**:
  - Civil Disobedience spread across India
  - **Sarojini Naidu** led raid on **Dharasana Salt Works** (May 1930)
  - **C. Rajagopalachari** led Salt Satyagraha in Tamil Nadu (Vedaranyam March)
  - **K. Kelappan** led Salt Satyagraha in Malabar (Payyannur)
  - **Durgabai Deshmukh** organized Salt Satyagraha in Madras
  - ~60,000 people arrested including Gandhi
  - First Round Table Conference boycotted by Congress

#### First Round Table Conference (November 1930 - January 1931)

- Held in **London**
- **Congress did not participate** (leaders in jail due to Civil Disobedience)
- Muslim League participated; demanded federation with safeguards
- Ambedkar demanded separate electorates for Depressed Classes
- Princes showed willingness to join federation

#### Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931)

- Signed between **Gandhi** and **Lord Irwin** (Viceroy)
- Terms:
  - Congress to suspend Civil Disobedience Movement
  - Gandhi to attend Second Round Table Conference
  - Political prisoners to be released (except those convicted of violence)
  - Confiscated property to be returned
  - Peaceful picketing of liquor and foreign cloth shops allowed

#### Second Round Table Conference (September - December 1931)

- Held in **London**
- **Gandhi** participated as sole representative of Congress
- **Issues**:
  - Gandhi vs. Muslim League on representation
  - Gandhi vs. Ambedkar on Depressed Classes representation
  - Minorities could not agree on communal representation
  - Princes reluctant to join federation
- **Result**: Conference failed; Gandhi returned empty-handed
- **Lord Willingdon** was Viceroy during Second and Third RTCs

#### Communal Award (August 1932)

- Announced by **British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald** (16 August 1932)
- Proposed **separate electorates** for Depressed Classes (Dalits)
- Gandhi opposed this; went on **fast unto death** in Yerwada Jail (20 September 1932)
- **Poona Pact** (24 September 1932):
  - Signed between **Gandhi** and **Ambedkar**
  - Separate electorates for Depressed Classes replaced by **reserved seats** in legislatures
  - 148 seats reserved for Depressed Classes in provincial legislatures
  - Ambedkar represented Depressed Classes

#### Third Round Table Conference (November - December 1932)

- Congress did not participate
- Minor discussions; inconclusive
- Led to **Government of India Act 1935**

### 17.10 Individual Satyagraha (1940)

- **Background**: Congress rejected the **August Offer** (1940) by Lord Linlithgow
- **Started**: **17 October 1940**
- **First Satyagrahi**: **Acharya Vinoba Bhave** (chosen by Gandhi)
- **Second Satyagrahi**: **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- **Purpose**: Not mass movement; individual defiance to assert right to freedom of speech
- **Method**: Selected individuals would publicly deliver anti-war speeches
- **Significance**: Vinoba Bhave became first Individual Satyagrahi

### 17.11 Quit India Movement (1942)

- **Resolution passed**: **8 August 1942** at **Gowalia Tank Maidan** (now August Kranti Maidan), Bombay
- **Resolution**: "British must quit India immediately"
- **Slogan**: **"Do or Die"** (Karenge ya Marenge) — given by Gandhi
- **Background**:
  - **Cripps Mission** (March 1942) failed — offered Dominion Status after war; rejected
  - WWII: Japan advancing toward India; fear of Japanese occupation
  - Congress demanded immediate British withdrawal
- **Events**:
  - **9 August 1942**: All top Congress leaders arrested (Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad, etc.)
  - Gandhi imprisoned at **Aga Khan Palace** (Pune) — from 10 August 1942 to 6 May 1944
  - **Kasturba Gandhi** died at Aga Khan Palace (22 February 1944)
  - **Mahadev Desai** (Gandhi's secretary) died at Aga Khan Palace (15 August 1942)
  - Leaderless movement turned violent in many places
  - **Underground activities**: **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan; **Sucheta Kripalani**, **Ram Manohar Lohia**, **Jayaprakash Narayan** went underground
  - **Congress Radio**: Secret radio broadcast by **Usha Mehta** from Bombay
  - Parallel governments established in some areas:
    - **Ballia** (UP): Chittu Pandey formed parallel government
    - **Tamluk** (Midnapore, Bengal): "Tamralipta Jatiya Sarkar"
    - **Satara** (Maharashtra): "Prati Sarkar" led by Nana Patil
  - British repression: Mass arrests, firing, lathi charges
  - **Gandhi** started 21-day fast (February 1943) in prison
- **Significance**:
  - Largest mass movement in Indian history
  - Demonstrated depth of nationalist sentiment
  - Made clear that British rule could not continue without Indian consent
  - After this, British seriously considered granting independence

## 18. Subhas Chandra Bose and INA

### 18.1 Subhas Chandra Bose (1897-1945)

- **Born**: 23 January 1897 in Cuttack, Odisha
- **Father**: Janakinath Bose (lawyer); mother: Prabhabati
- **Education**: Ravenshaw Collegiate School (Cuttack); Presidency College (Calcutta); Cambridge University (England)
- **ICS**: Ranked 4th in ICS exam (1920) but resigned in 1921 to join freedom struggle
- **Title**: **"Netaji"** (Leader) — given by Gandhi (some say by German/Indian community in Germany)
- **Political career**:
  - Joined INC; worked under **Chittaranjan Das** (his political mentor — "Deshbandhu")
  - Mayor of Calcutta (1930)
  - INC President: **1938 (Haripura)** and **1939 (Tripuri)**
  - 1939: Re-elected against Gandhi's candidate (Pattabhi Sitaramayya); Gandhi said "Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat"
  - Bose resigned from presidency due to differences with Gandhi
  - Founded **Forward Bloc** (22 June 1939) — within Congress for left-wing unity
  - Arrested and put under house arrest; escaped in January 1941 (disguised as Pathan — "Ziauddin")
  - Traveled to Germany via Afghanistan and Soviet Union; met Hitler
  - From Germany: Started **Free India Center**; formed **Indian Legion** (with German help)
  - Moved to Southeast Asia (1943) by submarine (German U-boat to Japanese submarine)

### 18.2 Indian National Army (INA)

- **First INA**: Formed by **Captain Mohan Singh** in **1942** in Singapore, with help of Japanese
  - Indian prisoners of war (from British Indian Army captured by Japan)
  - Disbanded in December 1942 due to differences between Mohan Singh and Japanese
- **Second INA**: Reorganized by **Subhas Chandra Bose** in **1943**
  - Bose arrived in Singapore on **2 July 1943**
  - Took over leadership of INA on **4 July 1943**
  - **Rash Behari Bose** (who had founded Indian Independence League in 1942) handed over to SCB
  - **Azad Hind Government** (Provisional Government of Free India):
    - Established: **21 October 1943** in Singapore
    - Head of State: Subhas Chandra Bose
    - Recognized by: Japan, Germany, Italy, Croatia, Burma, Philippines, etc. (9 countries)
    - Had its own currency, postage stamps, court system
  - **INA Army**:
    - Composed of Indian prisoners of war and civilians from Southeast Asia
    - **Women's Regiment**: **Rani of Jhansi Regiment** — led by **Captain Lakshmi Sahgal**
    - INA had about 40,000-50,000 soldiers
    - Slogan: **"Dilli Chalo"** (On to Delhi)
    - Famous quote: **"Give me blood, I shall give you freedom!"**
  - **Military Campaign**:
    - INA fought alongside Japanese against British in Burma and India
    - **Battle of Imphal** (March-July 1944): INA and Japanese reached Indian soil at Moirang (Manipur) — hoisted tricolor
    - **Battle of Kohima** (April-June 1944): Fought alongside Japanese
    - Japanese defeat and monsoon forced retreat
    - INA retreated to Burma; then to Singapore
  - **Bose's Death**: Reportedly died in a plane crash at **Taihoku** (Taipei, Taiwan) on **18 August 1945** — controversial; mystery persists

### 18.3 INA Trials (1945-1946)

- After Japanese surrender (August 1945), INA soldiers captured by British
- **Red Fort Trials** (November 1945 - May 1946):
  - First trial: **Colonel Prem Kumar Sahgal**, **Colonel Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon**, **Major Shah Nawaz Khan**
  - Charged with: Murder, abetment of murder, "waging war against the King"
  - Defended by: **Bhulabhai Desai** (lead defense counsel), Tej Bahadur Sapru, Jawaharlal Nehru, Kailash Nath Katju
  - All three found guilty but sentences remitted due to public pressure
  - **Public reaction**: Massive protests across India; naval mutiny (RIN Mutiny, February 1946)
  - **Impact**: INA trials created nationalist wave; even soldiers of British Indian Army sympathized

## 19. Independence and Partition

### 19.1 Cripps Mission (March 1942)

- **Head**: **Sir Stafford Cripps** (Lord Privy Seal, member of War Cabinet)
- **Background**: WWII — British needed Indian support; Japanese threat
- **Proposals**:
  - Dominion Status for India after the war
  - Constituent Assembly to frame constitution (elected by provincial assemblies)
  - Princely states could choose to join or stay out
  - Any province could opt out of the future Indian Union (right to secede)
  - Defense to remain under British control during the war
- **Rejection**:
  - **Congress rejected**: No immediate self-government; defense not in Indian hands; right to secede would weaken India
  - **Muslim League rejected**: Did not clearly provide for Pakistan
  - Gandhi: Called it a **"post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"**

### 19.2 Cabinet Mission (March-June 1946)

- **Members**: Lord Pethick-Lawrence (Secretary of State), Sir Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander
- **Purpose**: To negotiate transfer of power
- **Proposals**:
  - **Grouping of provinces** into three sections:
    - Section A: Hindu-majority provinces (Madras, Bombay, Central Provinces, UP, Bihar, Orissa)
    - Section B: Muslim-majority provinces (Punjab, NWFP, Sindh)
    - Section C: Bengal and Assam
  - Weak central government handling only defense, foreign affairs, and communications
  - Constituent Assembly to frame constitution
  - No separate Pakistan (rejected partition initially)
- **Initial acceptance**: Both Congress and Muslim League initially accepted
- **Rejection**: Muslim League withdrew acceptance (July 1946); Congress also had reservations about grouping
- **Direct Action Day** (16 August 1946): Muslim League called for; massive communal violence in Calcutta ("Great Calcutta Killing"); ~4,000+ killed; spread to Noakhali, Bihar, etc.

### 19.3 Attlee's Declaration (20 February 1947)

- **Clement Attlee** (Labour PM of Britain) announced:
  - British would leave India by **June 1948**
  - **Lord Mountbatten** appointed as last Viceroy (replaced Lord Wavell)
- **Significance**: Clear deadline for British withdrawal

### 19.4 Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)

- Also called **June 3rd Plan** or **Dickie Bird Plan**
- **Announced**: 3 June 1947 by Lord Mountbatten
- **Key Provisions**:
  - India to be partitioned into two dominions: **India** and **Pakistan**
  - Princely states free to join either dominion or remain independent
  - Provinces of Bengal and Punjab to be partitioned
  - Referendum in NWFP and Sylhet (Assam)
  - Boundary Commission to demarcate boundaries
  - Power transfer date advanced to **August 1947** (from June 1948)
- **Acceptance**: Both Congress and Muslim League accepted
- **Indian Independence Act**: Passed by British Parliament on **18 July 1947**

### 19.5 Indian Independence Act 1947

- **Passed**: 18 July 1947 by British Parliament
- **Effective**: 15 August 1947
- **Key Provisions**:
  - Two independent dominions: India and Pakistan
  - Pakistan to consist of: West Punjab, Sindh, NWFP, Baluchistan, East Bengal, Sylhet (from Assam)
  - British Crown's paramountcy over princely states ended
  - Princely states free to accede to India or Pakistan
  - Governor-General for each dominion (appointed by Crown, not Viceroy)
  - Constituent Assemblies for both dominions
  - No appeal to Privy Council
  - British Parliament could no longer legislate for India
  - Treaties with princely states lapsed

### 19.6 Partition and Independence

- **15 August 1947**: India became independent
  - **Jawaharlal Nehru**: First Prime Minister; delivered "Tryst with Destiny" speech at midnight
  - **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**: First President (1950)
  - **Lord Mountbatten**: First Governor-General of independent India (1947-1948)
  - **C. Rajagopalachari**: First Indian Governor-General (1948-1950); only Indian to hold the post
  - **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**: First Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister
  - Flag hoisted at **Lahori Gate** of Red Fort by Nehru
  - **Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan** addressed Parliament after Nehru's speech
- **Pakistan**: Independent on **14 August 1947**
  - **Muhammad Ali Jinnah**: First Governor-General
- **Radcliffe Line**: Boundary between India and Pakistan
  - Demarcated by **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** (chairman of Boundary Commission)
  - Two boundary commissions: one for Bengal, one for Punjab
  - Award announced on **17 August 1947** (two days after independence)
  - Controversial: Left large Muslim populations in India and Hindu/Sikh populations in Pakistan
- **Human cost**: ~10-15 million people displaced; ~1-2 million killed in communal violence
- **Integration of Princely States**:
  - **Sardar Patel** played decisive role; integrated 565 princely states
  - **Junagadh**: Through referendum (1948)
  - **Hyderabad**: Through **Operation Polo** (police action, September 1948)
  - **Kashmir**: Maharaja **Hari Singh** signed Instrument of Accession (October 1947)
  - **Travancore**, **Bhopal**, **Jodhpur**: Integrated through negotiations

### 22.3 Indo-Saracenic Architecture

- Also called **Indo-Gothic** or **Indo-Arabic**
- Fusion of Indian, Islamic, and Gothic architectural elements
- Features: Domes, arches, minarets, lattice work, Victorian Gothic details
- Examples: Victoria Memorial (Kolkata), Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus (Mumbai), Gateway of India, Lakshmi Vilas Palace (Vadodara), Madras High Court

### 23.5 Other Notable Facts

- **Asiatic Society of Bengal**: Founded by **Sir William Jones** on **15 January 1784** in Calcutta; for rediscovery of India's past; published "Asiatic Researches"
- **Royal Calcutta Golf Club**: Established **1829** — oldest golf club in India and first outside Great Britain
- **Shimla**: Summer capital of British India from **1864**; established after Anglo-Gorkha War (1814-16)
- **United Provinces**: Renamed **Uttar Pradesh** in January 1950
- **Madras State**: Renamed **Tamil Nadu** in 1969
- **Mysore**: Renamed **Karnataka** in 1973
- **Pondicherry**: French rule ended 1 November 1954; became UT in 1963
- **Goa**: Liberated from Portuguese on 17-19 December 1961
- **Sanchi Stupa**: Rediscovered in **1818** by General Henry Taylor (Bengal Cavalry)
- **Battle of Saragarhi**: 12 September 1897 — 21 Sikh soldiers fought 10,000 Pashtuns
- **Battle of Imphal and Kohima**: 1944 — WWII battles; Japanese attempt to invade India
- **Bengal Famine (1770)**: Killed one-third of Bengal's population (~10 million)
- **Indian Civil Service (ICS)**: Renamed Indian Administrative Service (IAS) after independence
- **Satyendra Nath Tagore**: First Indian selected for ICS (1863)
- **W.C. Bonnerjee**: First president of INC (1885)
- **Annie Besant**: First woman president of INC (1917)
- **Sarojini Naidu**: First Indian woman president of INC (1925)
- **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**: Youngest INC president (1923, age 35)
- **Gandhi presided over INC only once**: 1924 (Belgaum session)
- **C. Rajagopalachari**: First Indian Governor-General (1948-50)
- **Sardar Patel**: First Deputy Prime Minister; integrated 565 princely states
- **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan**: "Frontier Gandhi"; founded Red Shirts (Khudai Khidmatgar) in 1930
- **Vinoba Bhave**: First Individual Satyagrahi (1940); founded Bhoodan Movement (1951)
- **Jhanda Satyagraha (Flag Satyagraha)**: 1923 at Nagpur
- **Congress Socialist Party**: Founded 1934 at Bombay; Ram Manohar Lohia, Jayaprakash Narayan
- **C.R. Formula**: Proposed by C. Rajagopalachari in 1944 for Congress-League cooperation
- **Shimla Conference**: June-July 1945; Lord Wavell; failed — Congress and Muslim League could not agree
- **Economic Programme Committee**: Formed 1947 by AICC; chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru
- **Vithalbhai Patel**: President of Central Legislative Assembly (1925-1930); co-founder of Swaraj Party
- **Nathuram Godse**: Assassinated Gandhi (30 January 1948); expressed desire for ashes to be kept until India reunited
- **Haqeeqat**: 1964 film based on India-China War of 1962
- **Dalai Lama**: Came to India on 31 March 1959; settled at Dharamsala
- **Mayurbhanj**: Last princely state to merge with India (1 January 1949, merged with Odisha)
- **Manipur**: Maharaja Bodhachandra Singh signed Instrument of Accession; first part of India to hold election based on Universal Adult Franchise (June 1948)
- **Tripura**: Ruled by Manikya Dynasty
- **Ahom Dynasty**: Ruled Assam; first to raise banner of revolt against British (1827) under Gomdhar Konwar
