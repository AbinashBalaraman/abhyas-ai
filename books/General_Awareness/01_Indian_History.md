## 1. Comprehensive Content — Ancient India

### 1.1 Prehistoric & Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilization (c. 3300–1300 BCE)

**Prehistoric period** is divided into Stone Age, Bronze Age, Iron Age. Stone Age is further split into Palaeolithic (Old Stone Age), Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age), Neolithic (New Stone Age).

**Harappan (Indus Valley) Civilization:**
- Period: Mature phase c. 2600–1900 BCE; Early phase c. 3300–2600 BCE.
- Cities: **Mohenjo-Daro** (Sindh, Pakistan), **Harappa** (Punjab, Pakistan), **Lothal** (Gujarat, India), **Dholavira** (Gujarat, India), **Kalibangan** (Rajasthan), **Rakhigarhi** (Haryana — largest in India), **Banawali**, **Chanhudaro**, **Surkotada**.
- Key features: **Grid-pattern streets**, **multi-storeyed houses**, **underground drainage**, **Great Bath** (Mohenjo-Daro), **granaries** (Harappa), **dockyard** (Lothal — world's earliest), **fire altars** (Kalibangan), **ploughed field** (Kalibangan), **famous "Priest King" statue** (Mohenjo-Daro), **dancing girl** bronze statue.
- Script: **Undeciphered pictographic** script (boustrophedon — written right-to-left then left-to-right).
- Economy: Agriculture (wheat, barley, rice, cotton), trade with Mesopotamia, Mesopotamia seals found.
- **Discovered by:** Dayaram Sahni (Harappa, 1921); R.D. Banerji (Mohenjo-Daro, 1922); S.R. Rao (Lothal, 1957).
- **Decline:** Possibly due to river course change (Saraswati drying), floods, climate change, Aryan invasions.

### 1.2 Vedic Period (c. 1500–600 BCE)

**Early/Samhita Vedic (Rig Vedic, 1500–1000 BCE):**
- **Texts:** Rigveda (oldest; 1028 hymns, 10 mandalas), Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda.
- **Language:** Vedic Sanskrit.
- **Society:** Tribal (Jana); king (Rajan) not absolute; assemblies **Sabha** (council of elders) and **Samiti** (general assembly).
- **Economy:** Pastoral, agriculture secondary; cattle = wealth (gavyuti).
- **Religion:** Indra (most important god, 250 hymns), Agni, Varuna; no temples.
- **Family:** Joint family (Kula), patriarchal.

**Later Vedic (1000–600 BCE):**
- Texts: Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads.
- **Varnashrama** system rigidified: 4 varnas (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra).
- **Ashramas** (life stages): Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sannyasa.
- **Bali** and **Bhasmas** forms of taxation; king more powerful; kingdoms grew (Mahajanapadas).
- **Epics composed:** Mahabharata, Ramayana.

### 1.3 Religious Movements (6th century BCE)

**Jainism** — Founded by **Rishabhadeva** (first Tirthankara, traditionally); 24th Tirthankara = **Mahavira** (540–468 BCE; born in Vaishali).
- Teachings: Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha, Brahmacharya (5 vows); Anekantavada (multiple viewpoints); Syadvada.
- Symbol: Wheel with 24 spokes; **Shvetambara** (white-clad) and **Digambara** (sky-clad) sects.
- **First Council:** Pataliputra (300 BCE), under Sthulabhadra.
- **Language of canon:** Ardhamagadhi (Agamas).
- Famous centers: Vaishali, Rajgir, Pava.

**Buddhism** — Founded by **Siddhartha Gautama** (563–483 BCE) at Bodh Gaya (peepal tree).
- Four Noble Truths: Dukkha (suffering), Samudaya (cause), Nirodha (cessation), Magga (path).
- Eight-fold Path (Ashtangika Marg): Right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration.
- Three jewels: Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha.
- **Two main schools:** Hinayana (Theravada — elders; original) and Mahayana (great vehicle).
- **First Council:** Rajagriha (c. 483 BCE), under Ajatashatru; presided by Mahakassapa; compiled Sutta Pitaka and Vinaya Pitaka.
- **Second Council:** Vaishali (383 BCE); split into Sthaviras and Mahasanghikas.
- **Third Council:** Pataliputra (250 BCE), under Ashoka; presided by Moggaliputta Tissa; compiled Abhidhamma Pitaka; sent missions.
- **Fourth Council:** Kashmir (1st/2nd century CE), under Kanishka; presided by Vasumitra; Sanskrit commentaries.
- **Language of canon:** Pali.
- **Emperor Ashoka** (Mauryan) sent Buddhist missions to Sri Lanka (Mahinda), Egypt, Syria.

### 1.4 Maurya Empire (c. 322–185 BCE)

- **Chandragupta Maurya** (c. 324–301 BCE) — founder; defeated Nandas; married daughter of Seleucus; received Megasthenes (Greek ambassador).
- **Bindusara** (c. 298–272 BCE) — "Amitraghata" (slayer of foes).
- **Ashoka** (c. 268–232 BCE) — greatest Mauryan; fought Kalinga War (261 BCE); converted to Buddhism after war; sent missions; Rock and Pillar edicts.
- **Last Mauryan ruler:** Brihadratha (185 BCE), killed by Pushyamitra Sunga.
- **Administration:** Highly centralised; espionage network; Kautilya's **Arthashastra**; royal road; city administration.
- **Megasthenes** wrote **Indica**; divided India into 5 parts.

### 1.5 Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE) — "Golden Age"

- **Chandragupta I** (c. 320–335 CE) — founder; married Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi.
- **Samudragupta** (c. 335–375 CE) — "Indian Napoleon"; expanded empire; Allahabad Pillar inscription by Harishena.
- **Chandragupta II** (Vikramaditya) (c. 375–415 CE) — peak of Gupta power; defeated Shakas; Fa-Hien visited.
- **Vikramaditya** — semi-legendary court of Navaratnas (including Kalidasa, Varahamihira, Dhanvantari, Amarasimha).
- **Decline:** Hunas (Hephthalites) invasion; later Guptas weak.
- **Achievements:** Decimal system, zero, chess (chaturanga), iron pillar of Delhi; Kalidasa's Abhijnana Shakuntalam, Meghdoot, Raghuvamsha, Kumarasambhava; Aryabhata (mathematician-astronomer — calculated π, Earth's rotation; Aryabhatiya).
- **Samudragupta** issued gold coins (dinar); **Chandragupta II** issued silver coins.

### 1.6 Post-Gupta Period

- **Harsha** (606–647 CE) of Pushyabhuti dynasty (Thanesar/Kannauj); "Harshacharita" by Banabhatta; patronised Buddhism; Chinese traveller **Xuanzang** (Hiuen Tsang) visited.
- **Pallavas** (South India) — Kanchipuram; Mahendravarman, Narasimhavarman.
- **Chalukyas** of Badami (543–753 CE) — Pulakeshin II defeated Harsha; Aihole inscription.
- **Rashtrakutas** (753–982 CE) — Amoghavarsha; cave temples at Ellora.
- **Cholas** — Rajaraja I, Rajendra I; Gangaikonda Cholapuram; Brihadeeswarar Temple (Thanjavur).
- **Pandyas, Cheras, Hoysalas, Kakatiyas** — regional powers.

## 2. Medieval India

### 2.1 Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE)

Five dynasties:
1. **Mamluk/Slave (1206–1290):** Qutb-ud-din Aibak (founder, 1206), Iltutmish, Razia Sultana (first woman ruler of Delhi Sultanate), Balban.
2. **Khilji (1290–1320):** Jalal-ud-din Khilji, Alauddin Khilji (price control, market reforms).
3. **Tughlaq (1320–1414):** Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq, Muhammad bin Tughlaq (5 experiments — token currency, shifting capital, taxation in Doab), Firoz Shah Tughlaq.
4. **Sayyid (1414–1451):** Khizr Khan.
5. **Lodi (1451–1526):** Ibrahim Lodi defeated by Babur in First Battle of Panipat (1526).

**Architecture:** Qutub Minar (Qutb-ud-din Aibak/Iltutmish), Alai Darwaza, Tomb of Hauz Khas.

**Administration:** Iqta system (land revenue), Walis (provincial governors).

### 2.2 Mughal Empire (1526–1707 CE)

- **Babur** (1526–1530) — founder; First Battle of Panipat (1526), Battle of Khanwa (1527), Battle of Chanderi (1528); wrote **Baburnama** (in Chagatai Turkish).
- **Humayun** (1530–1540, 1555–1556) — defeated by Sher Shah Suri at Chausa (1539) and Kannauj (1540); built Din Panah.
- **Sher Shah Suri** (1540–1545) — built Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam); introduced **Rupia** (silver coin); revenue reforms (Qabuliyat & Patta).
- **Akbar** (1556–1605) — greatest Mughal; fought Second Battle of Panipat (1556) with Bairam Khan; **Din-i-Ilahi** (1582), **Sulh-i-Kul** (universal tolerance), "The Great" — married Jodha Bai (Mariam-uz-Zamani), etc.; **Navratnas** (Birbal, Tansen, Todarmal — revenue); **Mansabdari system** (numerical ranks, 33 grades).
- **Jahangir** (1605–1627) — "Chain of Justice"; wife **Nur Jahan**; encounters with **Sir Thomas Roe** (English ambassador).
- **Shah Jahan** (1627–1658) — "Architect of Mughal architecture"; built Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Jama Masjid; **Peacock Throne**.
- **Aurangzeb** (1658–1707) — last great Mughal; religious policies; expansion to Deccan; conflict with Marathas, Sikhs; death in Ahmednagar.

**Mughal administration:** Mansabdari system, Diwan (finance), Wazir, Sadr-us-Sudur (religious & charity), Kotwal (city police).

**Architecture:** Taj Mahal (Shah Jahan), Fatehpur Sikri (Akbar), Buland Darwaza, Panch Mahal, Humayun's Tomb (first garden tomb).

### 2.3 Bhakti & Sufi Movements

**Bhakti Movement:** Saints like Ramanuja, Madhvacharya, Nimbarka, Basavanna (Lingayat), Kabir, Nanak, Chaitanya, Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai; promoted monotheism, rejected caste, used vernacular languages.

**Sufi Movement:** Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti (Ajmer), Nizamuddin Auliya, Salim Chishti, Baba Farid. Silsilas: Chishti, Suhrawardi, Naqshbandi, Qadiri.

### 2.4 Maratha Empire

- **Shivaji** (1627/1630–1680) — founder; coronation Raigad (1674); guerrilla warfare (Ganimi Kava); forts system.
- **Sambhaji** (1680–1689) — captured and executed by Aurangzeb.
- **Rajaram** (1689–1700), **Tarabai** (regent).
- **Peshwas** (1713 onwards): Balaji Vishwanath, Bajirao I (won Battle of Bhopal, Palkhed), Balaji Bajirao, Madhavrao Peshwa, Narayanrao, Sawai Madhavrao.
- **Third Battle of Panipat** (1761) — Marathas defeated by Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani) — major setback.
- Confederacy of 5 Maratha houses: Peshwa, Holkar, Scindia, Bhonsle, Gaekwad.

## 3. Modern India — British Rule & Freedom Struggle

### 3.1 Advent of Europeans

- **Portuguese:** Vasco da Gama (1498 — Calicut); Albuquerque captured Goa (1510).
- **Dutch:** First to follow Portuguese; Pulicat (Andhra) as base; founded in 1602.
- **English:** East India Company founded 1600; first factory at Surat (1612); Madras (Fort St. George, 1639); Bombay (1668 from Portuguese); Calcutta (Fort William, 1696).
- **French:** East India Company 1664; Pondicherry (Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam, Chandernagore).

### 3.2 British Expansion (1757–1857)

**Battles:** Plassey (1757, Clive vs Siraj-ud-Daulah), Buxar (1764, Hector Munro vs combined forces of Shuja-ud-Daulah, Shah Alam II, Mir Qasim).
**Treaty of Allahabad (1765):** Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar, Odisha to East India Company.
**Subsidiary Alliance** (Lord Wellesley): Indian state pays for British troops; surrenders foreign relations.
**Doctrine of Lapse** (Lord Dalhousie): Annexation of states without natural heir (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, Awadh).

**Governor-Generals (after 1833 became Governor-General of India):**
- Warren Hastings (1773–1785) — first; founded Calcutta Madrassa (1781); Asiatic Society (1784).
- Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793) — Permanent Settlement (Bengal, 1793); Sadar Diwani Adalat.
- Lord Wellesley (1798–1805) — Subsidiary Alliance.
- Lord Minto I (1807–1813).
- Lord Hastings (1813–1823) — Anglo-Nepal War, Third Anglo-Maratha War.
- Lord Amherst (1823–1828) — First Anglo-Burmese War.
- Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835) — Abolition of Sati (1829), Thugs; founded Sanskrit College Calcutta.
- Lord Auckland (1836–1842) — First Afghan War.
- Lord Hardinge (1844–1848) — First Anglo-Sikh War.
- Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) — Railways, telegraph, postal; Second Anglo-Sikh War, Second Anglo-Burmese War; Doctrine of Lapse.
- **Viceroys:**
- Lord Canning (1856–1862) — Revolt of 1857.
- Lord Lawrence (1864–1869) — Punchayati Raj (set up panchayats).
- Lord Mayo (1869–1872) — First Census (1872); State Railway.
- Lord Northbrook (1872–1876) — Suez Canal.
- Lord Lytton (1876–1880) — Vernacular Press Act (1878); Arms Act; Second Afghan War.
- Lord Ripon (1880–1884) — Local Self-Government (1882); first Factory Act; Ilbert Bill (1883 — Indian judges for Indian criminals).
- Lord Dufferin (1884–1888) — Third Anglo-Burmese War.
- Lord Lansdowne (1888–1894) — Indian Councils Act 1892.
- Lord Curzon (1899–1905) — Partition of Bengal (1905); Archaeological Survey; Lady Curzon scheme.
- Lord Minto II (1905–1910) — Morley-Minto Reforms (1909).
- Lord Hardinge II (1910–1916) — Delhi Durbar 1911; annulment of Bengal partition.
- Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921) — Rowlatt Act 1919, Jallianwala Bagh (1919), Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919).
- Lord Reading (1921–1926).
- Lord Irwin (1926–1931) — Simon Commission (1928), Lahore Session (1929), Civil Disobedience (1930), Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931).
- Lord Willingdon (1931–1936) — Round Table Conferences.
- Lord Linlithgow (1936–1944) — WWII; Quit India Movement (1942).
- Lord Wavell (1943–1947) — Cabinet Mission (1946), INA trials.
- Lord Mountbatten (1947) — last Viceroy; Independence (15 Aug 1947).

### 3.3 Revolt of 1857 ("First War of Independence")

- **Causes:** Greased cartridges (cow + pig fat), Doctrine of Lapse, social reform interference, economic exploitation.
- **Centres:** Meerut (May 10, 1857), Delhi (Bahadur Shah Zafar — last Mughal emperor), Lucknow (Begum Hazrat Mahal), Kanpur (Nana Saheb), Jhansi (Rani Lakshmibai), Bareilly (Khan Bahadur), Bihar (Kunwar Singh).
- **Leaders:** Mangal Pandey, Rani Lakshmibai, Tantia Tope, Nana Saheb, Kunwar Singh, Begum Hazrat Mahal, Bahadur Shah Zafar.
- **Outcome:** British victory (Gwalior June 1858); end of East India Company rule; British Crown took over (Aug 2, 1858 — Queen's Proclamation); end of Mughal Empire (Bahadur Shah Zafar exiled to Burma).

### 3.4 Socio-Religious Reform Movements (19th–early 20th century)

- **Brahmo Samaj** (1828, Raja Ram Mohan Roy) — Calcutta; "Father of Modern India".
- **Arya Samaj** (1875, Dayanand Saraswati) — Mumbai; "Back to the Vedas".
- **Ramakrishna Mission** (1897, Vivekananda) — service to humanity.
- **Prarthana Samaj** (1867, Atmaram Pandurang) — Maharashtra.
- **Singh Sabha** (1873, Lahore) — Sikh reform.
- **Aligarh Movement** (Sir Syed Ahmad Khan) — Muslim modernisation; founded Aligarh Muslim University (1875/1920).
- **Sati abolition (1829), Widow remarriage (1856), Child Marriage Restraint Act (Sarda Act 1929).**

### 3.5 Indian National Congress (founded 1885, Bombay, A.O. Hume)

**Moderate Phase (1885–1905):** Methods — prayers, petitions, constitutional means. Leaders: Dadabhai Naoroji (Grand Old Man; "Poverty of India"), Gopal Krishna Gokhale (political guru of Gandhi), Surendranath Banerjee, W.C. Bonnerjee (first president).

**Extremist Phase (1905–1919):** Methods — Boycott, Swadeshi, National Education. Leaders: Bal Gangadhar Tilak ("Father of Indian Unrest"), Lala Lajpat Rai ("Punjab Kesari", "Sher-e-Punjab"), Bipin Chandra Pal; Aurobindo Ghosh. Partition of Bengal (1905) by Curzon triggered Swadeshi.

**Gandhian Era (1919–1947):**
- **Champaran (1917):** Indigo planters' issue; first Satyagraha in India.
- **Kheda (1918):** Peasants' tax issue.
- **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918):** Workers' issue.
- **Rowlatt Act (1919)** → Jallianwala Bagh massacre (April 13, 1919) — General Dyer.
- **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)** — withdrawn after Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922).
- **Simon Commission (1928)** — "Simon Go Back" — Lala Lajpat Rai died after lathi charge.
- **Lahore Session (1929, Jawaharlal Nehru president):** Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) demand; Jan 26, 1930 — first Independence Day celebrated.
- **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930):** Dandi March (Salt March) — 12 March 1930 from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi; breaking salt law (April 6, 1930).
- **Round Table Conferences** (1930–32) in London.
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)** — ended CDM; Gandhi attended RTC II as Congress sole representative.
- **Government of India Act 1935** — provincial autonomy, federal scheme, all-India federation.
- **Quit India Movement (1942, Aug 8, Mumbai/Gowalia Tank Maidan, "Do or Die")** — arrested immediately; leaders underground; parallel governments emerged.
- **Cabinet Mission (1946)** — proposed united India with weak centre (rejected by Muslim League → Direct Action Day, Aug 16, 1946 — Great Calcutta Killings).
- **Mountbatten Plan (June 3, 1947)** — Partition.
- **Indian Independence Act 1947** — Aug 15, 1947 (India), Aug 14, 1947 (Pakistan).
- **First PM:** Jawaharlal Nehru; **First President:** Rajendra Prasad; **First Home Minister:** Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.

### 3.6 Other Freedom Fighters

- **Subhas Chandra Bose** — Azad Hind Fauj / Indian National Army (INA); "Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom"; Forward Bloc; Singapore; died 1945 (Taipei air crash, alleged). Ras Behari Bose founded INA; Mohan Singh took over; Subhas Bose reorganised.
- **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev** — hanged March 23, 1931 (Lahore Conspiracy Case).
- **Chandrashekhar Azad** — "His last words shall be on India's freedom"; shot himself (Feb 27, 1931, Alfred Park, Allahabad).
- **Sardar Patel** — "Iron Man of India", "Architect of modern India"; integrated 562 princely states; Statue of Unity.
