## 1. World Geography — The Universe, Solar System & Earth

### 1.1 The Universe & Galaxy

**Universe** is the vast expanse of space containing all matter, energy, stars, planets, and galaxies. The currently accepted theory of the universe's origin is the **Big Bang Theory** (proposed by Georges Lemaître, 1927; supported by Edwin Hubble's observations). The universe is approximately **13.8 billion years old**.

**Key terms:**
- **Galaxy**: A massive collection of stars, gas, dust, and dark matter bound by gravity. There are an estimated 100-200 billion galaxies in the observable universe.
- **Milky Way** (Akash Ganga): Our galaxy; spiral-shaped; contains 100-400 billion stars; ~100,000 light-years in diameter; our Sun is located in the Orion Arm, about 26,000 light-years from the galactic center.
- **Local Group**: Cluster of ~54 galaxies including Milky Way and Andromeda.
- **Virgo Supercluster**: Larger grouping containing Local Group.
- **Laniakea Supercluster**: An even larger supercluster (2014 discovery).

**Light Year**: Distance traveled by light in one year ≈ 9.46 trillion km (9.46 × 10¹² km). Used to measure astronomical distances.

**Astronomical Unit (AU)**: Average distance between Earth and Sun ≈ 149.6 million km ≈ 1.496 × 10⁸ km.

**Parsec**: 1 parsec ≈ 3.26 light-years ≈ 206,265 AU.

### 1.2 The Solar System

The **Solar System** consists of the Sun, 8 planets, dwarf planets (Pluto, Eris, Ceres, Makemake, Haumea), 200+ moons, asteroids, comets, and meteoroids. Formed ~4.6 billion years ago from a giant molecular cloud.

**The Sun:**
- Star at center of solar system; yellow dwarf (G-type main-sequence)
- Surface temperature: ~5,778 K; Core temperature: ~15 million K
- Composed of ~73% hydrogen, ~25% helium, 2% other elements
- Source of all energy on Earth
- Source of light takes ~8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth

**Heliocentric vs Geocentric Model:**
- **Geocentric Model**: Earth at center (Ptolemy, 140 AD) — accepted for ~1,400 years
- **Heliocentric Model**: Sun at center (proposed by **Nicolaus Copernicus**, 1543; confirmed by Galileo Galilei, Johannes Kepler)

**Eight Planets (in order from Sun):**
1. **Mercury** — closest to Sun; smallest planet; no atmosphere; extreme temperatures (-173°C to 427°C); no moon
2. **Venus** — hottest planet (462°C due to greenhouse effect); "Earth's twin" (similar size); rotates backwards (retrograde rotation); thick CO₂ atmosphere
3. **Earth** — only planet with life; 71% water; 1 moon
4. **Mars** — "Red Planet" (iron oxide); Olympus Mons (largest volcano in solar system); 2 moons (Phobos, Deimos)
5. **Jupiter** — largest planet; "Gas Giant"; Great Red Spot (storm); 95+ known moons; Ganymede (largest moon in solar system)
6. **Saturn** — "Ringed Planet"; least dense (would float in water); 146+ moons; Titan (largest moon, has atmosphere)
7. **Uranus** — ice giant; rotates on its side (98° tilt); pale blue-green (methane); 27 moons
8. **Neptune** — windiest planet (2,100 km/h winds); deep blue; 14 moons; Triton (orbits backwards)

**Earth Fact File:**
- Diameter: ~12,742 km
- Mass: 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg
- Distance from Sun: ~150 million km (1 AU)
- Orbital period: 365.25 days
- Rotation period: 23h 56m 4s
- Axial tilt: 23.5° (causes seasons)
- Surface area: 510 million km² (71% water, 29% land)

### 1.3 Earth's Structure & Atmosphere

**Internal Structure (concentric layers):**
1. **Crust** (5-70 km thick): Thinnest; continental (avg 35 km) and oceanic (avg 5-10 km)
2. **Mantle** (~2,900 km): Solid rock that flows slowly; asthenosphere (plastic, partially molten)
3. **Outer Core** (~2,200 km): Liquid iron-nickel; generates Earth's magnetic field
4. **Inner Core** (~1,220 km radius): Solid iron-nickel; extreme pressure; temperature ~5,400°C

**Atmosphere Layers (from surface to space):**
1. **Troposphere** (0-12 km): Weather occurs; temperature decreases with height (lapse rate ~6.5°C/km)
2. **Stratosphere** (12-50 km): Contains ozone layer; temperature increases with height; commercial jets fly here
3. **Mesosphere** (50-85 km): Coldest layer (-90°C); meteors burn up
4. **Thermosphere** (85-600 km): Contains ionosphere (reflects radio waves); aurora
5. **Exosphere** (600+ km): Gradual transition to space

**Atmospheric Composition:** 78% Nitrogen, 21% Oxygen, 0.9% Argon, 0.04% CO₂ (rising), trace gases

**Five Oceans (by area):**

### 1.5 Latitude, Longitude & Time

- **Latitude** (parallels): 0° Equator to 90° N/S Poles. Important lines: **Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N)**, Tropic of Capricorn (23.5°S), Arctic Circle (66.5°N), Antarctic Circle (66.5°S).
- **Longitude** (meridians): 0° Prime Meridian (Greenwich, UK) to 180° E/W. 1° longitude = 4 minutes time difference.
- **Indian Standard Time (IST)**: Based on **82.5°E longitude** (passing through Mirzapur, UP). IST = **UTC+5:30**.
- **International Date Line**: ~180° longitude; crossing it changes date by 1 day.
- **Local Time**: Based on Sun's position at noon; varies by location.
- **Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)**: Reference time; now largely replaced by **UTC (Coordinated Universal Time)**.

## 2. Indian Geography — Physical Features

### 2.1 Physiographic Divisions of India

India is a peninsula extending into the Indian Ocean, with the Himalayas in the north, and surrounded by the Arabian Sea (west), Bay of Bengal (east), and Indian Ocean (south). It spans **8°4'N to 37°6'N** latitude and **68°7'E to 97°25'E** longitude.

**Six physiographic divisions:**

1. **The Northern Mountains (Himalayas)**
   - Young fold mountains; formed by collision of Indian and Eurasian plates ~50 million years ago
   - Length: ~2,400 km; Width: 400-150 km
   - Three parallel ranges:
     - **Greater Himalayas (Himadri)**: Highest; average 6,000 m; perpetual snow; Nanda Devi, Kanchenjunga (India's highest)
     - **Lesser Himalayas (Himachal)**: 3,700-4,500 m; hill stations (Shimla, Mussoorie, Manali, Darjeeling)
     - **Outer Himalayas (Shivaliks)**: 900-1,100 m; newest; fragile
   - Highest peak in India: **K2/Godwin Austen (8,611 m)** in PoK (held by Pakistan; claimed by India)
   - Highest peak in Indian-administered territory: **Kanchenjunga (8,586 m)** in Sikkim
   - Highest peak entirely in India: **Nanda Devi (7,816 m)** in Uttarakhand

2. **The Northern Plains**
   - Formed by alluvial deposits of Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra river systems
   - Highly fertile; densely populated
   - Subdivisions: Bhabar (pebble zone), Terai (marshy), Bangar (old alluvium), Khadar (new alluvium)
   - Length: ~3,200 km; Width: 150-300 km

3. **The Peninsular Plateau**
   - Oldest landmass; igneous and metamorphic rocks
   - Bounded by Delhi ridge, Rajmahal Hills, Gir Range, Cardamom Hills
   - Two broad divisions: **Central Highlands** and **Deccan Plateau**
   - Highest peak: **Anamudi (2,695 m)** in Anamalai Hills, Kerala
   - Important ranges: Aravalli, Vindhya, Satpura, Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats

4. **The Indian Desert (Thar)**
   - Lies west of Aravalli Hills; extends into Pakistan
   - Annual rainfall <25 cm
   - Largest desert in India: Thar (Great Indian Desert)

5. **The Coastal Plains**
   - **Western Coastal Plain**: Narrow, shallow, no deltas; rivers like Narmada, Tapti form estuaries
   - **Eastern Coastal Plain**: Wide, fertile, deltas; rivers like Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri form deltas
   - Total coastline: **7,516.6 km** (mainland + islands); mainland ~5,423 km

6. **The Islands**
   - **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**: Bay of Bengal; 572 islands; Port Blair (capital); Indira Point (southernmost point of India)
   - **Lakshadweep**: Arabian Sea; 36 islands; Kavaratti (capital); coral islands

### 2.3 River Systems of India

**Himalayan Rivers (Perennial — fed by glaciers + rainfall):**
- **Indus System**: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej (Punjab)
- **Ganga System**: Ganga, Yamuna, Ghaghara, Son, Gandak, Kosi

**Peninsular Rivers (Rain-fed, seasonal):**

- **West-flowing** (Arabian Sea): Narmada, Tapti, Sabarmati, Mahi, Zuari

**Major River Facts:**
- **Ganga**: Longest river in India (~2,525 km); originates at **Gangotri Glacier** (Devprayag where Bhagirathi meets Alaknanda)

- **Yamuna**: Largest tributary of Ganga; joins Ganga at Prayagraj (Sangam)
- **Mahanadi**: Major river of Odisha/Chhattisgarh
- **Damodar**: "Sorrow of Bengal" (flood control via Damodar Valley Corporation)

**Major River Valley Projects:**

## 3. Indian Geography — Climate, Soil & Natural Vegetation

### 3.1 Climate of India

India has a **tropical monsoon** climate. Key factors: latitude, altitude, distance from sea, ocean currents, relief features.

**Four Seasons (IMD classification):**
1. **Winter** (December-February): Cold; clear skies; fog in north
2. **Summer** (March-May): Hot; dry; dust storms; **Loo** (hot winds in north India)
3. **Southwest Monsoon** (June-September): ~75% of total rainfall; **Onset** in Kerala ~1 June; **Withdrawal** from Rajasthan ~15 September
4. **Northeast Monsoon** (October-November): Rain in Tamil Nadu coast; "winter monsoon"

**Monsoon Mechanism:**
- **El-Niño**: Warming of eastern Pacific; suppresses Indian monsoon (drought)
- **La-Niña**: Cooling of eastern Pacific; strengthens Indian monsoon
- **Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)**: Affects monsoon variability
- **Mascarene High** (cross-equatorial flow from south)
- **Somali Jet** (low-level jet stream)

**Rainfall Distribution:**
- Areas of **High Rainfall (>200 cm)**: Western Ghats (windward side), Northeast India (Cherrapunji/Mawsynram — world's wettest places)
- Areas of **Low Rainfall (<50 cm)**: Western Rajasthan, Ladakh, Kutch
- **Rain Shadow Areas**: Eastern slopes of Western Ghats (Pune receives less than Mahabaleshwar)

### 3.2 Soils of India

India has diverse soils. ICAR classifies into 8 types:

1. **Alluvial Soil** (~43% of India): Indo-Gangetic plain, river deltas; most fertile; **Khadar** (new), **Bangar** (old)
2. **Black Soil (Regur/Black Cotton Soil)**: Deccan Plateau; rich in iron, magnesium, alumina; self-ploughing (swelling/shrinking with moisture); best for cotton
3. **Red Soil**: Eastern Deccan, Eastern Ghats, Odisha, Chhattisgarh; rich in iron
4. **Laterite Soil**: Western Ghats, parts of Eastern Ghats, NE India; high iron & aluminum; poor in nitrogen; brick-making; cashew, tea
5. **Mountain/Forest Soil**: Himalayan region, Western & Eastern Ghats; acidic; forest cover
6. **Arid/Desert Soil**: Western Rajasthan, parts of Haryana, Punjab; sandy, porous
7. **Saline & Alkaline Soil**: Arid regions, coastal areas; high salt content (usar/reh/kallar)
8. **Peaty/Marshy Soil**: Kerala, Sundarbans, parts of Odisha; rich in organic matter; heavy; black

### 3.3 Natural Vegetation

India has ~21% forest cover (FSI Report 2021). Major types:

1. **Tropical Evergreen Rain Forests** — Western Ghats, NE India, Andaman; >250 cm rainfall; dense, tall trees; hardwoods (rosewood, mahogany, ebony)
2. **Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon) Forests** — Most common; shed leaves in dry season; two subtypes:
   - **Moist Deciduous**: NE India, foothills of Himalayas, Western Ghats; teak, sal, shisham, sandalwood
   - **Dry Deciduous**: Peninsular plateau; teak, sal, neem, peepal
3. **Tropical Thorn & Scrub Forests** — Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP; arid; babool (acacia), kikar, khejri
4. **Montane Forests** — Himalayas; varies with altitude (subtropical → temperate → alpine)
5. **Tidal/Mangrove Forests** — Sundarbans (largest), Bhitarkanika (Odisha), Godavari delta; Sundari tree; rich biodiversity

**Important Trees:**
- **Teak**: State tree of Kerala, MP
- **Sandalwood**: Karnataka (largest producer)
- **Sal**: National tree of India (declared 2019); state tree of Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, MP, Odisha, Uttarakhand
- **Banyan (Bargad)**: National tree (effective 2019, then replaced by Sal — verify)
- **Lotus**: National flower

## 5. Indian Geography — National Parks, Wildlife & Environment

### 5.1 Project Tiger & Tiger Reserves

Project Tiger launched **1 April 1973** by Indira Gandhi. India now has **56+ tiger reserves** (as of 2024).

**Major Tiger Reserves:**
- **Jim Corbett** (Uttarakhand) — first national park of India (1936 as Hailey NP; renamed 1957)
- **Ranthambore** (Rajasthan) — famous for tigers
- **Kanha** (MP) — inspiration for Rudyard Kipling's "The Jungle Book"
- **Bandhavgarh** (MP) — highest density of tigers
- **Panna** (MP)
- **Pench** (MP/Maharashtra)
- **Sundarbans** (WB) — largest mangrove; Royal Bengal Tiger
- **Kaziranga** (Assam) — Indian one-horned rhinoceros
- **Manas** (Assam) — UNESCO World Heritage Site; tiger reserve + rhino
- **Periyar** (Kerala) — elephants
- **Nagarhole/Rajiv Gandhi** (Karnataka)
- **Bandipur** (Karnataka)
- **Sariska** (Rajasthan) — tiger reintroduction
- **Tadoba** (Maharashtra)

**National Parks vs Wildlife Sanctuaries:**
- **National Park**: Higher protection; no human activity allowed; can be created by Central/State government
- **Wildlife Sanctuary**: Human activities like grazing, wood-cutting allowed with permission
- **Conservation Reserve**: Community/conservation areas
- **Community Reserve**: Local community protection
