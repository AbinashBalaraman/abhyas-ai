## 1. Comprehensive Theory

### 1.1 Rest and Motion

An object is said to be in **motion** if its position changes continuously with respect to a fixed point (reference point). It is said to be at **rest** if its position does not change with respect to a fixed point [General StudiesScience Encyclopedia, p. 12].

**Rest and motion are relative terms.** An object in one situation can be at rest, but in another situation the same object can be in motion. For example, if two cars are going side by side with the same velocity, then with respect to each other, each car is at rest, but with respect to a person on the road, both cars are in motion [General Studies Reference, p. 12].

**Types of motion** of a body [General Studies Reference, p. 12]:

1. **Rectilinear and translatory motion:** Motion in which a particle moves along a straight line. (e.g., a car moving on a straight road)
2. **Circular and rotatory motion:** Motion in which a particle moves along a circular path (e.g., a string whirled in a circular loop). Rotatory motion is when a body rotates about an axis (e.g., fan blades).
3. **Oscillatory and vibratory motion:** Motion in which a body moves to and fro or back and forth repeatedly about a fixed point (e.g., simple pendulum).

**Dimensions of motion** [General Studies Reference, p. 13]:

- **One-dimensional motion:** Position changes in one direction (e.g., car on a straight road, freely falling object).
- **Two-dimensional motion:** Position changes in two directions (e.g., motion of a planet around the sun, projectile motion).
- **Three-dimensional motion:** Position changes in three directions (e.g., a bird flying in the sky).

### 1.2 Distance and Displacement

**Distance** is the total path length covered by a body. It is a **scalar quantity** (only magnitude, no direction). SI unit: metre (m) [General Studies Reference, p. 13].

**Displacement** is the shortest straight-line distance between the initial and final positions of a body, along with direction. It is a **vector quantity**. SI unit: metre (m) [General Studies Reference, p. 13-14].

**Key differences:**

| Property | Distance | Displacement |
|----------|----------|--------------|
| Type | Scalar | Vector |
| Path | Total path | Shortest straight line |
| Direction | No | Yes |
| Value | Always positive (or zero) | Can be positive, negative, or zero |
| Between two points | May be more than displacement | Less than or equal to distance |

The magnitude of displacement for a course of motion may be zero when the object returns to the starting point, but the distance covered is non-zero [General Studies Reference, p. 14].

### 1.3 Speed and Velocity

**Speed** is the time rate of change of distance. It is a **scalar quantity**. SI unit: m/s [General Studies Reference, p. 14].

$$\text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total distance travelled}}{\text{Total time taken}}$$

**Velocity** is the time rate of change of displacement. It is a **vector quantity**. SI unit: m/s. Dimensional formula: [M⁰ L T⁻¹] [General Studies Reference, p. 14].

$$\text{Average velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}$$

Velocity of an object can be changed by changing the object's speed or direction of motion, or both [General Studies Reference, p. 14].

**|velocity| ≤ speed** — the magnitude of velocity of an object is always equal to or less than its speed [General Studies Reference, p. 15].

**Types of velocity** [General Studies Reference, p. 15-16]:

- **Uniform velocity:** When a body covers equal distances in equal intervals of time, however small these intervals may be.
- **Non-uniform (variable) velocity:** When a body covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time.
- **Average velocity:** Total displacement / total time.
- **Instantaneous velocity:** The velocity of a particle at any instant of time.

**Relative velocity:** The velocity with which one object moves with respect to another. If two objects are moving in the same direction: $v_{rel} = v_1 - v_2$ (or $v_2 - v_1$). If in opposite directions: $v_{rel} = v_1 + v_2$ [General Studies Reference, p. 16].

### 1.4 Acceleration

The time rate of change of velocity of a body is called **acceleration**. It is a vector quantity [General Studies Reference, p. 17]:

$$\text{Acceleration} = \frac{\text{Change in velocity}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{v - u}{t}$$

SI unit: m/s². Dimensional formula: [M⁰ L T⁻²].

**Sign convention:**
- If velocity increases with time, acceleration is **positive** (the body is said to be accelerated).
- If velocity decreases with time ($u > v$), acceleration becomes **negative** (the body is said to be retarded or decelerated).

**Types of acceleration** [General Studies Reference, p. 17-18]:

- **Uniform acceleration:** Velocity changes at a uniform rate.
- **Non-uniform acceleration:** Velocity changes at a non-uniform rate.
- **Average acceleration:** $\bar{a} = \frac{v - u}{t}$
- **Instantaneous acceleration:** $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$

### 1.5 Equations of Motion (for uniform acceleration)

For a body moving with uniform acceleration, the three equations of motion are [General Studies Reference, p. 19-20]:

1. $v = u + at$
2. $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
3. $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$

where:
- $u$ = initial velocity
- $v$ = final velocity
- $a$ = uniform acceleration
- $t$ = time taken
- $s$ = displacement

### 1.6 Graphical Representation of Motion

**Distance-time graph:**

- For an object at rest: a straight line parallel to the time axis.
- For an object moving with uniform speed: a straight line with slope = speed.
- For an object with non-uniform speed: a curved line; slope at any point gives the speed at that instant.

**Velocity-time graph:**

- For uniform velocity: a straight line parallel to the time axis.
- For uniform acceleration: a straight line with slope = acceleration.
- For non-uniform acceleration: a curved line.
- The area under the v-t graph gives the displacement.

### 1.7 Freely Falling Bodies

In the absence of air resistance, all bodies falling towards the Earth from a height fall with the same acceleration, called the **acceleration due to gravity** ($g$). Near the surface of Earth, $g = 9.8$ m/s² (approximately 10 m/s² for numerical problems).

For a freely falling body, using $u = 0$, $a = g$:
1. $v = gt$
2. $h = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
3. $v^2 = 2gh$

### 1.8 Force

A **force** is a push or a pull that changes or tends to change the state of rest or of uniform motion of a body, or its direction, or its shape [General Studies Reference, p. 27].

Force is a vector quantity. SI unit: newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². CGS unit: dyne. 1 N = $10^5$ dyne.

**Fundamental forces in nature** [General Studies Reference, p. 27-28]:

There are four basic forces in nature:
1. **Gravitational force** — between masses.
2. **Electromagnetic force** — between charges.
3. **Strong nuclear force** — within the nucleus.
4. **Weak nuclear force** — in radioactive decay.

### 1.9 Inertia and Newton's Laws of Motion

**Inertia** is the natural tendency of an object to remain in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line, unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force. Inertia is a property of matter. The mass of a body is a measure of its inertia [General Studies Reference, p. 28].

**Newton's Laws of Motion** [General Studies Reference, p. 29]:

**First Law (Law of Inertia):** Every body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line, unless compelled by an external unbalanced force to change its state.

**Second Law:** The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the net external force applied on it, and the change takes place in the direction of the force.

$$F = ma = \frac{dp}{dt}$$

where $F$ is the net force, $m$ is the mass, and $a$ is the acceleration. SI unit of force: newton (N).

**Third Law:** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Action and reaction act on different bodies.

$$\vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA}$$

### 1.10 Momentum

**Linear momentum** is the product of mass and velocity of a body. It is a vector quantity. SI unit: kg·m/s [General Studies Reference, p. 29-30]:

$$\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$$

**Impulse:** The product of force and time for which the force acts. SI unit: N·s. Impulse = change in momentum = $F \times t = mv - mu$ [General Studies Reference, p. 31].

**Law of conservation of momentum:** The total momentum of an isolated system of bodies remains constant. In other words, when two bodies collide, the total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after the collision (provided no external force acts).

$$m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$$

### 1.11 Friction

**Friction** is a force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact. It acts parallel to the surface and opposite to the direction of motion [General Studies Reference, p. 32].

**Causes of friction:** Surface roughness, interlocking of microscopic irregularities.

**Types of friction:**

- **Static friction:** Self-adjusting force that prevents motion up to a maximum value.
- **Limiting friction:** Maximum value of static friction; the force required to just start motion.
- **Kinetic friction (dynamic friction):** Force opposing motion once the body is moving.
- **Rolling friction:** Friction during rolling, much less than sliding friction.

**Laws of friction** [General Studies Reference, p. 32-33]:

1. Friction opposes relative motion.
2. Friction is proportional to the normal reaction.
3. Friction depends on the nature of surfaces in contact.
4. Friction is independent of the area of contact (for rigid bodies).
5. Kinetic friction is less than limiting friction.

**Friction is necessary:** Walking, braking, writing, holding objects. Friction is harmful: causes wear and tear, dissipates energy as heat.

**Methods of reducing friction:** Polishing, lubrication, ball bearings, streamlining (for fluids), use of wheels.

### 1.12 Work, Energy, Power

**Work** is said to be done when a force causes a displacement. Work is a scalar quantity. SI unit: joule (J). 1 J = 1 N × 1 m [General Studies Reference, p. 40-41]:

$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = Fs\cos\theta$$

where θ is the angle between force and displacement.

**Cases:**
- If θ = 0° (force along displacement): $W = Fs$ (maximum positive work).
- If θ = 90° (force perpendicular to displacement): $W = 0$ (no work).
- If θ = 180° (force opposite to displacement): $W = -Fs$ (maximum negative work).

**Energy** is the capacity of a body to do work. SI unit: joule (J). The total energy of a body is the sum of its kinetic and potential energies [General Studies Reference, p. 42].

**Kinetic energy (KE):** Energy possessed by a body by virtue of its motion [General Studies Reference, p. 42]:

$$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

**Potential energy (PE):** Energy possessed by a body by virtue of its position or configuration [General Studies Reference, p. 42]:

- Gravitational PE = $mgh$ (mass × gravity × height)
- Elastic PE (for a spring) = $\frac{1}{2}kx^2$

**Work-Energy Theorem:** The work done by the net force on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy [General Studies Reference, p. 43]:

$$W_{net} = \Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2$$

**Law of Conservation of Energy:** Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant [General Studies Reference, p. 43].

**Power** is the rate of doing work. It is a scalar quantity. SI unit: watt (W). 1 W = 1 J/s [General Studies Reference, p. 41]:

$$P = \frac{W}{t} = Fv\cos\theta$$

1 horsepower (HP) = 746 W. 1 kW = 1000 W.

### 1.13 Gravitation

**Universal Law of Gravitation (Newton):** Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them [General Studies Reference, p. 58]:

$$F = G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}$$

where:
- $G$ = Universal gravitational constant = $6.674 \times 10^{-11}$ N·m²/kg²
- $m_1, m_2$ = masses of the two bodies
- $r$ = distance between the centres of the two bodies

**Acceleration Due to Gravity (g):** The acceleration produced in a body due to the force of gravity. Near the Earth's surface, $g \approx 9.8$ m/s² [General Studies Reference, p. 59]:

$$g = \frac{GM}{R^2}$$

where M is the mass of Earth and R is its radius.

**Variations of g** [General Studies Reference, p. 59-60]:

- $g$ decreases with altitude (height above Earth's surface).
- $g$ decreases as we go inside the Earth.
- $g$ is maximum at the poles and minimum at the equator (because Earth is not a perfect sphere; radius is greater at the equator).
- $g$ also depends on the mass of the planet.

**Mass and Weight** [General Studies Reference, p. 60]:

- **Mass:** The amount of matter in a body. Constant everywhere. SI unit: kg.
- **Weight:** The force with which the Earth attracts a body. $W = mg$. SI unit: newton (N). Varies from place to place.

A body weighs more at the poles than at the equator.

### 1.14 Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

**Kepler's First Law (Law of Orbits):** All planets move around the Sun in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one of the two foci [General Studies Reference, p. 62].

**Kepler's Second Law (Law of Areas):** The line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal intervals of time. (The planet moves faster when closer to the Sun, slower when farther.)

**Kepler's Third Law (Law of Periods):** The square of the time period of revolution of a planet around the Sun is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its elliptical orbit:

$$T^2 \propto a^3$$

### 1.15 Escape Velocity and Orbital Velocity

**Escape velocity** is the minimum velocity with which a body must be projected from the Earth's surface so that it escapes the gravitational field [General Studies Reference, p. 63]:

$$v_e = \sqrt{2gR} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} \approx 11.2 \text{ km/s}$$

**Orbital velocity** is the velocity required to put a satellite into a given circular orbit around the Earth [General Studies Reference, p. 62-63]:

$$v_o = \sqrt{gR} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}} \approx 8 \text{ km/s}$$

## 3. Everyday Science & Applications

**Q1: Why does a passenger fall forward when a moving bus suddenly stops?**
A: Due to inertia. The body was in motion with the bus; when the bus stops, the body tends to continue in its state of motion.

**Q2: Why do passengers fall backward when a stationary bus suddenly starts?**
A: Due to inertia. The body was at rest; when the bus starts, the body tends to remain at rest while the bus moves forward, so it appears to fall backward relative to the bus.

**Q3: Why does a cricket player lower his hands while catching a ball?**
A: By lowering his hands, the player increases the time taken to stop the ball, reducing the rate of change of momentum and hence the force exerted.

**Q4: Why is it difficult to walk on a slippery floor?**
A: Friction between feet and floor is reduced on a slippery surface, so the foot cannot push the floor backward effectively to move forward.

**Q5: Why do athletes use spiked shoes?**
A: Spikes increase friction between shoes and ground, providing better grip for running and preventing slipping.

**Q6: Why are car tyres treaded?**
A: Tread patterns increase friction and provide better grip on wet roads, preventing skidding.

**Q7: Why does a heavier object fall with the same acceleration as a lighter object (in the absence of air)?**
A: Gravitational force is proportional to mass ($F = mg$), but by Newton's second law, $a = F/m = mg/m = g$. So the acceleration is independent of mass.

**Q8: Why is the value of g different at the poles and equator?**
A: Earth is not a perfect sphere — its radius at the equator is greater than at the poles. Since $g = GM/R^2$, g is larger at the poles (smaller R).

**Q9: Why does an astronaut feel weightless in space?**
A: The astronaut and the spaceship are both in free fall around the Earth with the same acceleration, so there is no normal force acting on the astronaut — apparent weight is zero.

**Q10: Why does a satellite not fall to the Earth?**
A: The satellite is moving tangentially with a high orbital velocity. The gravitational force provides the necessary centripetal force to keep it in circular motion.

**Q11: Why is a satellite's orbit usually circular?**
A: For a circular orbit, the gravitational force provides exactly the centripetal force needed, and the speed is constant.

**Q12: Why do we use ball bearings?**
A: Ball bearings convert sliding friction into rolling friction, which is much smaller, reducing wear and energy loss.

**Q13: Why does a car use oil/lubricant between moving parts?**
A: Lubricants form a thin layer between surfaces, reducing direct contact and hence reducing friction.

**Q14: Why does the kinetic energy of a freely falling body increase as it falls?**
A: As the body falls, its speed increases; KE = ½mv² increases. The loss in potential energy (mgh) is converted to kinetic energy (conservation of energy).

**Q15: Why does a projectile follow a parabolic path?**
A: It has constant horizontal velocity and uniform vertical acceleration due to gravity; the resultant path is a parabola.

**Q16: Why is the work done by the Earth's gravitational force positive when a body falls?**
A: Force (downward) and displacement (downward) are in the same direction, so θ = 0°; cos 0° = 1; work is positive.

**Q17: Why is the work done by centripetal force zero?**
A: Centripetal force is always perpendicular to the velocity (and hence to the instantaneous displacement), so θ = 90°; cos 90° = 0; work is zero.

**Q18: Why is a body projected up with a velocity equal to escape velocity lost to space forever?**
A: The body has enough kinetic energy to overcome the entire gravitational potential energy; it reaches infinity with zero or positive speed.

**Q19: Why is the value of G constant everywhere in the universe?**
A: G is a universal constant — it does not depend on the medium, the size or mass of the bodies, or the location in the universe. It is the same throughout.

**Q20: Why is the mass of a body constant, but its weight varies from place to place?**
A: Mass is the amount of matter, which is constant. Weight is the gravitational force on a body ($W = mg$), which depends on local g, which varies with location.

## 5. Solved Numerical Problems

### Problem 1 (Easy — Average speed)
**A car covers the first half of the distance between two places at a speed of 40 km/h and the second half at 60 km/h. Find the average speed.**

**Solution:**
For equal halves: $\bar{v} = \frac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2} = \frac{2 \times 40 \times 60}{40+60} = \frac{4800}{100} = 48$ km/h.

### Problem 2 (Easy — Equations of motion)
**A body starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s². What is the velocity after 5 seconds, and the distance covered?**

**Solution:**
$u = 0, a = 2$ m/s², $t = 5$ s.
$v = u + at = 0 + 2 \times 5 = 10$ m/s.
$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + 0.5 \times 2 \times 25 = 25$ m.

### Problem 3 (Medium — Newton's 2nd law)
**A force of 20 N acts on a body of mass 4 kg. What is the acceleration?**

**Solution:**
$a = F/m = 20/4 = 5$ m/s².

### Problem 4 (Medium — Momentum)
**A body of mass 2 kg moves with a velocity of 5 m/s. Find its momentum. What is the force required to stop it in 0.5 s?**

**Solution:**
$p = mv = 2 \times 5 = 10$ kg·m/s.
$F = \Delta p / t = 10 / 0.5 = 20$ N (opposite to motion).

### Problem 5 (Medium — Free fall)
**A stone is dropped from the top of a tower 78.4 m tall. How long does it take to reach the ground?**

**Solution:**
$u = 0, g = 9.8$ m/s², $s = 78.4$ m.
$s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \Rightarrow t = \sqrt{2s/g} = \sqrt{2 \times 78.4/9.8} = \sqrt{16} = 4$ s.

### Problem 6 (Medium — KE)
**A body of mass 10 kg moves with a velocity of 5 m/s. Find its kinetic energy.**

**Solution:**
$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.5 \times 10 \times 25 = 125$ J.

### Problem 7 (Medium — Power)
**A man lifts a 50 kg mass to a height of 5 m in 10 seconds. Find his power.**

**Solution:**
Work done = $mgh = 50 \times 9.8 \times 5 = 2450$ J.
Power = W/t = 2450/10 = 245 W.

### Problem 8 (Hard — Gravitation)
**The mass of Earth is $6 \times 10^{24}$ kg and that of the Moon is $7.4 \times 10^{22}$ kg. The distance between them is $3.84 \times 10^8$ m. Calculate the gravitational force between them. ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}$ N·m²/kg²)**

**Solution:**
$F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times \frac{6 \times 10^{24} \times 7.4 \times 10^{22}}{(3.84 \times 10^8)^2}$
$= 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times \frac{4.44 \times 10^{47}}{1.475 \times 10^{17}}$
$= 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 3.01 \times 10^{30}$
$\approx 2.0 \times 10^{20}$ N
