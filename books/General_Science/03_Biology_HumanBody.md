## 1. Comprehensive Theory

### 1.1 Life Processes (Ch 5)

Life processes are the maintenance functions of living organisms — they must continue even when the organism is not "doing" anything. The four main life processes are: Nutrition, Respiration, Transportation, Excretion.

**Nutrition:** Modes of obtaining food
- **Autotrophic:** Green plants, some bacteria — synthesise own food from CO₂ + H₂O + sunlight + chlorophyll (photosynthesis).
- **Heterotrophic:** Animals, fungi — depend on others.
- **Photosynthesis** (site: chloroplasts; chlorophyll essential):
  $6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light, chlorophyll}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$
  The raw materials: CO₂ (from air via stomata), H₂O (from soil via roots), sunlight, chlorophyll. Stomata are tiny pores on leaf surface, surrounded by guard cells. Desert plants take up CO₂ at night (CAM pathway).

**Human digestive system:**
- **Mouth:** Teeth crush food; saliva (salivary amylase) breaks down starch → sugar.
- **Oesophagus:** Food-pipe; peristalsis pushes food.
- **Stomach:** Gastric glands release HCl (acidic medium for pepsin), pepsin (digests proteins), mucus (protects lining). HCl also kills germs.
- **Small intestine:** Site of complete digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, fats. Receives bile juice from liver (makes acidic food alkaline; emulsifies fats) and pancreatic juice (trypsin for proteins, lipase for fats). Intestinal enzymes finally convert proteins → amino acids; carbohydrates → glucose; fats → fatty acids + glycerol. **Villi** (finger-like projections) increase surface area for absorption.
- **Large intestine:** Absorbs water; rest is egested via anus.

**Respiration:** Breaking down glucose to release energy
- **Aerobic:** Glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Energy (in mitochondria; much more energy)
- **Anaerobic (in yeast):** Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ + Energy (fermentation)
- **Anaerobic (in muscle cells):** Glucose → Lactic acid + Energy (causes muscle cramps)
- **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate):** Energy currency of the cell; ~30.5 kJ/mol released when terminal phosphate bond broken.

**Human respiratory system:**
- Air enters through nostrils → filtered by hairs and mucus → pharynx → larynx → trachea (with cartilage rings) → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.
- **Alveoli:** Balloon-like structures; rich blood capillary network; total surface area ~80 m² for efficient gas exchange.
- **Haemoglobin** in RBCs transports O₂; CO₂ is more soluble in water (transported in dissolved form).
- Smoking damages cilia (hair-like structures that filter air), causing lung diseases including cancer.

**Transportation in humans:**
- **Blood:** Plasma (55%) + RBCs (red, contain haemoglobin), WBCs (white, fight infection), platelets (clotting).
- **Heart:** Four-chambered muscular pump; double circulation (pulmonary + systemic).
- **Arteries:** Carry blood away from heart (oxygenated, except pulmonary); thick walls.
- **Veins:** Carry blood toward heart (deoxygenated, except pulmonary); thin walls, valves prevent backflow.
- **Capillaries:** Connect arteries and veins; thin walls for exchange.
- **Blood groups:** A, B, AB (universal recipient), O (universal donor); discovered by Karl Landsteiner.
- **Lymph:** Colourless fluid; drains back to blood; helps in immunity.
- **Plant transport:** Xylem (water + minerals, roots → leaves); phloem (food, leaves → other parts — translocation).

**Excretion in humans:**
- **Kidneys:** Bean-shaped; filter blood to form urine; contain nephrons (functional unit).
- **Nephron:** Glomerulus (filtration) → Bowman's capsule → tubule (reabsorption + secretion) → collecting duct.
- **Urine:** Urea, water, salts, uric acid. Excreted via ureter → bladder → urethra.
- **Dialysis:** Artificial kidney used when kidneys fail.
- **Plants excrete:** Stored in leaves (fall off), vacuoles, resins, gums.

### 1.2 Control and Coordination (Ch 6)

In animals, nervous and muscular tissues provide control. In plants, chemical signals (hormones) coordinate.

**Neuron structure:** Dendrite → cell body → axon
- Information acquired at dendrite → electrical impulse → axon → at synapse, chemical neurotransmitters released → next neuron's dendrite picks up.

**Reflex action:** Sudden, involuntary response. Path: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord (reflex arc) → motor neuron → muscle. Examples: pulling hand from hot object, knee-jerk.

**Human brain:**
- **Forebrain (Cerebrum):** Main thinking; sensory areas (vision, hearing, smell); motor areas (voluntary movement); association areas; hunger centre.
- **Midbrain:** Controls some involuntary actions.
- **Hindbrain:** **Cerebellum** (posture, balance, precision); **Pons** (respiration control); **Medulla** (involuntary actions — blood pressure, salivation, vomiting).
- **Spinal cord:** Reflex actions; conducts impulses to/from brain.
- **Protection:** Bony skull (cranium) + meninges + cerebrospinal fluid (shock absorption).

**Coordination in plants:**
- **Tropisms:** Growth movement in response to stimulus.
  - **Phototropism:** Light (stem bends toward light, mediated by auxin).
  - **Geotropism:** Gravity (root grows down, stem up).
  - **Hydrotropism:** Water (roots grow toward water).
  - **Chemotropism:** Chemicals.
- **Plant hormones (Phytohormones):**
  - **Auxin:** Promotes cell elongation, phototropism.
  - **Gibberellins:** Stem elongation, seed germination.
  - **Cytokinins:** Cell division.
  - **Abscisic acid (ABA):** Inhibits growth, promotes dormancy, wilting.
  - **Ethylene:** Fruit ripening, leaf fall.

**Animal hormones (Endocrine system):**
- **Endocrine glands:** Pituitary (master gland), thyroid (thyroxine — metabolism), pancreas (insulin — lowers blood sugar, glucagon — raises), adrenal (adrenaline — fight or flight), testes (testosterone), ovaries (oestrogen, progesterone).
- **Iodine** is essential for thyroxine; deficiency causes goitre.
- **Insulin** deficiency causes diabetes mellitus.

### 1.3 Reproduction (Ch 7)

**Asexual reproduction:** Single parent, no gametes; offspring genetically similar to parent.
- **Fission** (Amoeba, Leishmania, Plasmodium).
- **Budding** (Yeast — Hydra).
- **Fragmentation** (Spirogyra — break into pieces, each grows).
- **Spore formation** (Fungi, ferns).
- **Vegetative propagation** in plants: stem (potato, ginger), leaf (Bryophyllum), root (sweet potato, dahlia), runner (grass), tuber (potato), bulb (onion), cutting (rose).

**Sexual reproduction:** Two parents; gametes fuse to form zygote; introduces variation.
- **Human reproductive system:**
  - **Male:** Testes (produce sperm + testosterone) → epididymis → vas deferens → urethra.
  - **Female:** Ovaries (produce ova + oestrogen/progesterone) → fallopian tube (fertilisation site) → uterus (implantation + development) → vagina.
- **Fertilisation:** Sperm + ovum → zygote (in fallopian tube). Zygote → morula → blastocyst → embryo → foetus.
- **Implantation:** Embryo attaches to uterine wall (~7 days).
- **Placenta:** Tissue connecting mother to foetus; supplies O₂, nutrients; removes waste.
- **Gestation:** ~9 months (40 weeks).
- **Menstrual cycle:** ~28 days; ovulation ~day 14.
- **Sexually transmitted diseases:** AIDS (HIV), syphilis, gonorrhoea.

**Reproductive health:**
- Population control: contraception (barrier — condoms, IUDs, oral pills, surgical — vasectomy/tubectomy).
- MTP (Medical Termination of Pregnancy): legal up to 20 weeks under specific conditions.
- STDs prevented by condoms.
- Sex ratio: declining in some states (concerning).

### 1.4 Heredity and Evolution (Ch 8)

**Mendel's experiments** (Gregor Mendel, 19th century, on pea plants):
- **Monohybrid cross:** Tall (TT) × Dwarf (tt) → F₁ all Tall (Tt) → F₂ ratio 3 Tall: 1 Dwarf.
- **Phenotype ratio 3:1; Genotype ratio 1:2:1.**
- **Dihybrid cross:** RRYY × rryy → F₁ all RrYy (round, yellow) → F₂ ratio 9:3:3:1.
- **Law of Dominance:** One trait (dominant) expresses over another (recessive).
- **Law of Segregation:** Two alleles separate during gamete formation.
- **Law of Independent Assortment:** Genes on different chromosomes are inherited independently.

**Genes:** A segment of DNA that codes for a protein; located on chromosomes.
- **Alleles:** Different forms of a gene (e.g., T for tall, t for dwarf).
- **Genotype:** Genetic makeup (e.g., Tt).
- **Phenotype:** Observable trait (e.g., Tall).
- **Homozygous:** TT or tt; **Heterozygous:** Tt.
- **Dominant:** T (Tall); **Recessive:** t (dwarf).
- **Sex determination:** XX (female) × XY (male). Father determines sex of child.

**Sex-linked disorders:** Colour blindness (X-linked recessive), haemophilia.
- **Evolution:** Change in inherited traits over generations. Darwin's theory: Natural Selection (survival of the fittest).
- **Evidence of evolution:** Homologous organs (same structure, different function — e.g., forelimbs of man, whale, bat, horse, frog), analogous organs (different structure, similar function — wings of butterfly and bird), fossils.

**Speciation:** Formation of new species due to geographical isolation, genetic drift, mutation, natural selection.

### 1.5 Our Environment (Ch 13)

**Ecosystem:** Biotic (living) + Abiotic (non-living: temperature, rainfall, soil, minerals) components interacting.

**Trophic levels:**
- 1st: Producers (green plants) — make food.
- 2nd: Primary consumers (herbivores — deer, cow).
- 3rd: Secondary consumers (carnivores — frog, snake).
- 4th: Tertiary consumers (top carnivores — eagle, lion).

**Food chain:** Linear sequence of who-eats-whom. **Food web:** Interconnected food chains.
- Example: Grass → Deer → Tiger.

**Energy flow:**
- Green plants capture ~1% of sunlight energy.
- 10% law (Lindeman): Only 10% of energy passes to next trophic level; 90% lost as heat.
- Energy flow is **unidirectional** (not cyclic).
- Maximum number of individuals at producer level; decreases as we go up.

**Biological magnification:** Accumulation of non-degradable chemicals (pesticides like DDT) at each trophic level; maximum at top (humans).

**Waste management:**
- **5 R's:** Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repurpose, Recycle.
- **Biodegradable:** Decompose naturally (food waste, paper).
- **Non-biodegradable:** Do not decompose (plastic, metals).
- **E-waste:** Electronic waste — computers, phones; contains toxic heavy metals (lead, mercury, cadmium).

**Ozone layer:** O₃ in stratosphere; absorbs UV radiation. CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) deplete it → "ozone hole" over Antarctica.

**Greenhouse effect & global warming:** CO₂, CH₄, N₂O trap heat; deforestation aggravates.

## 3. Everyday Science & Applications

1. **Why do we yawn when sleepy or oxygen-deficient?** To inhale more oxygen, remove CO₂ buildup.
2. **Why do we vomit when sick?** Medulla oblongata triggers vomiting to expel toxic substances.
3. **Why is saliva important?** Contains amylase to start starch digestion; also lubricates food.
4. **Why is HCl necessary in the stomach?** Acidic medium for pepsin; kills bacteria; activates other enzymes.
5. **Why do athletes breathe faster?** Increased O₂ demand for cellular respiration during exercise.
6. **Why does a person climbing Mt. Everest breathe faster?** Decreased O₂ partial pressure at high altitude.
7. **Why does eating a banana prevent muscle cramps?** Banana has K⁺ (potassium) which prevents fatigue.
8. **Why does a cut on the tongue bleed heavily?** Tongue has rich blood supply.
9. **Why is iodine added to table salt?** Prevents iodine deficiency (goitre).
10. **Why are insulin injections given to diabetics?** Insulin lowers blood glucose; deficiency causes diabetes mellitus.
11. **Why do leaves fall in autumn?** Ethylene and abscisic acid cause abscission.
12. **Why does a plant grow toward the window?** Phototropism mediated by auxin (moves to shaded side → cell elongation).
13. **Why is DDT banned?** Bioaccumulation and biomagnification; harmful to top predators and humans.
14. **Why are food chains short (3-4 levels)?** Energy loss at each trophic level (90% lost as heat) means insufficient energy at higher levels.
15. **Why is the ozone layer important?** Absorbs harmful UV radiation; protects life.
16. **Why do identical twins look alike?** They develop from same zygote; same DNA.
17. **Why is colour blindness more common in males?** X-linked recessive; males have only one X (XY), so one recessive allele is enough to express.
18. **Why does a foetus develop inside the mother?** Placenta provides nutrition and protection.
19. **Why are contraceptives used?** To prevent unwanted pregnancies and STDs.
20. **Why are decomposers essential?** They recycle nutrients back to soil; without them, dead organic matter would accumulate.

## 5. Solved Conceptual Problems

**Problem 1 (Photosynthesis):** Why is sunlight necessary for photosynthesis? Can it occur in dim light?

**Solution:** Light provides the energy for the conversion of CO₂ + H₂O to glucose. Light is absorbed by chlorophyll. In dim light, rate of photosynthesis is much lower; below light compensation point, respiration > photosynthesis.

**Problem 2 (Mendelian cross):** A man with blood group A marries a woman with blood group B. Their child has blood group O. What are the genotypes of the parents?

**Solution:** For child to be O (ii), both parents must carry i allele. So father is I^A i, mother is I^B i.

**Problem 3 (Trophic level energy):** If 10,000 J of energy is available at producer level, how much is at tertiary consumer level?

**Solution:** 10% at each level.
- Producers: 10,000 J
- Primary consumers (1st transfer): 1,000 J
- Secondary consumers (2nd transfer): 100 J
- Tertiary consumers (3rd transfer): 10 J

**Problem 4 (Kidney function):** A patient has elevated blood urea. What does this indicate?

**Solution:** Kidney dysfunction; urea is normally filtered out by kidneys.

**Problem 5 (Reflex action):** Why is reflex action important?**

**Solution:** Reflexes are fast (don't need brain processing). Critical for survival — pulling hand from hot object before conscious thought; prevents serious burns. Reflex arc synapses in spinal cord for speed.
