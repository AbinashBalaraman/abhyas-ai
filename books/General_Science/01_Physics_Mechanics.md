## 1. Comprehensive Theory

### 1.1 Light — Reflection and Refraction (Ch 9)

Light is the form of energy that enables vision. We see objects because light reflected (or scattered) from them enters our eyes. Light travels in straight lines — a fact demonstrated by the sharp shadow cast by a small source through an opaque object.

**Reflection of Light.** The two laws of reflection are:
1. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
2. The incident ray, the normal at the point of incidence, and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane.

A plane mirror forms an image that is **virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted**, and at the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.

**Spherical Mirrors.** A spherical mirror whose reflecting surface is curved inwards (faces the centre of the sphere) is called a **concave mirror**; one curved outwards is a **convex mirror**.

Key terminology:
- **Pole (P):** Centre of the reflecting surface.
- **Centre of curvature (C):** Centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part.
- **Radius of curvature (R):** Distance PC.
- **Principal axis:** Straight line through P and C.
- **Principal focus (F):** Point where parallel rays converge (concave) or appear to diverge from (convex) after reflection.
- **Focal length (f):** Distance PF.
- **Aperture:** Diameter of the reflecting surface.

For spherical mirrors of small apertures, **R = 2f**, i.e., the focus lies midway between P and C.

**Image formation by concave mirror** (Table 9.1 of NCERT):
| Position of object | Position of image | Size | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| At infinity | At F | Highly diminished, point-sized | Real, inverted |
| Beyond C | Between F and C | Diminished | Real, inverted |
| At C | At C | Same size | Real, inverted |
| Between C and F | Beyond C | Enlarged | Real, inverted |
| At F | At infinity | — | Image not formed |
| Between P and F | Behind mirror | Enlarged | Virtual, erect |

**Convex mirror** always forms virtual, erect, diminished images, located between P and F. Convex mirrors are used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles because they always give an erect image and have a wider field of view.

**Mirror Formula and Magnification (New Cartesian Sign Convention):**
- All distances are measured from the pole.
- Distances in the direction of incident light (right of P) are positive; against the incident light (left of P) are negative.
- Heights above principal axis are positive; below are negative.

$$\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$

$$m = \frac{h'}{h} = -\frac{v}{u}$$

A negative magnification indicates a real image; positive indicates virtual.

**Refraction of Light.** When light travels obliquely from one transparent medium to another, it changes direction. This is called refraction. **Snell's law:** the ratio of sine of angle of incidence to sine of angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media and a given colour of light:

$$\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \text{constant} = n_{21}$$

The **refractive index** of medium 2 with respect to medium 1 is:

$$n_{21} = \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{\text{speed of light in medium 1}}{\text{speed of light in medium 2}}$$

The **absolute refractive index** (with respect to vacuum) is $n_m = c/v$. Light travels fastest in vacuum ($3 \times 10^8$ m/s) and slower in denser media. Diamond has the highest refractive index (2.42), air the lowest (1.0003).

**Refraction by spherical lenses.** A convex lens is thicker at the middle and converges parallel rays to a focus; a concave lens is thinner at the middle and diverges them. The **lens formula**:

$$\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$

Magnification for lenses: $m = h'/h = v/u$.

**Power of a lens** $P = 1/f$ (in metres); SI unit is dioptre (D).

### 1.2 The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Ch 10)

The human eye works like a camera: the cornea + eye lens system forms an image on the retina. The eyeball is approximately spherical, ~2.3 cm in diameter. Most refraction occurs at the cornea; the crystalline lens provides finer adjustment of focal length.

**Power of accommodation** is the ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length to focus objects at varying distances. The **least distance of distinct vision** (near point) for a normal adult is 25 cm; the **far point** is infinity.

**Defects of vision and their correction**:
- **Myopia (near-sightedness):** Image of distant object forms in front of retina. Corrected by **concave lens** of suitable power.
- **Hypermetropia (far-sightedness):** Image of nearby object forms behind the retina. Corrected by **convex lens** of suitable power.
- **Presbyopia:** Due to ageing; ciliary muscles weaken, lens loses flexibility. Corrected with **bi-focal lenses** (concave upper, convex lower).

**Atmospheric refraction** causes the twinkling of stars (because the atmosphere has varying refractive index, starlight's apparent position keeps fluctuating) and the advance sunrise / delayed sunset (about 2 minutes each, plus the apparent flattening of the Sun's disc).

**Dispersion:** White light passing through a prism splits into seven colours (VIBGYOR — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) because different colours bend through different angles; red bends least, violet the most.

**Tyndall effect** is scattering of light by colloidal particles. The **blue colour of the sky** is due to scattering of shorter wavelengths by air molecules (much smaller than the wavelength of visible light). The **red colour of the Sun at sunrise/sunset** is because light has to travel a longer distance through the atmosphere, scattering away most of the blue light.

**Why red is used for danger signals:** Red is scattered the least by fog and smoke, so it can be seen from a distance.

### 1.3 Electricity (Ch 11)

**Electric current** is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor:

$$I = \frac{Q}{t}$$

SI unit: ampere (A). 1 A = 1 C/s. Charge of one electron: $1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C. 1 C = charge of ~$6 \times 10^{18}$ electrons.

**Conventional current** direction is opposite to the flow of electrons. An **ammeter** measures current (connected in series); a **voltmeter** measures potential difference (connected in parallel).

**Potential difference** between two points is the work done per unit charge:

$$V = \frac{W}{Q}$$

SI unit: volt (V). 1 V = 1 J/C.

**Ohm's law (1827, Georg Simon Ohm):** The potential difference across a conductor is directly proportional to the current through it, provided the temperature is constant:

$$V = IR \quad \text{or} \quad R = \frac{V}{I}$$

SI unit of resistance: ohm (Ω). 1 Ω = 1 V/A.

**Resistance depends on:**
1. Length (R ∝ l)
2. Area of cross-section (R ∝ 1/A)
3. Nature of material

$$R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$$

where $\rho$ is **resistivity** (SI unit: Ω·m). Metals have very low resistivity ($10^{-8}$ to $10^{-6}$ Ω·m); insulators $10^{12}$ to $10^{17}$ Ω·m. Alloys (e.g., nichrome, constantan, manganin) have higher resistivity and are used in heating elements because they don't oxidise at high temperatures.

**Resistors in series:** $R_s = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$. Current is the same; voltage divides.

**Resistors in parallel:**

$$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$$

Voltage is the same; current divides.

**Joule's law of heating**:

$$H = I^2 R t = V I t = \frac{V^2 t}{R}$$

Heat produced is (i) proportional to $I^2$, (ii) proportional to R, (iii) proportional to t. Practical applications: electric iron, toaster, oven, kettle, bulb, fuse. A fuse is a safety device with a low-melting-point wire that breaks the circuit when current exceeds the safe limit.

**Electric power:** $P = VI = I^2R = V^2/R$. SI unit: watt (W). Commercial unit of energy: kilowatt hour (kWh). 1 kWh = $3.6 \times 10^6$ J.

### 1.4 Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Ch 12)

Hans Christian Oersted discovered in 1820 that an electric current produces a magnetic field (compass needle deflects).

**Magnetic field lines**:
- Emerge from N pole, merge at S pole; closed curves.
- Closeness of lines indicates field strength.
- Never cross each other.

**Right-Hand Thumb Rule** for a straight current-carrying conductor: if the thumb points in the direction of current, the curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field.

**Magnetic field of a circular loop:** Straight lines at the centre; field strength is n times for a coil of n turns.

**Solenoid:** A coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wrapped closely in a cylindrical shape. Its magnetic field resembles a bar magnet. Inserting a soft iron core makes it an **electromagnet**.

**Force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field** is given by **Fleming's left-hand rule**:
- Forefinger → direction of magnetic field
- Second finger → direction of current
- Thumb → direction of force (motion)

The force is largest when the field and current are perpendicular. Force is zero if they are parallel.

**Domestic electric circuits** in India use 220 V AC at 50 Hz:
- **Live wire** (red insulation) — positive
- **Neutral wire** (black insulation) — negative
- **Earth wire** (green insulation) — safety; connected to a metal plate deep in the earth; ensures any leakage current doesn't cause severe shock.

A fuse (in series) protects against overloading and short-circuiting.

## 3. Everyday Science & Applications

20+ "Why does X happen?" Q&A — each tied to NCERT principles:

1. **Why can we see ourselves in a mirror but not in a wall?** Because a polished surface (mirror) reflects light specularly (regularly) per the laws of reflection; a wall diffuses/scatters light.
2. **Why is the image in a concave mirror larger when we are close to it?** When the object is between F and P, a concave mirror forms a virtual, erect, enlarged image.
3. **Why do convex mirrors act as rear-view mirrors in vehicles?** They always produce erect, virtual, diminished images and have a wider field of view.
4. **Why does a pencil appear bent in a glass of water?** Refraction: light rays from the immersed portion change direction at the air-water interface.
5. **Why does a coin at the bottom of a bucket reappear when water is poured?** The coin's image is raised due to refraction at the water surface.
6. **Why is a diamond so sparkly?** It has a very high refractive index (2.42); total internal reflection traps light inside, which then emerges in flashes.
7. **Why does our eye see a clear image of an object at varying distances?** The ciliary muscles change the curvature (and thus focal length) of the eye lens — power of accommodation.
8. **Why do old people need bifocal lenses?** With age, the ciliary muscles weaken and lens flexibility decreases (presbyopia), requiring different powers for near and far vision.
9. **Why do stars twinkle but planets don't?** Stars are point-sized sources; their light refracts through turbulent atmosphere. Planets are extended sources, so variations average out.
10. **Why is the sky blue?** Molecules of air (smaller than visible wavelengths) scatter shorter (blue) wavelengths more than longer ones.
11. **Why is the Sun red at sunrise and sunset?** Light travels a longer atmospheric path, scattering away most of the blue, leaving red/orange.
12. **Why is danger signal red?** Red is scattered the least by fog and smoke, so visible from a distance.
13. **Why are electric wires coated with PVC/rubber?** Because metals are good conductors; the coating prevents current leakage and electric shock (mentioned in Ch 3 for metals — concept applies to Ch 11 wires).
14. **Why is the current same in a series circuit but different in a parallel circuit?** In series, the same charge flows through each component; in parallel, current divides at each branch.
15. **Why does an electric heater produce heat but a connecting wire doesn't?** The heater coil has high resistance (Nichrome), so $H = I^2Rt$ generates heat; connecting wires have low resistance.
16. **Why are heating coils made of alloys, not pure metals?** Alloys (e.g., nichrome) have high resistivity and don't oxidise (burn) at high temperatures.
17. **Why is tungsten used for bulb filaments?** High melting point (3380°C) — does not melt at bulb operating temperature.
18. **Why is the fuse wire placed in series?** So the entire circuit current passes through it; if too high, the wire melts and breaks the circuit.
19. **Why does a compass needle deflect near a current-carrying wire?** The current produces a magnetic field that exerts force on the needle's magnet.
20. **Why is the magnetic field stronger inside a solenoid than outside?** Field lines inside are parallel and close together (uniform field); outside they spread out.
21. **Why are domestic circuits connected in parallel, not series?** Parallel connection gives the same voltage to each appliance; if one fails, others still work; and each appliance can be independently switched.
22. **Why is the earth wire important in household circuits?** It provides a low-resistance path for leakage current, preventing severe electric shock.

## 5. Solved Numerical Problems

**Problem 1 (Mirror formula):** A convex mirror used for rear-view on an automobile has a radius of curvature 3.00 m. If a bus is located at 5.00 m from this mirror, find the position, nature and size of the image.

**Solution:** R = +3.00 m, so f = R/2 = +1.50 m. u = –5.00 m.
$1/v = 1/f - 1/u = 1/1.50 - 1/(-5.00) = 2/3 + 1/5 = 10/15 + 3/15 = 13/15$
$v = 15/13 \approx +1.15$ m (behind the mirror).
$m = -v/u = -1.15/(-5.00) = +0.23$. The image is virtual, erect, diminished by a factor of 0.23.

**Problem 2 (Lens formula):** A 2.0 cm tall object is placed 15 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find nature, position and size of the image.

**Solution:** h = +2.0 cm, f = +10 cm, u = –15 cm.
$1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/10 - 1/15 = 3/30 - 2/30 = 1/30$
$v = +30$ cm (real image on the other side).
$h' = h \cdot v/u = 2.0 \cdot 30/(-15) = -4.0$ cm (inverted, enlarged).

**Problem 3 (Refraction):** Light enters from air to glass (refractive index 1.50). What is the speed of light in glass? c = $3 \times 10^8$ m/s.

**Solution:** $n = c/v \Rightarrow v = c/n = 3 \times 10^8 / 1.50 = 2 \times 10^8$ m/s.

**Problem 4 (Resistivity):** Resistance of a metal wire of length 1 m is 26 Ω at 20°C; diameter 0.3 mm. Find the resistivity.

**Solution:** $R = 26$ Ω, l = 1 m, d = 0.3 mm = $3 \times 10^{-4}$ m.
$\rho = RA/l = 26 \times \pi \times (3 \times 10^{-4})^2 / (4 \times 1) = 1.84 \times 10^{-6}$ Ω·m. Material: **manganese**.

**Problem 5 (Resistors in parallel):** R1 = 5 Ω, R2 = 10 Ω, R3 = 30 Ω in parallel, V = 12 V. Find current in each.

**Solution:** $1/R_p = 1/5 + 1/10 + 1/30 = 6/30 + 3/30 + 1/30 = 10/30 = 1/3 \Rightarrow R_p = 3$ Ω.
$I_1 = 12/5 = 2.4$ A; $I_2 = 12/10 = 1.2$ A; $I_3 = 12/30 = 0.4$ A. Total: 4 A.

**Problem 6 (Joule heating):** An electric iron of resistance 20 Ω takes current of 5 A. Calculate heat developed in 30 s.

**Solution:** $H = I^2 R t = 25 \times 20 \times 30 = 15{,}000$ J.

**Problem 7 (Power & Energy):** A refrigerator rated 400 W operates 8 hr/day. Cost for 30 days at ₹3 per kWh?

**Solution:** Energy = 400 × 8 × 30 = 96,000 Wh = 96 kWh. Cost = 96 × 3 = ₹288.

**Problem 8 (Series-parallel):** A hot plate of an electric oven on 220 V has two resistance coils A and B, each 24 Ω. Currents in three cases (separate, series, parallel)?

**Solution:**
- Each alone: $I = 220/24 \approx 9.17$ A
- In series: $R = 48$ Ω, $I = 220/48 \approx 4.58$ A
- In parallel: $R = 12$ Ω, $I = 220/12 \approx 18.33$ A
