## 1. Dual Nature of Matter and Radiation

### 1.1 Photoelectric Effect

**Discovery**: Heinrich Hertz (1887); explained by Einstein (1905)

**Einstein's Photoelectric Equation**:
$$K_{max} = h\nu - \phi$$
where $h\nu$ = photon energy, $\phi$ = work function

### 1.2 Key Phenomena

| Phenomenon | Observation |
|---|---|
| **Threshold frequency** $\nu_0$ | Below this, no electrons emitted regardless of intensity |
| **Instantaneous emission** | No time lag (supports particle nature) |
| **KE independent of intensity** | Depends on frequency, not intensity |
| **Saturation current ∝ intensity** | More intensity = more photons = more electrons |

### 1.3 de Broglie Wavelength

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}$$

For electron accelerated through V volts:
$$\lambda = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}} \text{ Å}$$

## 2. Atomic Structure

### 2.1 Bohr's Model (1913)

Postulates:
1. Electrons move in fixed orbits (no radiation)
2. Angular momentum quantized: $mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$
3. Energy change = photon emission/absorption: $h\nu = E_2 - E_1$

### 2.2 Hydrogen Spectrum

$$E_n = -\frac{13.6}{n^2} \text{ eV}$$

| Series | Transition | Region |
|---|---|---|
| Lyman | $n_2 \to 1$ | UV |
| Balmer | $n_2 \to 2$ | Visible |
| Paschen | $n_2 \to 3$ | IR |
| Brackett | $n_2 \to 4$ | IR |
| Pfund | $n_2 \to 5$ | IR |

## 3. Nuclei

### 3.1 Nuclear Structure

- **Proton** ($p^+$): Charge $+e$, Mass = 1 u
- **Neutron** ($n^0$): Charge 0, Mass ≈ 1 u
- **Nucleus size**: ~$10^{-15}$ m (femtometer/fermi)
- **Density**: ~$2.3 \times 10^{17}$ kg/m³

### 3.2 Nuclear Density Formula

$$\rho = \frac{Mass}{Volume} = \frac{Am_u}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}$$

where $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$, $R_0 = 1.2$ fm

### 3.3 Mass Defect & Binding Energy

**Mass Defect** $\Delta m = Zm_p + Nm_n - M_{nucleus}$

**Binding Energy** $E_b = \Delta m \cdot c^2$

**Binding Energy per Nucleon** = $E_b / A$ (peaks at Fe-56 ≈ 8.8 MeV)

### 3.4 Nuclear Forces

- **Strongest** of all fundamental forces
- Short range (~1 fm); attractive
- Charge-independent (proton-proton = neutron-neutron = proton-neutron)
- Saturating (each nucleon interacts only with nearest neighbors)

## 4. Radioactivity

### 4.1 Discovery

- **Henri Becquerel** (1896): Discovered uranium salts emit radiation
- **Marie & Pierre Curie**: Isolated radium and polonium
- 3 types of radiation:
  - **Alpha** ($\alpha$): Helium nucleus ($^4_2He$); +2 charge; low penetration
  - **Beta** ($\beta^-$): Electron; -1 charge; moderate penetration
  - **Gamma** ($\gamma$): EM radiation; 0 charge; high penetration

### 4.2 Radioactive Decay Law

$$N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$$

- $\lambda$ = decay constant
- Half-life $T_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{\lambda}$
- Mean life $\tau = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{T_{1/2}}{0.693}$

### 4.3 SI Unit

**Becquerel (Bq)**: 1 disintegration/second

Earlier unit: **Curie (Ci)** = $3.7 \times 10^{10}$ Bq

## 5. Nuclear Fission & Fusion

### 5.1 Nuclear Fission (Splitting)

- **Discovery**: Hahn & Strassmann (1938); explained by Meitner & Frisch
- **Example**: $^1_0n + ^{235}_{92}U \to ^{141}_{56}Ba + ^{92}_{36}Kr + 3^1_0n + 200$ MeV
- **Chain reaction**: Each fission releases neutrons that cause more fissions
- **Critical mass**: Minimum mass for self-sustaining chain reaction
- **Uses**: Nuclear reactors, atomic bombs

### 5.2 Nuclear Fusion (Joining)

- Light nuclei combine to form heavier nucleus + energy
- **Example**: $^2_1H + ^3_1H \to ^4_2He + ^1_0n + 17.6$ MeV (deuterium-tritium)
- **Source**: Sun and stars; hydrogen bomb
- **Temperature required**: ~$10^8$ K (100 million K)

### 5.3 India's Nuclear Programme

- **Homi Bhabha**: Father of Indian nuclear programme; 3-stage programme (U-Pu-Th based breeders)
- **BARC** (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre): Mumbai, 1954
- **Apsara**: Asia's 1st reactor, 1956 (Trombay)
- **NFC** (Nuclear Fuel Complex): Hyderabad (founded 1971)
- **India's 3-stage strategy**:
  1. Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors (PHWR) — current
  2. Fast Breeder Reactors (FBR) — uses Thorium-232
  3. Thorium-based Reactors — long-term

## 6. Semiconductors

### 6.1 Energy Bands

- **Conduction Band** (CB): Electrons can move freely; conduct electricity
- **Valence Band** (VB): Electrons are bound; cannot conduct
- **Band Gap** $E_g$: Energy difference between CB and VB

| Material | Type | $E_g$ |
|---|---|---|
| **Conductors** (metals) | CB and VB overlap | 0 eV |
| **Semiconductors** (Si, Ge) | Small gap | 0.7-1.1 eV |
| **Insulators** (diamond) | Large gap | >5 eV |

### 6.2 Doping

Adding impurities to pure semiconductor:
- **N-type**: Add Group V (P, As) — adds free electrons
- **P-type**: Add Group III (B, Ga) — adds holes

### 6.3 P-N Junction Diode

- **Forward bias**: P connected to + → current flows
- **Reverse bias**: P connected to - → negligible current
- **Depletion region**: Region near junction with no mobile charge carriers

### 6.4 Applications

- **Diode**: Rectifier (AC → DC)
- **LED**: Light Emitting Diode
- **Photodiode**: Light → current
- **Solar cell**: Light → electricity (PV effect)
- **Zener diode**: Voltage regulator
- **Transistor**: Amplifier/Switch

## 7. Communication Systems

### 7.1 Electromagnetic Spectrum (Frequency Order)

Radio waves < Microwaves < Infrared < Visible < UV < X-rays < Gamma rays

### 7.2 Communication Types

| Type | Frequency | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Ground wave** | < 2 MHz | Local broadcasting (AM radio) |
| **Sky wave** | 2-30 MHz | Long-distance (Shortwave radio) |
| **Space wave** | > 30 MHz | TV, satellite (line-of-sight) |
| **Optical fiber** | $10^{14}$ Hz | Internet, telecom |

### 7.3 Modulation

- **AM** (Amplitude Modulation): $m = \frac{A_m}{A_c}$
- **FM** (Frequency Modulation): Better noise immunity
- **PM** (Phase Modulation)

## 8. Practice Questions (15 PYQs)

**Q1.** Photoelectric effect supports:
(a) Wave nature (b) Particle nature of light (c) Both (d) Neither
**Answer**: (b) Particle nature (Einstein's explanation)

**Q2.** de Broglie wavelength is associated with:
(a) Wave-particle duality (b) Bohr model (c) Photoelectric (d) Compton
**Answer**: (a) Wave-particle duality (every particle has wavelength)

**Q3.** SI unit of radioactivity:
(a) Becquerel (b) Curie (c) Gray (d) Sievert
**Answer**: (a) Becquerel (Bq); 1 disintegration/sec

**Q4.** Asia's first nuclear reactor:
(a) Dhruva (b) Apsara (c) CIRUS (d) Kamini
**Answer**: (b) Apsara (1956, Trombay)

**Q5.** E=mc² is:
(a) Newton's law (b) Mass-energy equivalence (c) Coulomb's law (d) Gravitational
**Answer**: (b) Mass-energy equivalence (Einstein, 1905)

**Q6.** In a P-N junction diode, current flows freely when:
(a) Reverse biased (b) Forward biased (c) No bias (d) Both
**Answer**: (b) Forward biased

**Q7.** Light with wavelength 500 nm has frequency:
(a) 6 × 10¹⁴ Hz (b) 6 × 10¹² Hz (c) 6 × 10⁸ Hz (d) 6 × 10¹⁵ Hz
**Answer**: (a) 6 × 10¹⁴ Hz ($\nu = c/\lambda$)

**Q8.** Bohr model applies to:
(a) Hydrogen only (b) Helium (c) All atoms (d) Molecules
**Answer**: (a) Hydrogen-like atoms (one electron)

**Q9.** Binding energy per nucleon is maximum for:
(a) Hydrogen (b) Uranium (c) Iron (Fe-56) (d) Helium
**Answer**: (c) Iron (Fe-56) — peak of binding energy curve

**Q10.** Light Emitting Diode (LED) works on principle of:
(a) Photoelectric effect (b) Electroluminescence (c) Compton scattering (d) Bremsstrahlung
**Answer**: (b) Electroluminescence (current → light)

**Q11.** The process of adding impurities to pure semiconductor is called:
(a) Crystallization (b) Doping (c) Annealing (d) Sintering
**Answer**: (b) Doping

**Q12.** Homi Bhabha is known as the father of:
(a) Indian Space programme (b) Indian Nuclear programme (c) Indian IT (d) Indian Defence
**Answer**: (b) Indian Nuclear programme

**Q13.** Nuclear fusion requires:
(a) Low temperature (b) High temperature (~10⁸ K) (c) Room temperature (d) Low pressure
**Answer**: (b) High temperature (millions of degrees) to overcome Coulomb barrier

**Q14.** X-rays were discovered by:
(a) Marie Curie (b) Wilhelm Röntgen (c) Henri Becquerel (d) Ernest Rutherford
**Answer**: (b) Wilhelm Röntgen (1895; Nobel 1901)

**Q15.** The depletion region in P-N junction has:
(a) Free electrons (b) Holes (c) No mobile carriers (d) Both
**Answer**: (c) No mobile carriers (only immobile ions)
