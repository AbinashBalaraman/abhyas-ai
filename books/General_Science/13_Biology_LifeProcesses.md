## 1. Comprehensive Theory

### 1.1 Life Processes

**Life processes** are the basic processes that are essential to maintain life. They include:

- **Nutrition** — obtaining food for energy and growth.
- **Respiration** — obtaining oxygen and using it to break down food for energy.
- **Transportation** — carrying food, oxygen, and wastes to/from cells.
- **Excretion** — removing metabolic wastes from the body.

These processes are needed because:
- Living structures are well-organised and need maintenance.
- The environment constantly breaks down this order.
- Energy is required for repair and maintenance.

### 1.2 Nutrition

**Nutrition** is the process of obtaining food (nutrients) from outside the body and using it for energy, growth, and repair.

**Two modes of nutrition:**

#### (a) Autotrophic Nutrition (Auto = self; trophic = nutrition)
Organisms make their own food from simple inorganic substances (CO₂ and H₂O) using energy. Example: green plants, some bacteria.

**Photosynthesis:** The process by which green plants synthesise their own food using sunlight, CO₂, and water:

$$6CO_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{sunlight, chlorophyll}} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2\uparrow$$

**Requirements for photosynthesis:**
- **Sunlight** (energy source).
- **Chlorophyll** (green pigment, in chloroplasts).
- **CO₂** (from air, through stomata).
- **Water** (from soil, through roots).

**Events of photosynthesis:**
1. Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll.
2. Conversion of light energy to chemical energy; splitting of water into H and O (photolysis).
3. Reduction of CO₂ to carbohydrates.

**Factors affecting photosynthesis:** Light, CO₂, water, temperature, chlorophyll.

#### (b) Heterotrophic Nutrition (Hetero = different)
Organisms depend on other organisms for food. Examples: animals, fungi, many bacteria.

**Types:**
- **Holozoic:** Ingestion of solid food (e.g., humans, animals).
- **Parasitic:** Living on/in another organism (e.g., tapeworm, lice).
- **Saprophytic:** Feeding on dead and decaying matter (e.g., fungi, bacteria).
- **Symbiotic:** Mutually beneficial association (e.g., Rhizobium in legume roots, lichens).

### 1.3 Human Digestive System

The **human digestive system** consists of the **alimentary canal** and **digestive glands**:

**Alimentary canal (parts in order):**
1. **Mouth (buccal cavity):** Teeth break food; tongue mixes with saliva; salivary glands secrete saliva (contains amylase → digests starch).
2. **Oesophagus (food pipe):** ~25 cm long; carries food from mouth to stomach by **peristalsis** (muscular contractions).
3. **Stomach:** J-shaped; secretes HCl, pepsin (digests proteins), and mucus. HCl kills germs, makes medium acidic for pepsin.
4. **Small intestine:** ~6-7 m long; main site of digestion and absorption. Secretions: bile (from liver), pancreatic juice (from pancreas), intestinal juice. Completes digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, fats.
5. **Large intestine (colon):** Absorbs water and salts. Undigested food is stored as faeces.
6. **Rectum and anus:** Egestion (expulsion of faeces).

**Digestive glands:**
- **Salivary glands** (mouth): Saliva → contains salivary amylase (digests starch).
- **Gastric glands** (stomach): HCl + pepsin + mucus.
- **Liver:** Bile (stored in gall bladder) → emulsifies fats.
- **Pancreas:** Pancreatic juice → contains trypsin, lipase, amylase.
- **Intestinal glands:** Maltase, sucrase, lactase, peptidases, lipase.

**Digestion summary:**

| Nutrient | Mouth | Stomach | Small intestine |
|----------|-------|---------|-----------------|
| Carbohydrates | Salivary amylase → maltose | — | Pancreatic amylase, maltase, sucrase, lactase → glucose |
| Proteins | — | Pepsin → polypeptides | Trypsin, peptidases → amino acids |
| Fats | — | — | Bile (emulsification) + lipase → fatty acids + glycerol |

**Absorption:** In the small intestine, the inner lining has **villi** (finger-like projections) that increase surface area for absorption. Digested food passes into the blood through the villi.

### 1.4 Respiration

**Respiration** is the process of obtaining oxygen from the environment and using it to break down food (oxidation) to release energy, with CO₂ as a by-product:

$$C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{enzymes}} 6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{Energy (ATP)}$$

**Types:**

#### (a) Aerobic Respiration
- Occurs in the presence of oxygen.
- Complete breakdown of glucose.
- Produces more energy (38 ATP per glucose in eukaryotes).
- Occurs in cytoplasm + mitochondria.
- Examples: most plants, animals.

#### (b) Anaerobic Respiration
- Occurs in the absence of oxygen.
- Incomplete breakdown of glucose.
- Produces less energy (2 ATP per glucose).
- Occurs in cytoplasm.
- Examples: in yeast (fermentation → ethanol + CO₂), in muscles during heavy exercise (lactic acid).

**In yeast:** $C_6H_{12}O_6 \rightarrow 2C_2H_5OH + 2CO_2 + \text{Energy}$
**In muscles:** $C_6H_{12}O_6 \rightarrow 2C_3H_6O_3$ (lactic acid) + Energy

### 1.5 Human Respiratory System

**Parts:**
1. **Nasal cavity:** Filters, warms, and moistens air.
2. **Pharynx:** Common passage for food and air.
3. **Larynx:** Voice box; contains vocal cords.
4. **Trachea (windpipe):** Tube supported by cartilage rings.
5. **Bronchi:** Two branches of trachea, one to each lung.
6. **Bronchioles:** Smaller branches inside lungs.
7. **Alveoli:** Tiny air sacs at the end of bronchioles; site of gas exchange.
8. **Lungs:** Pair of spongy organs; protected by the rib cage.

**Mechanism of breathing:**
- **Inhalation:** Diaphragm contracts (flattens) and rib cage rises → volume of chest cavity increases → air rushes in.
- **Exhalation:** Diaphragm relaxes (dome-shaped) and rib cage falls → volume of chest cavity decreases → air pushed out.

**Gas exchange in alveoli:** O₂ from inhaled air diffuses into blood capillaries; CO₂ from blood diffuses into alveoli. The blood becomes oxygenated.

### 1.6 Transportation in Humans

**Blood** is the main transport fluid in humans. It consists of:

- **Plasma (~55%):** Liquid matrix; carries nutrients, hormones, wastes.
- **Red Blood Cells (RBCs):** Contain haemoglobin; carry O₂.
- **White Blood Cells (WBCs):** Fight infection.
- **Platelets:** Help in blood clotting.

**Heart:** A muscular organ about the size of a fist, located slightly to the left of the chest. It has 4 chambers:

- **Right atrium:** Receives deoxygenated blood from the body.
- **Right ventricle:** Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs.
- **Left atrium:** Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs.
- **Left ventricle:** Pumps oxygenated blood to the body.

**Double circulation:**
1. **Pulmonary circulation:** Heart ↔ Lungs (deoxygenated to oxygenated).
2. **Systemic circulation:** Heart ↔ Body (oxygenated to deoxygenated).

**Arteries:** Carry blood away from the heart (oxygenated, except pulmonary artery).
**Veins:** Carry blood towards the heart (deoxygenated, except pulmonary vein).
**Capillaries:** Thin-walled vessels connecting arteries and veins; site of exchange.

**Blood pressure:** The force exerted by blood on the walls of arteries. Normal: 120/80 mm Hg (systolic/diastolic).

### 1.7 Transportation in Plants

**Plants have two types of conducting tissues:**

- **Xylem:** Carries water and minerals from roots to leaves (and other parts). Unidirectional.
- **Phloem:** Carries food (sucrose) from leaves to all parts of the plant. Bidirectional (translocation).

**Transpiration:** Loss of water vapour from leaves through stomata. It creates a suction force that helps pull water up through the xylem (transpiration pull).

**Xylem consists of:** Tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma, xylem fibres.
**Phloem consists of:** Sieve tubes (sieve elements), companion cells, phloem parenchyma, phloem fibres.

### 1.8 Excretion

**Excretion** is the process of removing metabolic wastes from the body.

**Excretory products in different organisms:**

| Organism | Main excretory product |
|----------|----------------------|
| Humans, mammals | Urea |
| Birds, reptiles | Uric acid |
| Fish (most) | Ammonia |
| Plants | O₂ (photosynthesis), CO₂ (respiration), tannins, resins, gums |

**Human excretory system:**
- **Kidneys (2):** Bean-shaped, located in the abdominal cavity. They filter blood to produce urine.
- **Ureters (2):** Carry urine from kidneys to the urinary bladder.
- **Urinary bladder:** Stores urine.
- **Urethra:** Releases urine from the body.

**Nephron:** The functional unit of the kidney. Each kidney has about 1 million nephrons. Parts:

- **Bowman's capsule (renal corpuscle):** Filters blood.
- **Glomerulus:** Network of capillaries inside Bowman's capsule.
- **Renal tubule:** Long tube where reabsorption and secretion occur.
- **Collecting duct:** Carries urine to the ureter.

**Steps of urine formation:**
1. **Filtration:** Blood is filtered in the glomerulus; filtrate enters Bowman's capsule.
2. **Reabsorption:** Useful substances (glucose, amino acids, salts, water) are reabsorbed into the blood.
3. **Secretion:** Waste substances (urea, creatinine) are added to the filtrate.
4. The final filtrate is **urine**, which is hypertonic (concentrated) compared to blood.

**Other excretory organs in humans:** Skin (sweat), lungs (CO₂), liver (bile pigments).

### 1.9 Excretion in Plants**

Plants do not have specialised excretory organs. Waste products are stored in leaves, bark, fruits, or released into the soil. Examples: tannins, gums, resins, alkaloids.

## 3. Everyday Science & Applications

**Q1: Why does our mouth feel dry when we are nervous?**
A: Stress or nervousness reduces saliva production, making the mouth feel dry.

**Q2: Why is saliva important?**
A: Saliva contains salivary amylase, which begins the digestion of starch. It also moistens food for swallowing.

**Q3: Why is the small intestine so long?**
A: To provide a large surface area for the complete digestion and absorption of food. The villi further increase the surface area.

**Q4: Why do we feel cramps in our legs after running?**
A: During heavy exercise, the oxygen supply to muscles is insufficient for aerobic respiration, so anaerobic respiration occurs, producing lactic acid. The accumulation of lactic acid causes cramps.

**Q5: Why do we breathe faster during exercise?**
A: To supply more O₂ to the muscles for aerobic respiration (energy production) and to remove the extra CO₂ produced.

**Q6: Why does the left ventricle have thicker walls than the right ventricle?**
A: The left ventricle pumps blood to the entire body, requiring more force, so its walls are thicker. The right ventricle pumps blood only to the lungs (less distance, less force needed).

**Q7: Why are arteries thicker than veins?**
A: Arteries carry blood from the heart at high pressure, so they have thick, elastic walls. Veins carry blood back to the heart at low pressure and have thinner walls with valves to prevent backflow.

**Q8: Why do plants not have blood?**
A: Plants have a slow metabolism and don't need a fast transport system. Xylem and phloem transport water, minerals, and food at low pressure.

**Q9: Why is transpiration important?**
A: Transpiration creates a suction force (transpiration pull) that helps pull water and minerals up from the roots to the leaves. It also helps in cooling the plant.

**Q10: Why is urine yellow?**
A: Urine contains urochrome (a yellow pigment) produced by the breakdown of haemoglobin from old red blood cells.

**Q11: Why is dialysis needed for patients with kidney failure?**
A: The kidneys filter blood and remove waste. In kidney failure, this function is lost, and waste accumulates in the body. A dialysis machine artificially filters the blood.

**Q12: Why do herbivores have a longer small intestine than carnivores?**
A: Plant cellulose is harder to digest, requiring more time and a longer intestine for complete digestion.

**Q13: Why is the heart called a double pump?**
A: The heart pumps blood twice in one cycle: once for pulmonary circulation (to the lungs) and once for systemic circulation (to the body).

**Q14: Why does a leaf become yellow if a plant is kept in the dark for a long time?**
A: Without light, chlorophyll production stops, and existing chlorophyll breaks down, revealing the yellow carotenoid pigments.

**Q15: Why do we get hiccups?**
A: Hiccups are caused by involuntary contractions of the diaphragm, often due to irritation of the phrenic nerve or distension of the stomach.

**Q16: Why does cutting the phloem of a plant kill it?**
A: The phloem transports food from the leaves to the rest of the plant. Without it, the roots and other parts cannot get food and die.

**Q17: Why does the left lung have two lobes while the right lung has three?**
A: The right lung has three lobes to accommodate the heart, which is slightly tilted to the left.

**Q18: Why is the trachea supported by cartilage rings?**
A: Cartilage rings keep the trachea open at all times for the free flow of air.

**Q19: Why is sweating considered a mode of excretion?**
A: Sweat contains water, salts, and small amounts of urea — the same wastes as urine, in lower concentration. So sweat is an excretory fluid.

**Q20: Why are capillaries very thin?**
A: Capillaries have walls only one cell thick, allowing easy diffusion of O₂, CO₂, nutrients, and wastes between blood and tissues.

10. **Pancreas** secretes pancreatic juice with enzymes.

12. **Aerobic respiration** uses oxygen; **anaerobic** does not.
13. **Anaerobic respiration in yeast:** Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂.
14. **Anaerobic respiration in muscles:** Glucose → Lactic acid.
15. **Human respiratory parts:** Nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi, lungs.
16. **Alveoli** are the site of gas exchange.
17. **Human heart has 4 chambers** (2 atria + 2 ventricles).
18. **Double circulation** in humans: Pulmonary + Systemic.
19. **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart; **veins** carry blood to the heart.
20. **Xylem** carries water and minerals; **phloem** carries food.

22. **Nephron** is the functional unit of the kidney.
23. **Each kidney has ~1 million nephrons.**
