## 1. Comprehensive Theory

### 1.1 Control and Coordination in Animals

In animals, control and coordination are brought about by two systems:

1. **Nervous system** (rapid, electrical signals).
2. **Endocrine system** (slower, chemical signals via hormones).

### 1.2 Nervous System

The nervous system receives information from the environment, processes it, and sends signals to the appropriate organs for a response. It includes:

- **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Brain + Spinal cord.
- **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS):** Nerves that connect CNS to all parts of the body.
- **Autonomic Nervous System:** Controls involuntary functions (heartbeat, digestion). Subdivided into sympathetic and parasympathetic.

### 1.3 Neuron (Nerve Cell)

The **neuron** is the functional unit of the nervous system. It carries nerve impulses.

**Parts of a neuron:**

- **Dendrites:** Branched projections that receive signals from other neurons.
- **Cell body (soma/cyton):** Contains the nucleus and cytoplasm; integrates signals.
- **Axon:** Long, slender projection that conducts impulses away from the cell body.
- **Myelin sheath:** Insulating layer (made of fat and protein) around the axon in some neurons; speeds up impulse conduction.
- **Nodes of Ranvier:** Gaps in the myelin sheath.
- **Synaptic knob (axon terminal):** End of the axon; releases neurotransmitters.
- **Synapse:** Small gap between the synaptic knob of one neuron and the dendrite of the next neuron.

**Types of neurons:**

1. **Sensory neurons:** Carry signals from receptors to CNS.
2. **Motor neurons:** Carry signals from CNS to effectors (muscles/glands).
3. **Interneurons (relay neurons):** Connect sensory and motor neurons (in CNS).

### 1.4 Nerve Impulse

A nerve impulse is an electrical signal that travels along the neuron. It involves the movement of sodium (Na⁺) and potassium (K⁺) ions across the cell membrane, generating an **action potential**.

**Transmission across a synapse:** When an impulse reaches the synaptic knob, it triggers the release of **neurotransmitters** (e.g., acetylcholine, dopamine) into the synaptic cleft. These chemicals bind to receptors on the next neuron, generating a new impulse. The neurotransmitter is then broken down or reabsorbed.

### 1.5 Reflex Action and Reflex Arc

A **reflex action** is a sudden, involuntary, automatic response to a stimulus. Examples: withdrawing hand from hot object, blinking, sneezing.

**Reflex arc:** The neural pathway of a reflex action. It includes:
1. **Receptor** (in skin, sense organ) — detects the stimulus.
2. **Sensory neuron** — carries the signal to the spinal cord.
3. **Interneuron** (in spinal cord) — processes the signal.
4. **Motor neuron** — carries the signal from spinal cord to muscle.
5. **Effector** (muscle/gland) — performs the response.

**Example:** When you touch a hot object, the heat is detected by receptors in the skin, signals travel through sensory neurons to the spinal cord, are processed, and motor neurons signal the muscles to contract → you withdraw your hand. All this happens in milliseconds, without involving the brain.

### 1.6 Human Brain

The brain is the main control centre of the body. It has three major parts:

#### (a) Forebrain (Prosencephalon)
- **Cerebrum:** Largest part; controls intelligence, memory, consciousness, voluntary actions, sensory perception, language.
- **Thalamus:** Relays sensory information to the cerebrum.
- **Hypothalamus:** Controls hunger, thirst, body temperature, sleep, hormonal control (via pituitary).

#### (b) Midbrain (Mesencephalon)
- Controls reflexes of the eye (e.g., pupil reflex) and ear.

#### (c) Hindbrain (Rhombencephalon)
- **Cerebellum:** Coordinates voluntary movements (balance, posture, fine motor control).
- **Pons:** Relays signals between cerebrum and cerebellum.
- **Medulla oblongata:** Controls involuntary functions (heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure).

**Brain protection:**
- **Skull (cranium):** Bony outer protection.
- **Meninges:** Three membranes covering the brain.
- **Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF):** Fluid that cushions the brain against shocks.

### 1.7 Spinal Cord

The spinal cord extends from the medulla oblongata through the vertebral column. It:

- Conducts signals to and from the brain.
- Controls many reflex actions.
- Protected by the vertebral column.

### 1.8 Coordination in Plants

Plants do not have a nervous system. They respond to stimuli using **plant hormones** (phytohormones):

- **Auxins:** Promote cell elongation, growth of shoots, phototropism.
- **Gibberellins:** Promote stem elongation, seed germination.
- **Cytokinins:** Promote cell division.
- **Abscisic acid (ABA):** Inhibits growth, promotes dormancy and abscission (falling of leaves).
- **Ethylene:** Promotes fruit ripening, ageing.

**Types of plant movements:**

- **Tropic movements (directional):**
  - **Phototropism:** Movement towards/away from light.
  - **Gravitropism (geotropism):** Roots grow downwards (positive); shoots grow upwards (negative).
  - **Chemotropism:** Growth in response to chemicals (e.g., pollen tube towards ovule).
  - **Hydrotropism:** Growth towards water.
  - **Thigmotropism:** Growth in response to touch (e.g., tendrils of plants).

- **Nastic movements (non-directional):**
  - **Seismonastic:** Response to touch (e.g., Mimosa pudica "touch-me-not" leaves).
  - **NCompetitive Exam Question Bankinastic:** Response to day/night (e.g., sleep movements of leaves).

### 1.9 Endocrine System

The **endocrine system** consists of **endocrine glands** that secrete **hormones** directly into the blood, which are then transported to target organs.

**Major endocrine glands and their hormones:**

| Gland | Hormones | Function |
|-------|----------|----------|
| **Hypothalamus** | Releasing hormones | Control pituitary |
| **Pituitary** | Growth hormone (GH), TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, etc. | Master gland; controls growth, other glands |
| **Thyroid** | Thyroxine | Regulates metabolism |
| **Parathyroid** | Parathormone | Regulates calcium and phosphate |
| **Pancreas** | Insulin, glucagon | Regulates blood sugar |
| **Adrenal** | Adrenaline (epinephrine), cortisol | Fight-or-flight, stress response |
| **Testes** | Testosterone | Male sex characteristics, sperm production |
| **Ovaries** | Oestrogen, progesterone | Female sex characteristics, menstrual cycle |

**Feedback mechanism:** Hormone levels are regulated by negative feedback — when the level of a hormone is high, it inhibits further release; when low, it stimulates release.

### 1.10 Common Endocrine Disorders

- **Diabetes mellitus:** High blood sugar due to insufficient insulin (Type 1) or insulin resistance (Type 2).
- **Goitre:** Enlarged thyroid due to iodine deficiency; causes hypothyroidism.
- **Cretinism:** Hypothyroidism in childhood — stunted growth, mental retardation.
- **Myxoedema:** Hypothyroidism in adults — lethargy, weight gain.
- **Gigantism/Acromegaly:** Excess GH (before/after puberty).
- **Dwarfism:** Deficient GH in childhood.
- **Cushing's disease:** Excess cortisol from adrenal gland.

### 1.11 Nutrition and Balanced Diet

A **balanced diet** contains all essential nutrients in the right proportions: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water, and roughage (fibre) [General Studies Reference, p. 72].

**Macronutrients:** Carbohydrates (energy), proteins (growth and repair), fats (energy storage, insulation).
**Micronutrients:** Vitamins (organic, in small amounts), minerals (inorganic, in small amounts).

**Vitamins:**

| Vitamin | Source | Deficiency disease |
|---------|--------|---------------------|
| A (Retinol) | Carrot, fish oil, milk | Night blindness, xerophthalmia |
| B1 (Thiamine) | Whole grains, nuts | Beriberi |
| B2 (Riboflavin) | Milk, eggs, green vegetables | Cheilosis, glossitis |
| B3 (Niacin) | Meat, fish, peanuts | Pellagra |
| B12 | Meat, fish, dairy | Anaemia |
| C (Ascorbic acid) | Citrus fruits, tomatoes | Scurvy |
| D (Calciferol) | Sunlight, fish, egg yolk | Rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults) |
| E (Tocopherol) | Vegetable oils, nuts, green leafy | Sterility, muscular weakness |
| K (Phylloquinone) | Green leafy vegetables | Slow blood clotting |

**Minerals:**

- **Calcium:** Milk, cheese, leafy vegetables. Deficiency → rickets, poor teeth.
- **Iron:** Spinach, jaggery, liver. Deficiency → anaemia.
- **Iodine:** Iodised salt, fish. Deficiency → goitre.
- **Phosphorus:** Milk, fish, nuts. Important for bones and teeth.

### 1.12 Diseases

A **disease** is a condition that impairs the normal functioning of the body [General Studies Reference, p. 187-201].

**Types of diseases:**

#### (a) Infectious (Communicable) Diseases
Caused by pathogens (microorganisms).

**Pathogens:**
- **Viruses:** Smallest pathogens; replicate inside host cells.
- **Bacteria:** Single-celled organisms with cell walls.
- **Fungi:** Eukaryotic; cause fungal infections.
- **Protozoa:** Single-celled eukaryotes; cause diseases like malaria.
- **Helminths (worms):** Cause worm infections.

**Examples of infectious diseases:**

| Disease | Pathogen | Transmission | Symptoms |
|---------|----------|--------------|----------|
| Common cold | Rhinovirus | Droplet/airborne | Runny nose, sneezing |
| Flu (Influenza) | Influenza virus | Droplet/airborne | Fever, body ache |
| COVID-19 | SARS-CoV-2 | Droplet/airborne | Fever, cough, breathlessness |
| Tuberculosis (TB) | Mycobacterium tuberculosis | Droplet/airborne | Persistent cough, weight loss |
| Cholera | Vibrio cholerae | Contaminated water | Severe diarrhoea, dehydration |
| Typhoid | Salmonella typhi | Contaminated food/water | High fever, abdominal pain |
| Malaria | Plasmodium | Female Anopheles mosquito | High fever with chills, shivering |
| Dengue | Dengue virus | Aedes mosquito | High fever, joint pain, rash |
| AIDS | HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) | Body fluids, sexual contact | Weak immunity, opportunistic infections |
| Hepatitis B | Hepatitis B virus | Body fluids | Jaundice, liver damage |
| Polio | Poliovirus | Contaminated water/food | Paralysis |
| Rabies | Rabies virus | Animal bite (dog) | Fear of water, paralysis |
| Tetanus | Clostridium tetani | Wound contamination | Muscle stiffness, lockjaw |
| Diphtheria | Corynebacterium diphtheriae | Droplet | Sore throat, breathing difficulty |
| Whooping cough | Bordetella pertussis | Droplet | Severe cough |
| Pneumonia | Streptococcus pneumoniae | Droplet/airborne | Cough, fever, breathlessness |
| Ringworm | Dermatophyte fungi | Direct contact | Ring-shaped skin lesions |
| Athlete's foot | Fungi | Contact with contaminated surfaces | Itchy, scaly feet |
| Amoebiasis | Entamoeba histolytica | Contaminated food/water | Dysentery |

[General Studies Reference, p. 187-201]

**Prevention of infectious diseases:**
- Personal hygiene.
- Vaccination.
- Proper sanitation and clean drinking water.
- Vector control (mosquito nets, insecticides).
- Avoiding contact with infected persons.

#### (b) Non-Infectious (Non-Communicable) Diseases
- **Deficiency diseases:** Caused by lack of nutrients (e.g., scurvy, rickets, anaemia, goitre, beriberi).
- **Genetic diseases:** Caused by defective genes (e.g., haemophilia, sickle cell anaemia, colour blindness).
- **Degenerative diseases:** Due to ageing or organ damage (e.g., arthritis, heart disease, diabetes).
- **Cancer:** Uncontrolled cell growth.

### 1.13 Cancer

**Cancer** is a disease caused by uncontrolled, abnormal cell growth. It can be caused by [General Studies Reference, p. 201]:
- **Carcinogens** (cancer-causing agents): tobacco, UV radiation, certain chemicals, viruses.
- **Genetic factors** (oncogenes).
- **Radiation** (X-rays, gamma rays).
- **Lifestyle factors** (smoking, alcohol, poor diet).

**Treatment:** Surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy.

### 1.14 Antibiotics and Vaccines

**Antibiotics** are drugs that kill or inhibit the growth of bacteria. They are produced by microorganisms (fungi, bacteria). Examples: penicillin, streptomycin, tetracycline. **Antibiotics do NOT work against viruses**.

**Vaccines** are biological preparations that provide immunity against specific diseases. They contain weakened or killed pathogens, or their antigens. Vaccines stimulate the body to produce antibodies. Examples: polio vaccine, BCG (TB), DPT (diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus), MMR (measles, mumps, rubella).

### 1.15 Drug Abuse

**Drug abuse** is the use of drugs for non-medical purposes, leading to addiction and harm [General Studies Reference, p. 199-201].

**Commonly abused substances:**
- **Alcohol:** Legal but addictive; damages liver, brain.
- **Tobacco:** Contains nicotine (addictive), tar (carcinogen), CO.
- **Cannabis (marijuana, hashish):** Psychoactive; impairs judgment.
- **Cocaine, heroin, LSD:** Highly addictive; dangerous.
- **Amphetamines, sedatives:** Prescription drugs, abused.

**Effects of drug abuse:**
- Addiction (physical and psychological dependence).
- Mental disorders, depression.
- Organ damage (liver cirrhosis from alcohol, lung cancer from tobacco).
- Social problems, family disruption, financial ruin.
- Risk of overdose and death.
- Spread of HIV/AIDS (through shared needles).

**Prevention:** Education, awareness, family support, de-addiction programmes, counselling.

## 3. Everyday Science & Applications

**Q1: Why do we withdraw our hand when we touch a hot object?**
A: This is a reflex action. The skin receptors detect heat; signals travel through sensory neurons to the spinal cord; motor neurons signal the muscles to contract → hand withdrawn. The brain is not involved; the response is instant.

**Q2: Why is the brain protected by the skull?**
A: The skull is a hard, bony covering that protects the brain from mechanical injury.

**Q3: Why is iodine added to common salt?**
A: Iodine is essential for the production of thyroxine by the thyroid gland. Iodine deficiency causes goitre. Adding iodine to salt prevents this deficiency.

**Q4: Why is insulin given to diabetic patients?**
A: Diabetic patients cannot produce enough insulin. Insulin helps cells absorb glucose from blood, lowering blood sugar.

**Q5: Why do we get tears when chopping onions?**
A: Chopping onions releases a chemical (sulfoxide) that irritates the eyes, causing the tear glands to produce tears. (Reference)

**Q6: Why is it not advisable to sleep under a tree at night?**
A: Trees release CO₂ at night (during respiration), reducing the O₂ level nearby. So sleeping under a tree at night may lead to a slight O₂ deficiency.

**Q7: Why does adrenaline make the heart beat faster?**
A: Adrenaline is the "fight or flight" hormone, released in response to stress or fear. It increases heart rate, blood pressure, and energy supply to muscles to prepare for action.

**Q8: Why are antibiotics not effective against viral infections like the common cold?**
A: Antibiotics target bacterial-specific processes (cell wall, ribosomes). Viruses lack these structures and replicate inside host cells, so antibiotics cannot selectively kill them.

**Q9: Why is vaccination important?**
A: Vaccines stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies against specific pathogens, providing immunity without causing the disease. This prevents future infections.

**Q10: Why does the doctor advise a person to take iron-rich food?**
A: Iron is needed for haemoglobin in red blood cells, which carries oxygen. Iron deficiency leads to anaemia. So iron-rich food prevents or treats anaemia.

**Q11: Why does a person become addicted to drugs?**
A: Drugs stimulate the release of dopamine (a pleasure chemical) in the brain, creating a feeling of pleasure. Repeated use causes the brain to depend on the drug, leading to addiction.

**Q12: Why is breast-feeding important for newborns?**
A: Breast milk contains colostrum (rich in antibodies), ideal nutrition, and protects against infections. It also strengthens the mother-child bond.

**Q13: Why is tobacco smoking harmful?**
A: Tobacco contains nicotine (addictive), tar (carcinogen), and CO. Smoking causes lung cancer, heart disease, bronchitis, and emphysema.

**Q14: Why do plants bend towards light?**
A: Auxin hormone is distributed to the shaded side of the plant, promoting cell elongation on that side. The shaded side grows faster, causing the plant to bend towards light (phototropism).

**Q15: Why are fruits ripened using ethylene?**
A: Ethylene is a natural plant hormone that promotes fruit ripening. Commercial fruit ripening uses ethylene gas.

**Q16: Why is sunlight important for vitamin D synthesis?**
A: Sunlight converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin to vitamin D, essential for calcium absorption and bone health.

**Q17: Why do some people take iodine supplements?**
A: Iodine is essential for thyroxine production. Iodine deficiency causes goitre. Iodine supplements prevent this.

**Q18: Why is roughage (fibre) important in the diet?**
A: Roughage adds bulk to food, helps peristalsis, prevents constipation, and reduces the risk of colon cancer.

**Q19: Why is breast cancer screening important?**
A: Early detection of breast cancer greatly improves treatment success. Regular self-examination and mammograms are recommended for women, especially after 40.

**Q20: Why are pesticides used in agriculture?**
A: Pesticides kill pests (insects, fungi, weeds) that damage crops, increasing yield. However, they can be harmful to the environment and humans.
