# Chapter 8: International Finance & Financial Markets

> **Chapter Overview:** International Finance and Financial Markets are **important topics in banking and SSC exams** — typically 2-4 questions on International Financial Institutions (IMF, World Bank, ADB, AIIB, NDB, FATF, BIS) and 1-2 questions on capital markets (BSE, NSE, SEBI). This chapter covers major international financial institutions, their structure, key personnel, India's role, and Indian financial markets.

## 1. International Monetary Fund (IMF)

### 1.1 Key Facts

- **Founded:** 22 July 1944 (Bretton Woods Conference)
- **Headquarters:** 700 19th Street NW, **Washington, D.C., U.S.**
- **Members:** 191 countries (190 UN members + Kosovo)
- **Current Managing Director:** **Kristalina Georgieva** (Bulgaria)
- **First Deputy MD:** Dan Katz
- **Chief Economist:** Pierre-Olivier Gourinchas

### 1.2 Special Drawing Rights (SDR)

- International reserve asset
- Basket of **5 currencies**: USD, EUR, RMB (Chinese Yuan), JPY, GBP
- Reviewed every 5 years (last review 2022)
- Weights (2022 review): USD 43.38%, EUR 29.31%, RMB 12.28%, JPY 7.59%, GBP 7.44%

### 1.3 Quota System

- Determines voting power and SDR allocations
- **India's rank:** Top 10 quota holder (around 8th position); ~2.75% quota

### 1.4 Main Purposes

- Promote international monetary co-operation
- Facilitate international trade
- Foster economic growth and high employment
- Provide temporary financial assistance to members for BoP
- Prevent financial crises

## 2. World Bank Group

### 2.1 Overview

- **Founded:** 1944 (Bretton Woods)
- **Headquarters:** Washington D.C.
- 5 institutions:

| Institution | Full Form | Year Est. | Purpose |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **IBRD** | International Bank for Reconstruction and Development | 1944 | Loans to middle-income and creditworthy low-income countries |
| **IDA** | International Development Association | 1960 | Concessional loans/grants to poorest countries |
| **IFC** | International Finance Corporation | 1956 | Private sector investment in developing countries |
| **MIGA** | Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency | 1988 | Political risk insurance |
| **ICSID** | International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes | 1966 | Investor-state dispute settlement |

### 2.2 Key Personnel

- **World Bank President** (current): Ajay Banga (since June 2023)
- **IDA** is "World Bank's window for the poorest"

## 3. Asian Development Bank (ADB)

### 3.1 Key Facts

- **Established:** 19 December 1966
- **Headquarters:** 6 ADB Avenue, Mandaluyong, **Metro Manila, Philippines**
- **Members:** 69 countries
- **Current President:** **Masato Kanda** (from 24 February 2025)
- **Tradition:** President always Japanese
- **India share:** 6.317% (as of 31 December 2020)

### 3.2 Aim

Reduce poverty in Asia-Pacific through inclusive economic growth, environmentally sustainable growth, and regional integration.

## 4. Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)

- **Established:** 25 December 2015 (operations); proposed by China in 2013
- **Headquarters:** Beijing, China
- India is a founding member
- Members: 100+ countries
- First President: Jin Liqun
- Focus: Infrastructure development in Asia

---

## 5. New Development Bank (NDB / BRICS Bank)

### 5.1 Key Facts

- **Treaty signed:** July 2014 (Fortaleza, Brazil)
- **Treaty in force:** July 2015
- **Headquarters:** Shanghai, China
- **Current President:** **Dilma Rousseff** (since 24 March 2023)
- **First President:** K. V. Kamath (India)
- **Initial authorized capital:** US$100 billion
- **Initial subscribed capital:** US$50 billion (US$10 billion paid-in, US$40 billion callable)

### 5.2 Members

- **Founding members (5):** Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa (BRICS)
- **Each founding member:** Equal share (20% initially; now 18.76% each due to new members)
- **New members:** Bangladesh, UAE, Uruguay (2021), Egypt (2021/23), Algeria (2024), Colombia, Ethiopia, Uzbekistan (2025)
- **BRICS share cannot fall below 55%** of total voting power
- **India's regional office:** Ahmedabad, Gujarat (May 2022)

## 6. FATF — Financial Action Task Force

### 6.1 Key Facts

- **Founded:** 1989 (G7 Paris Summit)
- **Headquarters:** Paris, France
- Members: 40 jurisdictions (39 countries + 2 regional organisations: EU, GCC)
- India is a member
- Sets standards (**40 Recommendations**) to prevent money laundering, terrorist financing

### 6.2 FATF Lists

- **Grey list:** Countries deficient in AML/CFT (Countering Financing of Terrorism)
- **Black list:** High-risk jurisdictions
- **White list:** Compliant jurisdictions

### 6.3 FATF Current President (2024-2026)

- T. Raja Kumar (Singapore) — 2022-2024
- Elisa de Anda Madrazo (Mexico) — 2024-2026

## 9. Indian Capital Markets

### 9.1 SEBI — Securities and Exchange Board of India

- **Non-statutory body:** Constituted 12 April 1988 (Government resolution)
- **Statutory body:** SEBI Act 1992; provisions came into force **30 January 1992**
- **Headquarters:** Mumbai
- **Preamble:** "...to protect the interests of investors in securities and to promote the development of, and to regulate the securities market and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto"

### 9.2 Stock Exchanges

- **BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange):** Established 1875 (Asia's oldest); Sensex (30 companies)
- **NSE (National Stock Exchange of India):** Established 1992; began trading in 1994; Nifty 50 index

### 9.3 Major Indices

- **Sensex:** 30 stocks on BSE (BSE Sensitive Index)
- **Nifty 50:** 50 stocks on NSE (National Stock Exchange Fifty)
- **Bank Nifty:** Banking sector index
- **Nifty IT:** IT sector index
- **India VIX:** Volatility index

### 9.4 Issue Types

- **IPO (Initial Public Offering):** First time a company offers shares to public
- **FPO (Follow-on Public Offering):** Already listed company issuing more shares
- **OFS (Offer for Sale):** Promoters selling shares to public
- **Rights Issue:** Existing shareholders given right to buy more shares
- **Bonus Issue:** Free shares to existing shareholders
- **QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement):** To institutional investors only

## 10. Mutual Funds in India

### 10.1 Regulating Body

- **SEBI** (SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996)
- **AMFI (Association of Mutual Funds in India):** Industry body

### 10.2 Types of Mutual Funds

1. **Equity Funds** — Growth, Value, Sectoral, Thematic
2. **Debt Funds** — Liquid, Ultra-short, Short-term, Corporate Bond, Gilt
3. **Hybrid Funds** — Balanced, Equity-oriented
4. **Solution-Oriented Funds** — Retirement, Children's funds
5. **Index Funds** — Track specific index (Nifty, Sensex)
6. **ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds)** — Listed on exchanges
7. **Fund of Funds (FoF)** — Invests in other MFs

### 10.3 Categories by Structure

- **Open-ended:** Continuous subscription/redemption
- **Close-ended:** Fixed tenure, listed
- **Interval:** Periodic subscription/redemption

## 12. Derivatives

- **Forwards:** OTC, customized, no margin
- **Futures:** Standardized, exchange-traded, daily settlement
- **Options:** Right but not obligation; **Call (right to buy) / Put (right to sell)**
- **Swaps:** OTC; exchange of cash flows; e.g., Interest Rate Swap, Currency Swap, Credit Default Swap

---

## 13. Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999

- Replaced FERA, 1973
- Administered by RBI (Department of External Investments and Operations)
- Applies to all foreign exchange transactions in India
- Penalty for contravention: Up to 3x the sum involved
- Adjudicating authority: Enforcement Directorate (for serious cases)
