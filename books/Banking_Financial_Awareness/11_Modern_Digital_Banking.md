## 1. Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) — e₹

### 1.1 Concept

CBDC is the **digital form of legal tender** issued by the central bank. India's CBDC is called **e₹ (Digital Rupee)**.

### 1.2 RBI's CBDC Pilot (Launched Dec 1, 2022)

| Phase | Date | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesale segment | Nov 1, 2022 | Settlement of secondary market G-Sec transactions |
| Retail pilot | Dec 1, 2022 | 4 banks, 4 cities |
| Expanded retail | Multiple stages | More banks, more cities |

### 1.3 Two Models of CBDC

| Model | Description | India's Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct (single-tier)** | RBI handles distribution directly | Not adopted |
| **Indirect (two-tier)** | RBI issues to banks; banks distribute to public | **Adopted by India** |
| **Hybrid** | RBI issues, but handles some distribution directly | Future possibility |

### 1.4 e₹ Usage Models

- **e₹-W**: Wholesale CBDC (banks use for settlement)
- **e₹-R**: Retail CBDC (public uses for transactions)
- **Token-based** vs **Account-based**:
  - Token: Like cash; anonymous (used for e₹-R pilot)
  - Account: Identified; lower anonymity

### 1.5 Key Features

- Issued by RBI (liability of RBI)
- Accepted as legal tender
- Convertible 1:1 with physical cash
- No interest earned on holdings
- Available in ₹1 to ₹100 denominations initially

### 1.6 Cross-border CBDC

- **Project mBridge** (BIS Innovation Hub): India, China, UAE, Hong Kong SAR
- Goal: Cross-border interbank settlements using CBDC
- Reduces dependence on USD correspondent banking

## 2. UPI 2.0 and Advanced Features

### 2.1 UPI Evolution

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 2016 | UPI launched by NPCI |
| 2019 | UPI 2.0 (overdraft, credit lines) |
| 2020 | UPI crossed 1 billion/month transactions |
| 2021 | UPI Lite (small-value, no PIN) |
| 2022 | UPI 123PAY (feature phones); Credit Line on UPI |
| 2023 | UPI for NRI; UPI X (offline) |
| 2024 | Cross-border expansion to multiple countries |
| 2025 | UPI for NPCI's Tokenization Network; ChatGPT integrations |

### 2.2 UPI Statistics (Mid-2025)

- **Monthly transactions**: 12+ billion
- **Annual value**: ₹200+ lakh crore
- **Banks live on UPI**: 600+
- **Apps live**: 500+
- **Merchants**: 100+ million

### 2.3 Key Features of UPI 2.0+

- **UPI Lite**: Small-value (< ₹500) without PIN
- **UPI 123PAY**: For feature phones (works via IVR)
- **Credit Line on UPI**: Borrow through UPI app
- **UPI One-time mandate**: Pre-authorize future payments
- **UPI X (Offline)**: Pay without internet (Bluetooth/NFC based)
- **UPI LITE X**: Offline payments up to ₹500
- **UPI Tap & Pay**: NFC-based contactless payments
- **UPI on ChatGPT/AI**: Conversational UPI payments

## 3. Account Aggregator (AA) Framework

### 3.1 What is AA?

AA is a RBI-licensed entity that **collects and consolidates financial information** of customers from multiple Financial Information Providers (FIPs) and provides it to Financial Information Users (FIUs) with customer consent.

### 3.2 Key Players (8 AAs licensed by RBI as of 2025)

- **Cookiejar Technologies** (Perfios)
- **Fintech Labs and Technologies** (FinBox)
- **OneMoney** (by FinSecutech)
- **CRIF High Mark** (CRIF)
- **CAMS Finserv**
- **Saafe Analytix**
- **FinPrim AA** (by Yodaplus)
- **Protean eGov Technologies**

### 3.3 Use Cases

- **Personal Finance Management (PFM)**: Consolidated view of all bank/investment accounts
- **Loan underwriting**: Lenders can assess full financial picture
- **Wealth advisory**: Better personalized advice
- **Tax filing**: Auto-populate income data

### 3.4 Consent Mechanism

- **Data Linkage**: Through customer's mobile number/PAN (not account number)
- **Consent Artefact**: AA-FIP standard (RBI mandated)
- **Consent Manager**: Validates user consent before sharing data

## 4. Open Banking & Fintech Regulations

### 4.1 Open Banking

Open Banking allows third-party financial service providers to access consumer banking data via APIs (with consent).

### 4.2 Account Aggregator (AA) = India's Open Banking Implementation

- Launched under RBI's Master Direction on NBFC-AA (Sept 2016, operational 2021)
- Inspired by UK's Open Banking (CMA 2017)

### 4.3 Key Fintech Regulations (RBI)

| Year | Regulation | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 | NBFC-AA Master Direction | Account Aggregators |
| 2018 | Peer-to-Peer Lending | P2P platforms |
| 2020 | Digital Lending Guidelines | First comprehensive digital lending framework |
| 2022 | Digital Lending Updates | Data privacy, FLDG (First Loss Default Guarantee) |
| 2023 | Digital Lending Analytics | AI/ML in credit decisions |
| 2024 | Digital Lending — Additional | Tightening on disclosure, recovery practices |
| 2025 | Digital Lending Norms | FLDG cap reduced to 5% of loan for digital lenders |

### 4.4 Digital Lending Framework (RBI, 2020)

Key provisions:
- All digital loans must be on a **Digital Lending App (DLA)** with disclosure
- **Data privacy**: Only data needed for credit assessment
- **Recovery**: Only through authorized channels
- **Disclosure**: All charges, APR must be disclosed upfront
- **Lenders must be RBI regulated**

### 4.5 Recommended Practice (2024 Update)

- **First Loss Default Guarantee (FLDG)** cap: 5% of loan amount (down from earlier higher limits)
- Disclosure of all fees
- Cooling-off period for borrowers
- Data protection (per DPDP Act 2023)

## 8. Cybersecurity in Banking

### 8.1 RBI Cybersecurity Framework (2016 + updates)

- **Cyber Security Framework in Banks** (2016): Mandatory
- **Cyber Crisis Management Plan**: For major incidents
- **CERT-In**: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (under MeitY)

### 8.2 Key Cyber Threats

- **Phishing**: Fraudulent emails/links
- **Vishing**: Voice-based phishing
- **Smishing**: SMS-based phishing
- **Malware**: Software attacks
- **Ransomware**: Encrypts data for ransom
- **DDoS**: Distributed denial of service
- **SIM Swap**: Hijacking mobile number

### 8.3 Cyber Security Measures

- **Two-factor authentication** (mandatory for digital transactions)
- **Encryption**: End-to-end for sensitive data
- **RBI guidelines**: Cyber audit, SOC (Security Operations Center)
- **Data Localization**: Critical financial data to be stored in India

## 9. Practice Questions (15 PYQs)

**Q1.** India's CBDC is called:
(a) Digital Rupee (e₹) (b) eRupi (c) Digital INR (d) Bharat Coin
**Answer**: (a) Digital Rupee (e₹); pilot started Dec 1, 2022

**Q2.** UPI 2.0 features include:
(a) Credit Line on UPI (b) UPI Lite (c) Offline payments (d) All of the above
**Answer**: (d) All of the above

**Q3.** Account Aggregators are licensed by:
(a) SEBI (b) RBI (c) IRDAI (d) PFRDA
**Answer**: (b) RBI (under NBFC-AA Master Direction 2016)

**Q4.** India's first CBDC pilot was launched in:
(a) 2016 (b) 2020 (c) 2022 (d) 2024
**Answer**: (c) Dec 1, 2022 (retail); Nov 1, 2022 (wholesale)

**Q5.** Project mBridge involves:
(a) India, China, UAE, Hong Kong (b) India, US, Japan (c) India, UK, Singapore (d) Only India
**Answer**: (a) India, China (now), UAE, Hong Kong SAR (cross-border CBDC pilot)

**Q6.** FLDG cap for digital lenders (RBI 2025):
(a) 5% (b) 10% (c) 15% (d) 20%
**Answer**: (a) 5% of loan amount

**Q7.** UPI crossed 12 billion transactions/month in:
(a) 2023 (b) 2024 (c) 2025 (d) 2026
**Answer**: (c) 2025

**Q8.** Account Aggregator framework operational in India since:
(a) 2018 (b) 2020 (c) 2021 (d) 2022
**Answer**: (c) 2021 (Master Direction 2016; operational in 2021)

**Q9.** Which model of CBDC has India adopted?
(a) Direct (single-tier) (b) Indirect (two-tier) (c) Hybrid (d) None
**Answer**: (b) Indirect (two-tier); RBI issues to banks, banks distribute

**Q10.** UPI international expansion includes all except:
(a) UAE (b) Singapore (c) Japan (d) France
**Answer**: (c) Japan (not yet operational as of 2025)

**Q11.** "Neo-banks" are:
(a) Fully digital banks (b) Cooperative banks (b) Foreign banks (d) Old banks
**Answer**: (a) Fully digital-only banks (no physical branches)

**Q12.** CERT-In stands for:
(a) Computer Emergency Response Team - India (b) Central Electronic Reserve Team (c) Cyber Expert Response Team (d) None
**Answer**: (a) Computer Emergency Response Team - India (under MeitY)

**Q13.** First Loss Default Guarantee (FLDG) is provided by:
(a) Borrower (b) Lender (c) Digital Lending Platform (DLA) (d) RBI
**Answer**: (c) Digital Lending Platform (DLA) bears first loss on default

**Q14.** Open Banking in India is implemented through:
(a) Direct RBI API (b) Account Aggregators (c) Banks' own APIs (d) NPCI
**Answer**: (b) Account Aggregators (AA framework operationalizes Open Banking)

**Q15.** CBDC retail pilot was launched with how many banks initially:
(a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 6 (d) 8
**Answer**: (b) 4 banks (SBI, ICICI, Yes Bank, IDFC First Bank) initially
