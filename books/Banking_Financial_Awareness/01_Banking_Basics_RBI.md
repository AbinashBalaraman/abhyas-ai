## 1. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) — Establishment and History

### 1.1 Establishment

- **Established:** 1 April 1935 under the **Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934**
- **Recommendation:** Based on the **Hilton Young Commission** (1926 Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance)
- **Initial share capital:** ₹5 crore; shares of ₹100 each, fully paid
- **First Governor:** Sir Osborne Smith (1935)
- **First Indian Governor:** C. D. Deshmukh (1943-1949)
- **Headquarters:** Originally Calcutta; moved to **Mumbai in 1937**
- **Nationalization:** 1 January 1949 (fully owned by Ministry of Finance, Government of India since then)

### 1.2 RBI Act 1934 — Preamble

"...to regulate the issue of Bank notes and keeping of reserves with a view to securing monetary stability in India... to maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth." (amended 2016)

### 1.3 Central Board of Directors (as on 18 June 2026)

- **21 members**: 1 Governor, 4 Deputy Governors, 2 Finance Ministry representatives, 10 government-nominated directors, 4 directors representing local boards
- **Current Governor (as of June 2026):** **Sanjay Malhotra, IAS** (since 11 December 2024)
- **Deputy Governors (4):** Swaminathan Janakiraman, Poonam Gupta, Shirish Chandra Murmu, Rohit Jain
- **Nominated on RBI Central Board (June 2026):** Shri Sanjay Lohiya, IAS, Secretary, Department of Financial Services

### 1.4 Zonal/Regional Offices

- Four regional representations: **North (New Delhi), South (Chennai), East (Kolkata), West (Mumbai)**
- Each regional board has 5 members

## 2. Core Functions of RBI

RBI is India's central bank — the banking regulatory authority and monetary policy issuer. The traditional 6 functions are:

1. **Monetary Authority** — formulates and implements monetary policy
2. **Issuer of Currency** — issues and manages currency notes (via **Bharatiya Reserve Bank Note Mudran - BRBNM**); coins by Government of India
3. **Banker to the Government** — manages government accounts, handles public debt
4. **Banker's Bank** — lender of last resort, accepts deposits from & lends to commercial banks
5. **Regulator of the Banking System** — regulates and supervises banks, UCBs, NBFCs
6. **Manager of Foreign Exchange** — manages forex reserves, administers FEMA 1999
7. **Regulator/Supervisor of Payment and Settlement Systems**

### 2.1 Subsidiaries of RBI

- **Bharatiya Reserve Bank Note Mudran (BRBNM)** — prints banknotes; presses in Mysore (Karnataka) & Salboni (West Bengal)
- **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC)** — deposit insurance (est. 1961)
- **Reserve Bank of India Information Technology (ReBIT)**
- **Indian Financial Technology and Allied Services (IFTAS)**
- **Reserve Bank of India Innovation Hub**
- **Indira Gandhi Institute of Development Research (IGIDR)**

## 3. Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)

### 3.1 Statutory Basis

- RBI Act, 1934 amended in **May 2016** (Section 45ZA, 45ZB)
- **First MPC constituted:** 29 September 2016

### 3.2 Composition (6 Members)

1. RBI Governor (Chairperson, ex officio)
2. RBI Deputy Governor in charge of Monetary Policy
3. One RBI officer nominated by Central Board
4–6. Three external members appointed by Central Government

### 3.4 Voting and Quorum

- **Term of external members:** 4 years or until further orders
- **Quorum:** 4 members required
- **Voting:** Each member has one vote; in case of equality, Governor has casting vote
- **Meetings:** At least 4 per year; minutes published 14 days after each meeting

## 4. Inflation Targeting Framework (Flexible Inflation Targeting - FIT)

- **Current target:** **4% CPI inflation**
- **Tolerance band:** **2% (lower) to 6% (upper)**
- **Latest review:** 25 March 2026 — Central Government retained the target for **April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2031**
- **Operating target:** Weighted Average Call Rate (WACR) aligned with policy repo rate
- **Failure trigger:** Average inflation >6% or <2% for any three consecutive quarters

## 4A. Modern Digital Payments Ecosystem (UPI, CBDC, etc.)

### 4A.1 Unified Payments Interface (UPI)

**UPI** is India's real-time payment system developed by the **National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)**. Launched on **11 April 2016** with 21 banks.

**Key Features:**
- **Real-time** 24/7/365 payments.
- **Mobile-based** (works on smartphones).
- **Interoperable** across banks.
- **Free for personal transactions** (P2P); small MDR for merchants.
- Uses **VPA (Virtual Payment Address)** like `name@bankname`.

**Volume & Value (2025):**
- **~12+ billion transactions per month** (world's highest real-time payment volume).
- **Annual value:** Over ₹200 lakh crore (₹200 trillion) processed in 2024-25.

**UPI Components:**
- **PSP (Payment Service Provider):** Banks (e.g., SBI, HDFC, ICICI).
- **TPAP (Third-Party Application Provider):** Apps like Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm.
- **NPCI:** Umbrella organization for retail payments.
- **BHIM:** Bharat Interface for Money — government UPI app.

**UPI Innovations (2024-2025):**
- **UPI Lite** — wallet-based small payments (no PIN, ≤₹500).
- **UPI 123PAY** — UPI for feature phones (works without internet via IVR/USSD).
- **UPI Circle** — delegated payments (parent → child).
- **Credit Line on UPI (September 2025)** — RBI allowed credit lines via UPI; users can now pay via pre-approved credit lines.
- **UPI for NRIs** — using international mobile numbers.
- **UPI at International locations** — UAE, Singapore, France, Sri Lanka, Bhutan, Mauritius, Nepal (2024-2025).
- **30% volume cap for third-party apps** — extended deadline (was originally Dec 2024; extended).
- **India's first credit card combining RuPay, Visa, and UPI** — 2025 (allows viewing both credit and UPI transactions in one place).
- **UPI-integrated ATMs** — for cash withdrawals.

### 4A.2 Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) — Digital Rupee (e₹)

**CBDC** is a digital form of fiat currency issued by the RBI. In India, it is called the **Digital Rupee** or **e₹**.

**Pilot launches:**
- **Retail segment (e₹-R):** 1 December 2022.
- **Wholesale segment (e₹-W):** 1 November 2022.

**Two Models:**
- **Direct (Single):** RBI issues directly to consumers (no intermediary).
- **Indirect (Two-tier):** RBI issues to banks/financial intermediaries; they distribute to consumers (currently used).

**Two Forms:**
- **Token-based:** Like physical cash, value-dependent on token.
- **Account-based:** Like bank deposits, value tied to account.

**Current Status (2025-2026):**
- **Programmable e₹** being piloted for specific use cases.
- Use cases tested: **e₹ for fuel**, **e₹ for agriculture input subsidies**, **e₹ for government securities** (wholesale).
- RBI exploring interoperability with UPI.

### 4A.3 Other Modern Payment Systems

| System | Full Form | Launch | Operator |
|--------|-----------|--------|----------|
| **IMPS** | Immediate Payment Service | 2010 | NPCI |
| **NEFT** | National Electronic Funds Transfer | 2004 | RBI |
| **RTGS** | Real Time Gross Settlement | 2004 | RBI |
| **AEPS** | Aadhaar Enabled Payment System | 2010 | NPCI |
| **NACH** | National Automated Clearing House | 2012 | NPCI |
| **CTS** | Cheque Truncation System | 2010 | RBI |

**Key difference between NEFT, RTGS, IMPS:**
- **NEFT:** Batch-based; half-hourly settlement; no minimum amount.
- **RTGS:** Real-time gross settlement; minimum ₹2 lakh; high-value.
- **IMPS:** 24/7 instant; max ₹5 lakh; used for small value.

### 4A.4 Aadhaar-Enabled Payment System (AEPS)

- **Use:** Aadhaar-based payments using biometric authentication.
- **Use cases:** Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT), MNREGA wages, PDS, pension.
- **Services:** Cash withdrawal, balance enquiry, mini statement, Aadhaar-to-Aadhaar transfer.

### 4A.7 Detailed UPI Working Mechanism

**How UPI Works (Step-by-Step):**

1. **User initiates payment** on UPI app (e.g., Google Pay) by entering VPA or scanning QR code.
2. **App sends request** to PSP (bank server) of sender.
3. **PSP authenticates** user via UPI PIN (or biometric).
4. **PSP sends request** to NPCI's UPI switch (central infrastructure).
5. **NPCI validates** VPA and routes request to receiver's PSP.
6. **Receiver's PSP debits** amount from receiver (if incoming), confirms to sender's PSP.
7. **Sender's PSP debits** amount from sender's account.
8. **Confirmation** sent back through NPCI to both PSPs.
9. **Both users see** "Payment Successful" message.

**Key Components of UPI Architecture:**
- **UPI App (TPAP):** Front-end user interface (Google Pay, PhonePe, etc.).
- **PSP (Payment Service Provider):** Bank backend (HDFC, SBI, etc.).
- **NPCI Switch:** Central routing infrastructure.
- **UPI PIN:** 4-6 digit PIN for authentication.
- **VPA (Virtual Payment Address):** Unique ID like `name@bankname`.
- **MMID (Mobile Money Identifier):** Alternative to VPA.

**UPI Transaction Limits (as of 2025-2026):**
- **Per-transaction limit:** ₹1,00,000 (₹1 lakh).
- **Hospital/Travel/Merchant limit:** ₹5,00,000 (₹5 lakh).
- **Specific categories (Insurance, Capital Markets):** ₹2,00,000 (₹2 lakh).
- **UPI Lite limit:** ₹500 per transaction, max ₹5,000 balance.
- **123PAY limit:** ₹5,000 per transaction (limited use).

**UPI vs IMPS:**
| Feature | UPI | IMPS |
|---------|-----|------|
| Real-time | Yes | Yes |
| 24/7 | Yes | Yes |
| Authentication | UPI PIN | MMID + MPIN |
| VPA support | Yes | No |
| Max amount | ₹1 lakh (most) | ₹5 lakh |
| Interoperability | Yes (any VPA) | Yes |

### 4A.8 Digital Lending Ecosystem

**Types of Digital Lending:**
- **P2P Lending:** Platforms connect individual lenders and borrowers.
- **Lending through NBFCs:** Digital loans via fintech partnerships.
- **Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL):** Short-term credit at point of sale.
- **Pay Later Products:** Credit lines on UPI (introduced Sept 2025).
- **MSME Lending:** Digital loans for small businesses.
- **Agricultural Lending:** Digital agri-credit platforms.

**RBI Digital Lending Guidelines (2022):**
- All digital lending must be via regulated entities.
- Mandatory disclosure of all fees.
- Cooling-off period for borrowers.
- Data privacy and consent requirements.
- Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) norms.

**Digital Lending Apps (DLAs):** Apps must be linked to a regulated entity (NBFC/Bank).

### 4A.9 Account Aggregator (AA) Framework

**AA** is a financial data-sharing platform regulated by RBI.

**Key Features:**
- **Consent-based:** User explicitly consents to share data.
- **Financial Information Providers (FIPs):** Banks, NBFCs, mutual funds (share data).
- **Financial Information Users (FIUs):** Lenders, financial apps (request data).
- **Account Aggregators:** Intermediaries (e.g., Finvu, CAMSRep, OneMoney).

**Use Case:** Borrower shares bank statement directly with lender via AA, avoiding manual document submission.

### 4A.10 Open Credit Enablement Network (OCEN)

**OCEN** is a protocol/framework for digital lending, especially for small businesses.

**Key Components:**
- **Loan Service Providers (LSPs):** Platforms connecting borrowers to lenders.
- **Integrators:** Tech providers.
- **Lenders:** Banks/NBFCs funding the loans.
- **Government Registries:** GST, MCA, Udyam for verification.

**Use Case:** A small shopkeeper can get instant working capital loan via OCEN-enabled apps.

## 5. Policy Rates

### 5.1 The LAF Corridor

The Liquidity Adjustment Facility corridor has:
- **MSF** (Marginal Standing Facility) as **ceiling** (upper bound)
- **SDF** (Standing Deposit Facility) as **floor** (lower bound)
- **Policy repo rate** in the middle
- **Corridor width:** 50 bps (25 bps above & below repo)

### 5.2 Rate Definitions

| Rate | Definition | Position in LAF Corridor |
|---|---|---|
| **Repo Rate** | Rate at which RBI provides liquidity under LAF against collateral of govt/approved securities | Mid-point of corridor (policy rate) |
| **SDF Rate** | Rate at which RBI accepts uncollateralised overnight deposits from LAF participants; replaced fixed reverse repo rate in **April 2022** | **Floor of LAF corridor** (25 bps below Repo) |
| **MSF Rate** | Penal rate at which banks can borrow overnight by dipping into SLR portfolio up to 2% | **Ceiling of LAF corridor** (25 bps above Repo) |
| **Bank Rate** | Rate at which RBI is ready to buy/rediscount bills; aligned with MSF rate under Sec 49 of RBI Act, 1934 | Same as MSF (changes automatically with MSF) |
| **Reverse Repo Rate** | Rate at which RBI absorbs liquidity from banks against collateral of govt securities | After SDF (April 2022), fixed rate reverse repo at RBI discretion |
| **CRR** (Cash Reserve Ratio) | % of Net Demand & Time Liabilities (NDTL) banks must maintain with RBI as cash | Statutory |
| **SLR** (Statutory Liquidity Ratio) | % of demand & time liabilities in India to be maintained in govt securities, cash, gold | Statutory |

### 5.3 Open Market Operations (OMO)

Outright purchase/sale of government securities for injection/absorption of durable liquidity.

## 6. Reserve Money (M0)

- Currency in circulation + Bankers' deposits with RBI + Other deposits with RBI
- Components: (1) Currency in Circulation, (2) Bankers' Deposits, (3) Other Deposits

---

## 7. Types of Money Supply

- **M1** = Currency with public + Demand deposits with banks + Other deposits with RBI
- **M2** = M1 + Savings deposits with post office
- **M3** = M1 + Time deposits with banks (= Broad money)
- **M4** = M3 + Total deposits with post office
