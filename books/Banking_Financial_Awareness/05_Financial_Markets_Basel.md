## 1. Conceptual Deep Dive: Financial Markets

### 1.1 What are Financial Markets?

Financial markets are platforms/venues that facilitate the exchange of financial instruments (stocks, bonds, currencies, derivatives) between buyers and sellers. They channel funds from savers to investors, enabling capital formation and economic growth.

### 1.2 Classification of Financial Markets

```
Financial Markets
├── Money Market (Short-term, ≤1 year)
└── Capital Market (Long-term, >1 year)
    ├── Primary Market (New issues)
    └── Secondary Market (Stock exchanges)
```

### 1.3 Money Market

The money market deals with short-term funds (≤1 year). It provides liquidity and supports the implementation of monetary policy.

**Money Market Instruments**:

| Instrument | Description | Tenure |
|---|---|---|
| **Call Money** | Overnight (1 day) funds | 1 day |
| **Notice Money** | 2-14 days | 2-14 days |
| **Term Money** | 15 days to 1 year | 15-365 days |
| **Treasury Bills (T-Bills)** | Zero coupon govt securities | 91-day, 182-day, 364-day |
| **Commercial Paper (CP)** | Short-term unsecured promissory note | 15-365 days; min ₹5 lakh |
| **Certificate of Deposit (CD)** | Short-term time deposit issued by banks | 7 days to 1 year; min ₹5 lakh |
| **Repo (Repurchase Agreement)** | Sale with agreement to repurchase | Short-term |
| **Reverse Repo** | Opposite of repo | Short-term |
| **CBLO (Collateralized Borrowing and Lending Obligation)** | Collateralized short-term loans managed by CCIL | Short-term |
| **Tri-Party Repo** | Market repo with clearing house | Short-term |
| **MIBOR (Mumbai Interbank Offered Rate)** | Benchmark overnight rate | Daily |

**Key Money Market Features**:
- Highly liquid
- Low risk (most instruments government-backed)
- Wholesale (large denominations)
- Anonymous trading
- Mainly institutional participants

### 1.4 Capital Market

The capital market deals with long-term funds (>1 year). It includes the primary market (new securities) and secondary market (trading existing securities).

**Components**:
- **Primary Market**: New securities issued (IPO, FPO, Rights, Bonus)
- **Secondary Market**: Trading of existing securities (BSE, NSE)

### 1.5 Issue Types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **IPO (Initial Public Offering)** | First time a company offers shares to public |
| **FPO (Follow-on Public Offering)** | Already listed company issuing more shares |
| **OFS (Offer for Sale)** | Promoters selling shares to public |
| **Rights Issue** | Existing shareholders given right to buy more shares |
| **Bonus Issue** | Free shares to existing shareholders |
| **QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement)** | To institutional investors only |
| **IDR (Indian Depository Receipt)** | Foreign company listing in India |

### 1.6 Stock Exchanges in India

**BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange)**:
- Established: **1875** (Asia's oldest stock exchange)
- Sensex (Sensitive Index): 30 companies
- ~5,500+ companies listed (as of 2025)
- Settled at Sensex ~80,000+ in 2025

**NSE (National Stock Exchange of India)**:
- Established: 1992
- Began trading: 1994
- Nifty 50 index: 50 stocks
- Largest exchange by trading volume (in India)
- Settled at Nifty ~24,000+ in 2025

**Other exchanges**:
- Calcutta Stock Exchange (oldest, 1908)
- BSE, NSE (national level)
- MCX (Multi Commodity Exchange)
- NCDEX (National Commodity & Derivatives Exchange)
- ICEX (Indian Commodity Exchange)

## 2. Regulatory Framework

### 2.1 SEBI — Securities and Exchange Board of India

**History**:
- **Non-statutory body**: Constituted 12 April 1988 (Government resolution)
- **Statutory body**: SEBI Act 1992; provisions came into force **30 January 1992**
- **Headquarters**: Mumbai
- **Current Chairman**: Tuhin Kanta Pandey (since Feb 2025) [⚠️ VERIFY current chair]

**Preamble**: "...to protect the interests of investors in securities and to promote the development of, and to regulate the securities market and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto"

**Functions** (Section 11 of SEBI Act, 1992):
- Regulate stock exchanges, brokers, sub-brokers, merchant bankers
- Register and regulate mutual funds, venture capital funds, collective investment schemes
- Prohibit insider trading and fraudulent trade practices
- Regulate substantial acquisition of shares and takeovers
- Conduct inquiries and audits
- Investor protection and education
- Regulate foreign portfolio investors (FPIs)

### 2.2 SEBI Structure
- Chairman (whole-time member)
- Two whole-time members
- One part-time member from Finance Ministry
- RBI nominee
- Total: 9 members (as per SEBI Act)

## 3. Mutual Funds

### 3.1 Overview

A Mutual Fund pools money from multiple investors to invest in securities (stocks, bonds, money market instruments). Managed by professional fund managers.

### 3.2 Regulation
- **SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996**
- **AMFI** (Association of Mutual Funds in India) — Industry body, HQ Mumbai

### 3.3 Types of Mutual Funds

**By Asset Class**:
1. **Equity Funds**: Growth, Value, Sectoral, Thematic
2. **Debt Funds**: Liquid, Ultra-short, Short-term, Corporate Bond, Gilt
3. **Hybrid Funds**: Balanced, Equity-oriented
4. **Solution-Oriented Funds**: Retirement, Children's
5. **Index Funds**: Track specific index (Nifty, Sensex)
6. **ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds)**: Listed on exchanges, passively managed
7. **Fund of Funds (FoF)**: Invests in other MFs

**By Structure**:
- **Open-ended**: Continuous subscription/redemption (most popular)
- **Close-ended**: Fixed tenure, listed
- **Interval**: Periodic subscription/redemption

### 3.4 SIP — Systematic Investment Plan
- Allows investing fixed amount at regular intervals (monthly/quarterly)
- Benefits of rupee-cost averaging
- Compounding effect
- Most popular retail investment mode

### 3.5 Key Mutual Fund Statistics (India, 2025-2026)
- **AUM (Assets Under Management):** ~₹75 lakh crore (as of mid-2025)
- **Folios:** ~17 crore investor folios
- **Number of MFs:** ~45 AMCs (Asset Management Companies)
- **SIP Inflows:** ₹25,000+ crore/month consistently in 2025

## 4. Derivatives

Derivatives are financial contracts whose value is derived from underlying assets (stocks, bonds, commodities, currencies, interest rates).

| Type | Description | Trading |
|---|---|---|
| **Forwards** | OTC, customized, no margin | OTC |
| **Futures** | Standardized, exchange-traded, daily settlement | Exchange |
| **Options** | Right but not obligation; Call/Put | Exchange |
| **Swaps** | OTC; exchange of cash flows (IRS, CCS, CDS) | OTC |

**Options Basics**:
- **Call Option**: Right to BUY at strike price
- **Put Option**: Right to SELL at strike price
- **Premium**: Price paid to buy option
- **Strike Price**: Pre-agreed price
- **In-the-Money (ITM)** / **At-the-Money (ATM)** / **Out-of-the-Money (OTM)**

## 6. Basel Norms / Accords

### 6.1 What is Basel?

The Basel Accords are international banking regulations set by the **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** — a committee of central banks and bank supervisors from major economies.

**Secretariat**: Bank for International Settlements (BIS), Basel, Switzerland
**Members**: 28 jurisdictions (as of 2024)
**Effect**: Recommendations, not directly enforceable — implemented via national laws

### 6.2 Basel I — 1988 Basel Capital Accord

**Published**: 1988
**Enforced**: G10 countries in 1992

**Key Features**:
- Focus on **Credit Risk** only
- **Minimum Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR): 8%** of risk-weighted assets (RWAs)
- Risk weights: 0%, 20%, 50%, 100%
- 1996 amendment: Added market risk framework

### 6.3 Basel II — New Capital Framework, 2004

**Published**: 2004
**Full enforcement**: 2007-2008 (in India: 2008-09)

**Key Features — Three Pillars**:
1. **Pillar 1: Minimum Capital Requirements**
   - Credit Risk (more sophisticated approaches: Standardized, Foundation IRB, Advanced IRB)
   - Market Risk
   - Operational Risk (Basic Indicator, Standardized, AMA)
2. **Pillar 2: Supervisory Review Process (SRP)**
   - ICAAP (Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process)
3. **Pillar 3: Market Discipline**
   - Disclosure requirements

### 6.4 Basel III — Post-2008 Reforms

**Published**: 2010 (original), revised 2017

**Key Reforms** (in response to 2008 financial crisis):
- **Higher Capital Quality**: Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) must be majority of capital
- **Capital Conservation Buffer**: 2.5% of RWAs (above minimum)
- **Countercyclical Buffer**: 0-2.5% (during excess credit growth)
- **Leverage Ratio**: 3% minimum (Tier 1 / Total Exposure)
- **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)**: 100% (stock of HQLA / net cash outflows over 30 days)
- **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)**: 100% (available stable funding / required stable funding)
- **Total Loss Absorbing Capacity (TLAC)**: For global systemically important banks (G-SIBs)

### 6.5 Basel IV / Final Reforms (2017 onwards)

**Published**: December 2017

**Key Changes**:
- **Standardized Approach for Credit Risk**: Revised risk-weights, removing internal models for certain exposures
- **Internal Models Approach** restrictions
- **Operational Risk**: Replaced Advanced Measurement Approach (AMA) with new Standardized Approach
- **Output Floor**: 72.5% of standardized approach output (for IRB banks)
- **Implementation**: Originally 2022, delayed to **1 January 2025** in some jurisdictions

### 6.6 India-Specific Implementation

| Standard | India Implementation |
|---|---|
| Basel I | 1992-93 onwards |
| Basel II | April 2008 (parallel run), full implementation 2009 |
| Basel III | Phased from 2013; LCR from Jan 2015, NSFR from Jan 2018 |
| Basel IV (Final) | RBI issued guidelines for implementation starting 1 April 2025 (phased) |

## 7. Other Important Market Infrastructure

### 7.1 Clearing Corporations

| Entity | Settlement |
|---|---|
| **CCIL** (Clearing Corporation of India Ltd) | Government securities, CBLO, forex |
| **NSCCL** (NSE Clearing Ltd) | NSE equity/derivatives |
| **ICCL** (Indian Clearing Corp Ltd) | BSE equity/derivatives |

### 7.2 Depositories

| Depository | Established | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **NSDL** (National Securities Depository Ltd) | 1996 | IDBI Bank, etc. |
| **CDSL** (Central Depository Services India Ltd) | 1999 | BSE, etc. |
| **Dematerialisation (DEMAT)** | Process of converting physical shares to electronic | Required for trading |

### 7.3 REIT and InvIT

- **REIT** (Real Estate Investment Trust): Invests in commercial real estate; mandatory listing; minimum 80% in completed rent-generating properties
- **InvIT** (Infrastructure Investment Trust): Invests in infrastructure projects (roads, power, telecom)
- Both regulated by SEBI; minimum 90% of distributable income paid out

### 7.4 Sovereign Green Bonds

- India issued its **first Sovereign Green Bond** in January 2023
- Used for funding eligible green projects (renewable energy, clean transport, etc.)
- 5-year and 10-year tenures
- Total issuance: ₹16,000 crore in FY23 (₹8,000 cr × 2 tranches)

## 8. Practice Questions (20 PYQs)

**Q1.** Basel Accords are issued by:
(a) World Bank (b) IMF (c) BIS (Bank for International Settlements) (d) WTO
**Answer**: (c) BIS

**Q2.** SEBI was established as a statutory body in:
(a) 1988 (b) 1990 (c) 1992 (d) 1995
**Answer**: (c) 1992

**Q3.** Under Basel III, the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) requirement is:
(a) 50% (b) 75% (c) 90% (d) 100%
**Answer**: (d) 100%

**Q4.** T-Bills are issued by:
(a) RBI on behalf of Central Government (b) SEBI (c) Commercial Banks (d) NABARD
**Answer**: (a) RBI on behalf of Central Government

**Q5.** Sensex is the index of:
(a) NSE (b) BSE (c) MCX (d) NCDEX
**Answer**: (b) BSE

**Q6.** Nifty is the index of:
(a) BSE (b) NSE (c) Both (d) Neither
**Answer**: (b) NSE

**Q7.** Commercial Paper (CP) is a:
(a) Long-term instrument (b) Short-term unsecured (c) Government security (d) Bank deposit
**Answer**: (b) Short-term unsecured

**Q8.** The minimum CAR under Basel I was:
(a) 4% (b) 6% (c) 8% (d) 10%
**Answer**: (c) 8%

**Q9.** CRISIL is a subsidiary of:
(a) Moody's (b) Fitch (c) S&P Global (d) None
**Answer**: (c) S&P Global

**Q10.** ICRA is associated with:
(a) S&P (b) Moody's (c) Fitch (d) DBRS
**Answer**: (b) Moody's

**Q11.** A Call Option gives the holder the right to:
(a) Sell at strike price (b) Buy at strike price (c) Hold the stock (d) Issue new shares
**Answer**: (b) Buy at strike price

**Q12.** Repo rate is the rate at which:
(a) RBI lends to banks (b) Banks lend to each other (c) Banks borrow from public (d) Public borrows from banks
**Answer**: (a) RBI lends to banks (technically: banks borrow against govt securities from RBI)

**Q13.** NSDL was established in:
(a) 1992 (b) 1996 (c) 1999 (d) 2003
**Answer**: (b) 1996

**Q14.** SIP stands for:
(a) Systematic Investment Plan (b) Standard Investment Product (c) Stock Investment Plan (d) None
**Answer**: (a) Systematic Investment Plan

**Q15.** Under Basel III, the Capital Conservation Buffer is:
(a) 1.5% (b) 2% (c) 2.5% (d) 3%
**Answer**: (c) 2.5%

**Q16.** Which is NOT a money market instrument?
(a) Treasury Bill (b) Commercial Paper (c) Equity Share (d) Certificate of Deposit
**Answer**: (c) Equity Share

**Q17.** Mutual Funds are regulated by:
(a) RBI (b) SEBI (c) IRDAI (d) PFRDA
**Answer**: (b) SEBI

**Q18.** India's first Sovereign Green Bond was issued in:
(a) 2020 (b) 2021 (c) 2022 (d) 2023
**Answer**: (d) January 2023

**Q19.** The countercyclical capital buffer under Basel III ranges from:
(a) 0-1% (b) 0-2.5% (c) 0-5% (d) 1-3%
**Answer**: (b) 0-2.5%

**Q20.** The Leverage Ratio under Basel III is:
(a) 1% (b) 2% (c) 3% (d) 5%
**Answer**: (c) 3%
